\title{L-functions for all meromorphic $\SLZ$ modular forms,\\
via manifestly finite contours}
\author{David A.\ McGady}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We give a single manifestly finite, regulator-free functional realizing in one object L-functions
quasi-periods and period polynomials of meromorphic modular forms on $\SLZ$, with generic 
poles throughout the upper half-plane $\HH$ and at the cusp of $\SLZ$. Additionally, our construction 
is preserves holomorphicity. It is a two-segment contour integral, 
$L^*(f,s) = L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) + L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0})$, where $L^*_T$ and $L^*_S$ 
have distinct integration kernels, and 
$\gamma^{S}_{\tau_0}$ is a path in $\HH$ from $S(\tau_0)$ to $\tau_0$, and the same for $\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$. 
For weakly holomorphic forms our L-function is independent of $\tau_0$ and of the path, and reproduces 
classical L-functions for holomorphic modular forms, reproduces the L-functions of weak modular forms 
in~\cite{BFK2011}, which previously needed non-holomorphic regularization to be defined, and reproduces 
Brown's quasi-periods~\cite{Brown2017}. For properly meromorphic modular forms with poles inside the 
interior of $\HH$, our L-function depends on the homotopy classes, defined in terms of pole-crossings. 
There is a natural choice of trivial homotopy class, which renders our L-functions canonical when the 
modular form has a pole in the interior of the fundamental domain, generalizing~\cite{1806}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and preliminary concepts}

Our primary goal in this note is to put forth a unifying generalization of the L-function 
associated to a given modular form. Our L-function naturally encompasses the classical 
case of L-functions associated to holomorphic modular forms~\cite{01-Apostol}, and 
seamlessly unifies Ref.~\cite{BFK2011}'s extension to L-functions of modular forms with 
poles at the cusp with Ref.~\cite{1806}'s extension to L-functions of modular forms with 
poles at finite locations, exclusively away from cusps. Secondarily, we show that our 
L-functions reproduce the numerical results for quasi-periods associated to weak cusp 
forms~\cite{Brown2017}. 

{\em Note:} Throughout this document, we refer to L-integrals and L-functions. The classical 
L-function-as-Dirichlet-series which has been suitably extended to the complex $s$ plane is 
simply not defined when the modular form that defines the L-function has poles either at the 
cusp or at finite locations within the upper half-plane. So, if there is ever a doubt, we state clearly: 
{\em any L-function in this note is defined by an L-integral}. The focus of this note is to define 
L-integrals for any integer weight $\SLZ$ modular form which may have poles at cusps or away 
from cusps.

\subsection{Modular forms}
Write ${\rm Re}(z)$ and ${\rm Im}(z)$ for the real and imaginary parts of a complex number 
$z\in \CC$. Let $\HH:=\{\tau\in\CC\mid {\rm Im}(\tau)>0\}$ denote the upper half-plane. $\SLZ$ 
is the group generated by $T := \left( \begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix} \right)$ 
and $S := \left( \begin{smallmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{smallmatrix} \right)$. 

There is a natural $\SLZ$ action on functions $f: \HH \to \CC$, graded by integers $k$: 
$(f|_k \gamma)(\tau) := (c \tau + d)^{-k}f(\gamma\cdot\tau)$ for all $\gamma = \left(\begin{smallmatrix}
a & b \\ c & d \end{smallmatrix}\right) \in \SLZ$. Similarly, $\gamma\cdot \tau := 
\frac{a \tau + b}{c \tau + d}$. 

In this vein, a {\em holomorphic modular form} of weight $k$ for $\SLZ$ is a 
holomorphic function $f:\HH\to \CC$ such that $(f|_k\gamma)(\tau) = f(\tau)$ 
for all $\gamma \in \SLZ$ (aka $f(\tau+1)=f(-\frac1\tau)\tau^{-k}=f(\tau)$) for 
all $\tau\in \HH$, and $f(\tau)={\cal~O}(1)$ as ${\rm Im}(\tau)\to \infty$, and 
which has no poles at finite locations within $\HH$. By virtue of the periodicity 
$f(\tau) = f(\tau+1)$ and holomorphicity, each holomorphic modular form has 
a q-series decomposition,
\begin{equation}
f(\tau) = \sum_n c_f(n) e^{2 \pi i n \tau} = \sum_n c_f(n) q^n~~,~~q:= e^{2 \pi i \tau}~~.
\end{equation}
In order for $f$ to be bounded as $\tau \to i \infty$, we conclude that $c_f(n) = 0$ 
for all $n < 0$, i.e. that $f = \sum_{n \geq 0} c_f(n) q^n$; for such modular forms $f$ 
we write $f \in M_k$. If a modular form $f$ additionally has a vanishing constant term, 
i.e. if $c_f(0) = 0$, then $f$ is a {\em cusp form}, 
and we write $f \in S_k$. Clearly, $S_k \subset M_k$. Further, $M_k$ is a  
finite-dimensional vector-space, as is $S_k$. Holomorphic $\SLZ$ modular forms must 
have even integer weights, and $k \geq 4$~\cite{01-Apostol}. 

A product of modular forms of weights $k_1$ and $k_2$ is a modular form of weight 
$k_1+k_2$, so $M_*:=\bigoplus_k  M_k$ is naturally a (graded) ring. This graded ring is 
freely generated by the two modular forms $E_4 \in M_4$ and $E_6 \in M_6$, the $k = 4, 6$
specializations of the {\em weight $k$ Eisenstein series} $E_k$, which may be defined as follows:
\begin{align}
E_k := \frac{1}{2}\sum_{(m,n)=1} \frac{1}{(m+n\tau)^k} = 1 - \frac{2k}{B_k}\sum_{n = 1}^{\infty} \sigma_{k-1}(n) q^n~,
\end{align}
where $\sum_{(m,n) = 1}$ denotes summing over all relatively prime $(m,n) \in \ZZ^2$, the Bernoulli 
number $B_n$ is the coefficient of $t^n/n!$ of the Laurent expansion of $t/(e^{t}-1)$ about $t = 0$, 
and $\sigma_m(n) := \sum_{d|n} d^m$ is the Divisor function where $d|n$ means $n/d \in \NN$. 
In short, $M_* = \CC[E_4, E_6]$. We now define the {\em modular discriminant}:
\begin{equation}
\Delta := \frac{E_4^3-E_6^2}{1728} = q + O(q^2) \in S_{12}~.
\end{equation}

Holomorphicity is most canonically relaxed via allowing a modular form to have a pole at the 
cusp of $\SLZ$, i.e. for a weight $k$ modular form for $\SLZ$ to have negative powers of $q$ 
in its q-series expansion. In this case, $f$ diverges as $\tau \to i \infty$ as $f \sim e^{-2 \pi N \tau}$, 
where $-N$ is the largest negative number such that $c_f(n)$ is non-zero. Such a modular form 
$f$ is called a {\em weak modular form}, and we write $f \in M_k^!$. If a weak modular form $f$ 
has a vanishing coefficient of $q^0$, i.e. if $c_f(0) = 0$, it is called a {\em weak cusp form} and 
we write $f \in S_k^!$. Clearly, $S_k^! \subset M_k^!$, $S_k \subset S_k^!$ and $M_k \subset M_k^!$. 
Two important weak cusp forms in this note are as follows: 
\begin{align}
J :=&~ \frac{E_4^3}{\Delta} - 744 = \frac{1}{q} + O(q) \in S_0^!~,~\\
\Dhat =&~ (J^2 + 24 J -393444) \Delta := \frac{1}{q} + O(q^2) \in S_{12}^!~.~
\end{align}
In this work, we further relax from even weakly holomorphic modular forms, and 
consider {\em meromorphic modular forms} of weight $k$ which are quotients: $f=\frac{f_1}{f_2}$ 
where $f_i\in M_{k_i}$, the denominator $f_2$ is not identically zero, and $k=k_1-k_2$. We 
write $F_k$ for the vector space of meromorphic modular forms of weight $k$, and set 
$F_*:=\bigoplus_k F_k$. Then $F_k$ vanishes unless $k$ is an even integer, and 
$F_*$ may be regarded as a ``graded field of fractions'' of $M_*$. 

\subsection{L-functions for holomorphic modular forms and periods for cusp forms}

For any $f \in S_k$, one may define the associated L-function:
\begin{align}
L(f,s) := \frac{(2 \pi)^s}{\Gamma(s)} L^*(f,s) \underset{f \in S_k}{:=} 
\frac{(2 \pi)^s}{\Gamma(s)} \int_0^{\infty} f(it) t^{s-1} dt = \sum_{n \geq 1} \frac{c_f(n)}{n^s}~.
\end{align}
A similar definition applies for $f \in M_k$. (The classical definition of $L^*(f,s)$ for $f \in M_k$ 
is the focus of Remark~\ref{natural-beauty-via-finiteness}, below.) Similarly, one may define a period 
polynomial for $f \in S_k$ in terms of this L-function,
\begin{align}
r_f(X, Y) := (2 \pi i)^{k-1} \sum_{\ell = 0}^{k-2} (-1)^\ell \binom{k-2}{\ell}\, i^{\ell+1}\, L^*(f,\ell+1)\,X^{k-2-\ell}Y^\ell~.
\end{align}
This polynomial, $r_f(X, Y) \in V_{k-2} := \CC[X, Y]_{k-2}$, has a natural action under $\SLZ$, 
$(P|_{\gamma})(X, Y) = P(a X + b Y, c X + d Y)$ for $\gamma = 
\left( \begin{smallmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{smallmatrix} \right) \in \SLZ$. This period polynomial 
can be decomposed into a basis of highly constrained polynomials, which are annihilated by $|(1+T)$ 
and $|(1 + U + U^2)$\cite{KZ1984}, where $U := TS$ generates an order-3 cyclic subgroup of $\SLZ$. 
We define the \emph{periods} of a cusp form to be coefficients of $r_f$ within this highly 
constrained sub-space $W$ of $V_{k-2}$, i.e. $W \subset V_{k-2}$.

{\bf Why this work?} As classically computed, these L-functions and associated period polynomials 
(when defined in terms of the special values of the associated L-function) are not defined when $f$ 
has poles either at the cusp of $\SLZ$ or within the interior of $\HH$. As stated above, there 
are extant, but partial and non-overlapping, extensions of the definition of $L(f,s)$ to $f \in M_k^!$~\cite{BFK2011} or 
$f \in F_k$ when $f$ is regular at the cusp of $\SLZ$~\cite{1806}. In this note, we provide a construction that unifies these disparate extensions.
We start with generic L-integrals in section~\ref{sec:Lint}, then specialize to $f \in M_k^!$ in section~\ref{sec:Lfun}, and finally explore the more general context of $f \in F_k$ in section~\ref{sec:mero}.

\section{A finite L-integral}\label{sec:Lint}


\begin{definition}\label{def:segments}
Let $\tau_0\in\HH$ and $f\in F_k$, let
$S=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
$T=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ denote 
the generators of $\Gamma=\SLZ$, and let $f^{-1}(\infty)$ be the locus 
of all poles of $f \in \HH$ (inclusive of the cusp of $\SLZ$).  
The \emph{$S$- and $T$-segments at $\tau_0$} are smooth paths in
$\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$ from $\sigma^{-1}\tau_0$ to $\tau_0$,
\begin{equation}
\gamma^S_{\tau_0}:\ -1/\tau_0\ \longrightarrow\ \tau_0,\qquad
\gamma^T_{\tau_0}:\ \tau_0-1\ \longrightarrow\ \tau_0.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Avoiding poles is automatic for $f \in M_k^!$, as long as the base-point $\tau_0$, 
is not at a cusp of $\SLZ$, i.e. there is only the trivial homotopy class. 
When $f \in F_k$, and $f$ has poles at finite and non-zero imaginary heights, 
integration contours fall into nontrivial homotopy classes.

\begin{definition}\label{def:kernel}
Let $\zeta(s,x)=\sum_{m\ge0}(m+x)^{-s}$ as a function 
$\zeta: \CC_s \times (\CC\setminus\NN)_x \to \CC$ denote the 
Hurwitz zeta function (meromorphic in $s$, holomorphic in $x\in\HH$). 
We define \emph{Hurwitz $T$-kernel} as
\begin{equation}
\tk(\tau,s):=\zeta(1-s,\tau+1)-e^{i\pi(s-1)}\,\zeta\big(1-(k-s),\tau+1\big).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Generic L-integral]
\label{def:Lint}
Let $f\in F_k$, $\tau_0\in\HH$, and $s\in\CC$. Further, let $\gamma^S_{\tau_0}$,
$\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$ be the $S$- and $T$-segments at $\tau_0$, as in
Definition~\ref{def:segments}, and let $\tk$ be as defined in 
Definition~\ref{def:kernel}. The \emph{L-integral} of $f$ is
\begin{equation}
L^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0})
:= L^*_S(f,s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}) + L^*_T(f,s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})
:=e^{-\pi i s/2}\left(\int_{\gamma^S_{\tau_0}}\!\!\!\! f(\tau)\tau^{s-1}\,d\tau
+\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}}\!\!\!\! f(\tau)\tk(\tau,s)d\tau\right)
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
We use this \emph{L-integral} to define a generalized 
\emph{L-function}:

\begin{definition}[Generic L-function]\label{def:Lfun}
Let $L^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ be as defined in Definition~\ref{def:Lint}. 
We define the generalized \emph{L-function} of $f\in F_k$ to be,
\begin{equation}
L(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0})
:=\frac{(2\pi)^s}{\Gamma(s)}\,L^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0}).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Having defined the L-integral and L-function, we prove they are manifestly finite for $f \in F_k$: 

\begin{lemma}[Finiteness of L-integrals]\label{lem:finite}
For $f\in F_k$, both $L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0})$ and 
$L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ in Definition~\ref{def:Lint} are each, 
separately finite for every $s\in\CC$, every $\tau_0\in\HH$, and for 
every path $\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Both segments are compact and lie in $\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, so $f$ is
continuous on them; $\tau^{s-1}$ is continuous on $\HH$, and $\tk(\tau,s)$ is continuous there
since $\zeta(\nu,\tau+1)$ is holomorphic in $\tau\in\HH$ (Definition~\ref{def:kernel}). A
continuous integrand on a compact path is integrable, for every $s\in\CC$.
\end{proof}

We now turn to path-dependence and $\tau_0$-independence of 
the L-integral and the L-function of $f \in F_k$. 
This holds for all $f \in M_k^!$:

\begin{proposition}[Basepoint \& path independence for $f \in M_k^!$]\label{prop:indep}
For $f\in M^!_k$ the value $L^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ is independent
of $\tau_0\in\HH$ and of the paths: with no interior poles, $\HH$ is simply connected,
so all admissible contours are homotopic. We write $L^*(f,s)$ for the common value and
$L(f,s):=\frac{(2\pi)^s}{\Gamma(s)}L^*(f,s)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With no interior poles the integrands $f(\tau)\tau^{s-1}$ and $f(\tau)\tk(\tau,s)$ are
holomorphic on $\HH$, which is simply connected; by Cauchy's theorem each integral is unchanged
under any deformation fixing its endpoints, hence depends on those endpoints alone. This is
path-independence.

For independence of $\tau_0$ show that variations in $\tau_0$ for the S-term and T-term in $L^*(f,s)$ 
cancel, i.e. that $\partial_{\tau_0} L^*_S(f,s:\gamma^S_{\tau_0}) = -\partial_{\tau_0} L^*_T(f,s:\gamma^T_{\tau_0})$. 
For $L^*_T$, note that the $T$-segment runs from $T^{-1}\tau_0=\tau_0-1$ to $\tau_0$ --- two endpoints a unit apart,
moving together with $\tau_0$ --- so by Leibniz's rule and the $T$-periodicity $f(\tau_0-1)=f(\tau_0)$,
\begin{equation}
\partial_{\tau_0} L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})
=f(\tau_0)\,\tk(\tau_0,s)-f(\tau_0-1)\,\tk(\tau_0-1,s)
=f(\tau_0)\,\delta_T\tk(\tau_0-1,s),
\end{equation}
where $\delta_T\tk(\tau,s):=\tk(\tau+1,s)-\tk(\tau,s)$. This is the whole role of $\delta_T$: the
unit gap between the endpoints turns the $\partial_{\tau_0}$ boundary term into a unit shift of
the kernel. That shift is elementary --- the kernel was built for it --- since
$\zeta(\nu,x+1)-\zeta(\nu,x)=-x^{-\nu}$ gives, on both terms at $x=\tau+1$,
\begin{align}
& \delta_T\tk(\tau,s)=-(\tau+1)^{s-1}+e^{i\pi(s-1)}(\tau+1)^{k-1-s}
\\
& \implies \partial_{\tau_0} L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) =f(\tau_0)\big[-\tau_0^{s-1}+e^{i\pi(s-1)}\tau_0^{k-1-s}\big].
\end{align}
The $S$-segment runs $-1/\tau_0\to\tau_0$; its lower endpoint moves with
$\partial_{\tau_0}(-1/\tau_0)=\tau_0^{-2}$, so by Leibniz, the modularity
$f(-1/\tau_0)=\tau_0^k f(\tau_0)$, and the phase $(-1/\tau_0)^{s-1}=e^{i\pi(s-1)}\tau_0^{1-s}$,
\begin{equation}
\partial_{\tau_0} L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}) 
=f(\tau_0)\tau_0^{s-1}-f(-1/\tau_0)\,(-1/\tau_0)^{s-1}\,\tau_0^{-2}
=f(\tau_0)\big[\tau_0^{s-1}-e^{i\pi(s-1)}\tau_0^{k-1-s}\big].
\end{equation}
Thus $\tau_0$-variations are equal and opposite, $\partial_{\tau_0}L^*_S = - \partial_{\tau_0}L^*_T$. Thus 
$\partial_{\tau_0}L^*=\partial_{\tau_0}L^*_S + \partial_{\tau_0}L^*_T=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Recovering the classical L-integral for $f \in S_k$]\label{rem:mellin}
Let $f\in S_k$. As $f^{-1}(\infty)$ does not include the cusp $i \infty$, 
$\lim_{\tau_0 \to i \infty} L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ exists. 
In this limit, the $S$-segment maps onto the standard 
L-function contour, $[0,i\infty]$. Simultaneously, as $f(it) \sim e^{-2\pi t}$ as 
$t \to \infty$, the $T$-segment vanishes exponentially. Thus, in this limit, $L^*(f,s)$ 
exactly maps onto the classical L-function integral~\cite{01-Apostol}:
\begin{equation}
\lim_{\tau_0\to i\infty}L^*(f,s)=\int_0^{\infty}\! t^{s-1}f(it)\,dt.
\end{equation}
\end{remark}

\section{L-functions for weak modular forms}\label{sec:Lfun}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:HGG}
Let $\Gamma(s, x) := \int_x^{\infty} e^{-t} t^{s-1} dt$ denote the {\em incomplete Gamma function}. For $n\ne0$, $\nu\in\CC$, and $z\in\HH$,
\begin{equation}
\int_{z}^{z+1}\! e^{2\pi inu}\,\zeta(\nu,u)\,du=e^{i \pi (1-\nu)/2} (2\pi n)^{\nu-1}\,\Gamma\big(1-\nu,-2\pi i n z\big).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\zeta(\nu,u)=\sum_{m\ge0}(m+u)^{-\nu}$ and shift $u\mapsto u+m$ in the $m$-th term; as
$e^{2\pi in(u+m)}=e^{2\pi inu}$ for integer $m$, the unit segments stack into one horizontal ray,
\begin{equation}
\int_z^{z+1}\! e^{2\pi inu}\zeta(\nu,u)\,du
=\sum_{m\ge0}\int_{z+m}^{z+1+m}\! e^{2\pi inv}v^{-\nu}\,dv
=\int_z^{z+\infty}\! e^{2\pi inv}v^{-\nu}\,dv.
\end{equation}
Substitute $w=-2\pi i n v$, so that $v=iw/(2\pi n)$, $dv=i\,dw/(2\pi n)$, and $e^{2\pi inv}=e^{-w}$:
the lower endpoint becomes $w=-2\pi i n z$, and for $n>0$ the ray rotates to $w\to+\infty$ along the
positive reals --- legal, since $e^{-w}w^{-\nu}$ decays in the right half-plane, so the closing arc
vanishes. With $v^{-\nu}=i^{-\nu}(2\pi n)^{\nu}w^{-\nu}$,
\begin{equation}
\int_z^{z+\infty}\! e^{2\pi inv}v^{-\nu}\,dv
=i^{1-\nu}(2\pi n)^{\nu-1}\!\int_{-2\pi i n z}^{\infty}\! e^{-w}w^{-\nu}\,dw
=e^{i \pi (1-\nu)/2}(2\pi n)^{\nu-1}\Gamma\big(1-\nu,-2\pi i n z\big).
\end{equation}
For $n<0$ the ray instead grows as ${\rm Im} v\to\infty$ and the rotation is illegal; but both sides are
analytic in $n$ and agree for $n>0$, so the identity continues to $n<0$ with $\Gamma$ on its principal
branch. At $n=0$ the prefactor $(2\pi n)^{\nu-1}$ is singular; that case is handled separately.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The L-function for $f \in M_k^!$]\label{thm:weakL}
For $f\in M^!_k$, we may set $\tau_0=i$, and prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weakL}
L^*(f,s)=-c_f(0)\left(\frac1s+\frac{i^k}{k-s}\right)
+\sum_{n\ne0}c_f(n)\left(\frac{\Gamma(s,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s}+i^k\frac{\Gamma(k-s,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}\right)~.
\end{equation}
Here, trivially, $L^*(f,s) = i^k L^*(f,k-s)$. The L-function is $L(f,s) := (2\pi)^s L^*(f,s) /\Gamma(s)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By path independence and base-point independence from Proposition~\ref{prop:indep}, 
we may freely set $\tau_0=i$. Here, $L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}) = 0$ (the $S$-contour 
vanishes in length and there are no nontrivial homotopy classes as $f \in M_k^!$ has no poles 
which a finite contour can wrap), and 
the L-integral simplifies dramatically:
\begin{equation}
L^*(f,s) = L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_i) =e^{-\pi i s/2}\int_{i-1}^{i} f(\tau)\tk(\tau, s)d\tau~. 
\end{equation}
We then expand $f$ in terms of its q-series, $\sum_n c_f(n)e^{2\pi in\tau}$,
and substitute the $u$-variable of Proposition~\ref{prop:HGG} as 
$u=\tau+1$, and note that $e^{2\pi i n(u-1)}=e^{2\pi i n u}$. 
When $n\neq0$, straightforward substitutions of 
$\nu, z$ into Proposition~\ref{prop:HGG} suffice: $1-\nu=s$ gives the 
contribution of the first term in $\tk(\tau, s)$, and $1-\nu = k-s$ gives the second: 
\begin{align}
e^{-\pi i s/2}\left(e^{\pi i s/2} \frac{\Gamma(s,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s} - e^{i\pi(s-1)}e^{\pi i (k-s)/2} \frac{\Gamma(k-s,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}\right) = \frac{\Gamma(s, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s} + i^k \frac{\Gamma(k-s, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}~.
\end{align}
We may then evaluate the $n=0$ mode's contribution follows from noting the 
closed form solution for the definite integral, 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hurwitz-antideriv}
\int_{i - 1}^{i}\! \zeta(\sigma, \tau + 1)\,d\tau
 = \int_i^{i + 1}\!\zeta(\sigma, u)\,du
 = \frac{i^{1 - \sigma}}{\sigma - 1}.
\end{equation}
This result follows from noting $\partial_a(\zeta(\sigma-1, a)/(1-\sigma)) = \zeta(\sigma,a)$ 
and $\zeta(\sigma-1, a+1) - \zeta(\sigma-1, a) = -a^{1-\sigma}$. 
Applying this to the two distinct terms of $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ at $\sigma = 1 - s$ and $\sigma = 1 - (k - s)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:const-mode-integral}
e^{-\pi i s/2}\int_{i - 1}^{i}c_f(0)\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
= -c_f(0)\left(\frac{1}{s}  + \frac{i^k}{k - s}\right).
\end{equation}
Combining the $n = 0$ contributions with the $n \neq 0$ contributions reproduces Eq.~\eqref{eq:weakL}. 
Finally, the functional equation, $L^*(f,s)=i^k L^*(f,k-s)$, follows by direct inspection.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Explicit S- and T-contours for general $\tau_0$}

\begin{lemma}[$L^*_T$ for $M^!_k$]\label{lem:Tclosed-Mk}
For $f\in M^!_k$, and $\tau_0 \in \HH$ away from cusps, 
$L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ evaluates to:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T-segment-closed-Mk}
L_T^*(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})
= -c_f(0)\!\left(\frac{(\tau_0/i)^{s}}{s} + i^k\frac{(\tau_0/i)^{k-s}}{k-s}\right) + \sum_{n \ne 0}\! c_f(n)\!\left(\frac{\Gamma(s, -2\pi i n\tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^s} + i^k\frac{\Gamma(k - s, -2\pi i n\tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}\right).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expand $f = \sum_n c_f(n)\,q^n$ on the compact segment
$[\tau_0 - 1, \tau_0]$, and restrict attention to the sub-sum, $\sum_{n \neq 0}$. 
For each such $n \neq 0$, apply the Proposition~\ref{prop:HGG} in
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s) = \zeta(1 - s, \tau + 1) - e^{i\pi(s-1)}\zeta(s - (k-1), \tau + 1)$
at $\nu = 1 - s$ and $\nu = s - (k - 1)$. The phases for the second term work-out 
identically as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL}.  

For $n = 0$, the constant $c_f(0)$ multiplies $e^{-\pi i s/2}\!\int_{\tau_0 - 1}^{\tau_0}\!\widetilde k_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau$;
the Hurwitz antiderivative $\int_{\tau_0 - 1}^{\tau_0}\zeta(\sigma, \tau + 1)\,d\tau = \tau_0^{1 - \sigma}/(\sigma - 1)$
--- from $\partial_\tau\zeta(\sigma - 1, \tau + 1) = -(\sigma - 1)\zeta(\sigma, \tau + 1)$ and the shift
$\zeta(\sigma - 1, \tau_0 + 1) - \zeta(\sigma - 1, \tau_0) = -\tau_0^{1 - \sigma}$ --- applied to the two
halves of $\widetilde k_T$ at $\sigma = 1 - s$ and $\sigma = s - k + 1$ yields
$-e^{-\pi i s/2}\tau_0^{\,s}/s = -(\tau_0/i)^s/s$ and $-e^{\pi i s/2}\tau_0^{\,k-s}/(k-s) = -i^k(\tau_0/i)^{k-s}/(k-s)$ respectively.
\end{proof}


\begin{corollary}[$L^*_T$ as $\tau_0 \to i \infty$]\label{lem:cusp-decay}
Let $f \in S^!_k$, where $f = \sum_{n \geq -N} c_f(n)q^n$ for some $-N \in \ZZ$. 
The T-contour's contribution to $L^*(f,s)$ is given by 
Lemma~\ref{lem:Tclosed-Mk}. As $\tau_0 = it$ goes to the cusp $i\infty$,
the $T$-contour scales exactly as $f(it)$ scales:
$L_T^*(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) \sim f(it) \sim e^{2\pi N t}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For the asymptotics, $\Gamma(\sigma, z) \sim z^{\sigma - 1}e^{-z}$
as $|z| \to \infty$ for $|\arg z| < \tfrac{3\pi}{2}$ \cite[\S8.11]{DLMF}
gives $|c_f(n)|$-weighted go as $e^{-2\pi n\,{\rm Im}(\tau_0)}$.  The smallest 
mode $n$ in the q-series, i.e. $n = -N$, dominates the scaling of $f(it)$ and of 
$L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{i t})$. Combining these results yields the claimed asymptotics.
\end{proof}

Now, exploiting $\tau_0$-independence of $L^*(f, s)$, its explicit value as given in 
Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL}, and the explicit T-contribution $L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ trivially 
implies clean, closed-form expressions for $L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0})$:

\begin{corollary}[$L^*_S$ for $M_k^!$]\label{cor:Sclosed}
For $f\in M^!_k$ and $\tau_0\in\HH$, 
$L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0})$ evaluates to
\begin{align}
L_S^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0})
&= c_f(0)\left(\frac{(\tau_0/i)^{\,s}-1}{s}
+ i^k\frac{(\tau_0/i)^{\,k-s}-1}{k-s}\right) \label{eq:S-segment-explicit}\\
&+ \sum_{n\ne0} c_f(n)\!\left(\frac{\Gamma(s,2\pi n)-\Gamma(s,-2\pi i n\tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^s}
+ i^k\,\frac{\Gamma(k-s,2\pi n)-\Gamma(k-s,-2\pi i n\tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}\right)~. \nonumber
\end{align}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Recall $L^*(f, s) - L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) = L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0})$. 
Now, $L^*(f,s)$ for $f \in M_k^!$ is given by Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL}, and 
$L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ is given by Lemma~\ref{lem:Tclosed-Mk}. 
Their difference yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:S-segment-explicit}.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Classical Mellin recovery and canonical uniqueness.}\label{sec:Connection-w-previous-weak-MF-l-functions}

As discussed in Remark~\ref{rem:mellin}, for $f \in S_k$, taking the cusp-limit
$\tau_0 \to i\infty$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL} collapses the $S$-segment 
onto the classical Mellin contour $[0, i\infty]$ while via Corollary~\ref{lem:cusp-decay} 
the $T$-segment vanishes exponentially as $L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) \sim f(\tau_0) 
\sim e^{-2\pi {\rm Im}(\tau_0) N}$ (as $f \in S_k \Leftrightarrow f = \sum_{n \geq N} c_f(n) q^n$ with $N \geq 1$). 
I.e., here, one recovers the classical L-integral of $f$ without obstruction:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Mellin-recovery}
\lim_{\tau_0 \to i\infty}\!
L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})
\;=\; \int_0^\infty\! t^{s - 1}\,f(it)\,dt~.
\end{equation}  
This story changes when $f = 1/q^N (1 + O(q)) \in S_k^!$ for some $N \geq 1$. 

In short, when $f$ has a polar q-series, it has a pole at the cusp of $\SLZ$. Thus, 
the limit $\tau_0 \to i \infty$, which was crucial to recovering the classical Mellin contour 
in Remark~\ref{rem:mellin} simply does not exist.

However, it can still be instructive to examine $L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$, 
and $L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0})$ as $\tau_0$ goes towards $i \infty$. 
In this context, while of course the sum $L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) + 
L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}) = L^*(f,s)$ is manifestly invariant under 
changes of the base-point, each of these two terms can indeed change values. Indeed, 
Corollary~\ref{lem:cusp-decay} clearly shows that as $\tau_0 \to i \infty$, when $f \in S_k^!$ 
has negative powers of $q$ in its q-series, then $L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) \sim 
e^{2 \pi N {\rm Im}(\tau_0)}$. Discarding $\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$ therefore does violence to the result: 
When the $S$-contour is isolated from its T-partner, and $\tau_0 \to i \infty$, it diverges. This is the precise
reason that~\cite{BFK2011} needed to regularize their divergent L-integral representation for properly weak 
cusp forms. 

That said, there is one further aspect of our L-function even in the classical 
case of $f \in M_k$ that is worthy of remark:

\begin{remark}[Recovering the classical L-integral for $f \in M_k$]\label{natural-beauty-via-finiteness}
In textbook treatments~\cite{01-Apostol}, when $f \in M_k$, its L-integral is defined as
\begin{align}
L^*(f,s)|_{\rm cl} :=&~ \int_0^\infty \big( f(it) - c_f(0) \big) t^{s-1} dt = \frac{\Gamma(s)}{(2 \pi)^s} \sum_{n = 1}^{\infty} \frac{c_f(n)}{n^s} \\
=& -c_f(0) \bigg(\frac{1}{s} + \frac{i^k}{k-s} \bigg) + \sum_{n = 1}^{\infty} c_f(n)\left(\frac{\Gamma(s,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s} + i^k \frac{\Gamma(k-s, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}\right) \nonumber
\end{align}
Classically, this constant-term subtraction is needed because $\int_0^\infty t^{s-1} dt$ is a pure 
divergence, so the q-series cannot commute with the integration contour.

However, as is clear from our general Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL} for L-integrals for $f \in M_k^!$, 
and from the specific results of the S- and T-contours in Lemma~\ref{lem:Tclosed-Mk} and 
Corollary~\ref{cor:Sclosed}, our L-integral requires no such explicit subtraction of the constant 
term $-c_f(0)$. Thus, not only does our prescription naturally handle divergences at poles when 
$f \in M_k^!$ has polar terms in its q-series, but it also obviates the need to by-hand remove the 
constant mode from the kernel of the L-integral that is needed when defining L-functions in terms 
of the classical Mellin contour, $\int_0^\infty$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Period polynomials, periods and quasi-periods}

Because $L^*(f,s)$ is canonical for every $f\in S^!_k$, and because period polynomials 
for a given modular form can be written in terms of their L-functions evaluated at integer-$s$ 
in the critical strip, they need no separate construction.

\begin{definition}[Period polynomial for $S_k^!$]\label{def:rf}
For $f\in S^!_k$ and $n=k-2$, the \emph{period polynomial} is
\begin{equation}
r_f(X,Y):=(2\pi i)^{n+1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}(-1)^\ell\binom{n}{\ell}\,i^{\ell+1}\,L^*(f,\ell+1)\,X^{n-\ell}Y^\ell.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Period polynomial as a contour integral]\label{prop:periodint}
Let $n:=k-2$. With $(X-\tau Y)^n$ in place of the L-weight $\tau^{s-1}$, and
$\mathbf K_T(\tau;X,Y):=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\binom{n}{\ell}(-1)^\ell\,\tk(\tau,\ell+1)\,X^{n-\ell}Y^\ell$
in place of $\tk$, the same two segments assemble $r_f$ directly:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf}
r_f(X, Y)=(2\pi i)^{n+1}\Big[\int_{\gamma^S_{\tau_0}} f(\tau)\,(X-\tau Y)^n\,d\tau
+\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}} f(\tau)\,\mathbf K_T(\tau;X,Y)\,d\tau\Big].
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By Definition~\ref{def:Lint}, the $X^{n-\ell}Y^\ell$ coefficient of the bracket is
\begin{equation}
r_f|_{X^{n-\ell}Y^\ell} = (-1)^\ell\binom{n}{\ell} i^{\ell+1} 
\big[
L^*_S(f, \ell+1) 
+
L^*_T(f, \ell+1)
\big]
=(-1)^\ell\binom{n}{\ell}\,i^{\ell+1}L^*(f,\ell+1)~.
\end{equation}
This matches Definition~\ref{def:rf} term by term, and proves the claim.
\end{proof}

Having now defined the period polynomial associated to a weak cusp form $f \in S_k^!$,
we now seek to find its {\em (quasi-)periods}. First, a number of helpful definitions. For 
starters, let $f \in S_k^!$, $\tau_0 \in \HH$ and $\gamma \in \SLZ$, and define
\begin{equation}
C_\gamma(f; \tau_0):= (2\pi i)^{n+1}\int_{\gamma^{-1}\tau_0}^{\tau_0} \omega_f ~~,~~{\rm where}~~\omega_f := f(\tau) (X - \tau Y)^n d\tau~~.\label{cocycleR1}
\end{equation} 
Finally, it is helpful to define two slight abstractions related to $\mathbf K_T(\tau;X,Y)$ and to 
$L_T^*(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$:
\begin{align}
\chi(\tau;X,Y)&:=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\binom{n}{\ell}(-1)^\ell\,\zeta(-\ell,\tau+1)\,X^{n-\ell}Y^\ell , \label{cocycleR2}\\
E(f; \tau_0)&:=(2\pi i)^{n+1}\!\!\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}}\!\! f(\tau)\,\chi(\tau; X, Y) d\tau ~.\label{cocycleR3}
\end{align}
For each $\tau$, $\chi(\tau;\cdot,\cdot)\in V_{k-2}=\CC[X,Y]_{k-2}$; the $\SLZ$-slash
$(P|_\gamma)(X,Y):=P(aX+bY,cX+dY)$ acts on the polynomial variables alone, holding $\tau$ fixed:
\begin{equation}
\chi|_\gamma(\tau;X,Y):=\chi\big(\tau;\,aX+bY,\,cX+dY\big),\qquad
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\SLZ .
\end{equation}
Thus $|_T$ sends $(X,Y)\mapsto(X+Y,Y)$ with $\tau$ untouched. Being linear and $\tau$-independent,
$|_\gamma$ commutes with $\int\!d\tau$, so slashing the polynomial $E$ agrees with slashing $\chi$
under the integral --- the two occurrences of $|_\gamma$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:cocycle} denote the same
operation.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cocycle}
Let $f\in S_k^!$, $\tau_0\in\HH$, and let $\omega_f$, $C_\gamma(f; \tau_0)$, $\chi(\tau; X, Y)$, and
$E(f;\tau_0)$ be as defined in Eqs.~\eqref{cocycleR1}, \eqref{cocycleR2} and \eqref{cocycleR3}. 
Then $\hat C_\gamma:=C_\gamma-(E|_\gamma-E)$ is a $1$-cocycle for $\SLZ$ with 
$\hat C_S=r_f$ and $\hat C_T=0$. 
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First, we prove $\hat C_\gamma$ is a cocycle. Path-additivity and covariance of $\omega_f$ give the Eichler--Shimura cocycle relation
$C_{\gamma\delta}=C_\gamma|_\delta+C_\delta$~\cite{BCD2018,KZ1984}; as $\gamma\mapsto E|_\gamma-E$ is a
coboundary, $\hat C$ is a cocycle.

Having established it is a cocyle, we now show that $\hat C_S=r_f$. At $s=\ell+1$, Definition~\ref{def:kernel} reads
$\tk(\tau,\ell+1)=\zeta(-\ell,\tau+1)-(-1)^\ell\zeta(-(n-\ell),\tau+1)$; assembling $\mathbf K_T$ and
re-indexing $\ell\mapsto n-\ell$ in the second term (with $n$ even) gives $\mathbf K_T=\chi-\chi|_S$. So the
$T$-term of $r_f$ is $E-E|_S$, and by Proposition~\ref{prop:periodint}
$r_f=C_S+(E-E|_S)=C_S-(E|_S-E)=\hat C_S$.

Finally, we show that $\hat C_T=0$. The Hurwitz shift $\zeta(-\ell,\tau)=\zeta(-\ell,\tau+1)+\tau^\ell$ 
gives the point-wise identity $\chi(\tau-1;X,Y)-\chi(\tau;X,Y)=(X-\tau Y)^n$; applied twice it shows
$A(\tau):=\chi(\tau;X+Y,Y)-\chi(\tau-1;X,Y)$ satisfies $A(\tau-1)=A(\tau)$. Crucially, because $A(\tau)$ 
is a polynomial, and because $A(\tau) = A(\tau+1)$ for generic $\tau$, then $A(\tau)$ is constant: 
$\partial_\tau A(\tau) = 0 \Leftrightarrow A(\tau) = A_0$. Thus, 
\begin{align}
\hat C_T
&=C_T-(E|_T-E)
\nonumber\\ &
=(2\pi i)^{n+1}\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}} f(\tau) (X - \tau Y)^n d\tau
- (2\pi i)^{n+1}\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}} f(\tau)\big[\chi|_T(\tau; X, Y)-\chi(\tau; X, Y)\big] d\tau \nonumber\\
&=(2\pi i)^{n+1}\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}} f(\tau)\,\bigg(\big[\chi(\tau-1; X, Y)-\chi(\tau; X, Y)\big] - \big[\chi(\tau; X+Y, Y)-\chi(\tau; X, Y)\big] \bigg) d\tau \nonumber\\
&=(2\pi i)^{n+1}\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}} f(\tau)\,\big[\chi(\tau-1; X, Y)-\chi(\tau; X+Y, Y)\big] d\tau 
=-(2\pi i)^{n+1} \int_{\tau_0-1}^{\tau_0} f(\tau)A(\tau)\,d\tau \nonumber\\
&=-(2\pi i)^{n+1}A_0 \int_{\tau_0-1}^{\tau_0} f(\tau)\,d\tau
=-(2\pi i)^{n+1}A_0\,c_f(0)=0,
\end{align}
as all elements $f \in S_k^!$ must have $c_f(0) = 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:w}
$W:=\{w\in\CC[X,Y]_{k-2}: w|_{(1+S)}=w|_{(1+U+U^2)}=0\} \subset V_n$
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:rfW}
For $f\in S_k^!$, and $W \subset V_n$ from Definition~\ref{def:w}, the period polynomial $r_f(X, Y) \in W$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\hat C_\gamma$ be the parabolic cocycle of Lemma~\ref{lem:cocycle}. Thus, $r_f=\hat C_S$ 
and $\hat C_T=0$. Now, note that $\hat C_\gamma(f; \tau_0) = 0$ when $\gamma = \pm I$ for the 
simple reason that when $\gamma = \pm I$, $\hat C_\gamma$ is a closed-contour integral of a function 
which has no poles in $\HH$. Finally, direct computation, starting from the identities $-I = U^3 = S^2$ 
and using the cocycle law $\hat C_{\gamma\delta}=\hat C_\gamma|_\delta+\hat C_\delta$ and clever 
use of $\hat C_T = 0$, shows $r_f|_{(1+S)}=r_f|_{(1+U+U^2)}=0$:
\begin{align}
&\hat C_U = \hat C_{TS} = \hat C_T|_S + \hat C_S = \hat C_S = r_f~,  \\
&0 = \hat C_{S^2} = \hat C_S|_S + \hat C_S = \hat C_S|_{(1+S)} = r_f|_{(1+S)}~,\\
&0 = \hat C_{U^3} = \hat C_U|_{UU}+\hat C_{UU}=\hat C_U|_{U^2}+\big(\hat C_U|_U+\hat C_U\big)=\hat C_U|_{(1+U+U^2)}=r_f|_{(1+U+U^2)}~.
\end{align}
Thus, $r_f \in W \subset V_n$, and finishes the proof. %
\end{proof}

To define periods and quasi-periods, we recall the standard fact 
that $\dim(W)=2\dim(S_k)+1$ (see, e.g.~\cite{KZ1984}), and define 
the inner-product:
\begin{align}\label{inner}
\langle P,Q\rangle:=\sum_a(-1)^a\binom{n}{a}^{-1}p_a\,q_{n-a}~.
\end{align}
Now for the basis elements for $k$ where $\dim(S_k) = 1$:
\begin{definition}\label{def:Wpm}
Let $\dim S_k=1$, $n:=k-2$, $p_0(X):=X^n-1$, $B_\ell := \tfrac{\ell!}{2\pi i}\oint \tfrac{t^{-\ell}}{e^{t}-1}\,dt$, and
$B^0_m(X):=\sum_{i\ne1}\binom{m}{i}B_i X^{m-i}$. Motivated by~\cite[Thm.~1$'$]{KZ1984}, we define:
\begin{align}
W^{(k)}_-(X)&:={\rm pp}\left(\tfrac{1}{n-1}\big(B^0_{n-1}(X)+X^nB^0_{n-1}(\tfrac1X)\big)
-\tfrac13\big(B^0_3(X)+X^nB^0_3(\tfrac1X)\big)\right),\\
W^{(k)}_+(X)&:={\rm pp}\left(\tfrac12\big(B^0_2(X)-X^nB^0_2(\tfrac1X)\big)
+\tfrac1n\big(B^0_n(X)-X^nB^0_n(\tfrac1X)\big)-c\,p_0(X)\right),\\
v_0^{(k)}(X)&:= p_0(X) - W^{(k)}_+(X) ~\big(\langle W^{(k)}_+,\,p_0\rangle/\langle W^{(k)}_+,\,W^{(k)}_+\rangle\big)~,
\end{align}
where ${\rm pp}$ acts on any polynomial with rational coefficients by multiplying by all
coefficient-denominators and dividing by the gcd, $c$ eliminates the $p_0(X)$ component
of $W^{(k)}_+$, and $\langle P, Q\rangle$ as in Eq.~\eqref{inner}.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:Wbasis}
For $k$ with $\dim S_k=1$, the space $W$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:rfW} has $\dim W=3$, and $\{
W^{(k)}_+$, $W^{(k)}_-$, $v_0^{(k)}\}$ is an orthogonal basis of $W$ under $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ from Eq.~\eqref{inner}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First, let $W_\pm^{(k)}(X, Y) := Y^{k-2} W_\pm^{(k)}(X/Y)$, and let $v_0^{(k)}(X, Y) := Y^{k-2} v_0^{(k)}(X/Y)$.

Now, by Eichler--Shimura, $\dim W=2\dim S_k+1=3$. Theorem~1$'$ of~\cite{KZ1984} gives the odd and even period polynomials of the cusp form $R_n\propto\Delta$ as modified-Bernoulli combinations; its $n=2$ case is $r^-(R_2)$, odd under $X\mapsto-X$, and its $n=1$ case is $r^+(R_1)$, even. Our $W^{(k)}_-$ is the primitive part of $r^-(R_2)$, and $W^{(k)}_+$ the primitive part of $r^+(R_1)$ with its Eisenstein $p_0$-component removed by $c$; both lie in $W$, as does $p_0=X^{k-2}-Y^{k-2}$ (even). The three are independent, hence a basis of $W$. Since $k-2$ is even, $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is symmetric and pairs opposite $X\mapsto-X$ parities to zero; the odd $W^{(k)}_-$ is thus orthogonal to the even $W^{(k)}_+$ and $v_0^{(k)}$, while $v_0^{(k)}\perp W^{(k)}_+$ by construction. Hence $\{W^{(k)}_+,W^{(k)}_-,v_0^{(k)}\}$ is orthogonal.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Periods and Quasi-Periods]\label{def:periods}
Let $k$ be such that $\dim(S_k) = 1$, $n:= k-2$, $W^{(k)}_\pm(X, Y) := Y^n W^{(k)}_\pm(X/Y)$ be as in Definition~\ref{def:Wpm}, and $r_f(X, Y)$ be as in Definition~\ref{def:rf}. Now, define the two numbers $\omega^\pm(f)$ as follows: 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:project}
\omega^\pm(f):=\frac{\langle r_f,W^{(k)}_\pm\rangle}{\langle W^{(k)}_\pm,W^{(k)}_\pm\rangle},
\end{equation}
where $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is as in Eq.~\eqref{inner}. When $f$ is a cusp form, $\omega^\pm(f)$ are its {\em periods}; when $f$ is a weak cusp form, $\omega^\pm(f)$ are its {\em quasi-periods}, sometimes denoted $\eta^\pm(f)$.
\end{definition}
Now, having defined our periods and quasi-periods, we use them together with our L-functions 
to extract periods and quasi-periods for $f \in S_k^!$ (when $\dim(S_k) = 1$). To this effect, 
we recall the notation of~\cite{DukeJenkins}. Decomposing the modular weight 
$k = 12 \lambda + k'$ where $k' \in \{ 0, 4, 6, 8, 10, 14\}$, we see $\lambda = \dim(S_k)$ when 
$\lambda \geq 0$. For any $\lambda$, we may denote $f_{k, m} := 1/q^m + O(q^{\lambda+1}) \in M_k^!$. 
When $\dim(S_k) = 1$, we have $f_{k,m} = 1/q^m + O(q^2)$. Here, define 
$\Delta_k := f_{k,-1} = q + O(q^2) \in S_k$ and $\Dhat_k := f_{k,1} = 1/q + O(q^2) \in S_k^!$.

{\rm pd}_k := \left( \begin{matrix}

\end{matrix}\right) ~~~~,~~~~

\end{align}

\section{Meromorphic modular forms}\label{sec:mero}

For $f\in F_k$ with poles in $\HH$, the two segment integrals of
Definition~\ref{def:Lint} depend on their contours; we show their sum depends only on the
homotopy class of the pair.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:indepHomotopy}
For $f\in F_k$ and $\tau_0\in\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, the L-integral
$L^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ depends only on the homotopy class of its
two segments in $\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, and not on the basepoint
$\tau_0$. At fixed $\tau_0$ each of $L^*_S,L^*_T$ is separately path-independent.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At fixed $\tau_0$ the integrands $f(\tau)\tau^{s-1}$ and $f(\tau)\tk(\tau,s)$ are
holomorphic on $\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, so Cauchy's theorem makes each
of $L^*_S,L^*_T$ constant on the homotopy class of its own segment. Under a motion of the
basepoint, $\partial_{\tau_0}L^*$ is evaluated at the moving endpoints alone; the
computation of Proposition~\ref{prop:indep} --- which uses only $f(\tau_0-1)=f(\tau_0)$ and
$f(-1/\tau_0)=\tau_0^{k}f(\tau_0)$ --- cancels the boundary terms of $L^*_S$ against those
of $L^*_T$, the interior poles lying off the contours. Hence $\partial_{\tau_0}L^*=0$ along
any motion in $\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, while each summand may 
separately vary.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:class}
For $f\in F_k$ and $\tau_0\in\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, let
$[\gamma^S_{\tau_0}]$, $[\gamma^T_{\tau_0}]$ denote the homotopy classes of the two
segments in $\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$.
\end{definition}

Thus, with $\gamma^S_{\tau_0} \in [\gamma^S_{\tau_0}]$ and 
$\gamma^T_{\tau_0} \in [\gamma^T_{\tau_0}]$, by Proposition~\ref{prop:indepHomotopy} 
the value
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lclass}
L^*\big(f,s;[\gamma^S_{\tau_0}],[\gamma^T_{\tau_0}]\big) 
:= L^*\big(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0} , \gamma^T_{\tau_0}\big)
= L^*_S(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0}) + L^*_T(f,s;\gamma^T_{\tau_0})
\end{equation}
is independent of the chosen representative paths and independent of $\tau_0$ (subject to 
natural constraints of $\tau_0$ monodromies around singular points, which move between 
homotopy classes).

We would like to speak of ``the'' L-integral. Homotopy classes of course split this into 
L-integrals for contours in different homotopy classes. And, so, in what follows, we define 
the reference homotopy classes: 
\begin{definition}[Reference homotopy class for $f \in F_k$]\label{def:null_homotopy}
Let $f\in F_k$, let $\tau_0$ be the base-point of $L^*(f,s)$, and let $f^{-1}(\infty)$ be the
poles of $f$. If $f$ has no poles on the line ${\rm Im}(\tau) = 1$, choose $\delta = 0$. 
Else, choose $\delta>0$ such that no pole lies in the strip $1<{\rm Im}(\tau)\leq 1+\delta$. 
Set $\hat\tau_0^f:=i(1+\delta)$. The \emph{reference homotopy class}
$[\hat\gamma_f]=[\hat\gamma_f^T]+[\hat\gamma_f^S]$ is:
\begin{itemize}
\item $[\hat\gamma_f^T]$: contains the horizontal segment from $T^{-1}\hat\tau_0^f$ to $\hat\tau_0^f$;
\item $[\hat\gamma_f^S]$: contains the near-vertical path from $S^{-1}\hat\tau_0^f$ to $\hat\tau_0^f$
along the imaginary axis, taking a pole at $i$ by principal value.
\end{itemize}
For $\delta=0$, the reference paths match the paths $\gamma^S_i\!:\! i \!\to\! i$ and $\gamma^T_i\!:\! (i\!-\!1)\!\to\! i$ for $L^*(f,s)$ when $f \in M_k^!$.
\end{definition}

%\smallskip\noindent For $f\in F_k$ with real $q$-coefficients, the reference class yields
%$\omega^+(f)\in\mathbb{R}$ and $\omega^-(f)\in i\mathbb{R}$: the axis-symmetric contour respects the
%reflection $\tau\mapsto-\bar\tau$, and the one on-axis pole (at $i$) reaches $\omega^\pm$ only through
%$(X-iY)^{k-2}$ --- which the period pairing annihilates when $k\equiv2\bmod4$ (there $W_\pm(i,1)=0$),
%or through $\widehat P_i$ with the real weights $(-2\pi i)^m a_{-m}$ when $4\mid k$.

With this definition, we may thus unambiguously write
\begin{align}
L^*(f,s) := L^*(f, s; [\hat\gamma_f^S], [\hat\gamma_f^T])~.
\end{align} 
Now, having fixed the reference value $L^*(f,s)$ in the reference
homotopy class of Definition~\ref{def:null_homotopy}, consider
two paths $\gamma^S_{\tau_0}$ and $\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$ which may 
be in any homotopy class. Further, let $X_S(p),X_T(p)\in\ZZ$ count 
the windings $\gamma^S_{\tau_0}$, $\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$
has about each pole $\tau_p\in f^{-1}(\infty)$ relative to the reference-class.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:wall}
Let $f\in F_k$, $r_S(f, \tau_p) := \Res_{\tau=\tau_p} (f(\tau) \tau^{s-1})$ 
and $r_T(f, \tau_p) := \Res_{\tau=\tau_p} (f(\tau) \tk(\tau, s))$. Then, 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wall}
L^*\big(f,s;[\gamma^S_{\tau_0}],[\gamma^T_{\tau_0}]\big)
= L^*(f,s)
+ 2\pi i\,e^{-\pi i s/2}\sum_{\tau_p\in f^{-1}(\infty)}\Big[
X_S(\tau_p) r_S(f, \tau_p)
+ X_T(\tau_p) r_T(f, \tau_p) \Big].
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Two pairs whose winding data differ by one unit at a single pole $p$, on one segment,
bound a loop encircling $p$ once; the residue theorem adds $2\pi i$ times the residue of
that segment's integrand, $f(\tau) \tau^{s-1}$ for $\gamma^S$ or $f(\tau)\tk(\tau, s)$ for $\gamma^T$,
and the prefactor $e^{-\pi i s/2}$ of Definition~\ref{def:Lint} multiplies through.
Summing over crossings gives \eqref{eq:wall}.
\end{proof}

This content provides the basics to discuss our finite L-functions for $f \in F_k$. 
However, we wish to present these L-functions in terms of their q-series coefficients, 
just as we have done for L-functions for $f \in M_k^!$ in the previous section. To do 
this, we must introduce polylogarithmic projections of Ref.~\cite{1806}:

This polylog projection isolates the q-series contribution of a given pole of an $f \in F_k$ 
into a known special function, an integral of a polylogarithm of negative index, and leaves 
behind an object $(I-\widehat P)f$ with that one pole removed. For our purposes, we find 
it essential to project-out all poles at, and above, the line ${\rm Im}\,\tau=1$. We must do 
this because the reference contours which describe our reference homotopy classes are, 
at a minimum, contour integrals of $f$ across the horizontal segment from $i -1$ to $i$. 
Were we to allow poles at/above this line, q-series diverges on the contour 
($|c_f(n)|\gtrsim n^{m-1}e^{2\pi n\,{\rm Im}\,\tau_p}$ for a pole of order $m$~\cite{1806}). 

Within this general context, we lay-out the starting definitions and facts:

\begin{definition}\label{def:proj}
%Let $f\in F_k$ have, at a pole $\tau_p$ of order $m_p$, the Laurent principal part
%$\sum_{m=1}^{m_p} a^{(p)}_{-m}\,(\tau-\tau_p)^{-m}$. With the polylogarithm
%$\Li_s(x):=\sum_{n\ge1}x^n n^{-s}$, the \emph{polylog projection} of $f$ at $\tau_p$ is
%\begin{equation}
%\widehat P_{\tau_p}f(\tau):=\sum_{m=1}^{m_p} r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)\,
%\Li_{1-m}\big(e^{2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)}\big),
%\end{equation}
%its weights $r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)$ fixed by matching principal parts with $f$ at $\tau_p$; and
%$\widehat P:=\sum_{{\rm Im}\tau_p\ge1}\widehat P_{\tau_p}$.
Let $f\in F_k$ have, at a pole $\tau_p$ of order $m_p$, the Laurent principal part
$\sum_{m=1}^{m_p} r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)\,(\tau-\tau_p)^{-m}$; the \emph{pole weights}
$r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)$ are thus its Laurent coefficients. With the polylogarithm
$\Li_s(x):=\sum_{n\ge1}x^n n^{-s}$, the \emph{polylog projection} of $f$ at $\tau_p$ is
\begin{equation}
\widehat P_{\tau_p}f(\tau):=\sum_{m=1}^{m_p} r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)\,
\frac{(-2\pi i)^m}{(m-1)!}\,\Li_{1-m}\big(e^{2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)}\big),
\end{equation}
and $\widehat P:=\sum_{{\rm Im}\tau_p\ge1}\widehat P_{\tau_p}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:proj}
%The weights $r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)$, $1\le m\le m_p$, exist and are unique, and
%$(I-\widehat P_{\tau_p})f$ is holomorphic at $\tau_p$.
%The weights are $r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)=(-2\pi i)^m a^{(p)}_{-m}/(m-1)!$, $1\le m\le m_p$, and
%$(I-\widehat P_{\tau_p})f$ is holomorphic at $\tau_p$.
The normalized block $\frac{(-2\pi i)^m}{(m-1)!}\Li_{1-m}\big(e^{2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)}\big)$ has
principal part exactly $(\tau-\tau_p)^{-m}$, so $\widehat P_{\tau_p}f$ matches the principal part of
$f$ and $(I-\widehat P_{\tau_p})f$ is holomorphic at $\tau_p$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
%$\Li_{1-m}\big(e^{2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)}\big)$ has a pole of order exactly $m$ at $\tau_p$, so
%matching the principal part of $f$ is a triangular---hence uniquely solvable---linear system
%relating $r^*_{f,\tau_p}(1),\dots,r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)$ to $a^{(p)}_{-1},\dots,a^{(p)}_{-m}$; equal
%principal parts leave $(I-\widehat P_{\tau_p})f$ regular at $\tau_p$.
%By the small-$t$ expansion $\Li_{1-m}(e^{-t})=(m-1)!\,t^{-m}+O(1)$ with $t=-2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)$, each
%$\Li_{1-m}\big(e^{2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)}\big)$ has a \emph{pure} pole of order $m$ at $\tau_p$, namely
%$\tfrac{(m-1)!}{(-2\pi i)^m}(\tau-\tau_p)^{-m}$ with no lower-order poles. Matching the principal part
%of $f$ is therefore diagonal, giving $r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)=(-2\pi i)^m a^{(p)}_{-m}/(m-1)!$ and leaving
%$(I-\widehat P_{\tau_p})f$ regular at $\tau_p$.
By the small-$t$ expansion $\Li_{1-m}(e^{-t})=(m-1)!\,t^{-m}+O(1)$ with $t=-2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)$, the
block $\frac{(-2\pi i)^m}{(m-1)!}\Li_{1-m}\big(e^{2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)}\big)$ has a \emph{pure} pole
$(\tau-\tau_p)^{-m}+O(1)$ at $\tau_p$, with no lower-order poles. Summing against the Laurent
coefficients $r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)$ therefore reproduces the principal part of $f$ term by term,
leaving $(I-\widehat P_{\tau_p})f$ regular at $\tau_p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:cRf}
With $\widehat P$ as in Definition~\ref{def:proj}, let $c_{Rf}(n)$ be the Fourier
coefficients of the \emph{pole-projected form} $(I-\widehat P)f$ in its expansion valid for large
${\rm Im}\tau$:
\begin{equation}
(I-\widehat P)f(\tau)=\sum_n c_{Rf}(n)\,q^n,\qquad q=e^{2\pi i\tau}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}\label{def:Finfty}
$F(\infty)$ is the space of meromorphic functions $g:\HH\to\CC$ with $g(\tau+1)=g(\tau)$, finitely many
poles in each compact subset of the strip $|{\rm Re}\,\tau|\le\tfrac12$, and bounded pole order at $q = 0$. 
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:projspace}
$F_k\subset F(\infty)$ and $(I-\widehat P)f\in F(\infty)$. Every pole of $(I-\widehat P)f$ has
${\rm Im}\,\tau<1$, with a maximal height $y_b<1$ (set $y_b=0$ if $(I-\widehat P)f$ is
holomorphic).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A weakly holomorphic form is $T$-periodic, meromorphic, and $O(e^{C{\rm Im}\tau})$ at the cusp,
so $F_k\subset F(\infty)$; the projected polylogs $\Li_{1-m}(e^{2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)})$ share
these properties, so $(I-\widehat P)f\in F(\infty)$ (and leaves $F_k$, being non-modular). By
Lemma~\ref{lem:proj}, $\widehat P$ cancels the principal part of every pole with
${\rm Im}\tau\ge1$, so the poles of $(I-\widehat P)f$ lie strictly below ${\rm Im}\tau=1$;
finite in each compact set and accumulating only at ${\rm Im}\tau=0$, they have a maximal
height $y_b<1$.
\end{proof}

The Fourier coefficients of a pole-projected form are controlled by its highest pole, an
estimate we import from~\cite{1806}:
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:growth}
With $y_b$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:projspace} and $m_b$ the maximal order among poles at height
$y_b$, there is a $C>0$ such that, for every $\varepsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:growth}
C\,n^{m_b-1}e^{2\pi n y_b}\;\le\;|c_{Rf}(n)|\;\le\;e^{2\pi n(y_b+\varepsilon)},
\end{equation}
the upper bound for all large $n$, the lower for infinitely many $n$. In particular
$|c_{Rf}(n)|\,e^{-2\pi n}\to0$ geometrically, at base $e^{-2\pi(1-y_b-\varepsilon)}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is the pole-height/order Fourier-growth estimate of~\cite{1806} for a $T$-periodic
meromorphic function, applied to $(I-\widehat P)f$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:projspace}).
\end{proof}

For $n\ne0$, define
\begin{align}\label{eq:gc}
G_{s,k}(n):=\frac{\Gamma(s,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s}
+i^k\,\frac{\Gamma(k-s,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}.
\end{align}
Each pole with ${\rm Im}\,\tau_p\ge1$ contributes one projected polylog to $L^*(f,s)$, whose
L-integral we now record.

\begin{definition}\label{def:IN}
For a pole projected at $z$ with ${\rm Im}\,z>1$, let $I_N(s,z)$ be the L-integral of the
projected polylog $\Li_{-N}(e^{2\pi i(\cdot-z)})$ in the minimal class $[\hat\gamma_f^S],[\hat\gamma_f^T]$
of Definition~\ref{def:null_homotopy}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:IN}
I_N(s,z):=L^*\big(\Li_{-N}(e^{2\pi i(\cdot-z)}),\,s;\,[\hat\gamma_f^S],[\hat\gamma_f^T]\big).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:polylogcf}
Let $f\in F_k$ have a pole of order $p$ at $z$, ${\rm Im}\,z>1$, with projection weights
$r^*_{f,z}(m)$ (Definition~\ref{def:proj}). Polylog inversion
$\Li_{-N}(w)=(-1)^{N+1}\Li_{-N}(1/w)-\delta_{N,0}$ ($N\in\NN_0$) resums \eqref{eq:IN} into
(each exponentially convergent, base $e^{2\pi(1-{\rm Im}(z))}$)
\begin{align}
L^*(\widehat P_z f,\,s;\,[\hat\gamma_f^S],[\hat\gamma_f^T])&=\sum_{m=1}^{p} r^*_{f,z}(m)\,\frac{(-2\pi i)^m}{(m-1)!}\,I_{m-1}(s,z),\label{eq:lem4.12.whole.z}\\
I_N(s,z)&=\delta_{N,0}\Big(\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}\Big)
+(-1)^{N+1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^N e^{2\pi inz}\,G_{s,k}(-n).
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:indepHomotopy} we may evaluate $I_N(s,z)$ in the $\delta\to0$
representative of the minimal class; there the projected polylog --- regular at $i$ since
${\rm Im}\,z>1$ --- has vanishing $\gamma^S$-integral and $\hat\gamma_f^T\to[i-1,i]$. On
$[i-1,i]$ one has $|e^{2\pi i(\tau-z)}|>1$, so the defining series of $\Li_{-N}$ diverges;
inversion rewrites it as $(-1)^{N+1}\sum_{n\ge1}n^Ne^{2\pi in(z-\tau)}$, convergent since
$|e^{2\pi i(z-\tau)}|<1$, and term-by-term integration against $\tk$ gives
$\sum_n n^N e^{2\pi inz}G_{s,k}(-n)$, the mode-$(-n)$ block of Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL}; for
$N=0$ the leftover $-1$ in $\Li_0(w)=-1-\Li_0(1/w)$ contributes $\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}$.
The rate is $e^{-2\pi({\rm Im}\,z-1)}$, from $\Gamma(s,-2\pi n)\sim(2\pi n)^{s-1}e^{2\pi n}$
against $|e^{2\pi inz}|=e^{-2\pi n\,{\rm Im}\,z}$.
\end{proof}

Extracting similar results for $L^*$ evaluated on the polar terms for poles on the line 
${\rm Im}(\tau) = 1$ requires more care. To do this away from the special point $z = i$, we
find it helpful to define the following two functions:
\begin{align}
\Pi_N(s,z)&:=\sum_{\lambda=0}^{N+1}\frac{\Li_{\lambda+1-N}\big(e^{2\pi i(z-i)}\big)}{(-2\pi)^{\lambda+1}} \big\{ (s-1)_{(\lambda)}+i^k(k-s-1)_{(\lambda)} \big\},\label{eq:tailpiece1}\\
R_N(s,z)&:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^N e^{2\pi inz}\Big[(s{-}1)_{(N{+}2)}\tfrac{\Gamma(s{-}N{-}2,{-}2\pi n)}{(-2\pi n)^{s}}
+i^k(k{-}s{-}1)_{(N{+}2)}\tfrac{\Gamma(k{-}s{-}N{-}2,{-}2\pi n)}{(-2\pi n)^{k-s}}\Big], \label{eq:tailpiece2}
\end{align}
where $(a)_{(\lambda)}:=a(a-1)\cdots(a-\lambda+1)$ is the falling factorial ($(a)_{(0)}:=1$), $\Li_s$ the
polylogarithm of Definition~\ref{def:proj}, and $\Gamma,\Gamma(a,x)$ the gamma and upper incomplete
gamma, on principal branches~\cite{DLMF}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:polylogedge}
%With $\Pi_N$ and $R_N$ as in
%Eqs.~\eqref{eq:tailpiece1} and~\eqref{eq:tailpiece2}, for ${\rm Im}\,z>1$ and every $s\in\CC$,
%\begin{equation}\label{eq:twolayer}
%%I_N(s,z)=\delta_{N,0}\Big(\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}\Big)
%L^*\big(\Li_{-N}(e^{2\pi i(\cdot-z)}),\,s\big)=I_N(s,z)=\delta_{N,0}\Big(\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}\Big)
%+(-1)^{N+1}\big[\Pi_N(s, z)+R_N(s, z)\big],
%\end{equation}
%and the right side extends $I_N(s,\cdot)$ continuously to the edge ${\rm Im}\,z=1$ for
%$z\notin i+\ZZ$.
Let $f\in F_k$ have a pole of order $p$ at $z$ with projection weights $r^*_{f,z}(m)$
(Definition~\ref{def:proj}). With $\Pi_N,R_N$ as in \eqref{eq:tailpiece1}--\eqref{eq:tailpiece2},
for ${\rm Im}\,z>1$ and every $s\in\CC$,
\begin{align}
L^*(\widehat P_z f,\,s;\,[\hat\gamma_f^S],[\hat\gamma_f^T])&=\sum_{m=1}^{p} r^*_{f,z}(m)\,\frac{(-2\pi i)^m}{(m-1)!}\,I_{m-1}(s,z),\label{eq:lem4.13.whole.z}\\
I_N(s,z)&=\delta_{N,0}\Big(\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}\Big)
+(-1)^{N+1}\big[\Pi_N(s, z)+R_N(s, z)\big],\label{eq:twolayer}
\end{align}
and the second line's right side extends $I_N(s,\cdot)$ continuously to ${\rm Im}\,z=1$ for
$z\notin i+\ZZ$.
\end{lemma}

The crux of this Lemma is to show the existence of the smooth continuation of $I_N(s, z)$ 
from Lemma~\ref{lem:polylogcf}, derived for ${\rm Im}(z) > 1$, to the ${\rm Im}(z) = 1$-line 
away from the elliptic point $z = i$. This elliptic point requires special care, which must focus 
on, below. 

\begin{proof}
%Write $w:=e^{2\pi i(z-i)}$ and $Q:=e^{2\pi iz}=e^{-2\pi}w$, so $|Q|\le e^{-2\pi}$ and $|w|\le1$, with
%$|w|=1$ exactly on the edge $\mathrm{Im}\,z=1$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:polylogcf},
We manipulate the main result of Lemma~\ref{lem:polylogcf},
\begin{align}
I_N(s,z)=\delta_{N,0}\Big(\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}\Big)+(-1)^{N+1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^N e^{2\pi inz} \left(
\frac{\Gamma(s,-2\pi n)}{(-2\pi n)^{s}}+i^k\frac{\Gamma(k-s,-2\pi n)}{(-2\pi n)^{k-s}} \right)~,
\end{align}
Our proof proceeds in two steps. First, we isolate the possible sources of divergence on the line ${\rm Im}(z) = 1$ into a finite sum of polylogarithms $\Li_{\lambda+1-N}(e^{2 \pi i (z-i)})$ for $\lambda = 0, \ldots, N+1$. To do this, we use the recurrence relation~\cite{DLMF} for each fixed-$n$ term, 
\begin{align}
\Gamma(a,x)=e^{-x}\sum_{\lambda=0}^{N+1}(a-1)_{(\lambda)}x^{a-1-\lambda}+(a-1)_{(N+2)}\Gamma(a-N-2,x)
\end{align}
to each gamma in $G_{s,k}(-n)$ at $x=-2\pi n$. Dividing each incomplete-Gamma term by $(-2\pi n)^{s}$
and $(-2\pi n)^{k-s}$ yields, then, a common factor of $1/(-2\pi n)^{\lambda+1}$ for each term in the finite sum. 
Further, this finite-sum is proportional to $e^{2\pi n}$; we leave the trailing incomplete gammas as-is:
\begin{align}
G_{s,k}(-n) = 
\left\{
e^{2\pi n}\sum_{\lambda = 0}^{N+1} \frac{(s-1)_{(\lambda)}}{(-2\pi n)^{\lambda+1}}
{+} \frac{(s{-}1)_{(N{+}2)} \Gamma(s{-}N{-}2,{-}2\pi n)}{(-2\pi n)^s} 
\right\} {+}i^k(s{\to}k{-}s)~.
\end{align}
Now, we consider the $n$-sum $G_{s,k}(-n)$, starting with the falling factorial terms:
\begin{align}
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n^N e^{2\pi i n z} e^{2 \pi n} \left(  \sum_{\lambda = 0}^{N+1} \frac{(s-1)_{(\lambda)}}{(-2\pi n)^{\lambda+1}}{+}i^k(s{\to}k{-}s) \right) = \sum_{\lambda = 0}^{N+1} \frac{\Li_{\lambda+1-N}(e^{2 \pi i (z -i)})}{(-2\pi)^{\lambda+1}} \big\{ (s-1)_{(\lambda)} + i^k (k- s-1)_{(\lambda)}\big\}~,
\end{align}
i.e., $\Pi_N(s,z)$ of \eqref{eq:tailpiece1}, while the reduced-gamma terms remaining are 
$R_N(s, z)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:tailpiece2}, which together establish the lemma's
identity~\eqref{eq:twolayer}.

The second main step of our proof is to show that $\Pi_N(s, z)$ is regular as 
${\rm Im}(z) \to 1$ for $z \neq i$, while $R_N(s, z)$ is regular for all $z$ with 
${\rm Im}(z) = 1$. We treat each piece in turn, beginning with $R_N$.


Each term of $R_N$ is internal to the $G_{s,k}(-n)$ in the mode $\sum e^{2\pi inz} G_{s,k}(-n)$ 
from Lemma~\ref{lem:polylogcf}. Clearly, $|e^{2\pi inz}|=e^{-2\pi n\,{\rm Im}\,z}$. Thus, the 
incomplete gamma's exponential growth cancels against the exponentially 
falling $e^{2 \pi i n z}$ factors. As $n \to \infty$, we have:
\begin{align}
\left|n^N e^{2\pi i n z} \frac{\Gamma(s{-}N{-}2,{-}2\pi n)}{({-}2\pi n)^s}\right| &
{\sim}\left| \frac{n^N e^{2\pi i n z} ({-}2\pi n)^{s{-}N{-}3}e^{2\pi n}}{({-}2\pi n)^s}\right|
{\sim}\left| \frac{e^{2\pi i n z}e^{2\pi n}}{ ({-}2\pi)^{3{+}N} n^3}\right|
{\sim} \frac{e^{2\pi n (1{-}{\rm Im}(z))}}{(2\pi)^{N{+}3} n^3}%~,
\end{align}
where the detailed asymptotics of $\Gamma(s, x)$ in the first scaling relation follow 
from~\cite[\S8.11]{DLMF}. (The $i^k (s \rightarrow k-s)$-partner follows identically.)
Hence the $n$-th term of $R_N$ is $O\!\big(n^{-3}e^{-2\pi n({\rm Im}\,z-1)}\big)$, uniformly for
${\rm Im}\,z\ge1$. There $e^{-2\pi({\rm Im}\,z-1)}\le1$, so
$\sum_{n}n^{-3}e^{-2\pi n({\rm Im}\,z-1)}\le\zeta(3)$: the series converges absolutely and uniformly,
up to and including the edge ${\rm Im}\,z=1$, so $R_N$ is continuous there.

For $\Pi_N$, recall that it is a sum over $N+2$ polylogarithms of integer index,
$\Pi_N(s, z) = c_2(s) \Li_2(w) + c_1(s) \Li_1(w) + ... + c_{1{-}N}(s) \Li_{1{-}N}(w)$, where $w := e^{2\pi i (z-i)}$. 
Each $\Li_a$ is single-valued and analytic on the cut plane
$\CC\setminus[1,\infty)$: for $a\le0$ it is rational with a pole of order $1-a$ at $w=1$;
$\Li_1(w)=-\log(1-w)$ has a logarithmic branch point at $w=1$; and $\Li_2(w)$ is finite 
at its branch point $w=1$. All three singularities sit at the endpoint
$w=1$, and the cuts of $\Li_1,\Li_2$ run along the ray $[1,\infty)$. The edge is the unit circle
$|w|=1$, which meets that radial cut only at $w=1$; approaching it from inside the disc
(${\rm Im}\,z>1$) gives the analytic boundary value of each $\Li_a$ at every $w\ne1$. Hence
$\Pi_N$ is analytic along the whole edge except at $z\in i+\ZZ$, where each index-$\le1$ term 
diverges. With the continuity of $R_N$, \eqref{eq:twolayer} extends
$I_N(s,\cdot)$ continuously to ${\rm Im}\,z=1$ for $z\notin i+\ZZ$.
\end{proof}


The one edge point the formula misses is $z=i$, where $\Pi_N$ is singular ($w=1$); we
compute $I_N(s,i)$ directly. Its finiteness rests on a relation among the Laurent
coefficients of $f$ at the $S$-fixed point.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:Laurent}
Let $f\in F_k$ have a pole of order $P$ at $i$, with pole weights $r^*_{f,i}(m)$
(Definition~\ref{def:proj}), i.e.\ principal part $\sum_{m=1}^{P}r^*_{f,i}(m)(\tau-i)^{-m}$.
Modularity $f(-1/\tau)=\tau^k f(\tau)$ is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Laurent}
%(-1)^r\sum_{m=r}^P a_{-m}\binom{m}{r}i^{m-r}
%=i^k\sum_{m=r}^P a_{-m}\binom{k}{m-r}(-i)^{m-r},\qquad r=1,\dots,P.
%\sum_{m=r}^{P} a_{-m}\binom{m}{r}\,i^{m}
%=i^{k}\sum_{m=r}^{P} a_{-m}\binom{k}{m-r}\,i^{-m},\qquad r=1,\dots,P.
\sum_{m=r}^{P} r^*_{f,i}(m)\binom{m}{r}\,i^{m}
=i^{k}\sum_{m=r}^{P} r^*_{f,i}(m)\binom{k}{m-r}\,i^{-m},\qquad r=1,\dots,P.
\end{equation}
The leading relation ($r=P$) is $\big((-1)^P-i^k\big)r^*_{f,i}(P)=0$, so the pole order matches
$k\bmod4$ ($P$ odd $\Leftrightarrow k\equiv2$, $P$ even $\Leftrightarrow 4\mid k$); at
$P=1$ this is $(1+i^k)r^*_{f,i}(1)=0$, and for a double pole the $r=1$ relation is
$r^*_{f,i}(1)=\tfrac{i(k-2)}{2}r^*_{f,i}(2)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\tau=i+\varepsilon$. Then $-1/\tau-i=-\varepsilon/(1-i\varepsilon)$, so the principal
part of $f(-1/\tau)$ is $\sum_m r^*_{f,i}(m)(i-\varepsilon^{-1})^m$ and that of $\tau^k f(\tau)$ is
$i^k(1-i\varepsilon)^k\sum_m r^*_{f,i}(m)\varepsilon^{-m}$. Matching the coefficient of
$\varepsilon^{-r}$ through
$(i-\varepsilon^{-1})^m=\sum_j\binom mj i^{m-j}(-1)^j\varepsilon^{-j}$ and
$(1-i\varepsilon)^k=\sum_l\binom kl(-i)^l\varepsilon^l$ gives
$(-1)^r\sum_{m=r}^P r^*_{f,i}(m)\binom{m}{r}i^{m-r}=i^k\sum_{m=r}^P r^*_{f,i}(m)\binom{k}{m-r}(-i)^{m-r}$;
dividing through by $i^r$ (with $-1=i^2$ and $-i=i^{-1}$) yields~\eqref{eq:Laurent}.
%$(1-i\varepsilon)^k=\sum_l\binom kl(-i)^l\varepsilon^l$ gives \eqref{eq:Laurent}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:FP}
For $N\ge0$ and any cutoff $J\ge N+2$, with $H_N:=\sum_{r=1}^N\tfrac1r$ and
$c^+_j(s):=(s-1)_{(j)}+i^k(k-s-1)_{(j)}$, set
\begin{align}\label{eq:Eplus}
\mathcal E^+_N(s):=\sum_{n\ge1}n^N e^{2\pi n}\Big[(s-1)_{(J)}\tfrac{\Gamma(s-J,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s}
+i^k(k-s-1)_{(J)}\tfrac{\Gamma(k-s-J,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}\Big],
\end{align}
and then
\begin{align}\label{eq:FP}
\mathrm{FP}_N(s,k):=\frac{c^+_N(s)\,H_N}{(2\pi)^{N+1}}
+\sum_{\substack{0\le j\le J\\ j\ne N}}\frac{c^+_j(s)\,\zeta(j+1-N)}{(2\pi)^{j+1}}
+\frac{(-1)^N N!}{(2\pi)^{N+1}}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\frac{(-1)^t(s-1)_{(t)}}{t!\,(N-t)}
+\mathcal E^+_N(s).
\end{align}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:wallvalue}
%Let $f\in F_k$ have a pole of order $P$ at $i$ with principal part
%$\sum_{m=1}^P a_{-m}(\tau-i)^{-m}$, and $\widehat P_i f$ its projection. The pole's
%contribution to $L^*(f,s)$ in the minimal class is
%\begin{equation}\label{eq:wallvalue}
%L^*(\widehat P_i f,\,s)=r^*_{f,i}(1)\Big(\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}\Big)
%+\sum_{m=1}^P r^*_{f,i}(m)\,\mathrm{FP}_{m-1}(s,k),
%\end{equation}
%Let $f\in F_k$ have a pole of order $P$ at $i$ with pole weights $r^*_{f,i}(m)$
%(Definition~\ref{def:proj}), and $\widehat P_i f$ its projection. The pole's
%contribution to $L^*(f,s)$ in the minimal class is
%\begin{equation}\label{eq:wallvalue}
%L^*(\widehat P_i f,\,s)=-2\pi i\,r^*_{f,i}(1)\Big(\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}\Big)
%+\sum_{m=1}^P \frac{(-2\pi i)^m r^*_{f,i}(m)}{(m-1)!}\,\mathrm{FP}_{m-1}(s,k),
%\end{equation}
%entire in $s$ but for the simple poles at $s=0,k$ carried by $r^*_{f,i}(1)$; the individually
%divergent contour pieces cancel by the relations of Lemma~\ref{lem:Laurent}.
Let $f\in F_k$ have a pole of order $P$ at $i$ with pole weights $r^*_{f,i}(m)$
(Definition~\ref{def:proj}), and $\widehat P_i f$ its projection. Evaluated in the minimal class at
basepoint $i(1+\delta)$, the pole's contribution to $L^*(f,s)$ has the finite $\delta\to0$ limit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wallvalue}
L^*(\widehat P_i f,\,s):=\lim_{\delta\to0}L^*\big(\widehat P_i f,\,s;\,i(1+\delta)\big)
=\sum_{m=1}^P \frac{(-2\pi i)^m r^*_{f,i}(m)}{(m-1)!}\,\mathrm{FP}_{m-1}(s,k),
\end{equation}
entire in $s$. The limit exists precisely because the pole weights obey the relations of
Lemma~\ref{lem:Laurent} at the $S$-fixed point $i$: they cancel the individually divergent
contour pieces.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
%Evaluate on the minimal-class contour with basepoint $i(1+\delta)$ and let $\delta\to0$.
%On the $T$-segment the pole at $i$ lies below the contour; inverting each
%$\Li_{1-m}(e^{2\pi i(\tau-i)})$ and integrating against $\tk$ yields the
%harmonic-, $\zeta$-, and $\mathcal E^+$-terms of $\mathrm{FP}_{m-1}$ together with a part
%$\sim\delta^{-(m-1)},\dots,\log\delta$; the $S$-segment, passing to the left of $i$, gives the
%mirror divergences. Summed over $m$ with weights $r^*(m)=(-2\pi i)^m a_{-m}/(m-1)!$, the
%divergent parts form a linear combination of the $a_{-m}$ that vanishes precisely by the
%modularity relations of Lemma~\ref{lem:Laurent}, leaving the $\mathrm{FP}$-sum of
%\eqref{eq:wallvalue}; the simple part ($m=1$) additionally carries the $-1$-leftover pole
%$-2\pi i\,a_{-1}(\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s})$, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:polylogcf}. The cutoff $J$
%is immaterial: raising it moves terms between the $\zeta$-sum and $\mathcal E^+_N$, which
%remains convergent as in Lemma~\ref{lem:polylogedge}.
Fix $s$; evaluate in the minimal class at basepoint $i(1+\delta)$, $\delta>0$, and set
$u:=2\pi i(\tau-i)$.

\emph{Block expansion.} At its branch point $w=1$ the polylogarithm expands as
$\Li_{1-m}(e^{u})=(m-1)!\,(-u)^{-m}+\sum_{p\ge0}\tfrac{\zeta(1-m-p)}{p!}u^{p}$
(\cite[\S25.12]{DLMF}); multiplying by $\tfrac{(-2\pi i)^m}{(m-1)!}$ and using $-u=-2\pi i(\tau-i)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blockexp}
\frac{(-2\pi i)^m}{(m-1)!}\,\Li_{1-m}\!\big(e^{u}\big)
=(\tau-i)^{-m}+\frac{(-2\pi i)^m}{(m-1)!}\sum_{p\ge0}\frac{\zeta(1-m-p)}{p!}\,u^{p},
\end{equation}
a pure order-$m$ pole (matching Lemma~\ref{lem:proj}) plus an entire, $T$-periodic tail. Hence
$\widehat P_i f=\sum_{m=1}^P r^*_{f,i}(m)(\tau-i)^{-m}+(\text{regular at }i)$. Because the pole lies
below the $T$-segment (height $1+\delta$), $|e^{u}|<1$ there and the block is used \emph{without}
inversion: no constant ($n{=}0$) mode appears, so no $\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}$ term arises and
$L^*(\widehat P_i f,s)$ is entire in $s$. (A pole \emph{above} the contour would invert
$\Li_0=-1-\Li_0(1/w)$, and the leftover $-1$ is the source of that pole in
Lemma~\ref{lem:polylogcf}; here it is absent.)

%\emph{Finite part is $\mathrm{FP}$.} On the $T$-segment
%$\Li_{1-m}(e^{u})=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{m-1}e^{2\pi n}e^{2\pi in\tau}$; integrating against $\tk$ and
%expanding each $\Gamma(s,2\pi n)$ by the recursion of Lemma~\ref{lem:polylogedge} at $x=2\pi n$, the
%convergent part of the $n$-sum produces the $\zeta$-values of~\eqref{eq:FP} and the reduced-$\Gamma$
%remainder $\mathcal E^+_{m-1}(s)$ of~\eqref{eq:Eplus}. With the $S$-segment principal value and the
%tail of~\eqref{eq:blockexp}, the finite part of block $m$ is
%$\tfrac{(-2\pi i)^m}{(m-1)!}r^*_{f,i}(m)\,\mathrm{FP}_{m-1}(s,k)$.
\emph{Finite part is $\mathrm{FP}$.} Write $N:=m-1$; the finite part collects a $T$-segment piece
(the $\zeta$-sum, harmonic term, and $\mathcal E^+$) and an $S$-segment piece (the polynomial term).

On $\gamma^T$ the block is the convergent series
$\Li_{1-m}(e^{u})=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{N}e^{2\pi n}e^{2\pi in\tau}$; integrating against $\tk$ from the
basepoint $\tau_0=i(1+\delta)$ gives, by Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL},
$\sum_{n\ge1}n^N e^{2\pi n}\big[\tfrac{\Gamma(s,X_n)}{(2\pi n)^{s}}+i^k\tfrac{\Gamma(k-s,X_n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}\big]$
with $X_n:=-2\pi in\tau_0=2\pi n(1+\delta)$. Expanding by the incomplete-$\Gamma$ recursion of
Lemma~\ref{lem:polylogedge}, $\Gamma(s,X)=e^{-X}\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}(s-1)_{(j)}X^{s-1-j}+(s-1)_{(J)}\Gamma(s-J,X)$,
and using $e^{2\pi n}e^{-X_n}=e^{-2\pi n\delta}$ and $X_n^{s-1-j}=(2\pi n)^{s-1-j}(1+\delta)^{s-1-j}$, the
$j$-sum becomes (with $c^+_j(s)$ collecting the $s$- and $i^k(k{-}s)$-parts)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Tsum}
\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\frac{c^+_j(s)\,(1+\delta)^{s-1-j}}{(2\pi)^{j+1}}\,\Li_{j+1-N}\!\big(e^{-2\pi\delta}\big)
\;+\;\mathcal E^+_N(s),
\end{equation}
$\mathcal E^+_N$ being the reduced-$\Gamma$ remainder of~\eqref{eq:Eplus}. With
$\Li_{j+1-N}(e^{-2\pi\delta})=\Gamma(N-j)(2\pi\delta)^{j-N}+\sum_{p\ge0}\tfrac{\zeta(j+1-N-p)}{p!}(-2\pi\delta)^p$,
two finite ($\delta^0$) parts survive:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item[(i)] the regular part at $\delta^0$ gives, for $j\ne N$, the $\zeta$-value
$\tfrac{c^+_j(s)}{(2\pi)^{j+1}}\zeta(j+1-N)$ --- the $\zeta$-sum of~\eqref{eq:FP};
\item[(ii)] for $j<N$ the singular $\Gamma(N-j)(2\pi\delta)^{j-N}$ crosses the $\delta^{N-j}$ coefficient
$\binom{s-1-j}{N-j}$ of $(1+\delta)^{s-1-j}$; by the falling-factorial identity
$(s-1)_{(j)}(N-j-1)!\binom{s-1-j}{N-j}=(s-1)_{(N)}/(N-j)$ (and its $k{-}s$ partner) these sum to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:harm}
\frac{c^+_N(s)}{(2\pi)^{N+1}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{N-j}=\frac{c^+_N(s)\,H_N}{(2\pi)^{N+1}},
\end{equation}
the harmonic term of~\eqref{eq:FP}.
\end{itemize}
(The $j=N$ term is $-\tfrac{c^+_N(s)}{(2\pi)^{N+1}}\log(2\pi\delta)+o(1)$, a divergence with no finite
part.) On $\gamma^S$ the pure pole $\sum_m r^*_{f,i}(m)(\tau-i)^{-m}$, integrated by principal value
against $\tau^{s-1}$ along the axis ($\tau=i(1+v)$), has the $\delta^0$ finite part
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Spoly}
i^{-m}\,\mathrm{PV}\!\int\frac{(1+v)^{s-1}}{v^{m}}\,dv\;\Big|_{\delta^0}
=i^{-m}(-1)^{N}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\frac{(-1)^{t}(s-1)_{(t)}}{t!\,(N-t)},
\end{equation}
the polynomial term of~\eqref{eq:FP}. Collecting, the finite part of block $m$ is
$\tfrac{(-2\pi i)^m}{(m-1)!}r^*_{f,i}(m)\,\mathrm{FP}_{m-1}(s,k)$.

\emph{Divergences cancel by~\eqref{eq:Laurent}.} The residual pieces are the $\delta\to0$ divergences
$\delta^{-(m-1)},\dots,\log\delta$ from the pole part $\sum_m r^*_{f,i}(m)(\tau-i)^{-m}$ at the
$S$-segment endpoints straddling $i$. At order $r$ their coefficient is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:walldiv}
\sum_{m=r}^{P} r^*_{f,i}(m)\binom{m}{r}i^{m}\;-\;i^{k}\sum_{m=r}^{P} r^*_{f,i}(m)\binom{k}{m-r}i^{-m},
\end{equation}
precisely the difference of the two sides of the modularity relation~\eqref{eq:Laurent}, hence $0$ for
$f\in F_k$. Every $\delta^{-r}$ and $\log\delta$ term therefore cancels and the $\delta\to0$ limit
exists; break~\eqref{eq:Laurent} at order $r$ and that divergence survives (at $r=1$, a $\log\delta$).

The finite part and the (vanishing) divergences give~\eqref{eq:wallvalue}. The cutoff $J$ is
immaterial, shifting terms between the $\zeta$-sum and $\mathcal E^+_N$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:polylogedge}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:mero}
Let $f\in F_k$ have poles $\tau_p$ of order $P_p$ with ${\rm Im}\,\tau_p\ge1$ in the strip
$|{\rm Re}\,\tau|\le\tfrac12$, pole-projected Fourier coefficients $c_{Rf}(n)$
(Definition~\ref{def:cRf}), and projection weights $r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)$
(Definition~\ref{def:proj}). In the minimal class,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mero}
L^*(f,s)=-c_{Rf}(0)\Big(\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}\Big)
+\sum_{n\ne0}c_{Rf}(n)\,G_{s,k}(n)
+\sum_{\tau_p\ne i}\ \sum_{m=1}^{P_p}r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)\,\frac{(-2\pi i)^m}{(m-1)!}\,I_{m-1}(s,\tau_p)
+L^*(\widehat P_i f,\,s),
\end{equation}
with $I_{m-1}$ from Lemmas~\ref{lem:polylogcf}--\ref{lem:polylogedge} and the last term
(present only for a pole at $i$) from Lemma~\ref{lem:wallvalue}. The right side is entire in
$s$ apart from at most simple poles at $s=0$ and $s=k$, and reduces to the weakly-holomorphic
$L^*$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL} when $f$ is holomorphic on $\HH$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Split $f=(I-\widehat P)f+\widehat Pf$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:growth} the Fourier series of the
pole-projected part converges on $\hat\gamma_f^T$; Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL} applied term by term
gives its $L^*$ as the first two sums. The projection
$\widehat Pf=\sum_{\tau_p}\sum_m r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)\,\frac{(-2\pi i)^m}{(m-1)!}\,\Li_{1-m}(e^{2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)})$
contributes, for each $\tau_p\ne i$, the L-integrals $I_{m-1}(s,\tau_p)$ of
Definition~\ref{def:IN}, evaluated by Lemmas~\ref{lem:polylogcf}--\ref{lem:polylogedge}; the
pole at $i$ contributes $L^*(\widehat P_i f,s)$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:wallvalue}. Entirety and the
pole locations follow from those of $G_{s,k}$ and the $\delta_{N,0}$ terms; when $f$ is
holomorphic on $\HH$, $\widehat Pf=0$ and $c_{Rf}=c_f$, recovering Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The $c_T$ term in the even weak-form entry.}
The weak form's period polynomial $r_{\widehat\Delta_k}$ is, like the cusp form's, annihilated by
$1+S$ and $1+U+U^2$, so it too lies in the period space $\langle W_+,\,p_0,\,W_-\rangle$; but it
carries a component along the Eisenstein polynomial $p_0=X^{k-2}-Y^{k-2}$ that the cusp form lacks.
Since $p_0$ occupies only the two extreme monomials $X^{k-2},Y^{k-2}$---the $s=1$ and $s=k-1$
coefficients---and $W_+$ is not orthogonal to $p_0$ under the Haberland pairing, the projection
$\eta^+$ of~\eqref{eq:project} absorbs part of this Eisenstein content but not all. The unabsorbed
remainder surfaces as an additive constant $c_T$ in the extreme even coefficient of
$r_{\widehat\Delta_k}$ in the matrices below. It occurs only there: the odd sector has no Eisenstein
direction (so $\eta^-$ is exact), the cusp form has no $p_0$-component (so no $c_T$), and, lying
outside the projection, $c_T$ enters neither $\eta^\pm$ nor $\alpha_k$.
