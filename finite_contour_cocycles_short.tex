\title{L-functions for all meromorphic $\SLZ$ modular forms,\\
via manifestly finite contours}
\author{David A.\ McGady}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We give a single manifestly finite, regulator-free functional realizing in one object L-functions
quasi-periods and period polynomials of meromorphic modular forms on $\SLZ$, with generic 
poles throughout the upper half-plane $\HH$ and at the cusp of $\SLZ$. Additionally, our construction
preserves holomorphicity. It is a two-segment contour integral,
$L^*(f,s) = L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) + L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0})$, where $L^*_T$ and $L^*_S$ 
have distinct integration kernels, and 
$\gamma^{S}_{\tau_0}$ is a path in $\HH$ from $S(\tau_0)$ to $\tau_0$, and the same for $\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$. 
For weakly holomorphic forms our L-function is independent of $\tau_0$ and of the path, and reproduces 
classical L-functions for holomorphic modular forms, reproduces the L-functions of weak modular forms 
in~\cite{BFK2011}, which previously needed non-holomorphic regularization to be defined, and reproduces 
Brown's quasi-periods~\cite{Brown2017}. For properly meromorphic modular forms with poles inside the 
interior of $\HH$, our L-function depends on the homotopy classes, defined in terms of pole-crossings. 
There is a natural choice of trivial homotopy class, which renders our L-functions canonical when the 
modular form has a pole in the interior of the fundamental domain, generalizing~\cite{1806}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and preliminary concepts}\label{sec:intro}

Our primary goal in this note is to put forth a unifying generalization of the L-function 
associated to a given modular form. Our L-function naturally encompasses the classical 
case of L-functions associated to holomorphic modular forms~\cite{01-Apostol}, and 
seamlessly unifies Ref.~\cite{BFK2011}'s extension to L-functions of modular forms with 
poles at the cusp with Ref.~\cite{1806}'s extension to L-functions of modular forms with 
poles at finite locations, exclusively away from cusps. Secondarily, we show that our 
L-functions reproduce the numerical results for quasi-periods associated to weak cusp 
forms~\cite{Brown2017}. 

{\em Note:} Throughout this document, we refer to L-integrals and L-functions. The classical 
L-function-as-Dirichlet-series which has been suitably extended to the complex $s$ plane is 
simply not defined when the modular form that defines the L-function has poles either at the 
cusp or at finite locations within the upper half-plane. So, if there is ever a doubt, we state clearly: 
{\em any L-function in this note is defined by an L-integral}. The focus of this note is to define 
L-integrals for any integer weight $\SLZ$ modular form which may have poles at cusps or away 
from cusps.

\subsection{Modular forms}
Write ${\rm Re}(z)$ and ${\rm Im}(z)$ for the real and imaginary parts of a complex number 
$z\in \CC$. Let $\HH:=\{\tau\in\CC\mid {\rm Im}(\tau)>0\}$ denote the upper half-plane. $\SLZ$ 
is the group generated by $T := \left( \begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix} \right)$ 
and $S := \left( \begin{smallmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{smallmatrix} \right)$. 

There is a natural $\SLZ$ action on functions $f: \HH \to \CC$, graded by integers $k$: 
$(f|_k \gamma)(\tau) := (c \tau + d)^{-k}f(\gamma\cdot\tau)$ for all $\gamma = \left(\begin{smallmatrix}
a & b \\ c & d \end{smallmatrix}\right) \in \SLZ$. Similarly, $\gamma\cdot \tau := 
\frac{a \tau + b}{c \tau + d}$. 

In this vein, a {\em holomorphic modular form} of weight $k$ for $\SLZ$ is a 
holomorphic function $f:\HH\to \CC$ such that $(f|_k\gamma)(\tau) = f(\tau)$ 
for all $\gamma \in \SLZ$ (aka $f(\tau+1)=f(-\frac1\tau)\tau^{-k}=f(\tau)$) for 
all $\tau\in \HH$, and $f(\tau)={\cal~O}(\tau^k)$ as ${\rm Im}(\tau)\to \infty$, and 
which has no poles at finite locations within $\HH$. By virtue of the periodicity 
$f(\tau) = f(\tau+1)$ and holomorphicity, each holomorphic modular form has 
a q-series decomposition,
\begin{equation}
f(\tau) = \sum_n c_f(n) e^{2 \pi i n \tau} = \sum_n c_f(n) q^n~~,~~q:= e^{2 \pi i \tau}~~.
\end{equation}
In order for $f$ to be bounded as $\tau \to i \infty$, we conclude that $c_f(n) = 0$ 
for all $n < 0$, i.e. that $f = \sum_{n \geq 0} c_f(n) q^n$; for such modular forms $f$ 
we write $f \in M_k$. If a modular form $f$ additionally has a vanishing constant term, 
i.e. if $c_f(0) = 0$, then $f$ is a {\em cusp form}, 
and we write $f \in S_k$. Clearly, $S_k \subset M_k$. Further, $M_k$ is a  
finite-dimensional vector-space, as is $S_k$. Holomorphic $\SLZ$ modular forms must 
have even integer weights, and $k \geq 4$~\cite{01-Apostol}. 

A product of modular forms of weights $k_1$ and $k_2$ is a modular form of weight 
$k_1+k_2$, so $M_*:=\bigoplus_k  M_k$ is naturally a (graded) ring. This graded ring is 
freely generated by the two modular forms $E_4 \in M_4$ and $E_6 \in M_6$, the $k = 4, 6$
specializations of the {\em weight $k$ Eisenstein series} $E_k$, which may be defined as follows:
\begin{align}
E_k := \frac{1}{2}\sum_{(m,n)=1} \frac{1}{(m+n\tau)^k} = 1 - \frac{2k}{B_k}\sum_{n = 1}^{\infty} \sigma_{k-1}(n) q^n~,
\end{align}
where $\sum_{(m,n) = 1}$ denotes summing over all relatively prime $(m,n) \in \ZZ^2$, the Bernoulli 
number $B_n$ is the coefficient of $t^n/n!$ of the Laurent expansion of $t/(e^{t}-1)$ about $t = 0$, 
and $\sigma_m(n) := \sum_{d|n} d^m$ is the Divisor function where $d|n$ means $n/d \in \NN$. 
In short, $M_* = \CC[E_4, E_6]$. We now define the {\em modular discriminant}:
\begin{equation}
\Delta := \frac{E_4^3-E_6^2}{1728} = q + O(q^2) \in S_{12}~.
\end{equation}

Holomorphicity is most canonically relaxed via allowing a modular form to have a pole at the 
cusp of $\SLZ$, i.e. for a weight $k$ modular form for $\SLZ$ to have negative powers of $q$ 
in its q-series expansion. In this case, $f$ diverges as $\tau \to i \infty$ as $f \sim e^{-2 \pi N \tau}$, 
where $-N$ is the largest negative number such that $c_f(n)$ is non-zero. Such a modular form 
$f$ is called a {\em weak modular form}, and we write $f \in M_k^!$. If a weak modular form $f$ 
has a vanishing coefficient of $q^0$, i.e. if $c_f(0) = 0$, it is called a {\em weak cusp form} and 
we write $f \in S_k^!$. Clearly, $S_k^! \subset M_k^!$, $S_k \subset S_k^!$ and $M_k \subset M_k^!$. 
Two important weak cusp forms in this note are as follows: 
\begin{align}
J :=&~ \frac{E_4^3}{\Delta} - 744 = \frac{1}{q} + O(q) \in S_0^!~,~\\
\Dhat =&~ (J^2 + 24 J -393444) \Delta := \frac{1}{q} + O(q^2) \in S_{12}^!~.~
\end{align}
In this work, we further relax from even weakly holomorphic modular forms, and 
consider {\em meromorphic modular forms} of weight $k$ which are quotients: $f=\frac{f_1}{f_2}$ 
where $f_i\in M_{k_i}$, the denominator $f_2$ is not identically zero, and $k=k_1-k_2$. We 
write $F_k$ for the vector space of meromorphic modular forms of weight $k$, and set 
$F_*:=\bigoplus_k F_k$. Then $F_k$ vanishes unless $k$ is an even integer, and 
$F_*$ may be regarded as a ``graded field of fractions'' of $M_*$. 

\subsection{L-functions for holomorphic modular forms and periods for cusp forms}

For any $f \in S_k$, one may define the associated L-function:
\begin{align}
L(f,s) := \frac{(2 \pi)^s}{\Gamma(s)} L^*(f,s) \underset{f \in S_k}{:=} 
\frac{(2 \pi)^s}{\Gamma(s)} \int_0^{\infty} f(it) t^{s-1} dt = \sum_{n \geq 1} \frac{c_f(n)}{n^s}~.
\end{align}
A similar definition applies for $f \in M_k$. (The classical definition of $L^*(f,s)$ for $f \in M_k$ 
is the focus of Remark~\ref{natural-beauty-via-finiteness}, below.) Similarly, one may define a period 
polynomial for $f \in S_k$ in terms of this L-function,
\begin{align}
r_f(X, Y) := (2 \pi i)^{k-1} \sum_{\ell = 0}^{k-2} (-1)^\ell \binom{k-2}{\ell}\, i^{\ell+1}\, L^*(f,\ell+1)\,X^{k-2-\ell}Y^\ell~.
\end{align}
This polynomial, $r_f(X, Y) \in V_{k-2} := \CC[X, Y]_{k-2}$, has a natural action under $\SLZ$, 
$(P|_{\gamma})(X, Y) = P(a X + b Y, c X + d Y)$ for $\gamma = 
\left( \begin{smallmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{smallmatrix} \right) \in \SLZ$. This period polynomial 
can be decomposed into a basis of highly constrained polynomials, which are annihilated by $|(1+S)$
and $|(1 + U + U^2)$\cite{KZ1984}, where $U := TS$ generates an order-3 cyclic subgroup of $\SLZ$.
We define the \emph{periods} of a cusp form to be coefficients of $r_f$ within this highly 
constrained sub-space $W$ of $V_{k-2}$, i.e. $W \subset V_{k-2}$.

{\bf Why this work?} As classically computed, these L-functions and associated period polynomials 
(when defined in terms of the special values of the associated L-function) are not defined when $f$ 
has poles either at the cusp of $\SLZ$ or within the interior of $\HH$. As stated above, there 
are extant, but partial and non-overlapping, extensions of the definition of $L(f,s)$ to $f \in M_k^!$~\cite{BFK2011} or 
$f \in F_k$ when $f$ is regular at the cusp of $\SLZ$~\cite{1806}. In this note, we provide a construction that unifies these disparate extensions.
We start with generic L-integrals in section~\ref{sec:Lint}, then specialize to $f \in M_k^!$ in section~\ref{sec:Lfun}, and finally explore the more general context of $f \in F_k$ in section~\ref{sec:arc}.

\section{A finite L-integral}\label{sec:Lint}


\begin{definition}\label{def:segments}
Let $\tau_0\in\HH$ and $f\in F_k$, let
$S=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
$T=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ denote 
the generators of $\Gamma=\SLZ$, and let $f^{-1}(\infty)$ be the locus 
of all poles of $f \in \HH$ (inclusive of the cusp of $\SLZ$).  
The \emph{$S$- and $T$-segments at $\tau_0$} are smooth paths in
$\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$ from $\sigma^{-1}\tau_0$ to $\tau_0$, for
$\sigma=S$ and $\sigma=T$ respectively,
\begin{equation}
\gamma^S_{\tau_0}:\ -1/\tau_0\ \longrightarrow\ \tau_0,\qquad
\gamma^T_{\tau_0}:\ \tau_0-1\ \longrightarrow\ \tau_0.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Avoiding poles is automatic for $f \in M_k^!$, as long as the base-point $\tau_0$, 
is not at a cusp of $\SLZ$, i.e. there is only the trivial homotopy class. 
When $f \in F_k$, and $f$ has poles at finite and non-zero imaginary heights, 
integration contours fall into nontrivial homotopy classes.

\begin{definition}\label{def:kernel}
Let $\zeta(s,x)=\sum_{m\ge0}(m+x)^{-s}$ as a function 
$\zeta: \CC_s \times (\CC\setminus\NN)_x \to \CC$ denote the 
Hurwitz zeta function (meromorphic in $s$, holomorphic in $x\in\HH$). 
We define \emph{Hurwitz $T$-kernel} as
\begin{equation}
\tk(\tau,s):=\zeta(1-s,\tau+1)-e^{i\pi(s-1)}\,\zeta\big(1-(k-s),\tau+1\big).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Generic L-integral]
\label{def:Lint}
Let $f\in F_k$, $\tau_0\in\HH$, and $s\in\CC$. Further, let $\gamma^S_{\tau_0}$,
$\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$ be the $S$- and $T$-segments at $\tau_0$, as in
Definition~\ref{def:segments}, and let $\tk$ be as defined in 
Definition~\ref{def:kernel}. The \emph{L-integral} of $f$ is
\begin{equation}
L^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0})
:= L^*_S(f,s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}) + L^*_T(f,s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})
:=e^{-\pi i s/2}\left(\int_{\gamma^S_{\tau_0}}\!\!\!\! f(\tau)\tau^{s-1}\,d\tau
+\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}}\!\!\!\! f(\tau)\tk(\tau,s)d\tau\right)
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
We use this \emph{L-integral} to define a generalized 
\emph{L-function}:

\begin{definition}[Generic L-function]\label{def:Lfun}
Let $L^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ be as defined in Definition~\ref{def:Lint}. 
We define the generalized \emph{L-function} of $f\in F_k$ to be,
\begin{equation}
L(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0})
:=\frac{(2\pi)^s}{\Gamma(s)}\,L^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0}).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Having defined the L-integral and L-function, we prove they are manifestly finite for $f \in F_k$: 

\begin{lemma}[Finiteness of L-integrals]\label{lem:finite}
For $f\in F_k$, both $L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0})$ and 
$L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ in Definition~\ref{def:Lint} are each, 
separately finite for every $s\in\CC$, every $\tau_0\in\HH$, and for 
every path $\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Both segments are compact and lie in $\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, so $f$ is
continuous on them; $\tau^{s-1}$ is continuous on $\HH$, and $\tk(\tau,s)$ is continuous there
since $\zeta(\nu,\tau+1)$ is holomorphic in $\tau\in\HH$ (Definition~\ref{def:kernel}). A
continuous integrand on a compact path is integrable, for every $s\in\CC$.
\end{proof}

We now turn to path-dependence and $\tau_0$-independence of 
the L-integral and the L-function of $f \in F_k$. 
This holds for all $f \in M_k^!$:

\begin{proposition}[Basepoint \& path independence for $f \in M_k^!$]\label{prop:indep}
For $f\in M^!_k$ the value $L^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ is independent
of $\tau_0\in\HH$ and of the paths: with no interior poles, $\HH$ is simply connected,
so all admissible contours are homotopic. We write $L^*(f,s)$ for the common value and
$L(f,s):=\frac{(2\pi)^s}{\Gamma(s)}L^*(f,s)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With no interior poles the integrands $f(\tau)\tau^{s-1}$ and $f(\tau)\tk(\tau,s)$ are
holomorphic on $\HH$, which is simply connected; by Cauchy's theorem each integral is unchanged
under any deformation fixing its endpoints, hence depends on those endpoints alone. This is
path-independence.

For independence of $\tau_0$ show that variations in $\tau_0$ for the S-term and T-term in $L^*(f,s)$ 
cancel, i.e. that $\partial_{\tau_0} L^*_S(f,s:\gamma^S_{\tau_0}) = -\partial_{\tau_0} L^*_T(f,s:\gamma^T_{\tau_0})$. 
For $L^*_T$, note that the $T$-segment runs from $T^{-1}\tau_0=\tau_0-1$ to $\tau_0$, 
so by Leibniz's rule and the $T$-periodicity $f(\tau_0-1)=f(\tau_0)$,
\begin{equation}
\partial_{\tau_0} L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})
=f(\tau_0)\,\tk(\tau_0,s)-f(\tau_0-1)\,\tk(\tau_0-1,s)
=f(\tau_0)\,\delta_T\tk(\tau_0-1,s),
\end{equation}
where $\delta_T\tk(\tau,s):=\tk(\tau+1,s)-\tk(\tau,s)$. This is the whole role of $\delta_T$: the
unit gap between the endpoints turns the $\partial_{\tau_0}$ boundary term into a unit shift of
the kernel. That shift is elementary, since $\zeta(\nu,x+1)-\zeta(\nu,x)=-x^{-\nu}$ gives, on 
both terms at $x=\tau+1$,
\begin{align}
& \delta_T\tk(\tau,s)=-(\tau+1)^{s-1}+e^{i\pi(s-1)}(\tau+1)^{k-1-s}
\\
& \implies \partial_{\tau_0} L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) =f(\tau_0)\big[-\tau_0^{s-1}+e^{i\pi(s-1)}\tau_0^{k-1-s}\big].
\end{align}
The $S$-segment runs $-1/\tau_0\to\tau_0$; its lower endpoint moves with
$\partial_{\tau_0}(-1/\tau_0)=\tau_0^{-2}$, so by Leibniz, the modularity
$f(-1/\tau_0)=\tau_0^k f(\tau_0)$, and the phase $(-1/\tau_0)^{s-1}=e^{i\pi(s-1)}\tau_0^{1-s}$,
\begin{equation}
\partial_{\tau_0} L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}) 
=f(\tau_0)\tau_0^{s-1}-f(-1/\tau_0)\,(-1/\tau_0)^{s-1}\,\tau_0^{-2}
=f(\tau_0)\big[\tau_0^{s-1}-e^{i\pi(s-1)}\tau_0^{k-1-s}\big].
\end{equation}
Thus $\tau_0$-variations are equal and opposite, $\partial_{\tau_0}L^*_S = - \partial_{\tau_0}L^*_T$. Thus 
$\partial_{\tau_0}L^*=\partial_{\tau_0}L^*_S + \partial_{\tau_0}L^*_T=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Recovering the classical L-integral for $f \in S_k$]\label{rem:mellin}
\emph{Let $f\in S_k$. As $f^{-1}(\infty)$ does not include the cusp $i \infty$, 
$\lim_{\tau_0 \to i \infty} L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ exists. 
In this limit, the $S$-segment maps onto the standard 
L-function contour, $[0,i\infty]$. Simultaneously, as $f(it) \sim e^{-2\pi t}$ as 
$t \to \infty$, the $T$-segment vanishes exponentially. Thus, in this limit, $L^*(f,s)$ 
exactly maps onto the classical L-function integral~\cite{01-Apostol}:}
\begin{equation}
\lim_{\tau_0\to i\infty}L^*(f,s)=\int_0^{\infty}\! t^{s-1}f(it)\,dt.
\end{equation}
\end{remark}

\section{L-functions, period polynomials and poles at the cusp}\label{sec:Lfun}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:HGG}
Let $\Gamma(s, x) := \int_x^{\infty} e^{-t} t^{s-1} dt$ denote the {\em incomplete Gamma function}. For $n\ne0$, $\nu\in\CC$, and $z\in\HH$,
\begin{equation}
\int_{z}^{z+1}\! e^{2\pi inu}\,\zeta(\nu,u)\,du=e^{i \pi (1-\nu)/2} (2\pi n)^{\nu-1}\,\Gamma\big(1-\nu,-2\pi i n z\big).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\zeta(\nu,u)=\sum_{m\ge0}(m+u)^{-\nu}$ and shift $u\mapsto u+m$ in the $m$-th term; as
$e^{2\pi in(u+m)}=e^{2\pi inu}$ for integer $m$, the unit segments stack into one horizontal ray,
\begin{equation}
\int_z^{z+1}\! e^{2\pi inu}\zeta(\nu,u)\,du
=\sum_{m\ge0}\int_{z+m}^{z+1+m}\! e^{2\pi inv}v^{-\nu}\,dv
=\int_z^{z+\infty}\! e^{2\pi inv}v^{-\nu}\,dv.
\end{equation}
Now, substitute $w=-2\pi i n v$, so that $v=iw/(2\pi n)$, $dv=i\,dw/(2\pi n)$, and $e^{2\pi inv}=e^{-w}$:
the lower endpoint becomes $w=-2\pi i n z$, and for $n>0$ the ray rotates to $w\to+\infty$ along the
positive reals. Since $e^{-w}w^{-\nu}$ decays in the right half-plane, the closing arc
vanishes. With $v^{-\nu}=i^{-\nu}(2\pi n)^{\nu}w^{-\nu}$,
\begin{equation}
\int_z^{z+\infty}\! e^{2\pi inv}v^{-\nu}\,dv
=i^{1-\nu}(2\pi n)^{\nu-1}\!\int_{-2\pi i n z}^{\infty}\! e^{-w}w^{-\nu}\,dw
=e^{i \pi (1-\nu)/2}(2\pi n)^{\nu-1}\Gamma\big(1-\nu,-2\pi i n z\big).
\end{equation}
For $n<0$ the ray instead grows as ${\rm Im} v\to\infty$ and the rotation is illegal; but both sides are
analytic in $n$ and agree for $n>0$, so the identity continues to $n<0$ with $\Gamma$ on its principal
branch. At $n=0$ the prefactor $(2\pi n)^{\nu-1}$ is singular; that case is handled separately.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The L-function for $f \in M_k^!$]\label{thm:weakL}
For $f\in M^!_k$, we may set $\tau_0=i$, and prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weakL}
L^*(f,s)=-c_f(0)\left(\frac1s+\frac{i^k}{k-s}\right)
+\sum_{n\ne0}c_f(n)\left(\frac{\Gamma(s,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s}+i^k\frac{\Gamma(k-s,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}\right)~.
\end{equation}
Here, trivially, $L^*(f,s) = i^k L^*(f,k-s)$. The L-function is $L(f,s) := (2\pi)^s L^*(f,s) /\Gamma(s)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By path independence and base-point independence from Proposition~\ref{prop:indep}, 
we may freely set $\tau_0=i$. Here, $L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}) = 0$ (the $S$-contour 
vanishes in length and there are no nontrivial homotopy classes as $f \in M_k^!$ has no poles 
which a finite contour can wrap), and 
the L-integral simplifies dramatically:
\begin{equation}
L^*(f,s) = L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_i) =e^{-\pi i s/2}\int_{i-1}^{i} f(\tau)\tk(\tau, s)d\tau~. 
\end{equation}
We then expand $f$ in terms of its q-series, $\sum_n c_f(n)e^{2\pi in\tau}$,
and substitute the $u$-variable of Proposition~\ref{prop:HGG} as 
$u=\tau+1$, and note that $e^{2\pi i n(u-1)}=e^{2\pi i n u}$. 
When $n\neq0$, straightforward substitutions of 
$\nu, z$ into Proposition~\ref{prop:HGG} suffice: $1-\nu=s$ gives the 
contribution of the first term in $\tk(\tau, s)$, and $1-\nu = k-s$ gives the second: 
\begin{align}
e^{-\pi i s/2}\left(e^{\pi i s/2} \frac{\Gamma(s,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s} - e^{i\pi(s-1)}e^{\pi i (k-s)/2} \frac{\Gamma(k-s,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}\right) = \frac{\Gamma(s, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s} + i^k \frac{\Gamma(k-s, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}~.
\end{align}
We may then evaluate the $n=0$ mode's contribution follows from noting the 
closed form solution for the definite integral, 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hurwitz-antideriv}
\int_{i - 1}^{i}\! \zeta(\sigma, \tau + 1)\,d\tau
 = \int_i^{i + 1}\!\zeta(\sigma, u)\,du
 = \frac{i^{1 - \sigma}}{\sigma - 1}.
\end{equation}
This result follows from noting $\partial_a(\zeta(\sigma-1, a)/(1-\sigma)) = \zeta(\sigma,a)$ 
and $\zeta(\sigma-1, a+1) - \zeta(\sigma-1, a) = -a^{1-\sigma}$. 
Applying this to the two distinct terms of $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ at $\sigma = 1 - s$ and $\sigma = 1 - (k - s)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:const-mode-integral}
e^{-\pi i s/2}\int_{i - 1}^{i}c_f(0)\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
= -c_f(0)\left(\frac{1}{s}  + \frac{i^k}{k - s}\right).
\end{equation}
Combining the $n = 0$ contributions with the $n \neq 0$ contributions reproduces Eq.~\eqref{eq:weakL}. 
Finally, the functional equation, $L^*(f,s)=i^k L^*(f,k-s)$, follows by direct inspection.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Explicit S- and T-contours for general $\tau_0$}

\begin{lemma}[$L^*_T$ for $M^!_k$]\label{lem:Tclosed-Mk}
For $f\in M^!_k$, and $\tau_0 \in \HH$ away from cusps, 
$L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ evaluates to:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T-segment-closed-Mk}
L_T^*(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})
= -c_f(0)\!\left(\frac{(\tau_0/i)^{s}}{s} + i^k\frac{(\tau_0/i)^{k-s}}{k-s}\right) + \sum_{n \ne 0}\! c_f(n)\!\left(\frac{\Gamma(s, -2\pi i n\tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^s} + i^k\frac{\Gamma(k - s, -2\pi i n\tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}\right).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expand $f = \sum_n c_f(n)\,q^n$ on the compact segment
$[\tau_0 - 1, \tau_0]$, and restrict attention to the sub-sum, $\sum_{n \neq 0}$. 
For each such $n \neq 0$, apply the Proposition~\ref{prop:HGG} in
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s) = \zeta(1 - s, \tau + 1) - e^{i\pi(s-1)}\zeta(s - (k-1), \tau + 1)$
at $\nu = 1 - s$ and $\nu = s - (k - 1)$. The phases for the second term work-out 
identically as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL}.  

For $n = 0$, the constant $c_f(0)$ multiplies $e^{-\pi i s/2}\!\int_{\tau_0 - 1}^{\tau_0}\!\widetilde k_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau$, 
which we compute as follows. First, the closed-form of the Hurwitz antiderivative 
$\int_{\tau_0 - 1}^{\tau_0}\zeta(\sigma, \tau + 1)\,d\tau = \tau_0^{1 - \sigma}/(\sigma - 1)$
comes from $\partial_\tau\zeta(\sigma - 1, \tau + 1) = -(\sigma - 1)\zeta(\sigma, \tau + 1)$ 
and the shift identity $\zeta(\sigma - 1, \tau_0 + 1) - \zeta(\sigma - 1, \tau_0) = -\tau_0^{1 - \sigma}$. 
Finally, note that the two terms in $\tk$ are simply specializations of $\sigma = 1 - s$ and $\sigma = s - k + 1$, 
which yield $-e^{-\pi i s/2}\tau_0^{\,s}/s = -(\tau_0/i)^s/s$ and 
$-e^{\pi i s/2}\tau_0^{\,k-s}/(k-s) = -i^k(\tau_0/i)^{k-s}/(k-s)$ respectively.
\end{proof}


\begin{corollary}[$L^*_T$ as $\tau_0 \to i \infty$]\label{lem:cusp-decay}
Let $f \in S^!_k$, where $f = \sum_{n \geq -N} c_f(n)q^n$ for some $-N \in \ZZ$. 
The T-contour's contribution to $L^*(f,s)$ is given by 
Lemma~\ref{lem:Tclosed-Mk}. As $\tau_0 = it$ goes to the cusp $i\infty$,
the $T$-contour scales exactly as $f(it)$ scales:
$L_T^*(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) \sim f(it) \sim e^{2\pi N t}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For the asymptotics, $\Gamma(\sigma, z) \sim z^{\sigma - 1}e^{-z}$
as $|z| \to \infty$ for $|\arg z| < \tfrac{3\pi}{2}$ \cite[\S8.11]{DLMF}
gives $|c_f(n)|$-weighted go as $e^{-2\pi n\,{\rm Im}(\tau_0)}$.  The smallest 
mode $n$ in the q-series, i.e. $n = -N$, dominates the scaling of $f(it)$ and of 
$L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{i t})$. Combining these results yields the claimed asymptotics.
\end{proof}

Now, exploiting $\tau_0$-independence of $L^*(f, s)$, its explicit value as given in 
Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL}, and the explicit T-contribution $L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ trivially 
implies clean, closed-form expressions for $L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0})$:

\begin{corollary}[$L^*_S$ for $M_k^!$]\label{cor:Sclosed}
For $f\in M^!_k$ and $\tau_0\in\HH$, 
$L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0})$ evaluates to
\begin{align}
L_S^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0})
&= c_f(0)\left(\frac{(\tau_0/i)^{\,s}-1}{s}
+ i^k\frac{(\tau_0/i)^{\,k-s}-1}{k-s}\right) \label{eq:S-segment-explicit}\\
&+ \sum_{n\ne0} c_f(n)\!\left(\frac{\Gamma(s,2\pi n)-\Gamma(s,-2\pi i n\tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^s}
+ i^k\,\frac{\Gamma(k-s,2\pi n)-\Gamma(k-s,-2\pi i n\tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}\right)~. \nonumber
\end{align}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Recall $L^*(f, s) - L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) = L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0})$. 
Now, $L^*(f,s)$ for $f \in M_k^!$ is given by Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL}, and 
$L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ is given by Lemma~\ref{lem:Tclosed-Mk}. 
Their difference yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:S-segment-explicit}.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Classical Mellin recovery and canonical uniqueness.}\label{sec:Connection-w-previous-weak-MF-l-functions}

As discussed in Remark~\ref{rem:mellin}, for $f \in S_k$, taking the cusp-limit
$\tau_0 \to i\infty$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL} collapses the $S$-segment 
onto the classical Mellin contour $[0, i\infty]$ while via Corollary~\ref{lem:cusp-decay} 
the $T$-segment vanishes exponentially as $L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) \sim f(\tau_0) 
\sim e^{-2\pi {\rm Im}(\tau_0) N}$ (as $f \in S_k \Leftrightarrow f = \sum_{n \geq N} c_f(n) q^n$ with $N \geq 1$). 
I.e., here, one recovers the classical L-integral of $f$ without obstruction:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Mellin-recovery}
\lim_{\tau_0 \to i\infty}\!
L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})
\;=\; \int_0^\infty\! t^{s - 1}\,f(it)\,dt~.
\end{equation}  
This story changes when $f = 1/q^N (1 + O(q)) \in S_k^!$ for some $N \geq 1$. 

In short, when $f$ has a polar q-series, it has a pole at the cusp of $\SLZ$. Thus, 
the limit $\tau_0 \to i \infty$, which was crucial to recovering the classical Mellin contour 
in Remark~\ref{rem:mellin} simply does not exist.

However, it can still be instructive to examine $L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$, 
and $L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0})$ as $\tau_0$ goes towards $i \infty$. 
In this context, while of course the sum $L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) + 
L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}) = L^*(f,s)$ is manifestly invariant under 
changes of the base-point, each of these two terms can indeed change values. Indeed, 
Corollary~\ref{lem:cusp-decay} clearly shows that as $\tau_0 \to i \infty$, when $f \in S_k^!$ 
has negative powers of $q$ in its q-series, then $L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) \sim 
e^{2 \pi N {\rm Im}(\tau_0)}$. Discarding $\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$ therefore does violence to the result: 
When the $S$-contour is isolated from its T-partner, and $\tau_0 \to i \infty$, it diverges. This is the precise
reason that~\cite{BFK2011} needed to regularize their divergent L-integral representation for properly weak 
cusp forms. 

That said, there is one further aspect of our L-function even in the classical 
case of $f \in M_k$ that is worthy of remark:

\begin{remark}[Recovering the classical L-integral for $f \in M_k$]\label{natural-beauty-via-finiteness}
\emph{In textbook treatments~\cite{01-Apostol}, when $f \in M_k$, its L-integral is defined as}
\begin{align}
L^*(f,s)|_{\rm cl} :=&~ \int_0^\infty \big( f(it) - c_f(0) \big) t^{s-1} dt = \frac{\Gamma(s)}{(2 \pi)^s} \sum_{n = 1}^{\infty} \frac{c_f(n)}{n^s} \\
=& -c_f(0) \bigg(\frac{1}{s} + \frac{i^k}{k-s} \bigg) + \sum_{n = 1}^{\infty} c_f(n)\left(\frac{\Gamma(s,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s} + i^k \frac{\Gamma(k-s, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}\right) \nonumber
\end{align}
\emph{Classically, this constant-term subtraction is needed because $\int_0^\infty t^{s-1} dt$ is a pure 
divergence, so the q-series cannot commute with the integration contour.}

\emph{However, as is clear from our general Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL} for L-integrals for $f \in M_k^!$, 
and from the specific results of the S- and T-contours in Lemma~\ref{lem:Tclosed-Mk} and 
Corollary~\ref{cor:Sclosed}, our L-integral requires no such explicit subtraction of the constant 
term $-c_f(0)$. Thus, not only does our prescription naturally handle divergences at poles when 
$f \in M_k^!$ has polar terms in its q-series, but it also obviates the need to by-hand remove the 
constant mode from the kernel of the L-integral that is needed when defining L-functions in terms 
of the classical Mellin contour, $\int_0^\infty$.}
\end{remark}

\subsection{Period polynomials, periods and quasi-periods}

Because $L^*(f,s)$ is canonical for every $f\in S^!_k$, and because period polynomials 
for a given modular form can be written in terms of their L-functions evaluated at integer-$s$ 
in the critical strip, they need no separate construction.

\begin{definition}[Period polynomial for $S_k^!$]\label{def:rf}
For $f\in S^!_k$ and $n=k-2$, the \emph{period polynomial} is
\begin{equation}
r_f(X,Y):=(2\pi i)^{n+1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}(-1)^\ell\binom{n}{\ell}\,i^{\ell+1}\,L^*(f,\ell+1)\,X^{n-\ell}Y^\ell.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Period polynomial as a contour integral]\label{prop:periodint}
Let $n:=k-2$. With $(X-\tau Y)^n$ in place of the L-weight $\tau^{s-1}$, and
$\mathbf K_T(\tau;X,Y):=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\binom{n}{\ell}(-1)^\ell\,\tk(\tau,\ell+1)\,X^{n-\ell}Y^\ell$
in place of $\tk$, the same two segments assemble $r_f$ directly:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf}
r_f(X, Y)=(2\pi i)^{n+1}\Big[\int_{\gamma^S_{\tau_0}} f(\tau)\,(X-\tau Y)^n\,d\tau
+\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}} f(\tau)\,\mathbf K_T(\tau;X,Y)\,d\tau\Big].
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By Definition~\ref{def:Lint}, the $X^{n-\ell}Y^\ell$ coefficient of the bracket is
\begin{equation}
r_f|_{X^{n-\ell}Y^\ell} = (-1)^\ell\binom{n}{\ell} i^{\ell+1} 
\big[
L^*_S(f, \ell+1) 
+
L^*_T(f, \ell+1)
\big]
=(-1)^\ell\binom{n}{\ell}\,i^{\ell+1}L^*(f,\ell+1)~.
\end{equation}
This matches Definition~\ref{def:rf} term by term, and proves the claim.
\end{proof}

Having now defined the period polynomial associated to a weak cusp form $f \in S_k^!$,
we now seek to find its {\em (quasi-)periods}. First, a number of helpful definitions.

All objects below take values in $V_n:=\CC[X,Y]_n$, $n=k-2$, with the slash action
$(P|_\gamma)(X,Y):=P(aX+bY,cX+dY)$ of \S\ref{sec:intro}. A map $\gamma\mapsto A_\gamma\in V_n$ is a
\emph{$1$-cocycle} for $\SLZ$ if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cocycle-condition}
A_{\gamma\delta}=A_\gamma|_\delta+A_\delta\qquad\text{for all }\gamma,\delta\in\SLZ ,
\end{equation}
and is \emph{parabolic} if in addition $A_T=0$. For fixed $P\in V_n$ the map
$\gamma\mapsto P|_\gamma-P$ is a \emph{coboundary}, and satisfies~\eqref{eq:cocycle-condition}
identically, since $(P|_\gamma-P)|_\delta+(P|_\delta-P)=P|_{\gamma\delta}-P$. Adding or subtracting
a coboundary from a cocycle therefore returns a cocycle, which is what lets $C_\gamma$ below be
replaced by a representative $\hat C_\gamma$ normalised so that $\hat C_T=0$.

For starters, let $f \in S_k^!$, $\tau_0 \in \HH$ and $\gamma \in \SLZ$, and define
\begin{equation}
C_\gamma(f; \tau_0):= (2\pi i)^{n+1}\int_{\gamma^{-1}\tau_0}^{\tau_0} \omega_f ~~,~~{\rm where}~~\omega_f := f(\tau) (X - \tau Y)^n d\tau~~.\label{cocycleR1}
\end{equation} 
Finally, it is helpful to define two slight abstractions related to $\mathbf K_T(\tau;X,Y)$ and to 
$L_T^*(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$:
\begin{align}
\chi(\tau;X,Y)&:=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\binom{n}{\ell}(-1)^\ell\,\zeta(-\ell,\tau+1)\,X^{n-\ell}Y^\ell , \label{cocycleR2}\\
E(f; \tau_0)&:=(2\pi i)^{n+1}\!\!\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}}\!\! f(\tau)\,\chi(\tau; X, Y) d\tau ~.\label{cocycleR3}
\end{align}
At non-positive integer first argument the Hurwitz zeta is elementary,
$\zeta(-\ell,x)=-B_{\ell+1}(x)/(\ell+1)$ with $B_m(x)$ the $m$-th Bernoulli
polynomial~\cite[\S25.11]{DLMF}, so $\chi(\tau;X,Y)$ is a polynomial in $\tau$ of degree $n+1$.
Its role is that of a discrete antiderivative of the Eichler kernel: the Hurwitz shift
$\zeta(-\ell,\tau)=\zeta(-\ell,\tau+1)+\tau^{\ell}$ gives, term by term,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chi-shift}
\chi(\tau-1;X,Y)-\chi(\tau;X,Y)=(X-\tau Y)^{n} .
\end{equation}
Both facts are used in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:cocycle}.

For each $\tau$, $\chi(\tau;\cdot,\cdot)\in V_{k-2}=\CC[X,Y]_{k-2}$; the $\SLZ$-slash
$(P|_\gamma)(X,Y):=P(aX+bY,cX+dY)$ acts on the polynomial variables alone, holding $\tau$ fixed:
\begin{equation}
\chi|_\gamma(\tau;X,Y):=\chi\big(\tau;\,aX+bY,\,cX+dY\big),\qquad
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\SLZ .
\end{equation}
Thus $|_T$ sends $(X,Y)\mapsto(X+Y,Y)$ with $\tau$ untouched. Being linear and $\tau$-independent,
$|_\gamma$ commutes with $\int\!d\tau$, so slashing the polynomial $E$ agrees with slashing $\chi$
under the integral; the two occurrences of $|_\gamma$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:cocycle} denote the same
operation.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cocycle}
Let $f\in S_k^!$, $\tau_0\in\HH$, and let $\omega_f$, $C_\gamma(f; \tau_0)$, $\chi(\tau; X, Y)$, and
$E(f;\tau_0)$ be as defined in Eqs.~\eqref{cocycleR1}, \eqref{cocycleR2} and \eqref{cocycleR3}. 
Then $\hat C_\gamma:=C_\gamma-(E|_\gamma-E)$ is a $1$-cocycle for $\SLZ$ with 
$\hat C_S=r_f$ and $\hat C_T=0$. 
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First, we prove $\hat C_\gamma$ is a cocycle. Path-additivity and covariance of $\omega_f$ give the Eichler--Shimura cocycle relation
$C_{\gamma\delta}=C_\gamma|_\delta+C_\delta$~\cite{BCD2018,KZ1984}; as $\gamma\mapsto E|_\gamma-E$ is a
coboundary, $\hat C$ is a cocycle.

Having established it is a cocyle, we now show that $\hat C_S=r_f$. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:periodint} the period polynomial splits along the two segments of
Definition~\ref{def:segments}, and we identify each contribution in turn. The $S$-segment carries
the kernel $\tau^{s-1}$, so assembling it over $\ell$ returns
$(2\pi i)^{n+1}\int_{\gamma^S_{\tau_0}}\omega_f=C_S$ exactly. For the $T$-segment, at $s=\ell+1$
Definition~\ref{def:kernel} reads
$\tk(\tau,\ell+1)=\zeta(-\ell,\tau+1)-(-1)^\ell\zeta(-(n-\ell),\tau+1)$; assembling $\mathbf K_T$ and
re-indexing $\ell\mapsto n-\ell$ in the second term (with $n$ even) gives $\mathbf K_T=\chi-\chi|_S$,
so the $T$-term of $r_f$ is $E-E|_S$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rfsplit}
r_f=C_S+(E-E|_S)=C_S-(E|_S-E)=\hat C_S .
\end{equation}
Neither summand in~\eqref{eq:rfsplit} vanishes in general: the $S$-segment runs from $-1/\tau_0$ to
$\tau_0$ and degenerates only at the fixed point $\tau_0=i$, where $C_S=0$ and $E|_S-E=-r_f$.

Finally, we show that $\hat C_T=0$. Applying the shift identity~\eqref{eq:chi-shift} twice shows
that $A(\tau):=\chi(\tau;X+Y,Y)-\chi(\tau-1;X,Y)$ satisfies $A(\tau-1)=A(\tau)$. Crucially,
$\chi$ is a polynomial in $\tau$, as recorded below Eq.~\eqref{cocycleR3}, so $A$ is a
$1$-periodic polynomial and hence constant:
$\partial_\tau A(\tau) = 0 \Leftrightarrow A(\tau) = A_0$. Thus,
\begin{align}
\hat C_T
&=C_T-(E|_T-E)
\nonumber\\ &
=(2\pi i)^{n+1}\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}} f(\tau) (X - \tau Y)^n d\tau
- (2\pi i)^{n+1}\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}} f(\tau)\big[\chi|_T(\tau; X, Y)-\chi(\tau; X, Y)\big] d\tau \nonumber\\
&=(2\pi i)^{n+1}\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}} f(\tau)\,\bigg(\big[\chi(\tau-1; X, Y)-\chi(\tau; X, Y)\big] - \big[\chi(\tau; X+Y, Y)-\chi(\tau; X, Y)\big] \bigg) d\tau \nonumber\\
&=(2\pi i)^{n+1}\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}} f(\tau)\,\big[\chi(\tau-1; X, Y)-\chi(\tau; X+Y, Y)\big] d\tau 
=-(2\pi i)^{n+1} \int_{\tau_0-1}^{\tau_0} f(\tau)A(\tau)\,d\tau \nonumber\\
&=-(2\pi i)^{n+1}A_0 \int_{\tau_0-1}^{\tau_0} f(\tau)\,d\tau
=-(2\pi i)^{n+1}A_0\,c_f(0)=0,
\end{align}
as all elements $f \in S_k^!$ must have $c_f(0) = 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:w}
$W:=\{w\in\CC[X,Y]_{k-2}: w|_{(1+S)}=w|_{(1+U+U^2)}=0\} \subset V_n$
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:rfW}
For $f\in S_k^!$, and $W \subset V_n$ from Definition~\ref{def:w}, the period polynomial $r_f(X, Y) \in W$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\hat C_\gamma$ be the parabolic cocycle of Lemma~\ref{lem:cocycle}. Thus, $r_f=\hat C_S$ 
and $\hat C_T=0$. Now, note that $\hat C_\gamma(f; \tau_0) = 0$ when $\gamma = \pm I$. Since
$n=k-2$ is even, $\pm I$ act trivially on $V_n$, so the coboundary term $E|_{\pm I}-E$ vanishes and
$\hat C_{\pm I}=C_{\pm I}$; and $C_{\pm I}$ is a closed-contour integral of a function which has no
poles in $\HH$. Finally, direct computation, starting from the identities $-I = U^3 = S^2$
and using the cocycle law $\hat C_{\gamma\delta}=\hat C_\gamma|_\delta+\hat C_\delta$ and clever 
use of $\hat C_T = 0$, shows $r_f|_{(1+S)}=r_f|_{(1+U+U^2)}=0$:
\begin{align}
&\hat C_U = \hat C_{TS} = \hat C_T|_S + \hat C_S = \hat C_S = r_f~,  \\
&0 = \hat C_{S^2} = \hat C_S|_S + \hat C_S = \hat C_S|_{(1+S)} = r_f|_{(1+S)}~,\\
&0 = \hat C_{U^3} = \hat C_U|_{UU}+\hat C_{UU}=\hat C_U|_{U^2}+\big(\hat C_U|_U+\hat C_U\big)=\hat C_U|_{(1+U+U^2)}=r_f|_{(1+U+U^2)}~.
\end{align}
Thus, $r_f \in W \subset V_n$, and finishes the proof. 
\end{proof}

To define periods and quasi-periods, we recall the standard fact
that $\dim(W)=2\dim(S_k)+1$ (see, e.g.~\cite{KZ1984}), and define 
the inner-product:
\begin{align}\label{inner}
\langle P,Q\rangle:=\sum_a(-1)^a\binom{n}{a}^{-1}p_a\,q_{n-a}~.
\end{align}
Now for the basis elements for $k$ where $\dim(S_k) = 1$:
\begin{definition}\label{def:Wpm}
Let $\dim S_k=1$, $n:=k-2$, $p_0(X):=X^n-1$, $B_\ell := \tfrac{\ell!}{2\pi i}\oint \tfrac{t^{-\ell}}{e^{t}-1}\,dt$, and
$B^0_m(X):=\sum_{i\ne1}\binom{m}{i}B_i X^{m-i}$. Motivated by~\cite[Thm.~1$'$]{KZ1984}, we define:
\begin{align}
W^{(k)}_-(X)&:={\rm pp}\left(\tfrac{1}{n-1}\big(B^0_{n-1}(X)+X^nB^0_{n-1}(\tfrac1X)\big)
-\tfrac13\big(B^0_3(X)+X^nB^0_3(\tfrac1X)\big)\right),\\
W^{(k)}_+(X)&:={\rm pp}\left(\tfrac12\big(B^0_2(X)-X^nB^0_2(\tfrac1X)\big)
+\tfrac1n\big(B^0_n(X)-X^nB^0_n(\tfrac1X)\big)-c\,p_0(X)\right),\\
v_0^{(k)}(X)&:= p_0(X)~,
\end{align}
where ${\rm pp}$ acts on any polynomial with rational coefficients by multiplying by all
coefficient-denominators and dividing by the gcd, and $\langle P, Q\rangle$ is as in
Eq.~\eqref{inner}. The bracketed polynomial defining $W^{(k)}_+$ has coefficients $a_i=-a_{n-i}$,
so the single constant $c:=a_n$ eliminates its $X^n$ and constant terms at once; this is the
$p_0$-component, and removing it is what makes $\langle W^{(k)}_+,p_0\rangle=0$ below.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:Wbasis}
For $k$ with $\dim S_k=1$, the space $W$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:rfW} has $\dim W=3$, and $\{
W^{(k)}_+$, $W^{(k)}_-$, $v_0^{(k)}\}$ is an orthogonal basis of $W$ under $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ from Eq.~\eqref{inner}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First, let $W_\pm^{(k)}(X, Y) := Y^{k-2} W_\pm^{(k)}(X/Y)$, and let $v_0^{(k)}(X, Y) := Y^{k-2} v_0^{(k)}(X/Y)$.

Membership in $W$ is the substantive point. Theorem~1$'$ of~\cite{KZ1984} gives the odd and even
period polynomials of the cusp form $E_{k-12}\Delta$ (where $E_0:=1$) as modified-Bernoulli
combinations and places them in $W$; the $W^{(k)}_\pm$ of Definition~\ref{def:Wpm} are
specializations of those formulae, $W^{(k)}_-$ odd and $W^{(k)}_+$ even under $X\mapsto-X$, so both
lie in $W$. So does $v_0^{(k)}=p_0=X^{n}-Y^{n}$, directly: $p_0|_S=-p_0$ since $n$ is even, and
$p_0|_{(1+U+U^2)}=0$.

By Eichler--Shimura $\dim W=2\dim S_k+1=3$, so it is enough that the three be independent. Each
satisfies $\langle P,P\rangle\neq0$~\cite{VerifyScripts}, and mutually orthogonal vectors with
$\langle P,P\rangle\neq0$ are independent: pairing $\sum_ic_iv_i=0$ against $v_j$ leaves
$c_j\langle v_j,v_j\rangle=0$.

For orthogonality, note first that since $n$ is even, $X\mapsto-X$ multiplies the coefficient of
$X^{n-a}Y^a$ by $(-1)^a$, so an odd polynomial has $p_a=0$ for even $a$ and an even one has $q_b=0$
for odd $b$. As Eq.~\eqref{inner} pairs $p_a$ against $q_{n-a}$, and $a$ odd forces $n-a$ odd,
opposite parities pair to zero: the odd $W^{(k)}_-$ is orthogonal to the even $W^{(k)}_+$ and
$v_0^{(k)}$. For the remaining pair, $p_0$ is supported on its two extreme coefficients, so
$\langle P,p_0\rangle$ is the coefficient of $Y^n$ in $P$ less that of $X^n$; $c$ annihilates both
of these in $W^{(k)}_+$, whence $\langle W^{(k)}_+,v_0^{(k)}\rangle=0$. Hence
$\{W^{(k)}_+,W^{(k)}_-,v_0^{(k)}\}$ is an orthogonal basis, with
$\langle v_0^{(k)},v_0^{(k)}\rangle=-2$ at every such $k$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Periods, Quasi-Periods]\label{def:periods}
Let $k$ be such that $\dim(S_k) = 1$, $n:= k-2$, $W^{(k)}_\pm(X, Y) := Y^n W^{(k)}_\pm(X/Y)$ be as in Definition~\ref{def:Wpm}, and $r_f(X, Y)$ be as in Definition~\ref{def:rf}. Now, define 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:project}
\omega^\pm(f):=\frac{\langle r_f,W^{(k)}_\pm\rangle}{\langle W^{(k)}_\pm,W^{(k)}_\pm\rangle},
\end{equation}

\end{definition}

\section{L-functions, period polynomials and poles at generic locations}\label{sec:arc}


For the reconstruction of period polynomials from special values of $L^*$, which
Section~\ref{sec:Lfun} carried out for $f\in S^!_k$, we needed a region free of poles. As it
happens, and as we show below, when the L-functions defined in this
section are used to build period polynomials that are naturally elements of $W$, choosing the base
point $\tau_0=\rho+1=\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\,i$ and taking the base contour of the
reference homotopy class to lie on the arc $|\tau|=1$ running from $\rho$ to $\rho+1$, along the
lower boundary of the fundamental domain and its $S$-image, makes that pole-free region of
codimension zero in the upper half-plane. Motivated by this, we derive here explicit expressions
for the L-function along this reference contour, defined so as to allow similarly explicit
representations in terms of the $q$-series coefficients of generic $f\in F_k$.

For $f\in F_k$ with poles in $\HH$, the two segment integrals of Definition~\ref{def:Lint} depend
on their contours; we show their sum depends only on the homotopy class of the pair.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:indepHomotopy_arc}
For $f\in F_k$ and $\tau_0\in\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, the L-integral
$L^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ depends only on the homotopy class of its
two segments in $\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, and not on the basepoint
$\tau_0$. At fixed $\tau_0$ each of $L^*_S,L^*_T$ is separately path-independent.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At fixed $\tau_0$ the integrands $f(\tau)\tau^{s-1}$ and $f(\tau)\tk(\tau,s)$ are
holomorphic on $\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, so Cauchy's theorem makes each
of $L^*_S,L^*_T$ constant on the homotopy class of its own segment. Under a motion of the
basepoint, $\partial_{\tau_0}L^*$ is evaluated at the moving endpoints alone; the
computation of Proposition~\ref{prop:indep}, which uses only $f(\tau_0-1)=f(\tau_0)$ and
$f(-1/\tau_0)=\tau_0^{k}f(\tau_0)$, cancels the boundary terms of $L^*_S$ against those
of $L^*_T$, the interior poles lying off the contours. Hence $\partial_{\tau_0}L^*=0$ along
any motion in $\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, while each summand may
separately vary.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:class_arc}
For $f\in F_k$ and $\tau_0\in\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, let
$[\gamma^S_{\tau_0}]$, $[\gamma^T_{\tau_0}]$ denote the homotopy classes of the two
segments in $\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$.
\end{definition}

Thus, with $\gamma^S_{\tau_0} \in [\gamma^S_{\tau_0}]$ and
$\gamma^T_{\tau_0} \in [\gamma^T_{\tau_0}]$, by Proposition~\ref{prop:indepHomotopy_arc}
the value
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lclass_arc}
L^*\big(f,s;[\gamma^S_{\tau_0}],[\gamma^T_{\tau_0}]\big)
:= L^*\big(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0} , \gamma^T_{\tau_0}\big)
= L^*_S(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0}) + L^*_T(f,s;\gamma^T_{\tau_0})
\end{equation}
is independent of the chosen representative paths and independent of $\tau_0$ (subject to
natural constraints of $\tau_0$ monodromies around singular points, which move between
homotopy classes).

The independence of $\tau_0$ is exact, and the mechanism is the $T$-shift of the kernel; we record
it, since the treatment of a pole at an elliptic point rests on it.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:tau0indep}
Let $f\in F_k$ and let $\tau_0,\tau_0'\in\HH$ be joined by a path $\sigma$ meeting no pole of $f$,
with the segments at $\tau_0'$ obtained from those at $\tau_0$ by appending $\sigma$ and its
$S$- and $T^{-1}$-images. Then
$L^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0'},\gamma^T_{\tau_0'})=L^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ for
every $s\in\CC$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\Delta$ for the change in each segment integral. On the $S$-side,
$\Delta L^*_S=-\int_\sigma f(\tau)\tau^{s-1}d\tau+\int_{S\sigma}f(\tau)\tau^{s-1}d\tau$, and
substituting $\tau\mapsto S\tau$ with $f|_kS=f$ gives
$f(S\tau)(S\tau)^{s-1}d(S\tau)=(-1)^{s-1}f(\tau)\tau^{k-s-1}d\tau$, so the second term is
$(-1)^{s-1}\int_\sigma f(\tau)\tau^{k-s-1}d\tau$. On the $T$-side, $T$-periodicity of $f$ gives
$\Delta L^*_T=-\int_\sigma f(\tau)\big[\tk(\tau,s)-\tk(\tau-1,s)\big]d\tau$, and
$\zeta(w,\tau+1)=\zeta(w,\tau)-\tau^{-w}$ applied to both Hurwitz terms of
Definition~\ref{def:kernel} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ktilshift}
\tk(\tau,s)-\tk(\tau-1,s)=-\tau^{s-1}+e^{i\pi(s-1)}\tau^{k-s-1}.
\end{equation}
Adding the two changes, the integrand is
$-\tau^{s-1}+(-1)^{s-1}\tau^{k-s-1}+\tau^{s-1}-e^{i\pi(s-1)}\tau^{k-s-1}$, which vanishes
identically since $(-1)^{s-1}=e^{i\pi(s-1)}$. The prefactor $e^{-\pi is/2}$ multiplies through.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis is only that $\sigma$ avoids the poles: crossing one moves between homotopy
classes, which is the content of Lemma~\ref{lem:wall_arc} below. In particular $L^*$ is
\emph{constant} in any parameter that slides $\tau_0$ without crossing a pole, not merely
convergent as that parameter tends to a limit.

We would like to speak of ``the'' L-integral, and homotopy classes split this into L-integrals for
contours in different classes. We therefore fix a reference class, and we fix it on the boundary of
the fundamental domain.

\begin{definition}[Reference homotopy class for $f \in F_k$]\label{def:georef}
Let $\gamma^{\rm arc}$ be the geodesic segment
$\{e^{i\theta}:\tfrac{\pi}{3}\le\theta\le\tfrac{2\pi}{3}\}$, oriented from $\rho$ to $\rho+1$, and
let $\tau_0=\rho+1$. The \emph{geodesic reference class} is the class of
$\hat\gamma^S_f=\hat\gamma^T_f=\gamma^{\rm arc}$.
\end{definition}

The choice is admissible by Definition~\ref{def:segments}, since $-1/(\rho+1)=\rho$ and
$(\rho+1)-1=\rho$: the two segments share their endpoints and may be taken to be the same path.

It is also the only such choice. The two segments share their endpoints exactly when
$S\tau_0=T^{-1}\tau_0$, that is $-1/\tau_0=\tau_0-1$, and $\tau^2-\tau+1$ has the single root
$\rho+1$ in $\HH$; the same equation says $\tau_0$ is the fixed point of $U=TS$. The segment is
then the geodesic from $\rho$ to $\rho+1$, which is the wall between the fundamental domain and
its $S$-image, and $S$ carries it to itself. $T$ admits no counterpart: being parabolic it fixes
no point of $\HH$, and it exchanges the two vertical walls rather than preserving either.

Poles on $\gamma^{\rm arc}$ never occur singly. As $S$ acts on the unit circle by
$e^{i\theta}\mapsto e^{i(\pi-\theta)}$, preserving $\tfrac{\pi}{3}\le\theta\le\tfrac{2\pi}{3}$,
while $f^{-1}(\infty)$ is $\Gamma$-stable and $f|_kS=f$, a pole of order $P$ at $e^{i\phi}$ on the
arc is accompanied by one of the same order at $e^{i(\pi-\phi)}$, again on the arc. These $S$-pairs
have two degenerations: the pair collapses to a single point exactly when $\phi=\tfrac{\pi}{2}$,
and it consists of the two endpoints exactly when $\phi\in\{\tfrac{\pi}{3},\tfrac{2\pi}{3}\}$,
since $S(\rho+1)=-1/(\rho+1)=\rho$. All three cases are treated the same way, by moving the poles
off the contour rather than moving the contour.

We move the contour off the poles, never the poles off the contour. Displacing a pole is not
available to us: $f^{-1}(\infty)$ is $\SLZ$-stable, so a pole cannot be moved alone, and at an
elliptic point of order $n$ the stabiliser acts as rotation by $2\pi/n$ about a point the contour
runs through, sending any displacement to the far side and pinching the contour. Displacing the
base point costs nothing, by Lemma~\ref{lem:tau0indep}.

\begin{definition}\label{def:arcshift}
For $0<\epsilon<\tfrac12$ put $\tau_0(\epsilon):=(1+\epsilon)e^{i\pi/3}$, so that
$S\tau_0(\epsilon)=(1+\epsilon)^{-1}e^{2i\pi/3}$ and $T^{-1}\tau_0(\epsilon)=\rho+\epsilon
e^{i\pi/3}$. Let $\hat\gamma^S_\epsilon$ be the logarithmic spiral
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arcspiral}
\tau(\theta)=\exp\Big(\log(1+\epsilon)\,\tfrac{\pi/2-\theta}{\pi/6}+i\theta\Big),
\qquad \theta:\tfrac{2\pi}{3}\to\tfrac{\pi}{3},
\end{equation}
and let $\hat\gamma^T_\epsilon$ be a path from $T^{-1}\tau_0(\epsilon)$ to $S\tau_0(\epsilon)$
inside $|\tau-\rho|=O(\epsilon)$, followed by $\hat\gamma^S_\epsilon$. The
\emph{$\epsilon$-shifted geodesic class} is the pair of their homotopy classes in
$\HH\setminus f^{-1}(\infty)$.
\end{definition}

Three features of \eqref{eq:arcspiral} are used below. Its $\log r$ is odd about
$\theta=\tfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $S$ acts by $(r,\theta)\mapsto(1/r,\pi-\theta)$, so
$\hat\gamma^S_\epsilon$ is $S$-symmetric and Lemma~\ref{lem:georetrace} applies to the
$S^2$-path. It lies outside $|\tau|=1$ for $\theta<\tfrac{\pi}{2}$ and inside for
$\theta>\tfrac{\pi}{2}$, so an $S$-pair of poles on the arc is passed on opposite sides
automatically, with no side chosen by hand. And it reduces to $\gamma^{\rm arc}$ as
$\epsilon\to0$, the two segments then sharing their endpoints as in
Definition~\ref{def:georef}.

Since $S\tau_0(\epsilon)$ and $T^{-1}\tau_0(\epsilon)$ are distinct for $\epsilon\ne0$, the
connector in Definition~\ref{def:arcshift} is not determined by its endpoints, and the classes
available to it are indexed by an integer. We name that integer through the relator path, where it
is what the period polynomial sees.

\begin{definition}\label{def:arcwind}
Let $W(f,\epsilon)\in\ZZ$ be the winding number about $\rho+1$ of the closed path traced by
$(ST)^3$ in the $\epsilon$-shifted geodesic class, computed in the coordinate
$w=(\tau-\rho-1)/(\tau-\overline{\rho+1})$.
\end{definition}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:wall_arc} the L-integral is an affine function of $W$, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:arcwind} below $W\equiv-1\pmod 3$, so $W=0$ is not realised by any single
contour. It is nonetheless the value at which the period polynomial lies in $W$, and $L^*$ is
linear, so we define the L-integral by evaluating that affine function there.

\begin{definition}\label{def:arczero}
Let $f\in F_k$ have a pole on $\SLZ\!\cdot\!\rho$ or at $i$. Then $L^*(f,s)$ is the value at
$W=0$ of the affine function $W\mapsto L^*(f,s;\hat\gamma^S_\epsilon,\hat\gamma^T_\epsilon)$, and
likewise for the period polynomial of Definition~\ref{def:rf}.
\end{definition}

Equivalently, $L^*(f,s)$ is the weighted average of L-integrals over two realisable classes, with
weights $w_j$ fixed by $\sum_jw_j=1$ and $\sum_jw_jW_j=0$: at $i$, where $W$ is odd, the two
classes $W=\pm1$ enter with weight $\tfrac12$ each, so $L^*$ is the mean of the two indentations;
at $\rho$ the classes $W=-1$ and $W=2$ enter with weights $\tfrac23,\tfrac13$. In both cases the
correction to the non-winding class is $\tfrac1n$ of one residue,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arczero}
L^*(f,s)=L^*\big(f,s;W=-1\big)+\frac{2\pi i}{n}\,e^{-\pi is/2}\Res_{\tau=\tau_e}\!\big(f\,\tk\big),
\end{equation}
the weight $\tfrac1n$ depending only on the order of the elliptic point, never on $f$ or on the
order of its pole. So \eqref{eq:arczero} is linear in $f$, and is independent of $\epsilon$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:tau0indep}. Poles of $f$ on $\gamma^{\rm arc}$ away from the elliptic points need
no prescription at all: the spiral of \eqref{eq:arcspiral} already separates each $S$-pair, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:arcon} either separation puts $\hat r_f$ in $W$.

With this definition we may unambiguously write
\begin{align}\label{eq:Lstararc}
L^*(f,s) := L^*\big(f, s; [\hat\gamma_f^S], [\hat\gamma_f^T]\big)~,
\end{align}
where for $f$ with a pole at an elliptic point the right-hand side is read through
Definition~\ref{def:arczero}: there the value is not an integral over one homotopy class but the
weighted average of two, so \eqref{eq:Lstararc} fixes a number and not a contour.
Having fixed this reference value, consider two paths $\gamma^S_{\tau_0}$ and
$\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$ which may lie in any homotopy class, and let $X_S(p),X_T(p)\in\ZZ$ count the
windings of $\gamma^S_{\tau_0}$, $\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$ about each pole $\tau_p\in f^{-1}(\infty)$
relative to the reference class.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:wall_arc}
Let $f\in F_k$, $r_S(f, \tau_p) := \Res_{\tau=\tau_p} (f(\tau) \tau^{s-1})$
and $r_T(f, \tau_p) := \Res_{\tau=\tau_p} (f(\tau) \tk(\tau, s))$. Then,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wall_arc}
L^*\big(f,s;[\gamma^S_{\tau_0}],[\gamma^T_{\tau_0}]\big)
= L^*(f,s)
+ 2\pi i\,e^{-\pi i s/2}\sum_{\tau_p\in f^{-1}(\infty)}\Big[
X_S(\tau_p) r_S(f, \tau_p)
+ X_T(\tau_p) r_T(f, \tau_p) \Big].
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Two pairs whose winding data differ by one unit at a single pole $p$, on one segment, bound a loop
encircling $p$ once; the residue theorem adds $2\pi i$ times the residue of that segment's
integrand, $f(\tau)\tau^{s-1}$ for $\gamma^S$ or $f(\tau)\tk(\tau,s)$ for $\gamma^T$, and the
prefactor $e^{-\pi i s/2}$ of Definition~\ref{def:Lint} multiplies through. Summing over crossings
gives~\eqref{eq:wall_arc}.
\end{proof}

In particular two contours passing on opposite sides of a pole differ by its residue, and nothing
else. For a pole on the arc away from the elliptic points this is the whole of the ambiguity.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:arcside}
Let $f\in F_k$ have poles on $\gamma^{\rm arc}$, none of them at $i$, $\rho$ or $\rho+1$. Write
$\gamma^{\rm arc}_{\pm}(p)$ for $\gamma^{\rm arc}$ indented near $p$ so as to pass outside,
respectively inside, $|\tau|=1$. Then $L^*(f,s)$ is defined on either contour, and replacing
$\gamma^{\rm arc}_{+}(p)$ by $\gamma^{\rm arc}_{-}(p)$ at one pole changes it by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sidediff}
-2\pi i\,e^{-\pi is/2}\big[r_S(f,p)+r_T(f,p)\big].
\end{equation}
The spiral $\hat\gamma^S_\epsilon$ of \eqref{eq:arcspiral} realises one such contour for every
$\epsilon>0$, passing outside at $\theta<\tfrac{\pi}{2}$ and inside at $\theta>\tfrac{\pi}{2}$, so
each $S$-pair is separated without a side being chosen by hand.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each indented contour keeps $p$ at positive distance, so both segment integrals converge and
$L^*$ is defined on either; the spiral does the same for every $\epsilon>0$, and the sides it
takes are as stated because $\log r$ in \eqref{eq:arcspiral} is positive for
$\theta<\tfrac{\pi}{2}$ and negative for $\theta>\tfrac{\pi}{2}$.

For the difference, $\gamma^{\rm arc}_+(p)$ and $\gamma^{\rm arc}_-(p)$ agree off a neighbourhood
of $p$ and together bound a loop encircling $p$ once. Along $\gamma^{\rm arc}$ the direction of
travel is $\theta$ decreasing, so it is $-ie^{i\theta}$ up to a positive factor, while the outward
radial direction is $e^{i\theta}=i\cdot(-ie^{i\theta})$: outward lies to the left of travel. The
sliver between the two indentations therefore lies to the right of $\gamma^{\rm arc}_+(p)$
traversed forward, and the loop encircles $p$ negatively. This is the winding datum
$X_S(p)=X_T(p)=-1$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:wall_arc}, which gives~\eqref{eq:sidediff}.
\end{proof}

The exclusion of $i$, $\rho$ and $\rho+1$ is not a technicality: there the stabiliser orbit meets
$\gamma^{\rm arc}$ on both sides at once, no single-sided indentation exists, and the winding of
Definition~\ref{def:arcwind} cannot be set to zero by any one contour.

This provides the basics for the finite L-functions of $f\in F_k$ in this class. We wish, however,
to present them in terms of their $q$-series coefficients, as was done for $f\in M^!_k$ in
Section~\ref{sec:Lfun}. The obstruction to doing so directly is not a technical one. For
$f\in M^!_k$ the series $\sum_n c_f(n)q^n$ converges on all of $\HH$, as $f$ is holomorphic there,
and term-by-term evaluation is legitimate throughout. A pole at finite height ends this. The
expansion about the cusp then represents $f$ only above the highest pole, on
${\rm Im}\,\tau>y_f:=\sup\{{\rm Im}\,\tau_p\}$, with coefficients growing as
$|c_f(n)|\gtrsim n^{m-1}e^{2\pi n\,{\rm Im}\,\tau_p}$ for a pole of order $m$~\cite{1806}. The arc
has height varying between $\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ and $1$, so the operative threshold is its lowest
point $\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$: as soon as $f$ has a pole at or above that height the $q$-series
diverges somewhere on the contour, and there is then no series to integrate term by term, rather
than a slowly convergent one to be handled with care. To do this, we must introduce the
polylogarithmic projections of Ref.~\cite{1806}.

\subsection{Explicit formulae for $L^*(f,s)$ on the arc}\label{sec:arcL}


We lay out the starting definitions and facts.

\begin{definition}\label{def:Finfty_arc}
$F(\infty)$ is the space of meromorphic functions $g:\HH\to\CC$ with $g(\tau+1)=g(\tau)$, finitely
many poles in each compact subset of the strip $|{\rm Re}\,\tau|\le\tfrac12$, and bounded pole
order at $q=0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}\label{def:proj_arc}
Let $f\in F_k$ have a pole of order $m_p$ at $\tau_p\in\HH$. On a punctured $\varepsilon$-ball
about $\tau_p$ small enough to exclude the other poles of $f$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:laurent_arc}
f(\tau)=\sum_{m=1}^{m_p}\frac{r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)}{(\tau-\tau_p)^{m}}+g(\tau),
\qquad
r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|\tau-\tau_p|=\varepsilon}
f(\tau)\,(\tau-\tau_p)^{m-1}\,d\tau ,
\end{equation}
with $g$ holomorphic at $\tau_p$. We call $r^*_{f,\tau_p}(1),\dots,r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m_p)$ the
\emph{pole weights} of $f$ at $\tau_p$; the first is the residue, and the last is nonzero, which is
what fixes the order. The \emph{polylog projection} of $f$ at $\tau_p$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polyproj_arc}
\widehat P_{\tau_p}f(\tau):=\sum_{m=1}^{m_p} r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)\,
\frac{(-2\pi i)^m}{(m-1)!}\,\Li_{1-m}\big(e^{2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)}\big),
\qquad \Li_s(x):=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{x^{n}}{n^{s}}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The composite projector is the one place where the contour enters. Its threshold is set so that
$(1-\widehat P)f$ admits a $q$-series everywhere it is used, which is a statement about a
half-plane: a $q$-series converges exactly on ${\rm Im}\,\tau>h$ with $h$ the height of the highest
surviving pole. The operative height is the lowest point of $\gamma^{\rm arc}$, namely
$\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, since the mode sums below are controlled by $e^{-2\pi n\,{\rm Im}\,\tau}$ and
the arc descends to that height at its endpoints.

\begin{definition}\label{def:geoproj}
For $f\in F_k$ with $T$-inequivalent finite-order poles $\{\tau_p\}$ of orders $\{m_p\}$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polesplit_arc}
\widehat P^{\,\rm geo}:=\sum_{{\rm Im}\,\tau_p\ge\frac{\sqrt3}{2}}\widehat P_{\tau_p},
\end{equation}
with $\widehat P_{\tau_p}$ as in Definition~\ref{def:proj_arc}, and write
$f=\widehat P^{\,\rm geo}f+(I-\widehat P^{\,\rm geo})f$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:proj_arc}
The normalized block $\frac{(-2\pi i)^m}{(m-1)!}\Li_{1-m}\big(e^{2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)}\big)$ has
principal part exactly $(\tau-\tau_p)^{-m}$, so $\widehat P_{\tau_p}f$ matches the principal part
of $f$ and $(I-\widehat P_{\tau_p})f$ is holomorphic at $\tau_p$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By the small-$t$ expansion $\Li_{1-m}(e^{-t})=(m-1)!\,t^{-m}+O(t^0)$ with
$t=-2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)$, the block has a \emph{pure} pole
$(\tau-\tau_p)^{-m}+O((\tau-\tau_p)^0)$ at $\tau_p$, with no lower-order poles. Summing against the
Laurent coefficients $r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)$ reproduces the principal part of $f$ term by term, leaving
$(I-\widehat P_{\tau_p})f$ regular at $\tau_p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:cRf_arc}
For $f\in F_k$ and $\widehat P^{\,\rm geo}$ as in Definition~\ref{def:geoproj}, write
\begin{equation}
(I-\widehat P^{\,\rm geo})f(\tau)=\sum_n c_{Rf}(n)\,q^n,\qquad q=e^{2\pi i\tau}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:projspace_arc}
$F_k\subset F(\infty)$ and $(I-\widehat P^{\,\rm geo})f\in F(\infty)$. Every pole of
$(I-\widehat P^{\,\rm geo})f$ has ${\rm Im}\,\tau<\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, with a maximal height
$y_b<\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ (set $y_b=0$ if $(I-\widehat P^{\,\rm geo})f$ is holomorphic).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A weakly holomorphic form is $T$-periodic, meromorphic, and $O(e^{C{\rm Im}\tau})$ at the cusp, so
$F_k\subset F(\infty)$; the projected polylogs $\Li_{1-m}(e^{2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)})$ share these
properties, so $(I-\widehat P^{\,\rm geo})f\in F(\infty)$ (and leaves $F_k$, being non-modular). By
Lemma~\ref{lem:proj_arc}, $\widehat P^{\,\rm geo}$ cancels the principal part of every pole with
${\rm Im}\,\tau\ge\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, so the poles of $(I-\widehat P^{\,\rm geo})f$ lie strictly
below $\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$; finite in each compact set and accumulating only at ${\rm Im}\,\tau=0$,
they have a maximal height $y_b<\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$.
\end{proof}

The Fourier coefficients of a $T$-periodic meromorphic function are controlled by its highest pole:
$|c_{Rf}(n)|\le e^{2\pi n(y_b+\varepsilon)}$ for every $\varepsilon>0$ and all large
$n$~\cite{1806}. Since $y_b<\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, the series $\sum_n c_{Rf}(n)q^n$ converges
geometrically down to ${\rm Im}\,\tau=\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, at base
$e^{-2\pi(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}-y_b-\varepsilon)}$, and so on $\gamma^{\rm arc}$ itself. This is the
point of the projection: convergence merely for large ${\rm Im}\,\tau$ would be automatic and of no
use, as the contour descends to height $\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$.

One further integral of $f$ along the contour will be needed, both for the residue of $L^*$ at
$s=0$ and, later, for period polynomials.

\begin{definition}\label{def:Phi}
For $f\in F_k$, set $\Phi(f):=\int_{\hat\gamma^T_f}f(\tau)\,d\tau$, the integral of $f$ along the
$T$-segment of the contour defining $L^*(f,s)$.
\end{definition}

$\Phi$ is not an independent choice: it is what the parabolicity condition $\hat C_T=0$ measures,
and $\hat C_T$ is assembled from the $T$-transport, so $\Phi$ follows the contour. Deforming the
segments replaces $\gamma^{\rm arc}$ in the integral by whatever $\hat\gamma^T_f$ then is, with no
further stipulation.

Since $\hat\gamma^T_f$ runs from $T^{-1}\tau_0$ to $\tau_0$, and $e^{2\pi in\tau}$ takes equal
values at those two endpoints, the modes $n\neq0$ of the $q$-series of $f$ cancel between them.
Hence $\Phi(f)=c_f(0)$ whenever that series converges along the segment, which is to say whenever
no pole of $f$ lies above $\hat\gamma^T_f$; a pole below is harmless. Otherwise $\Phi(f)$ is
$c_f(0)$ less $2\pi i$ times the residues of the poles in the strip between the segment and the
cusp. In particular $\Phi(E_k)=1$, and $\Phi(f)=0$ for $f\in S_k$.

The relations satisfied by the pole weights at an elliptic point are contour-free, so they hold
here verbatim; we record them now, as both the block integral below and the period polynomials
constructed later use them.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ellLaurent}
Let $\tau_e$ be $i$ or $\rho$, of order $n=2$ or $n=3$, let
$\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ be the generator $S$ or $ST$ of
its stabiliser in $\SLZ$, and set $\zeta:=c\tau_e+d$, so that $c=1$ and $\zeta=e^{i\pi/n}$. Let
$f\in F_k$ have a pole of order $P$ at $\tau_e$ with pole weights $r^*_{f,\tau_e}(m)$. Modularity is
equivalent to one relation for each principal-part order $\mu$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ellLaurent}
\sum_{m=\mu}^{P} r^*_{f,\tau_e}(m)\binom{m}{\mu}\,\zeta^{m}
=\zeta^{k}\sum_{m=\mu}^{P} r^*_{f,\tau_e}(m)\binom{k}{m-\mu}\,\zeta^{-m},
\qquad \forall\mu=1,\dots,P,
\end{equation}
and the leading relation is $\big(\zeta^{2P}-\zeta^{k}\big)r^*_{f,\tau_e}(P)=0$, so that
$2P\equiv k \pmod{2n}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $\tau=\tau_e+\varepsilon$. Since $\det\gamma=1$ and $\gamma\tau_e=\tau_e$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ellshift}
\gamma\tau-\tau_e=\frac{\tau-\tau_e}{(c\tau+d)(c\tau_e+d)}=\frac{\varepsilon}{\zeta(\zeta+\varepsilon)},
\qquad (c\tau+d)^{k}=\zeta^{k}\big(1+\varepsilon/\zeta\big)^{k},
\end{equation}
using $c=1$. Hence $(\gamma\tau-\tau_e)^{-m}=\zeta^{2m}\varepsilon^{-m}(1+\varepsilon/\zeta)^{m}$,
and matching the coefficient of $\varepsilon^{-\mu}$ in $f(\gamma\tau)=(c\tau+d)^{k}f(\tau)$
through $(1+\varepsilon/\zeta)^{a}=\sum_{l}\binom{a}{l}\zeta^{-l}\varepsilon^{l}$ gives
$\sum_{m\ge\mu}r^*(m)\zeta^{2m}\binom{m}{\mu}\zeta^{-(m-\mu)}
=\zeta^{k}\sum_{m\ge\mu}r^*(m)\binom{k}{m-\mu}\zeta^{-(m-\mu)}$; dividing by $\zeta^{\mu}$
yields~\eqref{eq:ellLaurent}. At $\mu=P$ only $m=P$ survives on each side.
\end{proof}

At $n=2$ the order condition forces $P$ odd when $k\equiv2\pmod4$ and $P$ even when $4\mid k$; at
$n=3$ it reads $2P\equiv k\pmod 6$.

Each pole with ${\rm Im}\,\tau_p\ge\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ contributes one projected polylog to
$L^*(f,s)$, whose L-integral we now record.

\begin{definition}\label{def:geoIN}
For a pole of order $N+1$ projected at $z$, let $\lambda_{N,z}$ denote the corresponding block
of~\eqref{eq:polyproj_arc}, and set
$I^{\rm geo}_N(s,z):=L^*\big(\lambda_{N,z},\,s;\,[\hat\gamma^S_f],[\hat\gamma^T_f]\big)$,
evaluated in the geodesic reference class of Definition~\ref{def:georef}.
\end{definition}

The blocks need not be integrated on the arc. Both kernels $\tau^{s-1}$ and $\tk(\tau,s)$ are
holomorphic on all of $\HH$, so the only obstruction to deforming is the block's own poles at
$z+\ZZ$, and one may evaluate on the horizontal chord from $\rho$ to $\rho+1$ instead. Let
$\Lambda$ be the open lens between the two, bounded above by $\gamma^{\rm arc}$ and below by the
chord, so that
$\Lambda\subset\{|\tau|<1,\ {\rm Im}\,\tau>\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2},\ |{\rm Re}\,\tau|<\tfrac12\}$; at
most one member of $z+\ZZ$ lies in it. If $z$ itself lies on $\gamma^{\rm arc}$ the contour is
indented past it as in Proposition~\ref{prop:arcside}. In either situation set $\varepsilon_z:=1$
when $z$ lies between the contour and the chord, that is when $z\in\Lambda$, or when
$z\in\gamma^{\rm arc}$ and the indentation is the outward one $\gamma^{\rm arc}_+(z)$; and
$\varepsilon_z:=0$ otherwise. The deformation to the chord costs one residue exactly when
$\varepsilon_z=1$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:geopolylog}
Let ${\rm Im}\,z>\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, set $\tau_0=\rho+1$, let
$\gamma^*(s,x):=\sum_{\ell\ge0}\frac{(-x)^{\ell}}{\ell!\,(\ell+s)}$, entire in $x$, read empty
products below as $1$, and set $(2\pi n)^{w}:=e^{w(\log 2\pi|n|+i\pi)}$ for $n<0$. With, for
$n\in\ZZ\setminus\{0\}$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:geoG}
G^{\rm geo}_{s,k}(n)&:=e^{-\pi is/2}\Big[\tau_0^{\,s}\,\gamma^*\big(s,-2\pi in\tau_0\big)
-\rho^{\,s}\,\gamma^*\big(s,-2\pi in\rho\big)\Big]
\;+\;\frac{\Gamma\big(s,-2\pi in\tau_0\big)}{(2\pi n)^{s}}
\;+\;i^{k}\frac{\Gamma\big(k-s,-2\pi in\tau_0\big)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}},\\
\label{eq:geoG0}
G^{\rm geo}_{s,k}(0)&:=e^{-\pi is/2}\,\frac{\tau_0^{\,s}-\rho^{\,s}}{s}
-\frac{(\tau_0/i)^{s}}{s}-i^{k}\frac{(\tau_0/i)^{k-s}}{k-s},
\\
\label{eq:geocross}
X_N(s,z)&:=\frac{e^{-\pi is/2}}{(2\pi i)^{N}}\bigg[(-1)^{N}z^{\,s-1-N}\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}(s-1-j)
+\zeta\big(1-s+N,\,z+1\big)
\\\nonumber&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad
\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}(1-s+j)
-e^{i\pi(s-1)}\zeta\big(1-k+s+N,\,z+1\big)\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}(1-k+s+j)\bigg],
\end{align}
we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geoIN}
I^{\rm geo}_N(s,z)=-\delta_{N,0}\,G^{\rm geo}_{s,k}(0)
+(-1)^{N+1}\sum_{n\ge1}n^{N}e^{2\pi inz}\,G^{\rm geo}_{s,k}(-n)
+\varepsilon_z\,X_N(s,z),
\end{equation}
the sum converging at rate $e^{-2\pi({\rm Im}\,z-\frac{\sqrt3}{2})}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Both kernels are holomorphic on $\HH$, and the only poles of $\lambda_{N,z}$ are at $z+\ZZ$, so
Cauchy's theorem moves both segment integrals to the chord at the cost of whatever residues lie
between the two paths; by the paragraph above that is $\{z\}$ when $\varepsilon_z=1$ and nothing
otherwise. The chord is not a
convenience: along $\gamma^{\rm arc}$ itself ${\rm Im}\,\tau$ ranges over
$[\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2},1]$, so for $\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}<{\rm Im}\,z<1$ the comparison below would change
sign partway along, whereas on the chord ${\rm Im}\,\tau\equiv\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ and it is a single
global inequality.

On the chord $|e^{2\pi i(\tau-z)}|>1$, so the defining series of $\Li_{-N}$ diverges and inversion
$\Li_{-N}(w)=(-1)^{N+1}\Li_{-N}(1/w)-\delta_{N,0}$ rewrites $\lambda_{N,z}$ as
$(-1)^{N+1}\sum_{n\ge1}n^{N}e^{2\pi inz}q^{-n}-\delta_{N,0}$, uniformly convergent there since
${\rm Im}\,z>\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$. Integrating term by term, $G^{\rm geo}_{s,k}(-n)=L^*(q^{-n},s)$ in
this class: the last two terms of~\eqref{eq:geoG} are $L^*_T$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:Tclosed-Mk} at
$\tau_0$, and the first is $L^*_S=e^{-\pi is/2}\int_{\rho}^{\tau_0}e^{-2\pi in\tau}\tau^{s-1}d\tau$,
which on expanding $e^{-2\pi in\tau}$ and integrating $\tau^{\ell+s-1}$ term by term gives
$e^{-\pi is/2}\sum_{\ell\ge0}\frac{(-2\pi in)^{\ell}}{\ell!\,(\ell+s)}
\big[\tau_0^{\,\ell+s}-\rho^{\,\ell+s}\big]$,
the first bracket of~\eqref{eq:geoG}. Likewise $G^{\rm geo}_{s,k}(0)=L^*(1,s)$. The rate follows
from $|e^{2\pi inz}|=e^{-2\pi n\,{\rm Im}\,z}$ against
$|G^{\rm geo}_{s,k}(-n)|\sim e^{\pi\sqrt3\,n}$.

For the crossing term put $u=\tau-z$ and $v=2\pi iu+t$. Then
$\sum_{N\ge0}\Li_{-N}\big(e^{2\pi iu}\big)\tfrac{t^{N}}{N!}=\tfrac{e^{v}}{1-e^{v}}
=-\sum_{m\ge0}\tfrac{B_m(-v)^{m}}{m!\,v}$, of which only the $m=0$ term $-1/v$ carries negative
powers of $u$; expanding $-1/(2\pi iu+t)=-\sum_{r\ge0}(-1)^{r}t^{r}(2\pi iu)^{-r-1}$ and reading
off $t^{N}/N!$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blockpp}
\lambda_{N,z}(\tau)=\frac{(-1)^{N+1}N!}{\big(2\pi i(\tau-z)\big)^{N+1}}+O(1),
\end{equation}
a lone singular term, so $\Res_{\tau=z}\big(\lambda_{N,z}\kappa\big)
=(-1)^{N+1}\kappa^{(N)}(z)/(2\pi i)^{N+1}$ for any $\kappa$ holomorphic at $z$. Both paths run
$\rho\to\rho+1$ with the arc above, so together they bound $\Lambda$ clockwise and the deformation
contributes $-2\pi i$ times that residue for each kernel. With
$\partial_z^{N}z^{s-1}=z^{s-1-N}\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}(s-1-j)$ and
$\partial_z^{N}\zeta(w,z+1)=(-1)^{N}\zeta(w+N,z+1)\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}(w+j)$ applied to both terms of
$\tk$, this is $X_N(s,z)$ of~\eqref{eq:geocross}.
\end{proof}


Corollary~\ref{cor:Sclosed} is deliberately not used here: it folds the $S$-segment about $i$ using
modularity, and $q^{n}$ is only $T$-periodic, so its $S$-integral must be taken directly. Nor does
that segment drop out, as it would for a base point at which the two segments coincide: on the arc
it contributes the first bracket of~\eqref{eq:geoG}, and the threshold governing the convergence
rate stands at $\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, in step with Definition~\ref{def:geoproj}.

The $S$-term is written with $\gamma^*$ because that form is branch-free: $\gamma^*$ is
single-valued, so the only multivalued factors are the explicit $\tau_0^{\,s}$ and $\rho^{\,s}$,
and those are unambiguous since $\arg\tau\in[\tfrac{\pi}{3},\tfrac{2\pi}{3}]$ along the chord. It
may equally be written with upper incomplete gammas, provided the sheet relation is carried. Under
$u=2\pi in\tau$ the chord's image runs from $\arg u=\tfrac{7\pi}{6}$ at $\rho$ to
$\tfrac{5\pi}{6}$ at $\tau_0$, so the $\rho$ endpoint sits one turn off principal; since
$\gamma(s,x)=x^{s}\gamma^*(s,x)$ with $\gamma^*$ single-valued, a shift of $\arg x$ by $2\pi$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sheet}
\Gamma^{\rm cont}(s,x)=e^{2\pi is}\,\Gamma(s,x)+\big(1-e^{2\pi is}\big)\Gamma(s),
\end{equation}
and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geoSupper}
S_n(s)=(2\pi in)^{-s}\Big[e^{2\pi is}\,\Gamma\big(s,2\pi in\rho\big)
+\big(1-e^{2\pi is}\big)\Gamma(s)-\Gamma\big(s,2\pi in\tau_0\big)\Big].
\end{equation}
Both the sheet factor and the additive constant are trivial at integer $s$, which is all that
Definition~\ref{def:rf} requires. The $T$-term needs neither, since by
Lemma~\ref{lem:Tclosed-Mk} only $\tau_0$ appears there, at $\arg(2\pi in\tau_0)=\tfrac{5\pi}{6}$,
the $\rho$ endpoint having been absorbed by the periodicity of $\tk$. It does need the branch of
$(2\pi n)^{w}$ at $n<0$ fixed above, which is the one making
$\arg(2\pi n)+\arg(\tau_0/i)=\tfrac{5\pi}{6}$; the opposite choice differs by $e^{2\pi is}$, and so
is invisible at integer $s$ and wrong elsewhere. In practice the $\gamma^*$
series loses about $2.7n$ digits to cancellation, so~\eqref{eq:geoSupper} is preferable if large
$n$ is needed.

On the threshold line itself the roles of the two series are exchanged. A pole at
${\rm Im}\,z=\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ lies \emph{below} the arc, which at abscissa ${\rm Re}\,z$ has
height $\sqrt{1-({\rm Re}\,z)^{2}}>\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, so it is the defining series of $\Li_{-N}$
that applies rather than its inversion. That series converges absolutely for
${\rm Im}\,z<\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ and is exactly marginal at $\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, since the arc
attains that height at its two endpoints. Write $(a)_{(\ell)}:=a(a-1)\cdots(a-\ell+1)$ for the
falling factorial, with $(a)_{(0)}:=1$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:geoedge}
Let ${\rm Im}\,z=\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, and either $z\not\equiv\rho\!\pmod{\ZZ}$ or
$c_N(s,k)=0$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geocl}
c_\ell(s,k):=(s-1)_{(\ell)}\Big[-i\,i^{\ell}e^{-\pi is/2}
\big(\tau_0^{\,s-1-\ell}-\rho^{\,s-1-\ell}\big)+(-i\tau_0)^{s-1-\ell}\Big]
+i^{k}(k-s-1)_{(\ell)}(-i\tau_0)^{k-s-1-\ell}.
\end{equation}
Then for every $L\ge N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geoedge}
I^{\rm geo}_N(s,z)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}\frac{c_\ell(s,k)}{(2\pi)^{\ell+1}}\,
\Li_{\ell+1-N}\big(e^{2\pi i(\tau_0-z)}\big)+R^{\rm geo}_{N,L}(s,z),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geoR}
R^{\rm geo}_{N,L}(s,z):=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{N}e^{-2\pi inz}
\Big[G^{\rm geo}_{s,k}(n)-e^{2\pi in\rho}\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}\frac{c_\ell(s,k)}{(2\pi n)^{\ell+1}}\Big]
\end{equation}
converges absolutely, its terms being $O(n^{\,N-L-2})$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For ${\rm Im}\,z<\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ the series
$\lambda_{N,z}=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{N}e^{-2\pi inz}q^{n}$ converges uniformly on $\gamma^{\rm arc}$,
both kernels are bounded there, and term-by-term integration gives
$I^{\rm geo}_N(s,z)=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{N}e^{-2\pi inz}G^{\rm geo}_{s,k}(n)$, absolutely convergent.

Repeated integration by parts of the $S$-integral gives
$\int_{\rho}^{\tau_0}e^{2\pi in\tau}\tau^{s-1}d\tau
\sim\sum_{\ell\ge0}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}(s-1)_{(\ell)}}{(2\pi in)^{\ell+1}}
\big[e^{2\pi in\tau}\tau^{s-1-\ell}\big]_{\rho}^{\tau_0}$, while
$\Gamma(a,X)\sim X^{a-1}e^{-X}\sum_{\ell\ge0}(a-1)_{(\ell)}X^{-\ell}$ at
$X=-2\pi in\tau_0=2\pi n(-i\tau_0)$ handles the two incomplete gammas. Since
$e^{2\pi in\tau_0}=e^{2\pi in\rho}$ by $T$-periodicity, both endpoints carry the same exponential
and, using $(-1)^{\ell}/i^{\ell+1}=-i\,i^{\ell}$,
$G^{\rm geo}_{s,k}(n)=e^{2\pi in\rho}\big[\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}c_\ell(s,k)(2\pi n)^{-\ell-1}
+O(n^{-L-2})\big]$ with $c_\ell$ as in~\eqref{eq:geocl}.

Splitting the sum along this expansion, the peeled part is
$\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}\frac{c_\ell}{(2\pi)^{\ell+1}}\sum_{n\ge1}n^{N-\ell-1}e^{2\pi in(\rho-z)}$, and
$e^{2\pi i(\rho-z)}=e^{2\pi i(\tau_0-z)}$, giving the polylogarithms
of~\eqref{eq:geoedge}; the remainder is~\eqref{eq:geoR}, whose terms are $O(n^{N-L-2})$ because
$|e^{-2\pi inz}e^{2\pi in\rho}|=1$ on the threshold line. Both sides of~\eqref{eq:geoedge} are holomorphic in
$z$ wherever $z$ avoids $\gamma^{\rm arc}+\ZZ$ and $e^{2\pi i(\tau_0-z)}\ne1$ --- the left because
the contour integral of Definition~\ref{def:geoIN} then has no singular integrand, the right
because $R^{\rm geo}_{N,L}$ converges locally uniformly --- so the identity, proved on
${\rm Im}\,z<\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, persists onto the threshold line away from $\rho+\ZZ$.

At $z\equiv\rho$ the argument $e^{2\pi i(\tau_0-z)}$ equals $1$ and the $\ell=N$ term carries
$\Li_1(1)$, so the statement needs $c_N=0$. That coefficient is exactly the endpoint mismatch: by
the computation above, $c_0$ is a non-zero multiple of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpointmismatch}
\big[\rho^{\,s-1}+\tk(\rho,s)\big]-\big[\tau_0^{\,s-1}+\tk(\tau_0,s)\big],
\end{equation}
which by the shift identity \eqref{eq:ktilshift} reads
$\rho^{\,s-1}=e^{i\pi(s-1)}\tau_0^{\,k-s-1}$ and so, with $\rho=e^{2\pi i/3}$ and
$\tau_0=e^{i\pi/3}$, vanishes for every $s$ precisely when $k\equiv2\pmod6$. This is not a side
condition to be hoped for. A pole \emph{at} $\rho$ has $P\equiv1\pmod n$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:ellLaurent}, and at $P=1$ --- the only order for which the block itself, rather
than the winding prescription of Definition~\ref{def:arczero}, is what one needs --- that forces
$k\equiv2\pmod6$ and so $c_0=0$. The divergence one might have expected at $\rho$ is absent
exactly when the theory calls on it.
\end{proof}


The blocks assemble into the projection at a pole by linearity alone.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:geowhole}
Let $f\in F_k$ have a pole of order $m_p$ at $\tau_p$ with ${\rm Im}\,\tau_p>\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$,
and pole weights $r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)$ as in Definition~\ref{def:proj_arc}. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geowhole}
L^*\big(\widehat P_{\tau_p}f,\,s;\,[\hat\gamma^S_f],[\hat\gamma^T_f]\big)
=\sum_{m=1}^{m_p}r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)\,\frac{(-2\pi i)^{m}}{(m-1)!}\,I^{\rm geo}_{m-1}(s,\tau_p).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By~\eqref{eq:polyproj_arc} the projection $\widehat P_{\tau_p}f$ is the finite combination
$\sum_{m}r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)\frac{(-2\pi i)^{m}}{(m-1)!}\lambda_{m-1,\tau_p}$ of the blocks of
Definition~\ref{def:geoIN}. Both segment integrals of Definition~\ref{def:Lint} are linear in the
integrand and the combination is finite, so $L^*$ distributes over it.
\end{proof}

What survives every such projection carries a $q$-series on the whole contour, and may be
integrated mode by mode.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:geotail}
Let $f\in F_k$, write $Rf:=(I-\widehat P^{\,\rm geo})f$, and let $c_{Rf}(n)$ be its Fourier
coefficients as in Definition~\ref{def:cRf_arc}. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geotail}
L^*\big(Rf,\,s;\,[\hat\gamma^S_f],[\hat\gamma^T_f]\big)
=c_{Rf}(0)\,G^{\rm geo}_{s,k}(0)+\sum_{n\ne0}c_{Rf}(n)\,G^{\rm geo}_{s,k}(n),
\end{equation}
the sum converging absolutely.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:projspace_arc} every pole of $Rf$ has height at most
$y_b<\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, so for any $\varepsilon>0$ with $y_b+\varepsilon<\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ one
has $|c_{Rf}(n)|\le e^{2\pi n(y_b+\varepsilon)}$ for all large $n$, while only finitely many
$n<0$ occur, $Rf$ being meromorphic at the cusp. On $\gamma^{\rm arc}$ one has
${\rm Im}\,\tau\ge\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, so those terms are bounded by
$e^{-2\pi n(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}-y_b-\varepsilon)}$ and $\sum_n c_{Rf}(n)q^{n}$ converges uniformly on
the contour. Both kernels are continuous, hence bounded, on that compact set, so each segment
integral may be taken term by term. The mode $q^{n}$ with $n\ne0$ contributes
$L^*(q^{n},s)=G^{\rm geo}_{s,k}(n)$, by the computation in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:geopolylog} read at general $n$, and $q^{0}=1$ contributes
$G^{\rm geo}_{s,k}(0)$ of~\eqref{eq:geoG0}.
\end{proof}

Before assembling, we record the functional equation. It holds for every $f\in F_k$, not only
for the weakly holomorphic ones, and for the same reason the arc was chosen: $\gamma^{\rm arc}$
is $S$-stable. The $T$-segment needs no property of the path at all.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:arcfunceq}
Let $k$ be even and $f\in F_k$. In the geodesic reference class,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arcfunceq}
L^*(f,s)=i^{k}\,L^*(f,k-s)
\end{equation}
for every $s\in\CC$, and the same holds for each segment separately.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the $S$-segment put $I(s):=\int_{\hat\gamma^S_f}f(\tau)\tau^{s-1}d\tau$ and substitute
$\tau\mapsto-1/\tau$. Since $f|_kS=f$ gives $f(-1/u)=u^{k}f(u)$, and $d\tau=du/u^{2}$,
$$I(s)=(-1)^{s-1}\int_{S\hat\gamma^S_f}f(u)\,u^{\,k-s-1}\,du .$$
By Lemma~\ref{lem:georetrace} the arc is carried by $S$ onto itself with reversed orientation,
so $S\hat\gamma^S_f=-\hat\gamma^S_f$ and $I(s)=-(-1)^{s-1}I(k-s)$. Multiplying by the prefactor
$e^{-\pi is/2}$ of Definition~\ref{def:Lint} and using $(-1)^{s-1}=e^{i\pi(s-1)}$, the phases
collect to $-e^{i\pi(k/2-1)}=e^{i\pi k/2}$, which is $i^{k}$.

For the $T$-segment no property of the path is used. Reading Definition~\ref{def:kernel} at
$k-s$ and factoring out $-e^{i\pi(k-s-1)}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ktilflip}
\tk(\tau,k-s)=-e^{i\pi(k-s-1)}\,\tk(\tau,s),
\end{equation}
the two Hurwitz terms exchanging places; the identification of the second with the first
requires $e^{-i\pi(k-s-1)}=e^{i\pi(s-1)}$, that is $e^{-i\pi(k-2)}=1$, which is where $k$ even
enters. Hence $L^*_T(f,k-s)=i^{k}L^*_T(f,s)$, i.e. $L^*_T(f,s)=i^{-k}L^*_T(f,s')$ at $s'=k-s$.
Finally $i^{k}=i^{-k}=(-1)^{k/2}$ for even $k$, so the two segments carry the same factor and
\eqref{eq:arcfunceq} follows for the sum.
\end{proof}

The two halves now assemble: every ingredient on the right is either a Laurent coefficient of $f$
at one of its own poles, a Fourier coefficient of what survives projection, or one of the two
explicit kernels $I^{\rm geo}_N$, $G^{\rm geo}_{s,k}$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:geomero}
Let $f\in F_k$ and let $\{\tau_p\}$ be its $T$-inequivalent poles with
${\rm Im}\,\tau_p\ge\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$, of orders $m_p$. Then the set $\{\tau_p\}$ is finite
and, in the geodesic reference class,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geomero}
L^*(f,s)=\sum_{p}\sum_{m=1}^{m_p}r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)\,\frac{(-2\pi i)^{m}}{(m-1)!}\,
I^{\rm geo}_{m-1}(s,\tau_p)
\;+\;c_{Rf}(0)\,G^{\rm geo}_{s,k}(0)+\sum_{n\ne0}c_{Rf}(n)\,G^{\rm geo}_{s,k}(n),
\end{equation}
with $r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)$ the pole weights of Definition~\ref{def:proj_arc}, $c_{Rf}(n)$ the
Fourier coefficients of Definition~\ref{def:cRf_arc}, and $I^{\rm geo}_N(s,z)$ given
by~\eqref{eq:geoIN} for ${\rm Im}\,z>\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ and by~\eqref{eq:geoedge} for
${\rm Im}\,z=\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$. Both sides are entire in $s$ away from the pole of
$G^{\rm geo}_{s,k}(0)$, the sum over $n$ converges absolutely, and both sides satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geomerofe}
L^*(f,s)=i^{k}L^*(f,k-s).
\end{equation}
If some $\tau_p$ lies on
$\gamma^{\rm arc}$ the identity holds on either indentation of
Proposition~\ref{prop:arcside}, the two sides changing together by~\eqref{eq:sidediff}; if some
$\tau_p$ is elliptic, \emph{both} sides are read at $W=0$ through
Definition~\ref{def:arczero}, each block $I^{\rm geo}_{m-1}(s,\tau_p)$ along with the
left-hand side. No pole configuration is excluded.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Finiteness: $f$ is meromorphic at the cusp, so it has no poles above some height, and its poles
are locally finite in the strip $|{\rm Re}\,\tau|\le\tfrac12$; the region cut out by
${\rm Im}\,\tau\ge\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ therefore contains finitely many of them modulo $T$.
Definition~\ref{def:geoproj} splits $f=\widehat P^{\,\rm geo}f+Rf$ with
$\widehat P^{\,\rm geo}=\sum_p\widehat P_{\tau_p}$ over exactly that finite set. Both segment
integrals of Definition~\ref{def:Lint} are linear in the integrand, and the splitting is a finite
sum, so $L^*$ distributes over it. Lemma~\ref{lem:geowhole} evaluates each
$L^*(\widehat P_{\tau_p}f,s)$ as the inner double sum, and Lemma~\ref{lem:geotail} evaluates
$L^*(Rf,s)$ as the remaining two terms, with the convergence asserted there. The description of
$I^{\rm geo}_N$ is Lemma~\ref{lem:geopolylog} off the threshold line and
Lemma~\ref{lem:geoedge} on it, the two agreeing where both apply. Equation
\eqref{eq:geomerofe} is Lemma~\ref{lem:arcfunceq}, which applies to $f$ itself and so to the
left-hand side; the right-hand side inherits it, being equal to the left.

For a pole on $\gamma^{\rm arc}$ the blocks are integrated on an indented contour, and the
$\varepsilon_z$ bookkeeping preceding Lemma~\ref{lem:geopolylog} records which indentation was
taken; changing it changes the left-hand side by~\eqref{eq:sidediff} and the corresponding block
by the same residue, by Proposition~\ref{prop:arcside}. For an elliptic pole no single indentation
exists, and Definition~\ref{def:arczero} supplies the value as the weighted average of two
admissible classes; \eqref{eq:geomero} holds in each of them, hence in the average, both sides
being affine in the winding of Definition~\ref{def:arcwind}.
\end{proof}

The formula is explicit in the sense that matters for computation: the pole weights are contour
integrals of $f$ about its own poles, the $c_{Rf}(n)$ are Fourier coefficients of an explicitly
constructed $T$-periodic function, and the two kernels are incomplete gammas and polylogarithms.
Nothing is left implicit except the arithmetic of $f$ itself.


Lemma~\ref{lem:geopolylog} assumes ${\rm Im}\,z>\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ strictly, so it says nothing on
the threshold line itself, where the chord meets the pole and neither the defining series of
$\Li_{-N}$ nor its inversion converges. That line is treated separately below, away from $\rho$
and at it.

\subsection{Relations to the L-functions of~\cite{1806}}\label{sec:arc1806}

We pause to connect the L-functions of this section to the definition in Ref.~\cite{1806}.

Compared term by term, the two look different: the $1/s$ residue of~\cite{1806} is proportional to
$c_f(0)$ alone, whereas ours receives a contribution from every simple pole above the contour. The
difference is one of reference class and not of object. Ref.~\cite{1806} anchors its contour
implicitly at $\tau_0\to i\infty$, which was legitimate in its setting because poles at the cusp
were excluded there, so that the limit is smooth; the present note anchors at $\tau_0=\rho+1$ on
the boundary of the fundamental domain, by Definition~\ref{def:georef}. Moving between the two
crosses pole orbits, and every discrepancy between the two definitions is the corresponding sum of
residues.

\begin{remark}\label{rem:arcresidue}
\emph{The $1/s$ residue is carried entirely by the $T$-segment. $L^*_S$ is a finite integral of
$f\,\tau^{s-1}$ and is entire in $s$, while $\tk$ has a simple pole at $s=0$ whose coefficient is
the integral of $f$ along that segment, the constant Fourier mode of $f$ in the strip containing
it. In the geodesic class that integral is $\Phi(f)$ of Definition~\ref{def:Phi}, so
$\mathop{\rm Res}_{s=0}L^*(f,s)=-\Phi(f)$, whereas Ref.~\cite{1806} anchors its contour at the cusp,
above every pole, and carries $-c_f(0)$. The two differ by the residues lying in the strip between
the contours, which is Lemma~\ref{lem:wall_arc}. The quantity that must be subtracted to reach $W$
is therefore the same object as the departure from the residue convention of~\cite{1806}, and not a
second normalisation imposed on top of it.}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:arcextremal}
\emph{The mechanism is already visible in~\eqref{eq:geoG0}, before any assembly. Its $S$-part
$e^{-\pi is/2}(\tau_0^{\,s}-\rho^{\,s})/s$ is finite at $s=0$, since $\tau_0^{\,s}-\rho^{\,s}$
vanishes like $s\log(\tau_0/\rho)$, while $-(\tau_0/i)^{s}/s$ carries the pole. The two classes sit
at opposite ends of one deformation: the contour of~\cite{1806} lies above every pole, making the
residue bookkeeping trivial and the pole-free region empty, while $\gamma^{\rm arc}$ lies below
every pole orbit not on it, making the pole-free region of codimension zero and the residue
bookkeeping maximal.}
\end{remark}

That deformation is the whole of the relation between the two definitions, and it needs no
matching of $q$-series coefficients.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:arc1806}
Let $f\in F_k$, let $\hat\gamma^{T\uparrow}$ be the $T$-segment deformed to pass above every pole
of $f$ with the $S$-segment left on $\gamma^{\rm arc}$, and write
$L^{*\uparrow}(f,s):=L^*\big(f,s;[\hat\gamma^S_f],[\hat\gamma^{T\uparrow}]\big)$. Then
$L^{*\uparrow}$ is the L-function of~\cite{1806}, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arc1806}
L^*(f,s)=L^{*\uparrow}(f,s)\;-\;2\pi i\,e^{-\pi is/2}
\sum_{\tau_p}X_T(\tau_p)\,r_T(f,\tau_p),
\end{equation}
the sum running over the pole orbits of $f$ lying between $\gamma^{\rm arc}$ and
$\hat\gamma^{T\uparrow}$, with $X_T$ their crossing numbers and $r_T$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:wall_arc}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Only the $T$-segment moves, so $X_S\equiv0$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:wall_arc} gives
\eqref{eq:arc1806} directly. For the identification, $L^*_S$ is entire in $s$, while the
$s=0$ pole of $\tk$ has coefficient $\int_{\hat\gamma^T}f\,d\tau$; on $\hat\gamma^{T\uparrow}$
that integral is the constant Fourier mode at the cusp, so
$\mathop{\rm Res}_{s=0}L^*=-c_f(0)$, which is the residue convention of~\cite{1806}, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:tau0indep} nothing further depends on where above the poles the segment is placed.
\end{proof}


One might then ask why the arc is used at all, since the raised class has the simpler residue
bookkeeping. The answer is that the period polynomial does not survive there. Raising
$\hat\gamma^T$ alone makes $\hat\gamma^S\ne\hat\gamma^T$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:arcon} then gives a
non-zero $(1+U+U^2)$ defect, proportional to the residues at the poles the two segments separate.
The arc is therefore not a convenient choice among equivalent ones: it is the class in which
$\hat r_f$ lies in $W$, and~\eqref{eq:arc1806} says exactly what is given up in exchange for the
simpler residue convention.


\subsection{Period polynomials in the geodesic class}\label{sec:arcperiod}


\begin{definition}\label{def:rfhat}
For $f\in F_k$, let $r_f$ be given by Definition~\ref{def:rf} with $L^*(f,s)$ evaluated in the
geodesic reference class of Definition~\ref{def:georef}, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rfhat}
\hat r_f\;:=\;r_f-\Phi(f)\,r_{E_k}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

$\SLZ$ is generated by $S$ and $T$ subject to $S^2=(ST)^3=-I$, so two closed paths control the
relations. Their degeneration on the arc is a statement about paths alone, independent of $f$; we
record it separately from its consequence, since the case of a pole \emph{on} the contour uses the
geometry but not the consequence.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:georetrace}
Let $\tau_0$ lie on $\gamma^{\rm arc}$ and let $\hat\gamma^S=\hat\gamma^T$. Then the closed path
traced by $S^2$ retraces itself, and if moreover $\tau_0=\rho+1$ the closed path traced by
$(ST)^3$ retraces itself. Both statements are independent of $f$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$S$ acts on the unit circle by $e^{i\theta}\mapsto e^{i(\pi-\theta)}$, preserving
$\tfrac{\pi}{3}\le\theta\le\tfrac{2\pi}{3}$, reversing orientation and fixing $i$; so
$S\hat\gamma^S$ is $\hat\gamma^S$ reversed and the $S^2$-path retraces, for any base point on the
arc. For $(ST)^3$, the fixed points of $U=TS$ are the roots of $\tau^2-\tau+1$, namely
$e^{\pm i\pi/3}$, so $U\tau_0=\tau_0$ exactly at $\tau_0=\rho+1$ and the $U$-path is closed. With
$\hat\gamma^S=\hat\gamma^T$ it is $S^{-1}\hat\gamma^T$ followed by $\hat\gamma^S$, the arc
traversed and then retraced; its $U$- and $U^2$-transports retrace $U\gamma^{\rm arc}$ and
$U^2\gamma^{\rm arc}$ through the same fixed point.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:geoloops}
In the geodesic reference class, $\hat C_{\pm I}=0$ for every $f\in F_k$ with no pole on
$\SLZ\!\cdot\!\gamma^{\rm arc}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:georetrace} both relator paths retrace, so each bounds nothing; the winding
number about every pole of $f$ vanishes and Cauchy's theorem gives $\hat C_{\pm I}=C_{\pm I}=0$.
\end{proof}

Definition~\ref{def:rfhat} covers those $f$ whose poles avoid the contour, and since
$\gamma^{\rm arc}$ is a curve that is the generic situation: the excluded locus is of codimension
one in $\HH$, so $\hat r_f$ is defined outright on a set of codimension zero. The remaining cases
are reached by continuity from it. Three cases exhaust the possibilities:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $f$ has no pole on $\SLZ\!\cdot\!\gamma^{\rm arc}$;
\item $f$ has a pole on $\gamma^{\rm arc}$, away from $i$ and $\rho$;
\item $f$ has a pole at $i$ or at $\rho$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:arcoff}
Let $f\in F_k$ have no pole on $\SLZ\!\cdot\!\gamma^{\rm arc}$. Then $\hat r_f\in W$, with no
condition on the location or the order of the poles of $f$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Throughout, $\hat C$ is the cocycle of Lemma~\ref{lem:cocycle}, so that $\hat C_S=r_f$ and
$\hat C_{\gamma\delta}=\hat C_\gamma|_\delta+\hat C_\delta$.

For the first relation, taking $\gamma=\delta=S$ gives
$\hat C_{S^2}=\hat C_S|_S+\hat C_S=r_f|_{(1+S)}$, and $S^2=-I$, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:geoloops} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arcoffS}
r_f\big|_{(1+S)}=\hat C_{-I}=0 .
\end{equation}

For the second, take $\gamma=T$, $\delta=S$, so that $U=TS$ and
$\hat C_U=\hat C_T|_S+\hat C_S$. Applying the cocycle relation twice more,
\begin{equation*}
\hat C_{U^3}=\hat C_U|_{U^2}+\hat C_{U^2}
=\hat C_U|_{U^2}+\hat C_U|_{U}+\hat C_U=\hat C_U\big|_{(1+U+U^2)} ,
\end{equation*}
and $U^3=-I$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:geoloops} makes the left side vanish. Substituting
$\hat C_U=\hat C_T|_S+\hat C_S$ and using $\hat C_S=r_f$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arcoffU}
0=\hat C_T\big|_{S(1+U+U^2)}+r_f\big|_{(1+U+U^2)},
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
r_f\big|_{(1+U+U^2)}=-\,\hat C_T\big|_{S(1+U+U^2)} .
\end{equation}
This is where the hypothesis $f\in S^!_k$ was used in Lemma~\ref{lem:rfW}, and it is the only
place: there $\hat C_T$ vanished, here it need not.

The $T$-cochain is controlled by $\Phi$ alone, whatever path represents the $T$-segment. We isolate
this, since it is used again below for segments that wind.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:CTPhi}
There is a constant $A_0$, depending on $k$ alone, such that for every $f\in F_k$ and every
admissible $\hat\gamma^T_f$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arcoffCT}
\hat C_T=-(2\pi i)^{n+1}A_0\int_{\hat\gamma^T_f}f(\tau)\,d\tau=-(2\pi i)^{n+1}A_0\,\Phi(f).
\end{equation}
In particular the right-hand side depends on the homotopy class of $\hat\gamma^T_f$ only through
$\Phi(f)$, and $\Phi$ is by Definition~\ref{def:Phi} the integral over that same path.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The computation of $\hat C_T$ in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:cocycle} does not use $c_f(0)=0$ until
its final step. It shows that $A(\tau):=\chi(\tau;X+Y,Y)-\chi(\tau-1;X,Y)$ is a $1$-periodic
polynomial, hence a constant $A_0$ depending on $k$ alone, and leaves $\hat C_T$ as the stated
multiple of $\int_{\hat\gamma^T_f}f\,d\tau$. No property of the path enters beyond its endpoints,
which are fixed by Definition~\ref{def:segments}.
\end{proof}

Since $A_0$ does not depend on $f$, neither does
$\mathcal D:=(2\pi i)^{n+1}A_0\big|_{S(1+U+U^2)}$, and \eqref{eq:arcoffU} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arcoffprop}
r_f\big|_{(1+U+U^2)}=\Phi(f)\,\mathcal{D}.
\end{equation}
The defect is therefore proportional to $\Phi(f)$, with a constant of proportionality common to
every $f\in F_k$ whose poles avoid $\SLZ\!\cdot\!\gamma^{\rm arc}$.

It remains to identify $\mathcal D$. Apply \eqref{eq:arcoffprop} to $f=E_k$, which is holomorphic
and so certainly admissible, and for which $\Phi(E_k)=1$: this gives
$\mathcal D=r_{E_k}|_{(1+U+U^2)}$. Hence
\begin{equation*}
\hat r_f\big|_{(1+U+U^2)}
=r_f\big|_{(1+U+U^2)}-\Phi(f)\,r_{E_k}\big|_{(1+U+U^2)}
=\Phi(f)\,\mathcal D-\Phi(f)\,\mathcal D=0 ,
\end{equation*}
while \eqref{eq:arcoffS} applied to both $f$ and $E_k$ gives
$\hat r_f|_{(1+S)}=0-\Phi(f)\cdot 0=0$. Both defining relations of $W$ hold. Nothing in the
argument constrains where the poles of $f$ lie, or how deep they are, beyond their absence
from $\SLZ\!\cdot\!\gamma^{\rm arc}$, which was used only through Lemma~\ref{lem:geoloops}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:arcon}
Let $f\in F_k$ have poles on $\gamma^{\rm arc}$, none at $i$ and none on $\SLZ\!\cdot\!\rho$, and
let $\hat\gamma^S$ be $S$-symmetric. Write $D_E:=r_{E_k}\big|_{(1+U+U^2)}$. Then
$\hat r_f\big|_{(1+S)}=0$ for every admissible $\hat\gamma^T$. If $\hat\gamma^T$ passes on the same
side of every pole as $\hat\gamma^S$ then $\hat r_f\big|_{(1+U+U^2)}=0$; otherwise, with
$p_1,\dots,p_m$ the poles at which the two segments part,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arcondefect}
\hat r_f\big|_{(1+U+U^2)}
=-(2\pi i)^{n+2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\nu_j\,a_{p_j}\,(X-p_jY)^{n}
\;-\;2\pi i\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{m}X_T(p_j)\,a_{p_j}\Big)D_E ,
\end{equation}
where $a_p=\Res_{\tau=p}f$ and $\nu_j\in\ZZ\setminus\{0\}$, and this is non-zero unless $D_E$ lies
in the span of $\{(X-p_jY)^{n}\}_{j\le m}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:cocycle}, $\hat C_S=r_f$ with $\hat C_S=C_S-(E|_S-E)$ and
$C_S=(2\pi i)^{n+1}\int_{\hat\gamma^S}\omega_f$; the $T$-segment enters $r_f$ only through the
coboundary $E-E|_S$. The cocycle relation gives $r_f|_{(1+S)}=\hat C_S|_{(1+S)}=\hat C_{S^2}$, and
as $n$ is even the coboundary drops, leaving the integral of $\omega_f$ over the closed path formed
by $\hat\gamma^S$ followed by its $S$-image. In polar coordinates $S$ acts by
$(r,\theta)\mapsto(1/r,\pi-\theta)$, so an $S$-symmetric $\hat\gamma^S$ is carried onto itself with
reversed orientation; that path retraces and the integral vanishes. No property of $\hat\gamma^T$ is
used, and the subtracted term contributes nothing since $r_{E_k}|_{(1+S)}=0$.

For the second relation, $\Phi$ is by construction the quantity $\hat C_T$ measures, so
Definition~\ref{def:rfhat} gives $\hat C_T=0$; then $\hat C_U=\hat C_T|_S+\hat C_S=\hat C_S$ and
$\hat r_f|_{(1+U+U^2)}=\hat C_{U^3}-\Phi(f)D_E$, with $\hat C_{U^3}=\hat C_{-I}$ the integral of
$\omega_f$ over the closed path traced by $(TS)^3$. As $U=TS$, that path is assembled from both
segments. If $\hat\gamma^S=\hat\gamma^T$ it retraces, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:geoloops}, and the two
terms cancel exactly as in Lemma~\ref{lem:arcoff}.

If the segments pass on opposite sides at $p_1,\dots,p_m$, the two paths bound regions containing
exactly those poles and no others, and the residue theorem gives the first term
of~\eqref{eq:arcondefect}; the second is the shift induced on $\Phi$, which is the $T$-segment
integral and therefore moves with $\hat\gamma^T$. For the last claim, the coefficient vector of
$(X-pY)^n$ is $\big(\binom{n}{l}(-p)^{l}\big)_{l}$, so the matrix of $\{(X-p_jY)^n\}_{j\le m}$ is
$\mathrm{diag}\binom{n}{l}$ times a Vandermonde in $(-p_1,\dots,-p_m)$, of rank $m$ whenever the
$p_j$ are distinct and $m\le n+1$. The residue terms therefore cannot cancel among themselves, and
\eqref{eq:arcondefect} can vanish only if $D_E$ lies in their span.
\end{proof}


The shifted contour realises one of the two sides without a second prescription being needed.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:arcdeform}
Let $f$ be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:arcon}. Then for every $\epsilon>0$ the pair
$(\hat\gamma^S_\epsilon,\hat\gamma^T_\epsilon)$ of Definition~\ref{def:arcshift} passes on the
same side of every pole of $f$ on $\gamma^{\rm arc}$, and $\hat r_f$ computed on it is
independent of $\epsilon$ and equals the value of Lemma~\ref{lem:arcon} on that pair.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The two segments differ only inside $|\tau-\rho|=O(\epsilon)$, where by hypothesis $f$ has no
pole, so they pass on the same side of each pole on the arc and Lemma~\ref{lem:arcon} applies with
$m=0$ in \eqref{eq:arcondefect}. Independence of $\epsilon$ is Lemma~\ref{lem:tau0indep}: varying
$\epsilon$ slides $\tau_0$ radially without crossing a pole, the poles on the arc lying at
$|\tau|=1$ and the sides taken by \eqref{eq:arcspiral} being the same for every $\epsilon>0$.
\end{proof}

The two relations are therefore sensitive to the two segments in quite different ways. The
$S$-relation constrains $\hat\gamma^S$ alone: $S$-symmetry makes the $S^2$-loop degenerate whatever
$\hat\gamma^T$ does. The $(1+U+U^2)$ relation mixes them, since $U=TS$, and vanishes only when the
two segments agree. Requiring $\hat\gamma^S=\hat\gamma^T$ in Definition~\ref{def:georef} is thus not
a stipulation of convenience: membership in $W$ selects it.

What it does not do is select a single class. The two conditions just isolated are preserved by a
whole family of deformations, and $\hat r_f$ is linear in the contour --- both $r_f$, through the
special values of Definition~\ref{def:rf}, and $\Phi$ are integrals over the segments --- so the
family may be combined.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:symspan}
Let $f\in F_k$ and let $\Gamma_1,\dots,\Gamma_N$ be reference pairs at $\tau_0=\rho+1$, each with
$S\hat\gamma^S_j=-\hat\gamma^S_j$ as a chain and $\hat\gamma^T_j=\hat\gamma^S_j$. Then
$\hat r_f(\Gamma_j)\in W$ for every $j$, and for any $w_1,\dots,w_N\in\CC$ with $\sum_jw_j=1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symspan}
\sum_{j}w_j\,\hat r_f(\Gamma_j)\ \in\ W .
\end{equation}
The values so obtained form a coset of ${\rm span}_\CC\{v_p\}\subseteq W$, where $v_p$ is the
shift produced by one unit of winding about a pole $p$ and, as the previous condition forces,
$-1$ unit about $Sp$, taken on both segments at once:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symshift}
v_p=\Big[(2\pi i)^{n+1}(-1)^{\ell}\tbinom{n}{\ell}\,2\pi i\,
\big(r_S+r_T\big)(f,p,\ell+1)\Big]_{\ell=0}^{n}
-\Big[\,p\mapsto Sp\,\Big]\;-\;2\pi i\,\big(a_p-a_{Sp}\big)\,r_{E_k},
\end{equation}
with $r_S,r_T$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:wall_arc}, evaluated at $s=\ell+1$, and $a_q=\Res_{\tau=q}f$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Each $\Gamma_j$ satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:georetrace}, so both relator paths
retrace and $\hat C_{\pm I}=0$; the argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:arcoff} then gives both defining
relations, whatever the winding data, so $\hat r_f(\Gamma_j)\in W$. For \eqref{eq:symspan}, the
segment integrals of Definition~\ref{def:Lint} are linear in the contour as a chain, hence so are
$L^*$, $r_f$ and $\Phi$, hence so is $\hat r_f=r_f-\Phi(f)r_{E_k}$; and $W$ is a linear subspace.
The shift \eqref{eq:symshift} is Lemma~\ref{lem:wall_arc} at $X_S(p)=X_T(p)=1$ and
$X_S(Sp)=X_T(Sp)=-1$, assembled through Definition~\ref{def:rf}, whose $i^{\ell+1}$ cancels the
prefactor $e^{-i\pi s/2}$ of Definition~\ref{def:Lint} at $s=\ell+1$, and corrected by the induced
change $\Delta\Phi=2\pi i\,(a_p-a_{Sp})$ in the subtraction. The two winding numbers are opposite
because $S\hat\gamma^S=-\hat\gamma^S$ is preserved only by windings that reverse under $S$; a
single unit about $p$ alone leaves the family.
\end{proof}

The generator $v_p$ deserves more than this incidental role, and Section~\ref{sec:residue_polys}
gives it one. It is $\Xi_{f;p}$ of Definition~\ref{def:respoly}, an integral of $f$ against a single
kernel over the closed contour $c_p-Sc_p$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:respolyW} proves
$\Xi_{f;p}\in W$ outright --- for a pole of any order, with no base point, no homotopy class and no
period polynomial entering. The antisymmetry demanded here is exactly what that proof consumes:
\eqref{eq:resSrel} is the relation $(1-S)(1+S)=1-S^2=0$, and \eqref{eq:ressym} records what the
symmetric choice costs instead.

Two consequences worth separating. First, $W$-membership is a property of the whole symmetric
family and not a selector within it: the residual freedom is exactly
$\dim{\rm span}_\CC\{v_p\}$, indexed by the $S$-orbits of poles the contour can wind around, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:arcside} is the case of a single such orbit. Second, \eqref{eq:symspan}
does not require the combination to be realised by any one contour, only that each constituent be.
This is what Definition~\ref{def:arczero} uses at an elliptic point, where $W=0$ is not attained by
a single path but is an affine combination of two that are.


\begin{lemma}\label{lem:arcwind}
Let $f\in F_k$ have a pole on $\SLZ\!\cdot\!\rho$ and let $W=W(f,\epsilon)$ be as in
Definition~\ref{def:arcwind}. Then $W\equiv-1\pmod3$; every such value is realised by some
connector; and there is a vector $c\in V_n$, independent of $W$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arcwind}
\hat r_f\big|_{(1+S)}=0,\qquad \hat r_f\big|_{(1+U+U^2)}=-\,c\,W .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\sigma:=\hat\gamma^S_\epsilon$ for the spiral, from $S\tau_0$ to $\tau_0$, and $\beta$ for
the connector of Definition~\ref{def:arcshift}, from $T^{-1}\tau_0$ to $S\tau_0$, so that
$\hat\gamma^T_\epsilon=\beta\cdot\sigma$. With $\gamma_g$ a path from $\tau_0$ to $g\tau_0$ and
the cocycle rule $\gamma_{gh}=\gamma_g\cdot(g\gamma_h)$, we have $\gamma_S=\bar\sigma$ and
$\gamma_T=T\hat\gamma^T_\epsilon=(T\beta)\,(T\sigma)$, whence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relatorcollapse}
\gamma_U=\gamma_T\cdot(T\gamma_S)=(T\beta)\,(T\sigma)\,(T\bar\sigma)\;\simeq\;T\beta ,
\end{equation}
the spiral occurring once forwards and once reversed. The cancellation is exact, not an estimate:
$\hat\gamma^S_\epsilon$ appears verbatim inside $\hat\gamma^T_\epsilon$, so
$(T\sigma)(T\bar\sigma)$ is a path followed by its own reverse and is null-homotopic rel
endpoints. Hence $\gamma_U$ is homotopic to the $T$-translated connector, which lies in the disc
$|\tau-(\rho+1)|=O(\epsilon)$.

Since $U$ fixes $\rho+1$, each $U^j\gamma_U$ lies in a disc of the same size about the same
point, so the relator path $\gamma_{U^3}=\gamma_U\cdot(U\gamma_U)\cdot(U^2\gamma_U)$ --- closed,
as $U^3=-I$ --- is supported in one $O(\epsilon)$ disc about $\rho+1$. This is the analogue for
$\epsilon\ne0$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:georetrace}, which is the case $\beta$ trivial.

In the coordinate $w$ of Definition~\ref{def:arcwind} the map $U$ fixes $w=0$ and is exactly the
rotation $w\mapsto\omega^{-1}w$, $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$, since $U'(\rho+1)=(\rho+1)^{-2}
=e^{-2\pi i/3}$; winding about $\rho+1$ is winding about $w=0$, and $U$ preserves increments of
$\arg w$ exactly. The three pieces are $U$-translates of one another, hence each changes
$\arg w$ by the same amount $\Delta$, and the endpoints
$\tau_0(\epsilon),U\tau_0(\epsilon),U^2\tau_0(\epsilon)$ sit at arguments
$\tfrac{\pi}{3},-\tfrac{\pi}{3},-\pi$, so $\Delta=-\tfrac{2\pi}{3}+2\pi k$ with $k\in\ZZ$ the
class of $\beta$. Then $2\pi W=3\Delta$ gives $W=-1+3k$, and every $k$ occurs.

For \eqref{eq:arcwind}, the $S$-relation holds because $\hat\gamma^S_\epsilon$ is $S$-symmetric,
so the $S^2$-path retraces by Lemma~\ref{lem:georetrace} and $\hat C_{S^2}=0$ whatever
$\hat\gamma^T_\epsilon$ does; as $r_{E_k}|_{(1+S)}=0$ the subtraction contributes nothing. For the
$U$-relation, \eqref{eq:arcoffU} gives
$r_f|_{(1+U+U^2)}=-\hat C_T|_{S(1+U+U^2)}-\hat C_{U^3}$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:CTPhi} holds at every
winding, so the first term is $\Phi(f)\mathcal D$ with $\mathcal D=D_E$ exactly as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:arcoff}; Definition~\ref{def:rfhat} cancels it for every $W$. There remains
$\hat C_{U^3}$, which by Cauchy's theorem is $2\pi i\sum_qW_q\Res_q\omega_f$ over the poles $q$ of
$f$. By \eqref{eq:relatorcollapse} the cycle lies in a single disc of radius $O(\epsilon)$ about
$\rho+1$. The poles of $f$ are discrete and $\SLZ$-stable, hence locally finite, so there is
$\epsilon_0>0$ below which that disc meets $f^{-1}(\infty)$ only at $\rho+1$ itself. Therefore
$W_q=0$ for every $q\ne\rho+1$, and $\hat C_{U^3}=2\pi i\,W\Res_{\rho+1}\omega_f$; setting
$c:=-2\pi i\Res_{\rho+1}\omega_f\big|_{(1+U+U^2)}$ gives \eqref{eq:arcwind}, and $c\ne0$ because
the defect is non-zero at $W=-1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:arcell}
For $f$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:arcwind}, $\hat r_f\in W$ in the sense of
Definition~\ref{def:arczero}, and that class is the unique one in which it does.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:arcwind} the defect vanishes exactly at $W=0$, and $c\ne0$ since the defect is
non-zero at $W=-1$; uniqueness follows because $W\mapsto-cW$ is injective. The same argument at
$i$, where $U$ is replaced by $S$ and the relator path is the $S^2$-path, gives $W$ odd and the
prescription the mean of the two indentations.
\end{proof}

Lemmas~\ref{lem:arcon} and~\ref{lem:arcwind} are complementary rather than overlapping: the first
excludes $i$ and $\SLZ\!\cdot\!\rho$ by hypothesis, the second treats only those points, where the
stabiliser forces $W$ into a class that misses $0$ and the L-integral must be read off the affine
family rather than from a single contour. Together with Lemma~\ref{lem:arcoff} they cover every
pole configuration.


\begin{theorem}\label{thm:arcperiod}
For $f\in F_k$, and $W\subset V_n$ from Definition~\ref{def:w}, the period polynomial
$\hat r_f(X,Y)\in W$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $E\subset f^{-1}(\infty)$ be the poles of $f$ lying on $\SLZ\!\cdot\!\rho$ or on
$\SLZ\!\cdot\!i$, and $N$ those lying on $\gamma^{\rm arc}$ but not at an elliptic point; the
remaining poles meet $\gamma^{\rm arc}$ nowhere. The three mechanisms are local to these three
families and may be applied together.

If $E$ and $N$ are both empty, Lemma~\ref{lem:arcoff} gives $\hat r_f\in W$ directly, with no
condition on where the other poles lie or how deep they are.

If $E$ is empty and $N$ is not, Definition~\ref{def:georef} takes $\hat\gamma^S_f=\hat\gamma^T_f$,
and both relations hold by Lemma~\ref{lem:arcon}; the shifted contour of
Definition~\ref{def:arcshift} gives the same value by Lemma~\ref{lem:arcdeform}, so the two
descriptions do not compete.

If $E$ is non-empty, read $L^*$ and $\hat r_f$ off the shifted class of
Definition~\ref{def:arcshift} at $W=0$, as in Definition~\ref{def:arczero}, at each elliptic
point of $E$ in turn. Lemma~\ref{lem:arcwind} shows the two defining relations vanish there, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:arcell} that $W=0$ is the only class in which they do. Poles of $f$ elsewhere
are untouched and fall under the two previous cases. Hence $\hat r_f\in W$.
\end{proof}

The construction uses only the $k-1$ special values $L^*(\,\cdot\,,\ell+1)$, $0\le\ell\le n$, of
the finite-contour $L$-function of Section~\ref{sec:arcL}, together with the single subtraction
$\Phi(f)\,r_{E_k}$. No reference family is chosen, and no projection onto $W$ is performed.

Two choices enter, and neither moves $\hat r_f$ out of $W$. In case~2 the side of the indentation
is free, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:arcon} the two differ within $W$. In case~3 nothing is free:
Corollary~\ref{cor:arcell} singles out $W=0$, and $\epsilon$ is inert by
Lemma~\ref{lem:tau0indep}. In Definition~\ref{def:rfhat} the reference
form $E_k$ may be replaced by any $g$ with $\Phi(g)=1$, and two such choices shift $\hat r_f$ by
$\Phi(f)\,r_{g-g'}$, where $g-g'$ runs over the forms annihilated by $\Phi$; how large that shift
space is depends on the class from which $g$ is drawn, and we do not settle it here.

A pole of prescribed order at a prescribed point is cut out by $J-J(z)$, and for that family
$\hat r_f$ is an explicit series in $J(z)^{-1}$ whose coefficients do not depend on $z$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:Jexp}
Let $h\in M_{k-2}$, let $z\in\HH$ with $|J(z)|>984$, and set $f_z:=h\,J'/(J-J(z))\in F_k$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Jexp}
\hat r_{f_z}\;=\;-\sum_{m\ge1}\hat r_{\,h\,J^{m-1}J'}\;J(z)^{-m}~,
\end{equation}
absolutely convergent in $V_n$, with $h\,J^{m-1}J'\in M^!_k$ for every $m$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On $\gamma^{\rm arc}$ we have $S\tau=-\bar\tau$, so $J(\tau)=J(S\tau)=\overline{J(\tau)}$ is real
there; it runs from $J(\rho)=-744$ to $J(i)=984$ and back, whence $|J|\le984<|J(z)|$ on the arc and
\begin{equation}
\frac{J'}{J-J(z)}\;=\;-\sum_{m\ge1}J^{m-1}J'\,J(z)^{-m}
\end{equation}
converges uniformly on it. By Definition~\ref{def:Phi} and Definition~\ref{def:rf}, $\Phi$ and each
$L^*(\,\cdot\,,\ell+1)$ are integrals over $\gamma^{\rm arc}$ against kernels continuous on that
compact set, so both are linear and continuous under uniform convergence there, and $\hat r$ is
assembled from them by \eqref{eq:rfhat}. Term by term, $J'=-2\pi i\,E_4^2E_6/\Delta$ is holomorphic
on $\HH$ because $\Delta$ does not vanish there, so $h\,J^{m-1}J'$ has no pole off the cusp.
\end{proof}

The $m=1$ term is where the choice left open in Definition~\ref{def:rfhat} becomes visible. Since
$J'=-2\pi i\,(q^{-1}+O(q))$ carries no constant term, $hJ'$ has constant term $-2\pi i\,c_h(1)$; so
when $c_h(1)\ne0$ the form $hJ'/(-2\pi i\,c_h(1))$ is itself admissible as the reference of
Definition~\ref{def:rfhat}, and with that choice $\hat r_{hJ'}=0$ and \eqref{eq:Jexp} begins at
$m=2$. Then $\hat r_{f_z}=O(J(z)^{-2})$, which is $O(e^{-4\pi\,{\rm Im}\,z})$ rather than
$O(e^{-2\pi\,{\rm Im}\,z})$. Any other admissible reference restores the $m=1$ term, by the shift
recorded above. The family thus selects a reference form from that shift space, whereas $f$ alone
does not.

\section{Residue polynomials}\label{sec:residue_polys}

As is profoundly clear in the sub-section~\ref{sec:arcperiod}, the route to assigning a period polynomial 
to a weight-$k$ $\SLZ$ meromorphic modular form $f \in F_k$, entirely via its underlying L-function, is fraught with 
ambiguities. To be explicit:
\begin{enumerate}
\item To define $L^*(f, s) := L^*(f, s; [\hat{\gamma}^T_{\tau_0}], [\hat{\gamma}^S_{\tau_0}])$ associated 
with a meromorphic $f \in F_k$, one must choose a reference homotopy class. There are countably infinite 
number of distinct choices, here.
\item For our preferred reference contour, $\gamma^{\rm arc}$, to define a period polynomial
$\hat r_f \in W \subset V_{k-2}$, one must subtract $\Phi(f)\,r_{E_k}$. That subtraction is
$c_f(0)\,r_{E_k}$ unless a pole lies between $\gamma^{\rm arc}$ and the horizontal line from
$\rho \to \rho+1$, in which case the residues in the intervening strip enter as well. But it is
well-defined only modulo $r(\ker\Phi)$.
\end{enumerate}
We were unable to naturally side-step either of these obstructions, either of which would render an attempt 
to make a canonical map between the space of meromorphic modular forms, $f \in F_k$, to the space of 
period polynomials $r_f \in W \subset V_{k-2}$, $f \in F_k \mapsto r_f \in W \subset V_{k-2}$. Indeed, both 
of these ambiguities are integrally tied to our definition of an L-function of a meromorphic modular form 
$f \in F_k$ via a contour integral and to our requirement that any candidate period polynomial of a modular 
form must lie in the privileged subspace $W \subset V_{k-2}$. This seems to imply some inherent difficulty 
in extending the notion of periods and quasi-periods of meromorphic modular forms, in the sense of this paper 
(i.e. $f \in F^* := \oplus_k F_k$) which is fundamentally tied to the nature of poles at finite locations in the 
fundamental domain.

Both obstructions, however, are statements about \emph{open} contours: the first is the choice of a
homotopy class joining $S\tau_0$ to $\tau_0$, and the second is the subtraction that repairs the
resulting $T$-defect. A \emph{closed} contour about a pole carries neither. What we show below is
that the winding of such a contour is by itself enough: it produces an element of $W$, directly,
with no base point, no homotopy class, and no period polynomial anywhere in the construction. This
opens the door to defining essentially complementary objects --- complementary in the sense that the
objects are trivial/fail to exist for the privileged subspace of weakly holomorphic forms,
$M_k^! \subset F_k$. To wit,

\begin{definition}\label{def:reskernel}
For $n=k-2$, with $\mathbf K_T$ as in Proposition~\ref{prop:periodint} and $r_{E_k}$ as in
Definition~\ref{def:rfhat}, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reskernel}
\mathcal K(\tau;X,Y):=(2\pi i)^{n+1}\Big[(X-\tau Y)^n+\mathbf K_T(\tau;X,Y)\Big]-r_{E_k}(X,Y).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The subtraction $r_{E_k}$ is constant in $\tau$, so it contributes $2\pi i\,r_{E_k}$ times the
enclosed residue of $f$ and no more; it is the counterterm in the single non-parabolic direction,
normalised by $\Phi(E_k)=1$. A loop about a pole of nonzero residue is a non-parabolic cycle, and
by the third display in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:rfW} it is parabolicity, $\hat C_T=0$, on which
the relation $w|_{(1+U+U^2)}=0$ rests.

\begin{definition}[Residue polynomial for $f \in F_k$ at $z$]\label{def:respoly}
Let $f \in F_k$, let $z \in \HH$ be a pole of $f$, let $c_z$ be a simple, positively oriented loop
enclosing $z$ and no other pole of $f$, and set $\gamma_\circ(z) := c_z - S c_z$. We define
\emph{the residue polynomial of $f$ at $z$}, $\Xi_{f;z}$ to be,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:respoly}
\Xi_{f;z} := \oint_{\gamma_\circ(z)} f(\tau)\,\mathcal K(\tau;X,Y)\,d\tau~.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The loop is $S$-\emph{anti}symmetric, $S\gamma_\circ=-\gamma_\circ$, and not merely a loop about
the pair $\{z,Sz\}$. This is forced: Lemma~\ref{lem:georetrace} makes the $S^2$- and $(ST)^3$-paths
retrace by $S\hat\gamma^S=-\hat\gamma^S$, so a winding added to both segments must reverse under
$S$. The symmetric alternative $c_z+Sc_z$ fails, and fails in the $S$-relation; see
\eqref{eq:ressym} below. Since $S$ is holomorphic, $Sc_z$ is a positively oriented loop about
$Sz$, so $\gamma_\circ$ encircles $z$ once positively and $Sz$ once negatively. At $z=i$ it
degenerates to the zero cycle, because $Si=i$; correspondingly $\Xi_{f;i}=0$. At
$z\in\SLZ\!\cdot\!\rho$ the two points are the arc endpoints $\rho$ and $S(\rho+1)=\rho$.

Write $P_\tau:=(X-\tau Y)^n$, and for a $1$-chain $c$ in $\HH$ avoiding the poles of $f$ set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resAB}
A(c):=\int_c f(\tau)\,P_\tau\,d\tau,\qquad B(c):=\int_c f(\tau)\,\mathbf K_T(\tau;X,Y)\,d\tau .
\end{equation}
Two facts about these, one for each kernel, give the result outright.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:resequiv}
For every $\gamma\in\SLZ$ and every such chain $c$, $A(\gamma c)=A(c)|_{\gamma^{-1}}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Substitute $\tau=\gamma w$ in $A(\gamma c)$. Then $d\tau=(cw+d)^{-2}dw$ and $f(\gamma w)=(cw+d)^kf(w)$,
while
\[
X-(\gamma w)Y=\frac{(cX-aY)w+(dX-bY)}{cw+d},
\]
so the integrand becomes $f(w)\big((cX-aY)w+(dX-bY)\big)^n\,dw$ because $k-n-2=0$. Since
$\gamma^{-1}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}d&-b\\-c&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, that bracket is
$P_w|_{\gamma^{-1}}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:resorbit}
$\mathbf K_T(\tau)-\mathbf K_T(\tau-1)=-P_\tau|_{(1-S)}$, and consequently, in the sense of Hurwitz
continuation, $\mathbf K_T(\tau)=\sum_{m\ge1}P_{\tau+m}\big|_{(1-S)}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At $s=\ell+1$ Definition~\ref{def:kernel} reads
$\tk(\tau,\ell+1)=\zeta(-\ell,\tau+1)-(-1)^\ell\zeta(\ell-n,\tau+1)$, and
$\zeta(w,x)-\zeta(w,x+1)=x^{-w}$ gives
$\tk(\tau,\ell+1)-\tk(\tau-1,\ell+1)=-\tau^\ell+(-1)^\ell\tau^{n-\ell}$. Assembling over $\ell$ with
the weights of Proposition~\ref{prop:periodint} yields $-P_\tau+(\tau X+Y)^n$, and
$P_\tau|_S=(\tau X+Y)^n$ for $n$ even. For the second form, $\zeta(-m,x)$ continues
$\sum_{j\ge0}(j+x)^m$, so $\zeta(-\ell,\tau+1)$ continues $\sum_{m\ge1}(\tau+m)^\ell$; assembling
gives $\sum_{m\ge1}\big(P_{\tau+m}-P_{\tau+m}|_S\big)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:respolyW}
$\Xi_{f;z} \in W$, for a pole of any order, and depends on neither $\tau_0$ nor the reference class
of Definition~\ref{def:class_arc}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $\mathfrak a:=A(c_z)$ and let $\mathcal T:=\sum_{m\ge1}T^{-m}$ act by
$A(T^mc_z)=\mathfrak a|_{T^{-m}}$, which is Lemma~\ref{lem:resequiv}. That lemma gives
$A(\gamma_\circ)=\mathfrak a|_{(1-S)}$, and, since $P_{\tau+m}=P_\tau|_{T^{-m}}$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:resorbit} gives $B(c_z)=\mathfrak a|_{\mathcal T(1-S)}$ and hence
$B(\gamma_\circ)=\mathfrak a|_{(1-S)\mathcal T(1-S)}$. So with $E:=1+\mathcal T(1-S)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resbracket}
A(\gamma_\circ)+B(\gamma_\circ)=\mathfrak a\big|_{(1-S)E}.
\end{equation}
Now $S^2=-I$ acts trivially on $V_n$ for $n$ even, so $(1-S)(1+S)=1-S^2=0$ and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resSrel}
(1-S)E(1+S)=(1-S)(1+S)+(1-S)\mathcal T(1-S^2)=0 ,
\end{equation}
every term of the sum carrying the factor $1-S^2$. With $r_{E_k}|_{(1+S)}=0$, which holds by
Lemma~\ref{lem:geoloops} and the second display in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:rfW}, this gives
$\Xi_{f;z}|_{(1+S)}=0$.

For the remaining relation, $\gamma_\circ$ changes $\Phi$ of Definition~\ref{def:Phi} by
$\oint_{\gamma_\circ}f\,d\tau$, so the bracket \eqref{eq:resbracket} alone is not parabolic; the
subtraction of $r_{E_k}$ in \eqref{eq:reskernel}, normalised by $\Phi(E_k)=1$, restores
$\hat C_T=0$, and the third display in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:rfW} then gives
$\Xi_{f;z}|_{(1+U+U^2)}=0$. Nothing in \eqref{eq:respoly} refers to $\tau_0$ or to a homotopy class,
and no step above used the order of the pole.
\end{proof}

The symmetric alternative fails at \eqref{eq:resSrel}, and fails there only: $E(1+S)=(1+S)$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ressym}
\Big[\oint_{c_z+Sc_z}f\,\mathcal K\,d\tau\Big]\Big|_{(1+S)}
=(2\pi i)^{n+1}\,\mathfrak a\big|_{(1+S)^2}=2\,(2\pi i)^{n+1}\,\mathfrak a\big|_{(1+S)},
\end{equation}
which vanishes only when $\mathfrak a|_{(1+S)}$ does. The same computation with $c_z$ alone gives
$\mathfrak a|_{(1+S)}$, half of \eqref{eq:ressym}. 

Further, we suspect that the related objects,
\begin{definition}[Full residue polynomial for $f \in F_k$]\label{def:respolyfull}
Let $f \in F_k$, let $Z$ denote a set of representatives, modulo $\SLZ$, for the pole locations of
$f$ in $\HH$, let $\Xi_{f;z}$ be as in Definition~\ref{def:respoly}, and let $n_z$ denote
the order of the stabiliser of $z$ in $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\ZZ)$, so that $n_z=2$ on $\SLZ\!\cdot\!i$,
$n_z=3$ on $\SLZ\!\cdot\!\rho$, and $n_z=1$ otherwise. We define \emph{the full residue polynomial
of $f$}, $\Xi_f$, to be,
\begin{align}
\Xi_f &:= \sum_{z \in Z} \frac{\Xi_{f;z}}{n_z}~,\label{eq:respolyfull}\\
R(f) &:= \sum_{z \in Z} \frac{1}{n_z}\Big(\Res_{\tau = z} f-\Res_{\tau = Sz} f\Big)~,\label{eq:Rf}\\
\Sigma_f &:= \Xi_f + 2\pi i\,R(f)\,r_{E_k}~.\label{eq:Sigmaf}
\end{align}
\end{definition}
to also be very interesting in their own right, perhaps even more interesting.

The weights $1/n_z$ are those of the valence formula~\cite{01-Apostol}, which counts orders at $i$
and at $\rho$ with $\tfrac12$ and $\tfrac13$; in that sense $\Xi_f$ is a divisor sum. Away from the
elliptic points $f|_kS=f$ gives $\Res_{\tau=Sz}f=z^{\,n}\Res_{\tau=z}f$ at a simple pole, so the
bracket in~\eqref{eq:Rf} is $(1-z^{\,n})\Res_{\tau=z}f$; at $z=i$ it vanishes, consistently with
$\Xi_{f;i}=0$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:resamb}
Let $f \in F_k$. Then
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\Xi_f$, $\Sigma_f$ and $R$ are linear in $f$, and vanish identically on $M^!_k$;
\item $\Sigma_f$ is independent of the reference form, while replacing $E_k$ in
Definition~\ref{def:rfhat} by any $g$ with $\Phi(g)=1$ sends
$\Xi_f \mapsto \Xi_f + 2\pi i\,R(f)\,r_{E_k-g}$;
\item $\Xi_f \in W$, whereas $\Sigma_f|_{(1+S)}=0$ and
$\Sigma_f|_{(1+U+U^2)} = 2\pi i\,R(f)\,r_{E_k}|_{(1+U+U^2)}$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular $\Xi_f=\Sigma_f$ is both free of the reference form and an element of $W$ precisely
when $R(f)=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Linearity is immediate from~\eqref{eq:respoly} and~\eqref{eq:respolyfull}, and $Z=\emptyset$ for
$f\in M^!_k$. For (2), $r_{E_k}$ enters~\eqref{eq:reskernel} additively and is constant in $\tau$,
so it contributes $-r_{E_k}\oint_{\gamma_\circ(z)}f\,d\tau
=-2\pi i\,r_{E_k}\big(\Res_{\tau=z}f-\Res_{\tau=Sz}f\big)$ to~\eqref{eq:respoly};
weighting by $1/n_z$ and summing over $Z$ gives the stated shift, which~\eqref{eq:Sigmaf} cancels.
For (3), $\Xi_f\in W$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:respolyW}. The second display in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:rfW} uses only $\hat C_{\pm I}=0$, which holds for $E_k$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:geoloops}, so $r_{E_k}|_{(1+S)}=0$; the third display uses $\hat C_T=0$, which fails
for $E_k$ since $\Phi(E_k)=1$. The two identities follow from~\eqref{eq:Sigmaf}.
\end{proof}

$R(f)$ is a canonical linear functional on $F_k$: no contour, base point or reference form enters
it. It is the whole of the obstruction, and it does not vanish in general --- for the family of
Proposition~\ref{prop:Jexp}, $Z=\{z\}$ and $\Res_{\tau=z}f_z=h(z)$, so $R(f_z)=h(z)\,(1-z^{\,n})$.

At a simple pole Definition~\ref{def:respoly} is wholly explicit. Writing
$\tk(w,\ell+1)=-B_{\ell+1}(w+1)/(\ell+1)+(-1)^\ell B_{n-\ell+1}(w+1)/(n-\ell+1)$ for the Bernoulli
polynomials of Definition~\ref{def:Wpm}, the coefficient of $X^{n-\ell}Y^\ell$ in $\Xi_{f;z}$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:respolyexp}
2\pi i\,a_z\Big\{(2\pi i)^{n+1}(-1)^\ell\tbinom{n}{\ell}
\Big[z^\ell-z^{n-\ell}+\tk(z,\ell+1)-z^{\,n}\,\tk(-1/z,\ell+1)\Big]
-(1-z^{\,n})\big(r_{E_k}\big)_\ell\Big\},
\end{equation}
$a_z=\Res_{\tau=z}f$. So $\Xi_{f;z}$ is $a_z(2\pi i)^{n+2}$ times a polynomial in $z$ with rational
coefficients, less $2\pi i\,a_z(1-z^n)\,r_{E_k}$: its only transcendental content beyond $a_z$ and
powers of $2\pi i$ is $r_{E_k}$, entering with the coefficient $(1-z^n)$. Write
$\Xi_{f;z}=a_z\,\Xi^\circ_k(z)$ for the resulting map $\Xi^\circ_k:\HH\to W$, which depends on $k$ alone.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:thetalaurent}
$\Xi^\circ_k(z)=\sum_{m=-1}^{n+1}c_m\,z^m$ with every $c_m\in W$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thetafe}
\Xi^\circ_k(-1/z)=-z^{-n}\,\Xi^\circ_k(z),\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad c_{n-m}=(-1)^{m+1}c_m .
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In \eqref{eq:respolyexp}, $\tk(w,\ell+1)$ is a polynomial in $w$ of degree
$\max(\ell+1,n-\ell+1)\le n+1$. Hence $\tk(z,\ell+1)$ contributes powers $z^0,\dots,z^{n+1}$, while
$z^n\tk(-1/z,\ell+1)$ contributes $z^{n-\max(\ell+1,n-\ell+1)},\dots,z^n$, the least exponent being
$z^{-1}$, attained at $\ell=0$ and $\ell=n$; the remaining terms lie in that range. So $\Xi^\circ_k$ is a
Laurent polynomial supported in $-1\le m\le n+1$. Since $\Xi^\circ_k(z)\in W$ for every $z$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:respolyW}, the monomials $z^m$ are linearly independent as functions of $z$,
and $W$ is a linear subspace, each $c_m$ lies in $W$.

For \eqref{eq:thetafe}, $S$ is holomorphic and $S^2=1$ in $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\ZZ)$, so
$\gamma_\circ(Sz)=c_{Sz}-Sc_{Sz}=c_{Sz}-c_z=-\gamma_\circ(z)$ and therefore
$\Xi_{f;Sz}=-\Xi_{f;z}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:resequiv} at $\gamma=S$ gives $a_{Sz}=z^{\,n}a_z$, so
$z^{\,n}a_z\Xi^\circ_k(-1/z)=-a_z\Xi^\circ_k(z)$. Comparing coefficients of $z^{m}$ on the two sides of
$\Xi^\circ_k(-1/z)=-z^{-n}\Xi^\circ_k(z)$ gives $(-1)^mc_m=-c_{n-m}$.
\end{proof}

So the dependence of $\Xi_{f;z}$ on the pole location is rational: $z\mapsto\Xi^\circ_k(z)$ is a curve in
$W$ of degree at most $n+2$, and \eqref{eq:thetafe} is an involution on it. Its coordinate functions
in any basis of $W$ are Laurent polynomials in $z$, so the curve is contained in no proper subspace
of $W$ precisely when the $c_m$ span; at $k=12$, where $\dim W=3$, they are computed to do so.

Two consequences for the ambiguity of Proposition~\ref{prop:resamb}. If $f$ has a single simple pole
orbit, then $R(f)=n_z^{-1}(1-z^{\,n})a_z$, which vanishes exactly when $z^{\,n}=1$, that is when the
pole lies on $|\tau|=1$ at an $n$-th root of unity; for such $f$ the residue polynomial is free of
the reference form. At $k=12$ the roots of unity meeting
$\tfrac{\pi}{3}\le\arg\tau\le\tfrac{2\pi}{3}$ are $e^{2\pi i/5}$ and $e^{3\pi i/5}$, a single
$S$-pair. And $\Xi_{f;z}$ determines $a_z$ whenever $\Xi^\circ_k(z)\neq0$, so within one orbit the whole
residue data is recovered, by Lemma~\ref{lem:resequiv}; across distinct orbits it is not, since
$\Xi_f$ records only the weighted sum.

A semi-structured list of reasons why, below:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Xi_f$ and $\Xi_{f;z}$ are inherently independent of $\tau_0$, and of the reference homotopy
class. They just depend on the contour which encircles their residue, just as period polynomials
are for the standard case $f \in M_k^!$.
\item $\Xi_f$ and $\Xi_{f;z}$ are naturally in $W \subset V_{k-2}$ for every $f \in F_k$ and every
pole $z$, elliptic or not
\item $\Xi_f$ carries global information about the divisor structure of the modular form: it is a
divisor sum, weighted at $i$ and at $\rho$ exactly as the valence formula weights them, and it
records the whole polar part of $f$ at each pole and not merely the residue
\item $\Xi_f$ denotes a unique polynomial in $W \subset V_{k-2}$ exactly when $R(f)=0$; otherwise
the reference form of Definition~\ref{def:rfhat} survives in it, and $R(f)$ measures how much
\end{itemize}
Poles at the cusp do not enter. The kernel $\mathcal K$ is integrated against $f$ on loops in $\HH$,
so only the polar parts of $f$ at finite locations contribute, and no $c_f(0)$ appears anywhere.
This is why $\Xi_f$ vanishes on $M^!_k$, and it means that admitting or excluding poles at the cusp
changes nothing.

\subsection{An example localised in $W$ at weight $k=18$}\label{sec:resex}

Take $g=\Delta E_4\in M_{16}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resex}
f_z:=g\,\frac{J'}{J-J(z)}\in F_{18},\qquad J'=-2\pi i\,(J+744)\,\frac{E_6}{E_4},
\end{equation}
for $z$ in the interior of the fundamental domain. Three things make this the tractable case.
$J'/(J-J(z))$ has weight $2$, so $f_z$ has weight $k=18$ and $n=16$; $f_z$ has a single pole orbit,
so $\Xi_{f_z}=\Xi_{f_z;z}$ and the local and global residue polynomials coincide; and
$\dim S_{18}=1$, so $\dim W=3$ and $W$ has a cuspidal part rather than reducing to the Eisenstein
line. By Lemma~\ref{lem:resequiv} the residue is $\Res_{\tau=z}f_z=g(z)$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resexfac}
\Xi_{f_z}\;=\;\Delta(z)E_4(z)\;\Xi^\circ_{18}(z),
\end{equation}
a weight-$16$ cusp form evaluated at $z$ times the universal map of
Proposition~\ref{prop:thetalaurent}, which depends on $k$ alone. In particular
$|\Xi_{f_z}|\asymp e^{-4\pi\,{\rm Im}\,z}$.

The parity involution $\varepsilon:P(X,Y)\mapsto P(-X,Y)$ preserves $W$: it acts as $(-1)^\ell$ on
the coefficient of $X^{n-\ell}Y^\ell$, and $\varepsilon S\varepsilon^{-1}=-S$ while
$\varepsilon U\varepsilon^{-1}=SU^{-1}S^{-1}$, so on $\ker(1+S)$ the two $U$-relations agree. Since
$p_0=X^n-Y^n$ is $\varepsilon$-even,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parity}
\dim W^{\rm even}=\dim S_k+1,\qquad \dim W^{\rm odd}=\dim S_k .
\end{equation}
At $k=18$ the odd part is therefore a line. Since $\dim S_{18}=1$, Definition~\ref{def:Wpm}
applies, and the three polynomials $W^{(18)}_-$, $W^{(18)}_+$, $p_0$ have \emph{disjoint} supports
in $\ell$ --- odd $\ell$; $\ell\in\{2,4,6,10,12,14\}$; and $\ell\in\{0,16\}$ --- so coordinates in
that basis are read off rather than solved for. Note $\ell=n/2$ is absent from $W^{(18)}_+$, its
defining bracket having $a_i=-a_{n-i}$ and hence $a_{n/2}=0$; the same midpoint forces
$c_{n/2}=0$ in \eqref{eq:thetafe} whenever $k\equiv2\pmod4$.

Writing $\Xi^\circ_k(z)=\sum_m c_m z^m$ and taking the reference form to be $E_k$ itself, the
coordinates of $c_m$ in this basis obey a selection rule: the $W_-$ coordinate is supported on
even $m$, and the $W_+$ and $p_0$ coordinates on odd $m$. All three are rational multiples of
$(2\pi i)^{n+2}$ with one exception. The exception is $p_0$ at $m\in\{0,n\}$, where the rational
part vanishes and the coordinate is instead the single constant by which $r_{E_k}$ fails to be
rational --- for $r_{E_{18}}$ the $W_+$ coordinate vanishes identically and the $W_-$ coordinate is
rational, so $r_{E_{18}}$ is rational along $W_-$ and irrational only along $p_0$. Consequently the
rational and the transcendental parts of $\Xi^\circ_k$ occupy disjoint powers of $z$.

Explicitly, writing
$\Xi_{f_z}=\Delta(z)E_4(z)\big[\mu_-(z)W^{(18)}_-+\mu_+(z)W^{(18)}_++\mu_0(z)p_0\big]$ and
dividing out $(2\pi i)^{18}$,
$\mu_-=\tfrac{2}{131601}P_-$ and $\mu_+=\tfrac{2}{3}P_+$, where
\begin{align}
P_-(z)&={-}18000+350936\,z^2-1096675\,z^4+1140542\,z^6\notag\\
&\qquad-1140542\,z^{10}+1096675\,z^{12}-350936\,z^{14}+18000\,z^{16},\label{eq:mum}\\
P_+(z)&={-}24\,z+154\,z^3-273\,z^5+143\,z^7+143\,z^9-273\,z^{11}
+154\,z^{13}-24\,z^{15}\label{eq:mup}
\end{align}
have coprime integer coefficients, and $131601=3\cdot43867$ carries the numerator of $B_{18}$. The
scalar
$\Delta(z)E_4(z)$ and the normalisations of $W^{(18)}_\pm$ both cancel in the ratio, so the position
of $\Xi_{f_z}$ inside the plane spanned by $W^{(18)}_-$ and $W^{(18)}_+$ is the single rational
function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Wpmloc}
R(z):=\frac{\mu_+(z)}{\mu_-(z)}=43867\,\frac{P_+(z)}{P_-(z)}\ \in\ \mathbb P^1 .
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:Wpmloc}
$R(-1/z)=R(z)$ and $R(-z)=-R(z)$, and $R(z)\in i\RR\cup\{\infty\}$ for $z$ on the imaginary axis
and for $|z|=1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:thetafe} each coordinate of $\Xi^\circ_k$ satisfies $\mu(-1/z)=-z^{-n}\mu(z)$, so the
common factor cancels in \eqref{eq:Wpmloc}; and $\mu_-$ is even in $z$ while $\mu_+$ is odd, which
gives the second identity. For the third, $\mu_\pm$ have real coefficients, so
$R(\bar z)=\overline{R(z)}$. On the imaginary axis $\mu_-(iy)$ is real and $\mu_+(iy)$ is $i$ times
a real number. For $|z|=1$ one has $\bar z=1/z$, hence
$\overline{R(z)}=R(1/z)=-R(-1/z)=-R(z)$.
\end{proof}

So the $W^\pm$-localisation depends only on the $\SLZ$-orbit of the pole through its $S$-class, and
is purely imaginary exactly on the two reflection loci. Since $R$ has rational coefficients,
$R(z)\in\QQ(z)$; at a point of class number one this is an explicit element of an imaginary
quadratic field. The two lying on the reflection loci of Lemma~\ref{lem:Wpmloc} are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rheeg}
R\!\left(\tfrac{1+\sqrt{-3}}{2}\right)=\frac{43867}{8129}\sqrt{-3},
\qquad
R\big(\sqrt{-2}\big)=\frac{43867\cdot18}{119513}\sqrt{-2},
\end{equation}
purely imaginary as they must be, while off those loci both coordinates appear, as in
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rheeg7}
R\!\left(\tfrac{1+\sqrt{-7}}{2}\right)
=\frac{43867\,\big({-}107+316769\sqrt{-7}\big)}{3931333248},
\qquad
R\!\left(\tfrac{1+\sqrt{-11}}{2}\right)
=\frac{43867\,\big({-}30607+4873558\sqrt{-11}\big)}{75795816699}.
\end{equation}
The remaining class-number-one points give elements of $\QQ(\sqrt{-d})$ of the same shape and
larger height. At $z=i$ both $\mu_\pm$ vanish and $R$ is undefined, consistently with
$\Xi_{f;i}=0$.

These values are algebraic because $R$ is a rational function with rational coefficients, and not
because the pole is a CM point. The arithmetic of $\Xi_{f_z}$ sits entirely in the scalar
$\Delta(z)E_4(z)$, a CM value of a weight-$16$ cusp form, which \eqref{eq:Wpmloc} divides out.

Both $\mu_\pm$ vanish at $z=\pm i$: in the variable $w=z^2$, $\mu_+/z$ is palindromic of odd degree
and $\mu_-$ anti-palindromic of degree $8$, so $w=-1$ is a root of each. This is the algebraic
shadow of $\Xi_{f;i}=0$. The anti-palindromy gives $\mu_-$ the further roots $z=\pm1$.

The reference form matters here and not only in Proposition~\ref{prop:resamb}. Replacing $E_k$ by
another $g$ with $\Phi(g)=1$ changes $r_{E_k}$ by the period polynomial of a cusp form, which has
nonzero $W_-$ and $W_+$ coordinates; the rationality above is a statement about the Eisenstein
series and is destroyed by such a change.
