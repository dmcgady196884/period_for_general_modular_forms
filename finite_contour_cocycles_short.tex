\title{$L$-functions for all meromorphic $\SLZ$ modular forms,\\
via manifestly finite contours}
\author{David A.\ McGady}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We give a single manifestly finite, regulator-free functional realizing in one object $L$-functions
quasi-periods and period polynomials of meromorphic modular forms on $\SLZ$, with generic 
poles throughout the upper half-plane $\HH$ and at the cusp of $\SLZ$. Additionally, our construction 
is preserves holomorphicity. It is a two-segment contour integral, 
$L^*(f,s) = L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) + L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0})$, where $L^*_T$ and $L^*_S$ 
have distinct integration kernels, and 
$\gamma^{S}_{\tau_0}$ is a path in $\HH$ from $S(\tau_0)$ to $\tau_0$, and the same for $\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$. 
For weakly holomorphic forms our L-function is independent of $\tau_0$ and of the path, and reproduces 
classical L-functions for holomorphic modular forms, reproduces the L-functions of weak modular forms 
in~\cite{BFK2011}, which previously needed non-holomorphic regularization to be defined, and reproduces 
Brown's quasi-periods~\cite{Brown2017}. For properly meromorphic modular forms with poles inside the 
interior of $\HH$, our L-function depends on the homotopy classes, defined in terms of pole-crossings. 
There is a natural choice of trivial homotopy class, which renders our L-functions canonical when the 
modular form has a pole in the interior of the fundamental domain, generalizing~\cite{1806}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and preliminary concepts}

Our primary goal in this note is to put forth a unifying generalization of the L-function 
associated to a given modular form. Our L-function naturally encompasses the classical 
case of L-functions associated to holomorphic modular forms~\cite{01-Apostol}, and 
seamlessly unifies Ref.~\cite{BFK2011}'s extension to L-functions of modular forms with 
poles at the cusp with Ref.~\cite{1806}'s extension to L-functions of modular forms with 
poles at finite locations, exclusively away from cusps. Secondarily, we show that our 
L-functions reproduce the numerical results for quasi-periods associated to weak cusp 
forms~\cite{Brown2017}. 

{\em Note:} Throughout this document, we refer to L-integrals and L-functions. The classical 
L-function-as-Dirichlet-series which has been suitably extended to the complex $s$ plane is 
simply not defined when the L-function in question has poles either at the cusp or at finite 
locations within the upper half-plane. So, if there is ever a doubt, we state clearly: 
{\em any L-function in this note is defined by an L-integral}. The focus of this note is to define 
L-integrals for any integer weight $\SLZ$ modular form which may have poles at cusps or away 
from cusps.

\subsection{Modular forms}
Write ${\rm Re}(z)$ and ${\rm Im}(z)$ for the real and imaginary parts of a complex number 
$z\in \CC$. Let $\HH:=\{\tau\in\CC\mid {\rm Im}(\tau)>0\}$ denote the upper half-plane. $\SLZ$ 
is the group generated by $T := \left( \begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix} \right)$ 
and $S := \left( \begin{smallmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{smallmatrix} \right)$. 

A {\em holomorphic modular form} of weight $k$ for $\SLZ$ is a 
holomorphic function $f:\HH\to \CC$ such that $f(\tau+1)=f(-\frac1\tau)\tau^{-k}=f(\tau)$ for 
all $\tau\in \HH$, and $f(\tau)={\cal~O}(1)$ as ${\rm Im}(\tau)\to \infty$, and which has no 
poles at finite locations within $\HH$. By virtue of the periodicity $f(\tau) = f(\tau+1)$ and 
holomorphicity, each holomorphic modular form has a q-series decomposition,
\begin{equation}
f(\tau) = \sum_n c_f(n) e^{2 \pi i n \tau} = \sum_n c_f(n) q^n~~,~~q:= e^{2 \pi i \tau}~~.
\end{equation}
In order for $f$ to be bounded as $\tau \to i \infty$, we conclude that $c_f(n) = 0$ 
for all $n < 0$, i.e. that $f = \sum_{n \geq 0} c_f(n) q^n$; for such modular forms $f$ 
we write $f \in M_k$. If a modular form $f$ additionally has a vanishing constant term, 
i.e. if $c_f(0) = 0$, then $f$ is a {\em cusp form}, 
and we write $f \in S_k$. Clearly, $S_k \subset M_k$. Further, $M_k$ is a  
finite-dimensional vector-space, as is $S_k$. Holomorphic $\SLZ$ modular forms must 
have even integer weights, and $k \geq 4$~\cite{01-Apostol}. 

A product of modular forms of weights $k_1$ and $k_2$ is a modular form of weight 
$k_1+k_2$, so $M_*:=\bigoplus_k  M_k$ is naturally a (graded) ring. This graded ring is 
freely generated by the two modular forms $E_4 \in M_4$ and $E_6 \in M_6$, the $k = 4, 6$
specializations of the {\em weight $k$ Eisenstein series} $E_k$, which may be defined as follows:
\begin{align}
E_k := \frac{1}{2}\sum_{(m,n)=1} \frac{1}{(m+n\tau)^k} = 1 - \frac{2k}{B_k}\sum_{n = 1}^{\infty} \sigma_{k-1}(n) q^n~,
\end{align}
where $\sum_{(m,n) = 1}$ denotes summing over all relatively prime $(m,n) \in \ZZ^2$, the Bernoulli 
number $B_n$ is the coefficient of $t^n/n!$ of the Laurent expansion of $t/(e^{t}-1)$ about $t = 0$, 
and $\sigma_m(n) := \sum_{d|n} d^m$ is the Divisor function where $d|n$ means $n/d \in \NN$. 
In short, $M_* = \CC[E_4, E_6]$. We now define the {\em modular discriminant}:
\begin{equation}
\Delta := \frac{E_4^3-E_6^2}{1728} = q + O(q^2) \in S_{12}~.
\end{equation}

Holomorphicity is most canonically relaxed via allowing a modular form to have a pole at the 
cusp of $\SLZ$, i.e. for a weight $k$ modular form for $\SLZ$ to have negative powers of $q$ 
in its q-series expansion. In this case, $f$ diverges as $\tau \to i \infty$ as $f \sim e^{-2 \pi N \tau}$, 
where $-N$ is the largest negative number such that $c_f(n)$ is non-zero. Such a modular form 
$f$ is called a {\em weak modular form}, and we write $f \in M_k^!$. If a weak modular form $f$ 
has a vanishing coefficient of $q^0$, i.e. if $c_f(0) = 0$, it is called a {\em weak cusp form} and 
we write $f \in S_k^!$. Clearly, $S_k^! \subset M_k^!$, $S_k \subset S_k^!$ and $M_k \subset M_k^!$. 
Two important weak cusp forms in our paper are as follows: 
\begin{align}
J :=&~ \frac{E_4^3}{\Delta} - 744 = \frac{1}{q} + O(q) \in S_0^!~,~\\
\Dhat =&~ (J^2 + a_1 J + a_0) \Delta := \frac{1}{q} + O(q^2) \in S_{12}^!~,~
\end{align}
where $a_0, a_1$ are integers which exist to ensure the q-series gap that defines 
$\Dhat$~\cite{DukeJenkins}.

In this work, we further relax from even weakly holomorphic modular forms, and 
consider {\em meromorphic modular forms} of weight $k$ which are quotients: $f=\frac{f_1}{f_2}$ 
where $f_i\in M_{k_i}$, the denominator $f_2$ is not identically zero, and $k=k_1-k_2$. We 
write $F_k$ for the vector space of meromorphic modular forms of weight $k$, and set 
$F_*:=\bigoplus_k F_k$. Then $F_k$ vanishes unless $k$ is an even integer, and 
$F_*$ may be regarded as a ``graded ring of fractions'' of $M_*$. 

\subsection{L-functions for holomorphic modular forms and periods for cusp forms}

For any $f \in S_k$, one may define the associated L-function:
\begin{align}
L(f,s) := \frac{(2 \pi)^s}{\Gamma(s)} L^*(f,s) \underset{f \in S_k}{:=} 
\frac{(2 \pi)^s}{\Gamma(s)} \int_0^{\infty} f(it) t^{s-1} dt = \sum_{n \geq 1} \frac{c_f(n)}{n^s}~.
\end{align}
A similar definition applies for $f \in M_k$. (The classical definition of $L^*(f,s)$ for $f \in M_k$ 
is the focus of Remark~\ref{natural-beauty-via-finiteness}, below.) Similarly, one may define a period 
polynomial for $f \in S_k$ in terms of this L-function,
\begin{align}
r_f(X, Y) := (2 \pi i)^{k-1} \sum_{\ell = 0}^{k-2} (-1)^\ell \binom{k-2}{\ell}\, i^{\ell+1}\, L^*(f,\ell+1)\,X^{k-2-\ell}Y^\ell~.
\end{align}
This polynomial, $r_f(X, Y) \in V_{k-2} := \CC[X, Y]_{k-2}$, has a natural action under $\SLZ$, 
$(P|_{\gamma})(X, Y) = P(a X + b Y, c X + d Y)$ for $\gamma = 
\left( \begin{smallmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{smallmatrix} \right) \in \SLZ$. This period polynomial 
can be decomposed into a basis of highly constrained polynomials, which are annihilated by $|(1+T)$ 
and $|(1 + U + U^2)$\cite{KZ1984}, where $U := TS$ generates an order-3 cyclic subgroup of $\SLZ$. 
We define the \emph{periods} of a cusp form to be the coefficients that localize $r_f$ within this highly 
constrained sub-space of $V_{k-2}$.

{\bf Why this paper?} As classically computed, these L-functions and associated period polynomials 
(when defined in terms of the special values of the associated L-function) are not defined when $f$ 
has poles either at the cusp of $\SLZ$ or within the interior of $\HH$. As stated above, there 
are extant, but partial and non-overlapping, extensions of the definition of $L(f,s)$ to $f \in M_k^!$~\cite{BFK2011} or 
$f \in F_k$ when $f$ is regular at the cusp of $\SLZ$~\cite{1806}. In this note, we provide a construction that unifies these disparate extensions.
We start with generic L-integrals in section~\ref{sec:Lint}, then specialize to $f \in M_k^!$ in section~\ref{sec:Lfun}, and finally explore the more general context of $f \in F_k$ in section~\ref{sec:mero}.

\section{A finite L-integral}\label{sec:Lint}


\begin{definition}\label{def:segments}
Let $\tau_0\in\HH$ and $f\in F_k$, let
$S=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
$T=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ denote 
the generators of $\Gamma=\SLZ$, and let $f^{-1}(\infty)$ be the locus 
of all poles of $f \in \HH$ (inclusive of the cusp of $\SLZ$).  
The \emph{$S$- and $T$-segments at $\tau_0$} are smooth paths in
$\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$ from $\sigma^{-1}\tau_0$ to $\tau_0$,
\begin{equation}
\gamma^S_{\tau_0}:\ -1/\tau_0\ \longrightarrow\ \tau_0,\qquad
\gamma^T_{\tau_0}:\ \tau_0-1\ \longrightarrow\ \tau_0.%,
\end{equation}
%running from $S^{-1}\tau_0=-1/\tau_0$ and $T^{-1}\tau_0=\tau_0-1$ to $\tau_0$.
\end{definition}

Avoiding poles is automatic for $f \in M_k^!$, as long as the base-point $\tau_0$, 
is not at a cusp of $\SLZ$, i.e. there is only the trivial homotopy class. 
When $f \in F_k$, and $f$ has poles at finite and non-zero imaginary heights, 
integration contours fall into nontrivial homotopy classes.

\begin{definition}\label{def:kernel}
Let $\zeta(s,x)=\sum_{m\ge0}(m+x)^{-s}$ as a function 
$\zeta: \CC_s \times (\CC\setminus\NN)_x \to \CC$ denote the 
Hurwitz zeta function (meromorphic in $s$, holomorphic in $x\in\HH$). 
We define \emph{Hurwitz $T$-kernel} as
\begin{equation}
\tk(\tau,s):=\zeta(1-s,\tau+1)-e^{i\pi(s-1)}\,\zeta\big(1-(k-s),\tau+1\big).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Generic L-integral]
\label{def:Lint}
Let $f\in F_k$, $\tau_0\in\HH$, and $s\in\CC$. Further, let $\gamma^S_{\tau_0}$,
$\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$ be the $S$- and $T$-segments at $\tau_0$, as in
Definition~\ref{def:segments}, and let $\tk$ be as defined in 
Definition~\ref{def:kernel}. The \emph{$L$-integral} of $f$ is
\begin{equation}
L^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0})
:= L^*_S(f,s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}) + L^*_T(f,s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})
:=e^{-\pi i s/2}\left(\int_{\gamma^S_{\tau_0}}\!\!\!\! f(\tau)\tau^{s-1}\,d\tau
+\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}}\!\!\!\! f(\tau)\tk(\tau,s)d\tau\right)
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
We use this \emph{L-integral} to define a generalized 
\emph{L-function}:

\begin{definition}[Generic L-function]\label{def:Lfun}
Let $L^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ be as defined in Definition~\ref{def:Lint}. 
We define the generalized \emph{$L$-function} of $f\in F_k$ to be,
\begin{equation}
L(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0})
:=\frac{(2\pi)^s}{\Gamma(s)}\,L^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0}).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Having defined the L-integral and L-function, we prove they are manifestly finite for $f \in F_k$: 

\begin{lemma}[Finiteness of L-integrals]\label{lem:finite}
For $f\in F_k$, both integrals in Definition~\ref{def:Lint} are finite for every
$s\in\CC$, every $\tau_0\in\HH$, and every choice of paths
$\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Both segments are compact and lie in $\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, so $f$ is
continuous on them; $\tau^{s-1}$ is continuous on $\HH$, and $\tk(\tau,s)$ is continuous there
since $\zeta(\nu,\tau+1)$ is holomorphic in $\tau\in\HH$ (Definition~\ref{def:kernel}). A
continuous integrand on a compact path is integrable, for every $s\in\CC$.
\end{proof}

We now turn to path-dependence and $\tau_0$-independence of 
the L-integral and the L-function of $f \in F_k$. 
%For $f \in F_k$ such that $L^*(f,s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ 
%is \emph{both} $\tau_0$-independant and path-independent, and we simply 
%write $L^*(f, s: \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) = L^*(f,s)$; similarly for $L(f,s)$. 
This holds for all $f \in M_k^!$:

\begin{proposition}[Basepoint \& path independence]\label{prop:indep}
For $f\in M^!_k$ the value $L^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ is independent
of $\tau_0\in\HH$ and of the paths: with no interior poles, $\HH$ is simply connected,
so all admissible contours are homotopic. We write $L^*(f,s)$ for the common value and
$L(f,s):=\frac{(2\pi)^s}{\Gamma(s)}L^*(f,s)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With no interior poles the integrands $f(\tau)\tau^{s-1}$ and $f(\tau)\tk(\tau,s)$ are
holomorphic on $\HH$, which is simply connected; by Cauchy's theorem each integral is unchanged
under any deformation fixing its endpoints, hence depends on those endpoints alone. This is
path-independence.

For independence of $\tau_0$ show that variations in $\tau_0$ for the S-term and T-term in $L^*(f,s)$ 
cancel, i.e. that $\partial_{\tau_0} L^*_S(f,s:\gamma^S_{\tau_0}) = -\partial_{\tau_0} L^*_T(f,s:\gamma^T_{\tau_0})$. 
For $L^*_T$, note that the $T$-segment runs from $T^{-1}\tau_0=\tau_0-1$ to $\tau_0$ --- two endpoints a unit apart,
moving together with $\tau_0$ --- so by Leibniz's rule and the $T$-periodicity $f(\tau_0-1)=f(\tau_0)$,
\begin{equation}
\partial_{\tau_0} L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})
=f(\tau_0)\,\tk(\tau_0,s)-f(\tau_0-1)\,\tk(\tau_0-1,s)
=f(\tau_0)\,\delta_T\tk(\tau_0-1,s),
\end{equation}
where $\delta_T\tk(\tau,s):=\tk(\tau+1,s)-\tk(\tau,s)$. This is the whole role of $\delta_T$: the
unit gap between the endpoints turns the $\partial_{\tau_0}$ boundary term into a unit shift of
the kernel. That shift is elementary --- the kernel was built for it --- since
$\zeta(\nu,x+1)-\zeta(\nu,x)=-x^{-\nu}$ gives, on both terms at $x=\tau+1$,
\begin{align}
& \delta_T\tk(\tau,s)=-(\tau+1)^{s-1}+e^{i\pi(s-1)}(\tau+1)^{k-1-s}
\\
& \implies \partial_{\tau_0} L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) =f(\tau_0)\big[-\tau_0^{s-1}+e^{i\pi(s-1)}\tau_0^{k-1-s}\big].
\end{align}
The $S$-segment runs $-1/\tau_0\to\tau_0$; its lower endpoint moves with
$\partial_{\tau_0}(-1/\tau_0)=\tau_0^{-2}$, so by Leibniz, the modularity
$f(-1/\tau_0)=\tau_0^k f(\tau_0)$, and the phase $(-1/\tau_0)^{s-1}=e^{i\pi(s-1)}\tau_0^{1-s}$,
\begin{equation}
\partial_{\tau_0} L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}) 
=f(\tau_0)\tau_0^{s-1}-f(-1/\tau_0)\,(-1/\tau_0)^{s-1}\,\tau_0^{-2}
=f(\tau_0)\big[\tau_0^{s-1}-e^{i\pi(s-1)}\tau_0^{k-1-s}\big].
\end{equation}
Thus $\tau_0$-variations are equal and opposite, $\partial_{\tau_0}L^*_S = - \partial_{\tau_0}L^*_T$. Thus 
$\partial_{\tau_0}L^*=\partial_{\tau_0}L^*_S + \partial_{\tau_0}L^*_T=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Recovering the classical L-integral for $f \in S_k$]\label{rem:mellin}
Let $f\in S_k$. As $\tau_0\to i\infty$, the $S$-segment maps onto the standard 
L-function contour, $[0,i\infty]$. Simultaneously, as $f(it) \sim e^{-2\pi t}$ as 
$t \to \infty$, the $T$-segment vanishes exponentially. Thus, in this limit, $L^*(f,s)$ 
exactly maps onto the classical L-function integral~\cite{01-Apostol}:
\begin{equation}
\lim_{\tau_0\to i\infty}L^*(f,s)=\int_0^{\infty}\! t^{s-1}f(it)\,dt.
\end{equation}
\end{remark}

\section{$L$-functions for weak modular forms}\label{sec:Lfun}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:HGG}
Let $\Gamma(s, x) := \int_x^{\infty} e^{-t} t^{s-1} dt$ denote the {\em incomplete Gamma function}. For $n\ne0$, $\nu\in\CC$, and $z\in\HH$,
\begin{equation}
\int_{z}^{z+1}\! e^{2\pi inu}\,\zeta(\nu,u)\,du=e^{i \pi (1-\nu)/2} (2\pi n)^{\nu-1}\,\Gamma\big(1-\nu,-2\pi i n z\big).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\zeta(\nu,u)=\sum_{m\ge0}(m+u)^{-\nu}$ and shift $u\mapsto u+m$ in the $m$-th term; as
$e^{2\pi in(u+m)}=e^{2\pi inu}$ for integer $m$, the unit segments stack into one horizontal ray,
\begin{equation}
\int_z^{z+1}\! e^{2\pi inu}\zeta(\nu,u)\,du
=\sum_{m\ge0}\int_{z+m}^{z+1+m}\! e^{2\pi inv}v^{-\nu}\,dv
=\int_z^{z+\infty}\! e^{2\pi inv}v^{-\nu}\,dv.
\end{equation}
Substitute $w=-2\pi i n v$, so that $v=iw/(2\pi n)$, $dv=i\,dw/(2\pi n)$, and $e^{2\pi inv}=e^{-w}$:
the lower endpoint becomes $w=-2\pi i n z$, and for $n>0$ the ray rotates to $w\to+\infty$ along the
positive reals --- legal, since $e^{-w}w^{-\nu}$ decays in the right half-plane, so the closing arc
vanishes. With $v^{-\nu}=i^{-\nu}(2\pi n)^{\nu}w^{-\nu}$,
\begin{equation}
\int_z^{z+\infty}\! e^{2\pi inv}v^{-\nu}\,dv
=i^{1-\nu}(2\pi n)^{\nu-1}\!\int_{-2\pi i n z}^{\infty}\! e^{-w}w^{-\nu}\,dw
=e^{i \pi (1-\nu)/2}(2\pi n)^{\nu-1}\Gamma\big(1-\nu,-2\pi i n z\big).
\end{equation}
For $n<0$ the ray instead grows as ${\rm Im} v\to\infty$ and the rotation is illegal; but both sides are
analytic in $n$ and agree for $n>0$, so the identity continues to $n<0$ with $\Gamma$ on its principal
branch. At $n=0$ the prefactor $(2\pi n)^{\nu-1}$ is singular; that case is handled separately.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The $L$-function for $f \in M_k^!$]\label{thm:weakL}
For $f\in M^!_k$, we may set $\tau_0=i$, and prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weakL}
L^*(f,s)=-c_f(0)\left(\frac1s+\frac{i^k}{k-s}\right)
+\sum_{n\ne0}c_f(n)\left(\frac{\Gamma(s,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s}+i^k\frac{\Gamma(k-s,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}\right)~.
\end{equation}
Here, trivially, $L^*(f,s) = i^k L^*(f,k-s)$. The L-function is $L(f,s) := (2\pi)^s L^*(f,s) /\Gamma(s)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By path independence and base-point independence from Proposition~\ref{prop:indep}, 
we may freely set $\tau_0=i$. Here, $L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}) = 0$ (the $S$-contour 
vanishes in length and there are no nontrivial homotopy classes as $f \in M_k^!$ has no poles 
which a finite contour can wrap), and 
the L-integral simplifies dramatically:
\begin{equation}
L^*(f,s) = L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_i) =e^{-\pi i s/2}\int_{i-1}^{i} f(\tau)\tk(\tau, s)d\tau~. 
\end{equation}
We then expand $f$ in terms of its q-series, $\sum_n c_f(n)e^{2\pi in\tau}$,
and substitute the $u$-variable of Proposition~\ref{prop:HGG} as 
$u=\tau+1$, and note that $e^{2\pi i n(u-1)}=e^{2\pi i n u}$. 
When $n\neq0$, straightforward substitutions of 
$\nu, z$ into Proposition~\ref{prop:HGG} suffice: $1-\nu=s$ gives the 
contribution of the first term in $\tk(\tau, s)$, and $1-\nu = k-s$ gives the second: 
\begin{align}
e^{-\pi i s/2}\left(e^{\pi i s/2} \frac{\Gamma(s,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s} - e^{i\pi(s-1)}e^{\pi i (k-s)/2} \frac{\Gamma(k-s,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}\right) = \frac{\Gamma(s, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s} + i^k \frac{\Gamma(k-s, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}~.
\end{align}
We may then evaluate the $n=0$ mode's contribution follows from noting the 
closed form solution for the definite integral, 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hurwitz-antideriv}
\int_{i - 1}^{i}\! \zeta(\sigma, \tau + 1)\,d\tau
 = \int_i^{i + 1}\!\zeta(\sigma, u)\,du
 = \frac{i^{1 - \sigma}}{\sigma - 1}.
\end{equation}
This result follows from noting $\partial_a(\zeta(\sigma-1, a)/(1-\sigma)) = \zeta(\sigma,a)$ 
and $\zeta(\sigma-1, a+1) - \zeta(\sigma-1, a) = -a^{1-\sigma}$. 
Applying this to the two distinct terms of $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ at $\sigma = 1 - s$ and $\sigma = 1 - (k - s)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:const-mode-integral}
e^{-\pi i s/2}\int_{i - 1}^{i}c_f(0)\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
= -c_f(0)\left(\frac{1}{s}  + \frac{i^k}{k - s}\right).
\end{equation}
Combining the $n = 0$ contributions with the $n \neq 0$ contributions reproduces Eq.~\eqref{eq:weakL}. 
Finally, the functional equation, $L^*(f,s)=i^k L^*(f,k-s)$, follows by direct inspection.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Explicit S- and T-contours for general $\tau_0$}

\begin{lemma}[$L^*_T$ for $M^!_k$]\label{lem:Tclosed-Mk}
For $f\in M^!_k$, and $\tau_0 \in \HH$ away from cusps, 
$L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ evaluates to:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T-segment-closed-Mk}
L_T^*(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})
= -c_f(0)\!\left(\frac{(\tau_0/i)^{s}}{s} + i^k\frac{(\tau_0/i)^{k-s}}{k-s}\right) + \sum_{n \ne 0}\! c_f(n)\!\left(\frac{\Gamma(s, -2\pi i n\tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^s} + i^k\frac{\Gamma(k - s, -2\pi i n\tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}\right).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expand $f = \sum_n c_f(n)\,q^n$ on the compact segment
$[\tau_0 - 1, \tau_0]$, and restrict attention to the sub-sum, $\sum_{n \neq 0}$. 
For each such $n \neq 0$, apply the Proposition~\ref{prop:HGG} in
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s) = \zeta(1 - s, \tau + 1) - e^{i\pi(s-1)}\zeta(s - (k-1), \tau + 1)$
at $\nu = 1 - s$ and $\nu = s - (k - 1)$. The phases for the second term work-out 
identically as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL}.  

For $n = 0$, the constant $c_f(0)$ multiplies $e^{-\pi i s/2}\!\int_{\tau_0 - 1}^{\tau_0}\!\widetilde k_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau$;
the Hurwitz antiderivative $\int_{\tau_0 - 1}^{\tau_0}\zeta(\sigma, \tau + 1)\,d\tau = \tau_0^{1 - \sigma}/(\sigma - 1)$
--- from $\partial_\tau\zeta(\sigma - 1, \tau + 1) = -(\sigma - 1)\zeta(\sigma, \tau + 1)$ and the shift
$\zeta(\sigma - 1, \tau_0 + 1) - \zeta(\sigma - 1, \tau_0) = -\tau_0^{1 - \sigma}$ --- applied to the two
halves of $\widetilde k_T$ at $\sigma = 1 - s$ and $\sigma = s - k + 1$ yields
$-e^{-\pi i s/2}\tau_0^{\,s}/s$ and $-e^{\pi i s/2}\tau_0^{\,k-s}/(k-s)$ respectively.
\end{proof}


\begin{corollary}[$L^*_T$ as $\tau_0 \to i \infty$]\label{lem:cusp-decay}
Let $f \in S^!_k$, where $f = \sum_{n \geq -N} c_f(n)q^n$ for some $-N \in \ZZ$. 
The T-contour's contribution to $L^*(f,s)$ is given by 
Lemma~\ref{lem:Tclosed-Mk}. As $\tau_0 = it$ goes to the cusp $i\infty$,
the $T$-contour scales exactly as $f(it)$ scales:
$L_T^*(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) \sim f(it) \sim e^{2\pi N t}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For the asymptotics, $\Gamma(\sigma, z) \sim z^{\sigma - 1}e^{-z}$
as $|z| \to \infty$ for $|\arg z| < \tfrac{3\pi}{2}$ \cite[\S8.11]{DLMF}
gives $|c_f(n)|$-weighted go as $e^{-2\pi n\,{\rm Im}(\tau_0)}$.  The smallest 
mode $n$ in the q-series, i.e. $n = -N$, dominates the scaling of $f(it)$ and of 
$L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{i t})$. Combining these results yields the claimed asymptotics.
\end{proof}

Now, exploiting $\tau_0$-independence of $L^*(f, s)$, its explicit value as given in 
Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL}, and the explicit T-contribution $L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ trivially 
implies clean, closed-form expressions for $L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0})$:

\begin{corollary}[$L^*_S$ for $M_k^!$]\label{cor:Sclosed}
For $f\in M^!_k$ and $\tau_0\in\HH$, 
$L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0})$ evaluates to
\begin{align}
L_S^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0})
&= c_f(0)\left(\frac{(\tau_0/i)^{\,s}-1}{s}
+ i^k\frac{(\tau_0/i)^{\,k-s}-1}{k-s}\right) \label{eq:S-segment-explicit}\\
&+ \sum_{n\ne0} c_f(n)\!\left(\frac{\Gamma(s,2\pi n)-\Gamma(s,-2\pi i n\tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^s}
+ i^k\,\frac{\Gamma(k-s,2\pi n)-\Gamma(k-s,-2\pi i n\tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}\right)~. \nonumber
\end{align}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Recall $L^*(f, s) - L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) = L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0})$. 
Now, $L^*(f,s)$ for $f \in M_k^!$ is given by Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL}, and 
$L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ is given by Lemma~\ref{lem:Tclosed-Mk}. 
Their difference yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:S-segment-explicit}.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Classical Mellin recovery and canonical uniqueness.}\label{sec:Connection-w-previous-weak-MF-l-functions}

As discussed in Remark~\ref{rem:mellin}, for $f \in S_k$, taking the cusp-limit
$\tau_0 \to i\infty$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL} collapses the $S$-segment 
onto the classical Mellin contour $[0, i\infty]$ while via Corollary~\ref{lem:cusp-decay} 
the $T$-segment vanishes exponentially as $L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) \sim f(\tau_0) 
\sim e^{-2\pi {\rm Im}(\tau_0) N}$ (as $f \in S_k \Leftrightarrow f = \sum_{n \geq N} c_f(n) q^n$ with $N \geq 1$). 
I.e., here, one recovers the classical L-integral of $f$ without obstruction:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Mellin-recovery}
\lim_{\tau_0 \to i\infty}\!
L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})
\;=\; \int_0^\infty\! t^{s - 1}\,f(it)\,dt~.
\end{equation}  
This story changes when $f = 1/q^N (1 + O(q)) \in S_k^!$ for some $N \geq 1$. 

In this context, while of course the sum $L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) + 
L^*_S(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}) = L^*(f,s)$ is manifestly invariant under 
changes of the base-point, each of these two terms can indeed change values. Indeed, 
Corollary~\ref{lem:cusp-decay} clearly shows that as $\tau_0 \to i \infty$, when $f \in S_k^!$ 
has negative powers of $q$ in its q-series, then $L^*_T(f, s; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) \sim 
e^{2 \pi N {\rm Im}(\tau_0)}$. Discarding $\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$ therefore does violence to the result: 
When the $S$-contour is isolated from its T-partner, and $\tau_0 \to i \infty$, it diverges. This is the precise
reason that~\cite{BFK2011} needed to regularize their divergent L-integral representation for properly weak 
cusp forms.

\begin{remark}[Recovering the classical L-integral for $f \in M_k$]\label{natural-beauty-via-finiteness}
In textbook treatments~\cite{01-Apostol}, when $f \in M_k$, its L-integral is defined as
\begin{align}
L^*(f,s)|_{\rm cl} :=&~ \int_0^\infty \big( f(it) - c_f(0) \big) t^{s-1} dt = \frac{\Gamma(s)}{(2 \pi)^s} \sum_{n = 1}^{\infty} \frac{c_f(n)}{n^s} \\
=& -c_f(0) \bigg(\frac{1}{s} + \frac{i^k}{k-s} \bigg) + \sum_{n = 1}^{\infty} c_f(n)\left(\frac{\Gamma(s,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s} + i^k \frac{\Gamma(k-s, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}\right) \nonumber
\end{align}
Classically, this constant-term subtraction is needed because $\int_0^\infty t^{s-1} dt$ is a pure 
divergence, so the q-series cannot commute with the integration contour.

However, as is clear from our general Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL} for L-integrals for $f \in M_k^!$, 
and from the specific results of the S- and T-contours in Lemma~\ref{lem:Tclosed-Mk} and 
Corollary~\ref{cor:Sclosed}, our L-integral requires no such explicit subtraction of the constant 
term $-c_f(0)$. Thus, not only does our prescription naturally handle divergences at poles when 
$f \in M_k^!$ has polar terms in its q-series, but it also obviates the need to by-hand remove the 
constant mode from the kernel of the L-integral that is needed when defining L-functions in terms 
of the classical Mellin contour, $\int_0^\infty$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Period polynomials, periods and quasi-periods}

Because $L^*(f,s)$ is canonical for every $f\in S^!_k$, and because period polynomials 
for a given modular form can be written in terms of their L-functions evaluated at integer-$s$ 
in the critical strip, they need no separate construction.

\begin{definition}[Period polynomial for $S_k^!$]\label{def:rf}
For $f\in S^!_k$ and $n=k-2$, the \emph{period polynomial} is
\begin{equation}
r_f(X,Y):=(2\pi i)^{n+1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}(-1)^\ell\binom{n}{\ell}\,i^{\ell+1}\,L^*(f,\ell+1)\,X^{n-\ell}Y^\ell.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Period polynomial as a contour integral]\label{prop:periodint}
With $(X-\tau Y)^n$ in place of the $L$-weight $\tau^{s-1}$, and
$\mathbf K_T(\tau;X,Y):=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\binom{n}{\ell}(-1)^\ell\,\tk(\tau,\ell+1)\,X^{n-\ell}Y^\ell$
in place of $\tk$, the same two segments assemble $r_f$ directly:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf}
r_f(X, Y)=(2\pi i)^{n+1}\Big[\int_{\gamma^S_{\tau_0}} f(\tau)\,(X-\tau Y)^n\,d\tau
+\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}} f(\tau)\,\mathbf K_T(\tau;X,Y)\,d\tau\Big].
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By Definition~\ref{def:Lint}, the $X^{n-\ell}Y^\ell$ coefficient of the bracket is
\begin{equation}
r_f|_{X^{n-\ell}Y^\ell} = (-1)^\ell\binom{n}{\ell} i^{\ell+1} 
\big[
L^*_S(f, \ell+1) 
+
L^*_T(f, \ell+1)
\big]
=(-1)^\ell\binom{n}{\ell}\,i^{\ell+1}L^*(f,\ell+1)~.
\end{equation}
This matches definition~\ref{def:rf} term by term, and proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The role of the $T$-kernel in Eichler--Shumura theory]\label{cohomology}
The Hurwitz $T$-kernel in this note was originally uncovered in order to reproduce the manifestly
finite computations of the quasi-periods for $\Dhat = 1/q + O(q^2) \in S_{12}^!$ originally given
in~\cite{Brown2017}. Its role in the period polynomial~\eqref{eq:rf} is sharp: the $S$-segment alone
is not invariant under the parabolic shift $\tau_0 \mapsto \tau_0 + 1$, and the $T$-segment
$\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}} f(\tau)\,\mathbf K_T(\tau;X,Y)\,d\tau$ is precisely the term that cancels
this variation, leaving $r_f(X,Y)$ independent of the basepoint $\tau_0$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:indep}). This basepoint-independence is the finite-contour counterpart of the
parabolicity condition in Eichler--Shimura theory.

\end{remark}

Having now defined the period polynomial associated to a cusp form $f \in S_k^!$, 
we now seek to find its {\em (quasi-)periods}, which we define presently. First, though, 
we briefly mention some important facts from the theory of period polynomials, but 
refer the newcomer to the field to standard references such as~\cite{KZ1984} for more 
systematic discussions. To begin, because period polynomials are integrals of the $\SLZ$ 
covariant differential,\footnote{Technically, period polynomials are the sum of contour integrals 
$\int_{\gamma^{-1}(\tau_0)}^{\tau_0}\omega_f$, plus an additional piece which fixes parabolicity. 
This extra piece is the focus of Remark~\ref{cohomology}.}
\begin{equation}
\omega_f := f(\tau) (X - \tau Y)^{n} d\tau~.
\end{equation} 
Due to its origin as an integral of this covariant differential, it must satisfy two nontrivial 
relations 
\begin{align}
r_f(X, Y)|_{(1+S)} = r_f(X, Y)|_{(1 + U + U^2)} = 0~,
\end{align}
where $U := TS$ and $r_f(X, Y)|_{\gamma} := r_f(aX + bY, cX + dY)$ for all 
$\gamma = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} a & b \\ c & d\end{smallmatrix}\right) \in \SLZ$, and 
$r_f|_{\gamma + \sigma} := r_f|_\gamma + r_f|_\sigma$. These two constraints together 
impose extremely stringent constraints on $r_f(X, Y) \in \CC[X, Y]_{k-2}$. Specifically, 
they require it to lie within a $2\dim(S_k)+1$-dimensional subspace of the $k-2$-dimensional 
space $\CC[X, Y]_{k-2}$. When $\dim(S_k) = 1$, this 3-dimensional space is spanned by the 
three polynomials, whose definition we (hopefully correctly lifted) from~\cite[\S1.3]{KZ1984}: 

\begin{definition}\label{def:Wpm}
For $k$ with $\dim S_k=1$, $n:=k-2$, $p_0:=X^n-1$, $B_i = \oint t^{j-1} e^t/(t-1) dt$, and 
$B^0_m(X):=\sum_{i\ne1}\binom{m}{i}B_i X^{m-i}$. The even and odd Kohnen--Zagier 
bases~\cite[Thm.~1$'$]{KZ1984} are
\begin{align}
W^{(k)}_-(X)&:={\rm pp}\left(\tfrac{1}{n-1}\big(B^0_{n-1}(X)+X^nB^0_{n-1}(\tfrac1X)\big)
-\tfrac13\big(B^0_3(X)+X^nB^0_3(\tfrac1X)\big)\right),\\
W^{(k)}_+(X)&:={\rm pp}\left(\tfrac12\big(B^0_2(X)-X^nB^0_2(\tfrac1X)\big)
+\tfrac1n\big(B^0_n(X)-X^nB^0_n(\tfrac1X)\big)-c\,p_0\right),
\end{align}
where ${\rm pp}$ act on any polynomial with rational coefficients by multiplying it by all 
coefficient-denominators and dividing by the gcd, and $c$ eliminates the $p_0$ component 
of $W^{(k)}_+$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Periods and Quasi-Periods]\label{def:periods}
Let $k$ be such that $\dim(S_k) = 1$, $n:= k-2$, let $W^{(k)}_\pm(X, Y) := Y^n W^{(k)}_\pm(X/Y)$ be as in Definition~\ref{def:Wpm}, and let $r_f(X, Y)$ be as in Definition~\ref{def:rf}. Further, define the two numbers, projections
\begin{equation}\label{eq:project}
\omega^\pm(f)=\frac{\langle r_f,W^{(k)}_\pm\rangle}{\langle W^{(k)}_\pm,W^{(k)}_\pm\rangle},
\qquad
\langle P,Q\rangle=\sum_a(-1)^a\binom{n}{a}^{-1}p_a\,q_{n-a}.
\end{equation}
When $f$ is a cusp form, $\omega^\pm(f)$ are its {\em periods}; when $f$ is a weak cusp form, $\omega^\pm(f)$ are its {\em quasi-periods}.
\end{definition}
Now, having defined our periods and quasi-periods, we use them together with our L-functions 
to extract periods and quasi-periods for $f \in S_k^!$ (when $\dim(S_k) = 1$). To this effect, 
we recall the notation of~\cite{DukeJenkins}. Decomposing the modular weight 
$k = 12 \lambda + k'$ where $k' \in \{ 0, 4, 6, 8, 10, 14\}$, we see $\lambda = \dim(S_k)$ when 
$\lambda \geq 0$. For any $\lambda$, we may denote $f_{k, m} := 1/q^m + O(q^{\lambda+1}) \in M_k^!$. 
When $\dim(S_k) = 1$, we have $f_{k,m} = 1/q^m + O(q^2)$. Here, define 
$\Delta_k := f_{k,-1} = q + O(q^2) \in S_k$ and $\Dhat_k := f_{k,1} = 1/q + O(q^2) \in S_k^!$.
Further, define
\begin{align}
{\rm pd}_k := \left( \begin{matrix} 
\omega^+(\Delta_k) & \omega^-(\Delta_k) \\
\eta^+(\Dhat_k) & \eta^-(\Dhat_k)
\end{matrix}\right) ~~~~,~~~~
\alpha_k := \frac{(-1)^{1+k/2} \det({\rm pd}_k)}{(2\pi i)^{k-1}(k-2)!}
\end{align}
Brown--Hain~\cite[Prop.~5.6]{BrownHain2018} show that $\det({\rm pd}_k)$ satisfies a 
Legendre-type relation, $\det({\rm pd}_k)\in\QQ^\times\cdot(2\pi i)^{k-1}$. Brown~\cite[\S8.3]{Brown2017}

\section{Meromorphic modular forms}\label{sec:mero}

For $f\in F_k$ with poles in $\HH$, the two segment integrals of
Definition~\ref{def:Lint} depend on their contours; we show their sum depends only on the
homotopy class of the pair.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:indepHomotopy}
For $f\in F_k$ and $\tau_0\in\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, the $L$-integral
$L^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ depends only on the homotopy class of its
two segments in $\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, and not on the basepoint
$\tau_0$. At fixed $\tau_0$ each of $L^*_S,L^*_T$ is separately path-independent; a change
of basepoint moves value between the two, leaving only their sum invariant.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At fixed $\tau_0$ the integrands $f(\tau)\tau^{s-1}$ and $f(\tau)\tk(\tau,s)$ are
holomorphic on $\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, so Cauchy's theorem makes each
of $L^*_S,L^*_T$ constant on the homotopy class of its own segment. Under a motion of the
basepoint, $\partial_{\tau_0}L^*$ is evaluated at the moving endpoints alone; the
computation of Proposition~\ref{prop:indep} --- which uses only $f(\tau_0-1)=f(\tau_0)$ and
$f(-1/\tau_0)=\tau_0^{k}f(\tau_0)$ --- cancels the boundary terms of $L^*_S$ against those
of $L^*_T$, the interior poles lying off the contours. Hence $\partial_{\tau_0}L^*=0$ along
any motion in $\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, while each summand separately
varies.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:class}
For $f\in F_k$ and $\tau_0\in\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, let
$[\gamma^S_{\tau_0}]$, $[\gamma^T_{\tau_0}]$ denote the homotopy classes of the two
segments in $\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$.
\end{definition}

Thus, with $\gamma^S_{\tau_0} \in [\gamma^S_{\tau_0}]$ and 
$\gamma^T_{\tau_0} \in [\gamma^T_{\tau_0}]$, by Proposition~\ref{prop:indepHomotopy} 
the value
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lclass}
L^*\big(f,s;[\gamma^S_{\tau_0}],[\gamma^T_{\tau_0}]\big) 
:= L^*\big(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0} , \gamma^T_{\tau_0}\big)
= L^*_S(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0}) + L^*_T(f,s;\gamma^T_{\tau_0})
\end{equation}
is independent of the chosen representative paths and independent of $\tau_0$ (subject to 
natural constraints of $\tau_0$ monodromies around singular points, which move between 
homotopy classes).

We would like to speak of ``the'' L-integral. Homotopy classes of course split this into 
L-integrals for contours in different homotopy classes. And, so, in what follows, we define 
the reference homotopy classes: 
\begin{definition}\label{def:null_homotopy}
Let $f \in F_k$, $\tau_0$ be the base-point of $L^*(f,s)$ in the usual way, and let 
$f^{-1}(\infty)$ be the full set of all poles of $f$. Further, let $\tau_p^*$ denote the 
pole of lowest height at/above the ${\rm Im}(\tau) = 1$ line, and denote its imaginary 
height by $y^* := {\rm Im}(\tau_p^*)$. If $y^* = 1$, then let $\tau_p^\dagger = x^\dagger 
+ i y^\dagger$ denote $f$s pole of next lowest height above $\tau_p^*$. I.e., $y^* 
< y^\dagger$; if no such pole exists, then $y^\dagger := \infty$.

We define the {\em minimal homotopy class of $f$}, 
which we denote $[\gamma_f] = [\gamma^T_f] + [\gamma^S_f]$, as follows. 
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $y^* > 1$, then $[\gamma^T_f]$ is the homotopy class containing the 
horizontal line from $i-1$ to $i$, and $[\gamma^S_f]$ is the homotopy 
class containing the trivial path from $i$ to itself.
\item If $y^* = 1$, let $\delta$ be any positive constant such that $y^* < 1+\delta < 
y^\dagger$. Let $\tau_0^* := i + i\delta$. Here, $[\gamma^T_f]$ is the homotopy class containing the 
horizontal line from $\tau_0^*-1$ to $\tau_0^*$, and $[\gamma^S_f]$ is the homotopy 
class containing the almost-straight-line path that goes from $-1/\tau_0^*$ to $\tau_0^*$ 
along the imaginary-$\tau$ axis {\em except when it passes a pole on the axis}. When there is 
a pole on the imaginary-$\tau$ axis e.g. at $\tau_p = i$, this special contour $\gamma^S_{\tau_0^*}$ 
passes to the left of such a pole.
 \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

With this definition, we may thus unambiguously define
\begin{align}
L^*(f,s) := L^*(f, s; [\gamma^S_f], [\gamma^T_f])~.
\end{align} 
Now, having fixed the reference value $L^*_0(f,s)$ in the trivial
homotopy class in Definition~\ref{def:null_homotopy}, now consider 
two paths $\gamma^S_{\tau_0}$ and $\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$ which may 
be in any homotopy class. Further, let $X_S(p),X_T(p)\in\ZZ$ count 
the windings $\gamma^S_{\tau_0}$, resp.$\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$
has about each pole $\tau_p\in f^{-1}(\infty)$ relative to the null-classes.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:wall}
Let $f\in F_k$, $r_S(f, \tau_p) := \Res_{\tau=\tau_p} (f(\tau) \tau^{s-1})$ 
and $r_T(f, \tau_p) := \Res_{\tau=\tau_p} (f(\tau) \tk(\tau, s))$. Then, 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wall}
L^*\big(f,s;[\gamma^S_{\tau_0}],[\gamma^T_{\tau_0}]\big)
= L^*_0(f,s)
+ 2\pi i\,e^{-\pi i s/2}\sum_{\tau_p\in f^{-1}(\infty)}\Big[
X_S(\tau_p) r_S(f, \tau_p)
+ X_T(\tau_p) r_T(f, \tau_p) \Big].
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Two pairs whose winding data differ by one unit at a single pole $p$, on one segment,
bound a loop encircling $p$ once; the residue theorem adds $2\pi i$ times the residue of
that segment's integrand --- $f\,\tau^{s-1}$ for $\gamma^S$, $f\,\tk$ for $\gamma^T$ ---
and the prefactor $e^{-\pi i s/2}$ of Definition~\ref{def:Lint} multiplies through.
Within-class constancy is Proposition~\ref{prop:indepHomotopy}; summing over crossings
gives \eqref{eq:wall}.
\end{proof}


The polylog projection renders every pole with ${\rm Im}\,\tau_p\ge1$ as an explicit tail,
leaving $(I-\widehat P)f$ with all poles strictly below the line ${\rm Im}\,\tau=1$. This is
forced for poles with ${\rm Im}\,\tau_p>1$: lying above $\gamma^T_f$, they make the
$q$-series of $f$ diverge on the contour ($|c_f(n)|\gtrsim n^{m-1}e^{2\pi n\,{\rm Im}\,\tau_p}$
for a pole of order $m$~\cite{1806}). A pole on the line itself sits just below the lifted
$\gamma^T_f$ of Definition~\ref{def:null_homotopy}; we project it too, so the regularized
part stays clean and it appears as a tail rather than a $\gamma^S$-integral around the pole.

\begin{definition}\label{def:proj}
Let $f\in F_k$ have, at a pole $\tau_p$ of order $m_p$, the Laurent principal part
$\sum_{m=1}^{m_p} a^{(p)}_{-m}\,(\tau-\tau_p)^{-m}$, where $a^{(p)}_{-m}$ is the coefficient
of $(\tau-\tau_p)^{-m}$. Since $\Li_{1-m}\big(e^{2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)}\big)$ has a pole of
order $m$ at $\tau_p$, there are unique $r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)$, $1\le m\le m_p$, for which the
\emph{polylog projection}
\begin{equation}
\widehat P_{\tau_p}f(\tau):=\sum_{m=1}^{m_p} r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)\,
\Li_{1-m}\big(e^{2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)}\big)
\end{equation}
has the same principal part as $f$ at $\tau_p$ (a triangular, hence invertible, linear
system relating $r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)$ to $a^{(p)}_{-1},\dots,a^{(p)}_{-m}$). The
\emph{projection} $\widehat P:=\sum_{{\rm Im}\tau_p\ge1}\widehat P_{\tau_p}$ removes the
principal part of every pole with ${\rm Im}\tau_p\ge1$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}\label{def:cRf}
With $\widehat P$ as in Definition~\ref{def:proj}, let $c_{Rf}(n)$ be the Fourier
coefficients of the regularized form $(I-\widehat P)f$ in its expansion valid for large
${\rm Im}\tau$:
\begin{equation}
(I-\widehat P)f(\tau)=\sum_n c_{Rf}(n)\,q^n,\qquad q=e^{2\pi i\tau}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}\label{def:Finfty}
$F(\infty)$ is the space of meromorphic $g$ on $\HH$ with $g(\tau+1)=g(\tau)$, finitely
many poles in each compact subset of $\{{\rm Im}\tau>0,\ |{\rm Re}\tau|\le\tfrac12\}$, and
$g(\tau)=O(e^{C{\rm Im}\tau})$ as ${\rm Im}\tau\to\infty$ for some $C$.
\end{definition}

Then $F_k\subset F(\infty)$, and $(I-\widehat P)f\in F(\infty)$: the polylog tails are
$T$-periodic but not modular, so the projection leaves $F_k$. After projection every pole
of $(I-\widehat P)f$ lies strictly below ${\rm Im}\tau=1$; pole heights accumulate only at $0$,
so there is a maximal one, $y_b<1$ (take $y_b=0$ if $(I-\widehat P)f$ is holomorphic).

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:growth}
Let $y_b<1$ be the maximal pole height of $(I-\widehat P)f$, and $m_b$ the maximal order
among poles at height $y_b$. There is a $C>0$ such that, for every $\varepsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:growth}
C\,n^{m_b-1}e^{2\pi n y_b}\;\le\;|c_{Rf}(n)|\;\le\;e^{2\pi n(y_b+\varepsilon)},
\end{equation}
the upper bound for all large $n$, the lower for infinitely many $n$. In particular
$|c_{Rf}(n)|\,e^{-2\pi n}\to0$ geometrically, at base $e^{-2\pi(1-y_b-\varepsilon)}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$(I-\widehat P)f$ is $T$-periodic and continuous on ${\rm Im}\tau=y$ for each $y\in(y_b,1]$, so
Parseval gives $\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}|(I-\widehat P)f(\sigma+iy)|^2\,d\sigma
=\sum_n|c_{Rf}(n)e^{-2\pi yn}|^2<\infty$, whence $c_{Rf}(n)e^{-2\pi yn}\to0$; $y=y_b+\varepsilon$
gives the upper bound~\cite{1806}. For the lower bound, subtract the polylog projection of
the height-$y_b$ poles: the remainder's coefficients are $o(e^{2\pi ny_b})$, while the
subtracted tails contribute $\sum_j C_j n^{m_j-1}e^{-2\pi inx_j}e^{2\pi ny_b}$ with
$C_j\ne0$ at order $m_b$; the $x_j$ distinct mod $1$, the mean value
$\sum_{m_j=m_b}|C_j|^2>0$ bounds the sum below along infinitely many $n$.
\end{proof}

For $n\ne0$, the single-mode contribution of Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL} is
\begin{align}\label{eq:gc}
G_{s,k}(n):=\frac{\Gamma(s,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s}
+i^k\,\frac{\Gamma(k-s,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}.
\end{align}
Each projected pole contributes a single polylog tail.

\begin{definition}\label{def:IN}
With $\Li_s(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}x^n n^{-s}$, the \emph{tail integral} of a projected pole at $z$
with ${\rm Im}\,z>1$ is the $L$-integral of that tail in the minimal class of $f$
(Definition~\ref{def:null_homotopy}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:IN}
I_N(s,z):=L^*\big(\Li_{-N}(e^{2\pi i(\cdot-z)}),\,s;\,[\gamma^S_f],[\gamma^T_f]\big).
\end{equation}
The tail's poles then lie above $\gamma^S_f$, whose contribution vanishes, so $I_N(s,z)$ is
the $T$-segment integral alone.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:polylogcf}
For ${\rm Im}\,z>1$, polylog inversion $\Li_{-N}(w)=(-1)^{N+1}\Li_{-N}(1/w)$ resums
\eqref{eq:IN} into
\begin{equation}
I_N(s,z)=\delta_{N,0}\Big(\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}\Big)
+(-1)^{N+1}\sum_{n\ge1} n^N e^{2\pi inz}\,G_{s,k}(-n),
\end{equation}
convergent exponentially with base $e^{-2\pi({\rm Im}\,z-1)}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:indepHomotopy} we may evaluate $I_N(s,z)$ in the $\delta\to0$
representative of the minimal class; there the tail --- regular at $i$ since
${\rm Im}\,z>1$ --- has vanishing $\gamma^S$-integral and $\gamma^T_f\to[i-1,i]$. On
$[i-1,i]$ one has $|e^{2\pi i(\tau-z)}|>1$, so the defining series of $\Li_{-N}$ diverges;
inversion rewrites it as $(-1)^{N+1}\sum_{n\ge1}n^Ne^{2\pi in(z-\tau)}$, convergent since
$|e^{2\pi i(z-\tau)}|<1$, and term-by-term integration against $\tk$ gives
$\sum_n n^N e^{2\pi inz}G_{s,k}(-n)$, the mode-$(-n)$ block of Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL}; for
$N=0$ the leftover $-1$ in $\Li_0(w)=-1-\Li_0(1/w)$ contributes $\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}$.
The rate is $e^{-2\pi({\rm Im}\,z-1)}$, from $\Gamma(s,-2\pi n)\sim(2\pi n)^{s-1}e^{2\pi n}$
against $|e^{2\pi inz}|=e^{-2\pi n\,{\rm Im}\,z}$.
\end{proof}

For ${\rm Im}\,z>1$, write $w:=e^{2\pi i(z-i)}$, $Q:=e^{2\pi iz}$, and
$(a)_{(j)}:=a(a-1)\cdots(a-j+1)$, $(a)_{(0)}:=1$ (principal branches).
\begin{definition}\label{def:tailpieces}
Define $\Pi_N(s;w),\ \Lambda_N(s;Q),\ \mathcal E_N(s,z)$ by
\begin{align}
\Pi_N(s;w)&:=\sum_{j=0}^{N+1}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}\big[(s-1)_{(j)}+i^k(k-s-1)_{(j)}\big]}{(2\pi)^{j+1}}\,\Li_{j+1-N}(w),\\
\Lambda_N(s;Q)&:=\frac{\Gamma(s)}{(-2\pi)^{s}}\,\Li_{s-N}(Q)+i^k\,\frac{\Gamma(k-s)}{(-2\pi)^{k-s}}\,\Li_{k-s-N}(Q),\\
\mathcal E_N(s,z)&:=-\sum_{n\ge1}n^N Q^n\Big[(s{-}1)_{(N+2)}\tfrac{\Gamma(s{-}N{-}2)-\Gamma(s{-}N{-}2,-2\pi n)}{(-2\pi n)^{s}}
+i^k(k{-}s{-}1)_{(N+2)}\tfrac{\Gamma(k{-}s{-}N{-}2)-\Gamma(k{-}s{-}N{-}2,-2\pi n)}{(-2\pi n)^{k-s}}\Big].
\end{align}
Here $\Pi_N$ is finite and carries the entire $w\to1$ singularity ($\Li_{\le1}(w)$); the
$\Li_{s-N}(Q),\Li_{k-s-N}(Q)$ in $\Lambda_N$ are polylogarithms of non-integer order; and
$\mathcal E_N$ is absolutely convergent, its terms $O(n^{-3}|w|^n)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:polylogedge}
With $\Pi_N,\Lambda_N,\mathcal E_N$ as in Definition~\ref{def:tailpieces}, for
${\rm Im}\,z>1$ and every $s\in\CC$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:threelayer}
I_N(s,z)=\delta_{N,0}\Big(\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}\Big)
+(-1)^{N+1}\big[\Pi_N(s;w)+\Lambda_N(s;Q)+\mathcal E_N(s,z)\big],
\end{equation}
and the right side extends $I_N(s,\cdot)$ continuously to the edge ${\rm Im}\,z=1$ for
$z\notin i+\ZZ$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Insert into Lemma~\ref{lem:polylogcf} the recursion
$\Gamma(s,x)=e^{-x}\sum_{j<J}(s-1)_{(j)}x^{s-1-j}+(s-1)_{(J)}\Gamma(s-J,x)$ at $x=-2\pi n$,
$J=N+2$, for both incomplete gammas in $G_{s,k}(-n)$. The $e^{2\pi n}$ factors recombine with
$e^{2\pi inz}$ into $w^n$, and $\sum_n n^{N-j-1}w^n=\Li_{j+1-N}(w)$ gives $\Pi_N$. Writing
$\Gamma(s-J,x)=\Gamma(s-J)-[\Gamma(s-J)-\Gamma(s-J,x)]$ and using
$(s-1)_{(J)}\Gamma(s-J)=\Gamma(s)$, the complete parts resum to $\Lambda_N$ via
$\sum_n n^{N-s}Q^n=\Li_{s-N}(Q)$, the lower-gamma parts to $\mathcal E_N$. From
$\Gamma(\sigma,x)\sim x^{\sigma-1}e^{-x}$, the terms of $\mathcal E_N$ are
$O(n^{N-J-1}e^{2\pi n})|Q|^n=O(n^{-3}|w|^n)$, uniform on ${\rm Im}\,z\ge1$; $\Lambda_N$ is
continuous there since $|Q|\le e^{-2\pi}$; and each $\Li_{j+1-N}(w)$ is continuous up to
$|w|=1$ away from $w=1$.
\end{proof}

The one edge point the formula misses is $z=i$, where $\Pi_N$ is singular ($w=1$); we
compute $I_N(s,i)$ directly. Its finiteness rests on a relation among the Laurent
coefficients of $f$ at the $S$-fixed point.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:Laurent}
Let $f\in F_k$ have a pole of order $P$ at $i$, with principal part
$\sum_{m=1}^{P}a_{-m}(\tau-i)^{-m}$. Modularity $f(-1/\tau)=\tau^k f(\tau)$ is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Laurent}
(-1)^r\sum_{m=r}^P a_{-m}\binom{m}{r}i^{m-r}
=i^k\sum_{m=r}^P a_{-m}\binom{k}{m-r}(-i)^{m-r},\qquad r=1,\dots,P.
\end{equation}
The leading relation ($r=P$) is $\big((-1)^P-i^k\big)a_{-P}=0$, so the pole order matches
$k\bmod4$ ($P$ odd $\Leftrightarrow k\equiv2$, $P$ even $\Leftrightarrow 4\mid k$); at
$P=1$ this is $(1+i^k)a_{-1}=0$, and for a double pole the $r=1$ relation is
$a_{-1}=\tfrac{i(k-2)}{2}a_{-2}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\tau=i+\varepsilon$. Then $-1/\tau-i=-\varepsilon/(1-i\varepsilon)$, so the principal
part of $f(-1/\tau)$ is $\sum_m a_{-m}(i-\varepsilon^{-1})^m$ and that of $\tau^k f(\tau)$ is
$i^k(1-i\varepsilon)^k\sum_m a_{-m}\varepsilon^{-m}$. Matching the coefficient of
$\varepsilon^{-r}$ through
$(i-\varepsilon^{-1})^m=\sum_j\binom mj i^{m-j}(-1)^j\varepsilon^{-j}$ and
$(1-i\varepsilon)^k=\sum_l\binom kl(-i)^l\varepsilon^l$ gives \eqref{eq:Laurent}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:FP}
For $N\ge0$, with $H_N:=\sum_{r=1}^N\tfrac1r$ and $c^+_j(s):=(s-1)_{(j)}+i^k(k-s-1)_{(j)}$,
\begin{align}
\mathrm{FP}_N(s,k):=\frac{c^+_N(s)\,H_N}{(2\pi)^{N+1}}
+\sum_{\substack{j\ge0\\ j\ne N}}\frac{c^+_j(s)\,\zeta(j+1-N)}{(2\pi)^{j+1}}
+\frac{(-1)^N N!}{(2\pi)^{N+1}}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\frac{(-1)^t(s-1)_{(t)}}{t!\,(N-t)}
+\mathcal E^+_N(s),
\end{align}
the $j$-sum taken to any $J\ge N+2$, with
$\mathcal E^+_N(s):=\sum_{n\ge1}n^N e^{2\pi n}\big[(s-1)_{(J)}\tfrac{\Gamma(s-J,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s}
+i^k(k-s-1)_{(J)}\tfrac{\Gamma(k-s-J,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}\big]$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:wallvalue}
Let $f\in F_k$ have a pole of order $P$ at $i$ with principal part
$\sum_{m=1}^P a_{-m}(\tau-i)^{-m}$, and $\widehat P_i f$ its projection. The pole's
contribution to $L^*(f,s)$ in the minimal class is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wallvalue}
L^*(\widehat P_i f,\,s)=-2\pi i\,a_{-1}\Big(\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}\Big)
+\sum_{m=1}^P \frac{(-2\pi i)^m a_{-m}}{(m-1)!}\,\mathrm{FP}_{m-1}(s,k),
\end{equation}
entire in $s$ but for the simple poles at $s=0,k$ carried by $a_{-1}$; the individually
divergent contour pieces cancel by the relations of Lemma~\ref{lem:Laurent}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Evaluate on the minimal-class contour with basepoint $i(1+\delta)$ and let $\delta\to0$.
On the $T$-segment the pole at $i$ lies below the contour; inverting each
$\Li_{1-m}(e^{2\pi i(\tau-i)})$ and integrating against $\tk$ yields the
harmonic-, $\zeta$-, and $\mathcal E^+$-terms of $\mathrm{FP}_{m-1}$ together with a part
$\sim\delta^{-(m-1)},\dots,\log\delta$; the $S$-segment, passing to the left of $i$, gives the
mirror divergences. Summed over $m$ with weights $r^*(m)=(-2\pi i)^m a_{-m}/(m-1)!$, the
divergent parts form a linear combination of the $a_{-m}$ that vanishes precisely by the
modularity relations of Lemma~\ref{lem:Laurent}, leaving the $\mathrm{FP}$-sum of
\eqref{eq:wallvalue}; the simple part ($m=1$) additionally carries the $-1$-leftover pole
$-2\pi i\,a_{-1}(\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s})$, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:polylogcf}. The cutoff $J$
is immaterial: raising it moves terms between the $\zeta$-sum and $\mathcal E^+_N$, which
remains convergent as in Lemma~\ref{lem:polylogedge}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:mero}
Let $f\in F_k$ have poles $\tau_p$ of order $P_p$ with ${\rm Im}\,\tau_p\ge1$ in the strip
$|{\rm Re}\,\tau|\le\tfrac12$, regularized Fourier coefficients $c_{Rf}(n)$
(Definition~\ref{def:cRf}), and projection weights $r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)$
(Definition~\ref{def:proj}). In the minimal class,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mero}
L^*(f,s)=-c_{Rf}(0)\Big(\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}\Big)
+\sum_{n\ne0}c_{Rf}(n)\,G_{s,k}(n)
+\sum_{\tau_p\ne i}\ \sum_{m=1}^{P_p}r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)\,I_{m-1}(s,\tau_p)
+L^*(\widehat P_i f,\,s),
\end{equation}
with $I_{m-1}$ from Lemmas~\ref{lem:polylogcf}--\ref{lem:polylogedge} and the last term
(present only for a pole at $i$) from Lemma~\ref{lem:wallvalue}. The right side is entire in
$s$ apart from at most simple poles at $s=0$ and $s=k$, and reduces to the weakly-holomorphic
$L^*$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL} when $f$ is holomorphic on $\HH$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Split $f=(I-\widehat P)f+\widehat Pf$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:growth} the Fourier series of the
regularized part converges on $\gamma^T_f$; Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL} applied term by term
gives its $L^*$ as the first two sums. The projection
$\widehat Pf=\sum_{\tau_p}\sum_m r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)\,\Li_{1-m}(e^{2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)})$
contributes, for each $\tau_p\ne i$, the tail integrals $I_{m-1}(s,\tau_p)$ of
Definition~\ref{def:IN}, evaluated by Lemmas~\ref{lem:polylogcf}--\ref{lem:polylogedge}; the
pole at $i$ contributes $L^*(\widehat P_i f,s)$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:wallvalue}. Entirety and the
pole locations follow from those of $G_{s,k}$ and the $\delta_{N,0}$ terms; when $f$ is
holomorphic on $\HH$, $\widehat Pf=0$ and $c_{Rf}=c_f$, recovering Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The $c_T$ term in the even weak-form entry.}
The weak form's period polynomial $r_{\widehat\Delta_k}$ is, like the cusp form's, annihilated by
$1+S$ and $1+U+U^2$, so it too lies in the period space $\langle W_+,\,p_0,\,W_-\rangle$; but it
carries a component along the Eisenstein polynomial $p_0=X^{k-2}-Y^{k-2}$ that the cusp form lacks.
Since $p_0$ occupies only the two extreme monomials $X^{k-2},Y^{k-2}$---the $s=1$ and $s=k-1$
coefficients---and $W_+$ is not orthogonal to $p_0$ under the Haberland pairing, the projection
$\eta^+$ of~\eqref{eq:project} absorbs part of this Eisenstein content but not all. The unabsorbed
remainder surfaces as an additive constant $c_T$ in the extreme even coefficient of

\section{mero MFs}\label{sec:meroOLD-20260706}

For $f\in F_k$ with poles in $\HH$, the integrals of Definition~\ref{def:Lint} depend on
the contours. Write the $L$-integral as the sum of its two segments,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:LSLT}
L^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0})
= L^*_S(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0}) \;+\; L^*_T(f,s;\gamma^T_{\tau_0}),
\qquad
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
L^*_S &:= e^{-\pi i s/2}\!\int_{\gamma^S_{\tau_0}}\! f(\tau)\,\tau^{s-1}\,d\tau,\\
L^*_T &:= e^{-\pi i s/2}\!\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}}\! f(\tau)\,\tk(\tau,s)\,d\tau,
\end{aligned}
\right.
\end{equation}
each finite for every $s\in\CC$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:finite}). This section shows the value
depends only on the homotopy class of the pair, quantifies the class dependence --- each
segment contributes its own residues --- and proves an exponentially convergent series
representation, valid in every class and at every $s\in\CC$.

For $f\in F_k$, poles of finite order away from the cusp of $\SLZ$ make the contours
matter: the $L$-integral is organized by the homotopy classes of
$(\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ in $\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:indepHomotopy}
For $f\in F_k$, the $L$-integral $L^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ depends
only on the homotopy class of its contours, and not on the basepoint $\tau_0$.  At fixed
$\tau_0$ each of $L^*_S$, $L^*_T$ is separately path-independent; a change of basepoint
moves value between the two, leaving only their sum invariant.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At fixed $\tau_0$ the integrands $f\,\tau^{s-1}$ and $f\,\tk$ are holomorphic on
$\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, so Cauchy's theorem makes each summand of
\eqref{eq:LSLT} constant on the homotopy class of its own segment. Under a motion of
the basepoint, the derivative $\partial_{\tau_0}L^*$ is evaluated at the moving
endpoints alone, and the computation of Proposition~\ref{prop:indep} --- which uses
only $f(\tau_0-1)=f(\tau_0)$ and $f(-1/\tau_0)=\tau_0^{k}f(\tau_0)$ --- cancels the
boundary terms of $L^*_S$ against those of $L^*_T$; the interior poles lie off the
contours and never enter. Hence $\partial_{\tau_0}L^*=0$ along any motion through
$\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, while each summand separately varies.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:class}
Fix $f\in F_k$ and $\tau_0\in\HH$, and let $[\gamma^S_{\tau_0}]$, $[\gamma^T_{\tau_0}]$
denote homotopy classes of the two segments in
$\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, endpoints fixed. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:indepHomotopy} the assignment
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lclass}
L^*\big(f,s;[\gamma^S_{\tau_0}],[\gamma^T_{\tau_0}]\big)
:= L^*_S(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0}) + L^*_T(f,s;\gamma^T_{\tau_0}),
\qquad\text{any representatives,}
\end{equation}
is well-defined; by the same proposition, motion of the basepoint changes the two
classes jointly but not the value.
\end{definition}

Poles strictly above the line $\im\tau=1$ force exponential growth on $q$-series
coefficients --- $|c_f(n)|\gtrsim n^{m-1}e^{2\pi n\,\im\tau_p}$ for a pole of order $m$
at $\tau_p$~\cite{1806} --- and defeat term-by-term integration along $\gamma^T$. We
remove exactly these poles, with the projection of~\cite{1806}.

\begin{definition}\label{def:proj}
Let $f\in F_k$ have interior poles at the points $\tau_p$ of a set $\mathcal P_f$ of
$\Gamma$-inequivalent representatives, and write $\Li_s(z)=\sum_{n>0}z^n n^{-s}$. Since
$\Li_{1-m}\big(e^{2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)}\big)$ has a pole of order $m$ at $\tau_p$, the
principal part of $f$ there is matched by a unique \emph{polylog projection}
\begin{equation}
\widehat P_{\tau_p}f(\tau):=\sum_{m>0}r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)\,\Li_{1-m}\big(e^{2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)}\big),
\end{equation}
its coefficients $r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)$ being the principal-part data of $f$ at $\tau_p$. The
\emph{projection} $\widehat P:=\sum_{\im\tau_p>1}\widehat P_{\tau_p}$ removes the principal
part of every pole strictly above the line $\im\tau=1$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}\label{def:cRf}
With $\widehat P$ as in Definition~\ref{def:proj}, $c_{Rf}(n)$ denotes the Fourier
coefficients of $(I-\widehat P)f$ in its expansion valid for large $\im\tau$:
\begin{equation}
(I-\widehat P)f = \sum_n c_{Rf}(n)\,q^n .
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}\label{def:Finfty}
$F(\infty)$ is the space of meromorphic $g$ on $\HH$ with $g(\tau)=g(\tau+1)$, finitely
many poles in any compact subset of the strip
$\{{\rm Im}\,\tau>0,\ |{\rm Re}\,\tau|\le\tfrac12\}$, and $g(\tau)=O(e^{C\,{\rm Im}\,\tau})$
as ${\rm Im}\,\tau\to\infty$ for some $C$.
\end{definition}

Then $F_k\subset F(\infty)$, and $(I-\widehat P)f\in F(\infty)$ --- the polylog tails are
$T$-periodic but not modular, so the projection leaves $F_k$.

We further define, for $n\ne0$, the single-mode contribution of Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL},
\begin{align}\label{eq:gc}
G_{s,k}(n) := \frac{\Gamma(s,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s} + i^k\,\frac{\Gamma(k-s,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}~,
\end{align}
and, for one tail of Definition~\ref{def:proj}, the $L$-integral along the $T$-segment
at $\tau_0=i$, where $\gamma^S$ degenerates ($-1/i=i$):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:IN}
I_N(s,z) := e^{-\pi i s/2}\int_{\gamma^T_{i}} \Li_{-N}\big(e^{2\pi i(\tau-z)}\big)\,\tk(\tau,s)\,d\tau .
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:polylogcf}
For ${\rm Im}\,z>1$, polylog inversion
$\Li_{-N}(w)=(-1)^{N+1}\Li_{-N}(1/w)$ resums \eqref{eq:IN} into
\begin{equation}
I_N(s,z)=\delta_{N,0}\Big(\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}\Big)
+(-1)^{N+1}\sum_{n\ge1} n^N e^{2\pi inz}\,G_{s,k}(-n),
\end{equation}
convergent exponentially with base $e^{-2\pi({\rm Im}\,z-1)}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For ${\rm Im}\,z>1$ one has $|e^{2\pi i(\tau-z)}|>1$ on $[i-1,i]$, so the defining
series of $\Li_{-N}$ diverges there; the inversion rewrites it through
$\Li_{-N}\big(e^{2\pi i(z-\tau)}\big)=\sum_{n\ge1}n^Ne^{2\pi in(z-\tau)}$, convergent
since $|e^{2\pi i(z-\tau)}|<1$. Integrating term by term against $\tk$, each
$e^{-\pi is/2}\int_{i-1}^{i}e^{-2\pi in\tau}\,\tk\,d\tau$ is the mode $-n$ building
block of Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL}, namely $G_{s,k}(-n)$; for $N=0$ the leftover $-1$ in
$\Li_0(w)=-1-\Li_0(1/w)$ integrates to $\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}$. The rate follows
from $\Gamma(s,-2\pi n)\sim(2\pi n)^{s-1}e^{2\pi n}$ against
$|e^{2\pi inz}|=e^{-2\pi n\,{\rm Im}\,z}$.
\end{proof}

The series of Lemma~\ref{lem:polylogcf} extends to the edge $z=i+x$,
$x\in[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$. Write $w:=e^{2\pi i(z-i)}$, $Q:=e^{2\pi iz}$, and
$(a)_{(j)}:=a(a-1)\cdots(a-j+1)$, $(a)_{(0)}:=1$; principal branches throughout.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:polylogedge}
As identities of meromorphic functions of $s\in\CC$, for ${\rm Im}\,z>1$ and $N\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:threelayer}
I_N(s,z)=\delta_{N,0}\Big(\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}\Big)
+(-1)^{N+1}\Big[\Pi_N(s;w)+\Lambda_N(s;Q)+\mathcal E_N(s,z)\Big],
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
\Pi_N(s;w)&:=\sum_{j=0}^{N+1}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}\big[(s-1)_{(j)}+i^k\,(k-s-1)_{(j)}\big]}{(2\pi)^{j+1}}\,\Li_{j+1-N}(w),\label{eq:polarlayer}\\
\Lambda_N(s;Q)&:=\frac{\Gamma(s)}{(-2\pi)^{s}}\,\Li_{s-N}(Q)+i^k\,\frac{\Gamma(k-s)}{(-2\pi)^{k-s}}\,\Li_{k-s-N}(Q),\label{eq:lerchlayer}\\
\mathcal E_N(s,z)&:=-\sum_{n\ge1}n^N Q^n\bigg[(s{-}1)_{(N+2)}\,
\frac{\Gamma(s{-}N{-}2)-\Gamma(s{-}N{-}2,-2\pi n)}{(-2\pi n)^{s}} \label{eq:tamelayer}\\
& \qquad \qquad \qquad \quad+i^k\,(k{-}s{-}1)_{(N+2)}\,
\frac{\Gamma(k{-}s{-}N{-}2)-\Gamma(k{-}s{-}N{-}2,-2\pi n)}{(-2\pi n)^{k-s}}\bigg].\nonumber
\end{align}
The terms of $\mathcal E_N$ are $O(n^{-3}|w|^n)$, uniformly on ${\rm Im}\,z\ge1$; hence
\eqref{eq:threelayer} extends $I_N(s,\cdot)$ continuously to the edge away from $w=1$,
for every $s$ and every $N$. At $w=1$, i.e.\ $z\in i+\ZZ$, the obstruction is confined
to the $\Li_{\le1}$ terms of $\Pi_N$; for $N=0$ the $\Li_1$ coefficient is
$(1+i^k)/(2\pi)$, so for $k\equiv2\pmod4$ the extension is finite at $z=i$ as well.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Insert into the series of Lemma~\ref{lem:polylogcf} the exact recursion
$\Gamma(s,x)=e^{-x}\sum_{j<J}(s-1)_{(j)}x^{s-1-j}+(s-1)_{(J)}\Gamma(s-J,x)$ at
$x=-2\pi n$, $J=N+2$, for both incomplete gammas in $G_{s,k}(-n)$. The factors
$e^{-x}=e^{2\pi n}$ recombine with $e^{2\pi inz}$ into $w^n$, and
$\sum_nn^{N-j-1}w^n=\Li_{j+1-N}(w)$ yields $\Pi_N$. In the leftover, write
$\Gamma(s-J,x)=\Gamma(s-J)-\big[\Gamma(s-J)-\Gamma(s-J,x)\big]$ and use
$(s-1)_{(J)}\,\Gamma(s-J)=\Gamma(s)$: the complete parts resum to $\Lambda_N$ through
$\sum_nn^{N-s}Q^n=\Li_{s-N}(Q)$, and the lower-gamma parts are $\mathcal E_N$. From
$\Gamma(\sigma,x)\sim x^{\sigma-1}e^{-x}$ as $x\to-\infty$, the terms of $\mathcal E_N$
are $O\big(n^{N-J-1}e^{2\pi n}\big)|Q|^n=O\big(n^{-3}|w|^n\big)$, uniformly on
${\rm Im}\,z\ge1$; $\Lambda_N$ is continuous there since $|Q|\le e^{-2\pi}$. Claim (i)
follows since each $\Li_{j+1-N}(w)$ is continuous up to the unit circle away from
$w=1$; claim (ii) since $\mathcal E_N$ and $\Lambda_N$ are continuous on the whole
closed line; claim (iii) since the $\Li_1$-coefficient of $\Pi_0$ carries the factor
$1+i^k=0$ and the remaining $\Li_2(w)$ is continuous on the closed disk.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:edgevalues}
At integer $s\in\{1,\dots,k-1\}$ the Pochhammer symbols terminate all three layers and
\eqref{eq:threelayer} closes in polylogarithms of $w$: e.g.\ at $k=6$,
$I_0(2,z)=\tfrac14+\tfrac{\Li_2(w)}{2\pi^2}-\tfrac{3\Li_3(w)}{4\pi^3}+\tfrac{3\Li_4(w)}{8\pi^4}$,
so $I_0(2,i)=\tfrac{27}{80}-\tfrac{3\zeta(3)}{4\pi^3}$. At the central point the kernel
itself vanishes for $k\equiv2\pmod4$ --- $\tk(\tau,k/2)=\big[1-e^{i\pi(k/2-1)}\big]
\zeta(1-\tfrac k2,\tau+1)\equiv0$ --- so there $L^*=L^*_S$; for $\Delta/E_6$ the two
$\gamma^S$-chambers at $s=3$ carry $\pm\pi\,\Res_{\tau=i}(\Delta/E_6)$ with principal
value $0$: the central $L$-value of a $k\equiv2$ block is half a wall jump of
Lemma~\ref{lem:wall}.
\end{remark}

The excluded point $z=i$ is reached through the wall: anchor the contour at
$\tau_0=i(1+\delta)$ and let $\delta\to0^+$. Write $H_N:=\sum_{r=1}^N\tfrac1r$ and
$c^+_j(s):=(s-1)_{(j)}+i^k\,(k-s-1)_{(j)}$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:wallvalue}
Let $f\in F_k$ have a pole at $i$ of order $P$, with principal part
$\sum_{m=1}^Pa_{-m}(\tau-i)^{-m}$, and let $\widehat P_if$ be its polylog projection.
In the class of $\tau_0=i(1+\delta)$, the $\gamma^S$-passage through $i$ taken as a
principal value,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wallvalue}
\lim_{\delta\to0^+}L^*\big(\widehat P_if,\,s\big)
=\sum_{m=1}^{P}\frac{(-2\pi i)^m\,a_{-m}}{(m-1)!}\;\mathrm{FP}_{m-1}(s,k)~,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FPN}
\mathrm{FP}_N(s,k)=\frac{c^+_N(s)\,H_N}{(2\pi)^{N+1}}
+\sum_{\substack{j\ge0\\ j\ne N}}\frac{c^+_j(s)\,\zeta(j+1-N)}{(2\pi)^{j+1}}
+\mathcal E^+_N(s)
+\frac{(-1)^NN!}{(2\pi)^{N+1}}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\frac{(-1)^t\,(s-1)_{(t)}}{t!\,(N-t)}~,
\end{equation}
the $j$-sum taken to any $J\ge N+2$, with remainder
\begin{equation}\label{eq:EplusN}
\mathcal E^+_N(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}n^Ne^{2\pi n}\Big[(s-1)_{(J)}\,\frac{\Gamma(s-J,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{s}}
+i^k\,(k-s-1)_{(J)}\,\frac{\Gamma(k-s-J,2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}\Big].
\end{equation}
The individually divergent $\delta$-powers and logarithms cancel by modularity alone;
for a double pole the cancellation condition is exactly the $S$-fixed-point constraint
$a_{-1}=\tfrac{i(k-2)}{2}\,a_{-2}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $u:=2\pi i(\tau-i)$ and $g_N(\tau):=\Li_{-N}\big(e^{u}\big)$. The expansion
$\Li_\sigma(e^{-\mu})=\Gamma(1-\sigma)\,\mu^{\sigma-1}+\sum_{r\ge0}\zeta(\sigma-r)\frac{(-\mu)^r}{r!}$
at $\mu=-u$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gNLaurent}
g_N(u)=\frac{(-1)^{N+1}N!}{u^{N+1}}+\sum_{p\ge0}\frac{\zeta(-N-p)}{p!}\,u^{p},
\end{equation}
a single negative power per tail; matching principal parts is therefore diagonal and
gives $\widehat P_if=\sum_{m=1}^{P}r_m\,g_{m-1}$ with $r_m=(-2\pi i)^m a_{-m}/(m-1)!$.
Split \eqref{eq:LSLT} at $\tau_0=i(1+\delta)$ and set $v:=e^{-2\pi\delta}$.

\emph{$T$-segment.} The poles of $g_N$ lie on $i+\ZZ$, below $\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$, so
$g_N=\sum_{n\ge1}n^Ne^{2\pi n}e^{2\pi in\tau}$ converges uniformly on the segment and
integrates mode by mode. For
$F_n(\tau_0):=e^{-\pi is/2}\int_{\tau_0-1}^{\tau_0}e^{2\pi in\tau}\,\tk(\tau,s)\,d\tau$,
periodicity of the mode and the difference equation of the kernel,
$\tk(\tau_0,s)-\tk(\tau_0-1,s)=-\tau_0^{s-1}+e^{i\pi(s-1)}\tau_0^{k-s-1}$ (from
$\zeta(\sigma,a)-\zeta(\sigma,a+1)=a^{-\sigma}$), determine $\partial_{\tau_0}F_n$;
comparison with Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL} at $\tau_0=i$ then fixes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Fmode}
F_n(\tau_0)=\frac{\Gamma(s,-2\pi in\tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^{s}}
+i^k\,\frac{\Gamma(k-s,-2\pi in\tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}~,
\end{equation}
principal branches, the argument $-2\pi in\tau_0=2\pi n(1+\delta)$ positive. $J$-fold
integration by parts,
$\Gamma(s,x)=e^{-x}\sum_{j<J}(s-1)_{(j)}x^{s-1-j}+(s-1)_{(J)}\Gamma(s-J,x)$, resums the
$T$-side of one tail into
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Tside}
T_N(\delta)=\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}
\frac{(s-1)_{(j)}(1+\delta)^{s-1-j}+i^k(k-s-1)_{(j)}(1+\delta)^{k-s-1-j}}{(2\pi)^{j+1}}
\,\Li_{j+1-N}(v)\;+\;\mathcal E^+_N(s;\delta),
\end{equation}
whose remainder has terms $O\big(n^{N-J-1}v^{n}\big)$ uniformly in $\delta\ge0$ and
equals \eqref{eq:EplusN} at $\delta=0$. Expanding \eqref{eq:Tside} with the same
$\Li_\sigma(e^{-\mu})$ formula, now at $\mu=2\pi\delta$: each $j\ne N$ contributes
$c^+_j(s)\,\zeta(j+1-N)\,(2\pi)^{-j-1}$ at order $\delta^0$; for $j<N$ the singular
powers $\Gamma(N-j)\,\mu^{j-N}$ meet the $\delta^{N-j}$-coefficients of the
$(1+\delta)$-binomials, and the composition
$(s-1)_{(j)}\,(s-1-j)_{(N-j)}=(s-1)_{(N)}$ collapses their total to
$c^+_N(s)\,H_N\,(2\pi)^{-N-1}$; the term $j=N$ contributes only the logarithm
$-c^+_N(s;\delta)\ln(2\pi\delta)\,(2\pi)^{-N-1}$.

\emph{$S$-segment.} On the imaginary axis,
$e^{-\pi is/2}\int_{\gamma^S}g_N\,\tau^{s-1}\,d\tau
=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-2\pi\delta}^{2\pi\delta/(1+\delta)}
\big(1-\tfrac{u}{2\pi}\big)^{s-1}g_N(u)\,du$. The analytic part of
\eqref{eq:gNLaurent} integrates to $O(\delta)$. In the pole part, expand
$(1-\tfrac u{2\pi})^{s-1}=\sum_t\frac{(-1)^t(s-1)_{(t)}}{t!\,(2\pi)^t}u^t$: each power
$u^{t-N-1}$ with $t<N$ is an exact derivative, so its integral is a difference of
endpoint antiderivatives --- independent of the indentation at $u=0$ --- with
$\delta^0$-coefficient $(2\pi)^{t-N}/(t-N)$; the power $t=N$ is the principal value
$\ln(1+\delta)=O(\delta)$; $t>N$ is $O(\delta)$. Collecting gives the last term of
\eqref{eq:FPN}. (A side-indentation at $u=0$ differs from the principal value by half
the wall jump of Lemma~\ref{lem:wall}.)

\emph{Cancellation.} By Proposition~\ref{prop:indepHomotopy}, $L^*(f,s)$ is constant
for $\delta>0$ small, and $L^*\big((I-\widehat P_i)f,s\big)$ is continuous at
$\tau_0=i$, its $\gamma^S$-integral vanishing as the segment contracts onto the
regular point $i$. Hence
$L^*(\widehat P_if,s)=L^*(f,s)-L^*\big((I-\widehat P_i)f,s\big)$ converges as
$\delta\to0^+$, so the coefficients of the divergent powers $\delta^{-r}$ and of
$\ln\delta$ in $\sum_mr_m\big(T_{m-1}+S_{m-1}\big)$ vanish identically --- in $s$ as
well, one relation per power of $s$ --- and the limit is the sum of the
$\delta^0$-coefficients: \eqref{eq:wallvalue} with \eqref{eq:FPN}. For $P=2$ the
vanishing of the logarithm reads
$r_1c^+_0(s)(2\pi)^{-1}+r_2c^+_1(s)(2\pi)^{-2}=0$, i.e.\
$a_{-1}=\tfrac{i(k-2)}{2}\,a_{-2}$, which is the order-$\varepsilon^{-1}$ term of
$f(-1/\tau)=\tau^kf(\tau)$ at $\tau=i+\varepsilon$.
\end{proof}

After projection, the surviving poles of $(I-\widehat P)f$ all lie strictly below the
line $\im\tau=1$; since the heights of a $\Gamma$-orbit accumulate only at $0$, when $f$
has no pole with $\im\tau_p=1$ there is a maximal surviving height $y_b<1$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:growth}
Let $f\in F_k$ have no pole with $\im\tau_p=1$; let $y_b<1$ be the maximal height of a
pole of $(I-\widehat P)f$, and $m_b$ the maximal order among its poles at height $y_b$.
Then there is a $C>0$ such that, for every $\varepsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:growth}
C\,n^{m_b-1}\,e^{2\pi n y_b}\;\le\;|c_{Rf}(n)|\;\le\;e^{2\pi n(y_b+\varepsilon)}~,
\end{equation}
the upper bound holding for all sufficiently large $n$, the lower for infinitely many
$n$. In particular $|c_{Rf}(n)|\,e^{-2\pi n}\to0$ geometrically, with base
$e^{-2\pi(1-y_b-\varepsilon)}$ for every $\varepsilon>0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$(I-\widehat P)f$ is $T$-periodic and continuous on the line ${\rm Im}\,\tau=y$ for
every $y\in(y_b,1]$, so Parseval gives
$\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\big|(I-\widehat P)f(\sigma+iy)\big|^2d\sigma
=\sum_n\big|c_{Rf}(n)\,e^{-2\pi yn}\big|^2<\infty$, whence
$c_{Rf}(n)\,e^{-2\pi yn}\to0$; $y=y_b+\varepsilon$ gives the upper bound for large
$n$~\cite{1806}. For the lower bound, subtract the polylog projection of the
height-$y_b$ poles $x_j+iy_b$: the difference has all poles strictly lower, so its
coefficients are $o(e^{2\pi ny_b})$ by the upper bound, while the subtracted tails
contribute $\sum_jC_j\,n^{m_j-1}e^{-2\pi inx_j}e^{2\pi ny_b}$ with $C_j\ne0$ at the
maximal order $m_j=m_b$. The $x_j$ being distinct modulo $1$, the mean value
$\lim_M\frac1M\sum_{n\le M}\big|\sum_{m_j=m_b}C_je^{-2\pi inx_j}\big|^2
=\sum_{m_j=m_b}|C_j|^2>0$ bounds $\big|\sum_{m_j=m_b}C_je^{-2\pi inx_j}\big|$ below
along infinitely many $n$; the triangle inequality finishes.
\end{proof}

Classes are labelled by winding data: relative to a reference pair, let
$X_S(p),X_T(p)\in\ZZ$ count the net turns of $\gamma^S$, resp.\ $\gamma^T$, about each
pole $p\in\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:wall}
For $f\in F_k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wall}
L^*\big(f,s;[\gamma^S_{\tau_0}],[\gamma^T_{\tau_0}]\big)
= L^*_0(f,s)
+2\pi i\,e^{-\pi i s/2}\sum_{p}\Big[
X_S(p)\,\Res_{\tau=\tau_p}\!\big(f(\tau)\,\tau^{s-1}\big)
+X_T(p)\,\Res_{\tau=\tau_p}\!\big(f(\tau)\,\tk(\tau,s)\big)\Big],
\end{equation}
with $L^*_0$ the value at the reference pair: $L^*_S$ shifts by the residues of
$f\,\tau^{s-1}$, and $L^*_T$ by those of $f\,\tk$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Two pairs whose winding data differ by one unit at a single pole $p$ on one segment
bound a loop encircling $p$ once; the residue theorem adds $2\pi i$ times the residue
of that segment's integrand --- $f\,\tau^{s-1}$ for $\gamma^S$, $f\,\tk$ for
$\gamma^T$ --- and the prefactor $e^{-\pi is/2}$ multiplies through. Within-class
constancy is Proposition~\ref{prop:indepHomotopy}; summing over crossings gives
\eqref{eq:wall}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:mero}
Let $f\in F_k$ have no pole with $\im\tau_p=1$, and let $y_b<1$ be as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:growth}. In the class of the pair at $\tau_0=i$ --- $\gamma^T$ the
straight segment $[i-1,i]$, $\gamma^S$ contracted to the point $i$ ($-1/i=i$); a
convenient reference class, not a canonical one (Remark~\ref{rem:ES-mero}) ---
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mero}
L^*(f,s)
= -c_{Rf}(0)\Big(\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}\Big)
+\sum_{n\ne0}c_{Rf}(n)\,G_{s,k}(n)
+\sum_{\im\tau_p>1}\,\sum_{m>0}r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)\,I_{m-1}(s,\tau_p)~,
\end{equation}
and a general class adds the wall term of Lemma~\ref{lem:wall}. Each sum converges
exponentially: the $n$-sum with base $e^{-2\pi(1-y_b-\varepsilon)}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:growth}), each $I_{m-1}$ with base $e^{-2\pi(\im\tau_p-1)}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:polylogcf}). On the excluded line $\im\tau_p=1$ the tail integrals
extend continuously away from the orbit of $i$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:polylogedge}), and at
$i$ itself the wall value is that of Lemma~\ref{lem:wallvalue}; assembling these into
the statement of the theorem at the wall is a principal-value bookkeeping we do not
carry out here.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In the stated class, $L^*(f,s)=e^{-\pi is/2}\int_{\gamma^T_i}f\,\tk\,d\tau$. Split
$f=(I-\widehat P)f+\widehat Pf$ (Definition~\ref{def:proj}). By
Lemma~\ref{lem:growth}, $|c_{Rf}(n)\,q^n|\le e^{-2\pi(1-y)n}$ on the segment for any
$y\in(y_b,1)$ and $n$ large, and the $n<0$ modes are finitely many (meromorphicity at
the cusp); the Fourier series of $(I-\widehat P)f$ therefore converges uniformly on
$\gamma^T_i$ and integrates term by term, each mode being the building block of
Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL}. This gives the first two terms of \eqref{eq:mero}, at the
stated rate since $G_{s,k}(n)=O\big(n^{{\rm Re}\,s-1}e^{-2\pi n}\big)$. The projected
part is a finite sum of tails, each integrating to $I_{m-1}(s,\tau_p)$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:polylogcf}, at rate $e^{-2\pi({\rm Im}\,\tau_p-1)}$. A general class
adds the wall term of Lemma~\ref{lem:wall}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:ES-mero}
Coefficient by coefficient, $r_f|(1+S)=0$ is the functional equation
$L^*(f,s)=i^kL^*(f,k-s)$; the substantive further condition on a period polynomial is
$r_f|(1+U+U^2)=0$. For $f\in F_k$ both are properties of the homotopy class: by
Lemma~\ref{lem:wall} the period polynomial shifts by residue polynomials under
wall-crossing, and whether a given class satisfies either relation is arithmetic of
those shifts. In a class satisfying both, $r_f$ is a genuine Eichler--Shimura period
polynomial, and such a class would anchor a canonical $L^*(f,s)$ for meromorphic $f$; we
take this question up elsewhere.
\end{remark}


\section{Meromorphic modular forms}\label{sec:meroOLD2}

\begin{proposition}[Basepoint and path independence within a homotopy class]\label{prop:indepHomotopy}
For $f\in F_k$, the value $L^*(f,s;\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ is independent of $\tau_0$
and of the path so long as both vary within a single homotopy class of admissible contours in
$\HH\setminus\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$. For $f\in M^!_k$ there is a single class, recovering
Proposition~\ref{prop:indep}; across different classes the value changes by the residues the
contour sweeps, as quantified in Corollary~\ref{cor:wall}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The $\tau_0$-derivative of Proposition~\ref{prop:indep} uses only the $T$-periodicity
$f(\tau_0-1)=f(\tau_0)$ and the modularity $f(-1/\tau_0)=\tau_0^k f(\tau_0)$, both valid for every
$f\in F_k$, and is evaluated at the moving endpoints alone. While $\tau_0$ stays in one chamber of
$\HH\setminus f^{-1}(\infty)$ --- where the segments meet no pole --- the boundary terms cancel
exactly as there, so $\partial_{\tau_0}L^*=0$; the interior poles lie off the contour and never
enter. Within a fixed class the integrands $f\,\tau^{s-1}$ and $f\,\tk$ are holomorphic along the
contours, so any two are joined by a deformation avoiding $\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)$, and
Cauchy's theorem equates their integrals.
\end{proof}


% §5.14a — [Def] polylog projection (def:proj)
\begin{definition}[Polylog projection]\label{def:proj}
Let $f\in F_k$ have interior poles at the points $\tau_p$ of a set $\mathcal P_f$ of
$\Gamma$-inequivalent representatives, and write $\Li_s(z)=\sum_{n>0}z^n n^{-s}$. Since
$\Li_{1-m}\big(e^{2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)}\big)$ has a pole of order $m$ at $\tau_p$, the principal part of
$f$ there is matched by a unique \emph{polylog projection}
\begin{equation}
\widehat P_{\tau_p}f(\tau):=\sum_{m>0}r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)\,\Li_{1-m}\big(e^{2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)}\big),
\end{equation}
its coefficients $r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)$ being the principal-part data of $f$ at $\tau_p$. The
\emph{projection} $\widehat P:=\sum_{\im\tau_p\ge1}\widehat P_{\tau_p}$ removes the principal
part of every pole on or above the line $\im\tau=1$.
\end{definition}

% §5.14b — [Def] regularized cusp coefficients (def:cRf)
\begin{definition}[Regularized cusp coefficients]\label{def:cRf}
With $\widehat P$ as in Definition~\ref{def:proj},
\begin{equation}
c_{Rf}(n):=\big[(I-\widehat P)f\big]_{q^n}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The space $F(\infty)$]\label{def:Finfty}
$F(\infty)$ is the space of meromorphic $g$ on $\HH$ with $g(\tau)=g(\tau+1)$, finitely many
poles in any compact subset of the strip $\{{\rm Im}\,\tau>0,\ |{\rm Re}\,\tau|\le\tfrac12\}$,
and $g(\tau)=O(e^{C\,{\rm Im}\,\tau})$ as ${\rm Im}\,\tau\to\infty$ for some $C$. Each such
$g$ has a Fourier expansion $g=\sum_n c_g(n)q^n$.
\end{definition}

Then $F_k\subset F(\infty)$, and $(I-\widehat P)f\in F(\infty)$ --- the polylog tails are
$T$-periodic but not modular, so the projection leaves $F_k$.

\begin{lemma}[$q$-series--integral commutation]\label{lem:commute}
Let $g\in F(\infty)$ have no pole on or above the line ${\rm Im}\,\tau=1$ (e.g.\
$g=(I-\widehat P)f$). Then $g=\sum_n c_g(n)q^n$ converges along the $T$-segment at $\tau_0=i$
and integrates term by term:
\begin{equation}
\int_{\gamma^T_i} g(\tau)\,\tk(\tau,s)\,d\tau
=\sum_n c_g(n)\int_{i-1}^{i} e^{2\pi in\tau}\,\tk(\tau,s)\,d\tau.
\end{equation}
A pole at ${\rm Im}\,\tau_p\ge1$ defeats this --- there the coefficients grow at least like
$n^{m-1}e^{2\pi n\,{\rm Im}\,\tau_p}$~\cite[Lem.~2.6]{1806}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
With no pole on or above ${\rm Im}\,\tau=1$, the finitely many poles of $g$ in the strip have
heights $<1$; let $h<1$ be the largest. The Fourier series $g=\sum_n c_g(n)q^n$ converges
absolutely and uniformly on compact subsets of $\{{\rm Im}\,\tau>h\}$, in particular on the
$T$-segment $[i-1,i]$; with $\tk(\tau,s)$ bounded there, the sum passes through the integral,
which is the displayed identity. Conversely a pole at height ${\rm Im}\,\tau_p\ge1$ forces
$|c_g(n)|\gtrsim n^{m-1}e^{2\pi n\,{\rm Im}\,\tau_p}$~\cite[Lem.~2.6]{1806}, so
$|c_g(n)q^n|\gtrsim n^{m-1}e^{2\pi n({\rm Im}\,\tau_p-1)}\not\to0$ on $[i-1,i]$ and the series
diverges.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Polylog integral]\label{def:polylog}
For $N\ge0$ and $z\in\HH$, the \emph{polylog integral} is the $L$-integral of a single tail
$\Li_{-N}(e^{2\pi i(\tau-z)})$ along the $T$-segment at $\tau_0=i$ (where $\gamma^S$ degenerates, since
$-1/i=i$):
\begin{equation}
I_N(s,z):=e^{-\pi i s/2}\int_{i-1}^{i}\Li_{-N}\big(e^{2\pi i(\tau-z)}\big)\,\tk(\tau,s)\,d\tau.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
We further find it convenient to define the following function,
\begin{align}\label{eq:gc}
G_{s, k}(n) := \frac{\Gamma(s, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s} + i^k \frac{\Gamma(k-s, 2 \pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}.
\end{align}
\begin{lemma}[Specialized polylog integral]\label{lem:polylogcf}
Let $G_{s, k}(n)$ be as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:gc}. For the projected poles
${\rm Im}\,z>1$, where $|e^{2\pi i(\tau-z)}|\ge1$ on the contour, polylog inversion
$\Li_{-N}(w)=(-1)^{N+1}\Li_{-N}(1/w)$ resums the integral into the convergent series
\begin{equation}
I_N(s,z)=\delta_{N,0}\Big(\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}\Big)
+(-1)^{N+1}\sum_{n\ge1} n^N e^{2\pi inz}\,G_{s,k}(-n).
\end{equation}
This sum converges exponentially quickly, with base $e^{-2\pi n (y -1)}$, where $y = {\rm Im}(z)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For ${\rm Im}\,z>1$, $|e^{2\pi i(\tau-z)}|\ge1$ on $[i-1,i]$, so the series of $\Li_{-N}(e^{2\pi i(\tau-z)})$
diverges; the inversion rewrites it through $\Li_{-N}(e^{2\pi i(z-\tau)})=\sum_{n\ge1}n^N e^{2\pi in(z-\tau)}$,
convergent since $|e^{2\pi i(z-\tau)}|\le1$. Integrating term by term against $\tk$, each
$e^{-\pi i s/2}\!\int_{i-1}^i e^{-2\pi in\tau}\tk\,d\tau$ is the building block $G_{s,k}(n)$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL} at mode $-n$, giving the displayed sum; for $N=0$ the leftover $-1$ in
$\Li_0(w)=-1-\Li_0(1/w)$ adds $-e^{-\pi i s/2}\!\int_{i-1}^i\tk=\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}$. 

Exponential convergence $\sim e^{-2\pi n(y-1)}$ for $y = {\rm Im}(z) > 1$ follows from combining the asymptotic 
scaling $\Gamma(s,-2\pi n) \sim (2\pi n)^{s-1} e^{2\pi n}$ and the exact result $|e^{2\pi i n z}| = e^{-2\pi n y}$.
\end{proof}

%  pole --- principal value, with the residue jump governed by Corollary~\ref{cor:wall} ---

\begin{theorem}[$L$-function of a meromorphic modular form]\label{thm:mero}
For $f\in F_k$ with no pole on the edge ${\rm Im}\,\tau=1$, at $\tau_0=i$,
\begin{equation}
L^*(f,s)=-c_{Rf}(0)\Big(\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s}\Big)
+\sum_{n\ne0}c_{Rf}(n)\,G_{s, k}(n)
+\sum_{{\rm Im}\,\tau_p>1}\sum_{m>0}r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)\,I_{m-1}(s,\tau_p),
\end{equation}
where, again $G_{s, k}(n)$ is the sum of incomplete gammas defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:gc};
the regularized cusp sum (Definition~\ref{def:cRf}, Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL}) plus one polylog
integral (Lemma~\ref{lem:polylogcf}) per interior pole above the edge. A pole on the edge
${\rm Im}\,\tau=1$ --- the elliptic point $\tau=i$ included --- instead sits on the contour: there
$L^*$ is the principal value, and sweeping the contour across the pole changes it by the residue
of Corollary~\ref{cor:wall}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At $\tau_0=i$ the $S$-segment degenerates, so $L^*(f,s)=e^{-\pi i s/2}\int_{i-1}^i f\,\tk\,d\tau$. Split
$f=(I-\widehat P)f+\widehat Pf$. The regularized part has no pole on or above ${\rm Im}\,\tau=1$
(Definition~\ref{def:proj}), so by Lemma~\ref{lem:commute} its Fourier series integrates term by
term; mode by mode this is Theorem~\ref{thm:weakL}, giving
$-c_{Rf}(0)(\tfrac1s+\tfrac{i^k}{k-s})+\sum_{n\ne0}c_{Rf}(n)G_{s,k}(n)$. The projected part
$\widehat Pf=\sum_{\tau_p}\sum_{m>0}r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)\Li_{1-m}(e^{2\pi i(\tau-\tau_p)})$ integrates to
$\sum_{{\rm Im}\,\tau_p>1}\sum_{m>0}r^*_{f,\tau_p}(m)I_{m-1}(s,\tau_p)$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:polylogcf}. Their sum is the stated formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Wall-crossing and the winding data $X_S,X_T$]\label{cor:wall}
Fix $f\in F_k$ and record, for an admissible pair $(\gamma^S_{\tau_0},\gamma^T_{\tau_0})$, its
\emph{winding data} --- functions $X_S,X_T:\Gamma\!\cdot\!f^{-1}(\infty)\to\ZZ$ giving the net number of
turns of $\gamma^S$, resp.\ $\gamma^T$, about each pole relative to a fixed reference pair (value
$L^*_0(f,s)$ there). By Proposition~\ref{prop:indepHomotopy} the integral depends only on $(X_S,X_T)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wall-crossing}
L^*(f,s;X_S,X_T) = L^*_0(f,s) + 2\pi i\,e^{-\pi i s/2}\!\left[ %Should separate into LT + LS, and have residue thms for each
\sum_{\tau_p} X_S(\tau_p)\,\Res_{\tau=\tau_p}\!\big(f\,\tau^{s-1}\big)
+ \sum_{\tau_p} X_T(\tau_p)\,\Res_{\tau=\tau_p}\!\big(f\,\tk\big)\right].
\end{equation}
A single wall-crossing is the rank-one case: sweeping one segment once across one pole $\tau_p$ shifts
$L^*$ by $2\pi i\,e^{-\pi i s/2}\Res_{\tau_p}(f\,\tau^{s-1})$ for $\gamma^S$, or
$2\pi i\,e^{-\pi i s/2}\Res_{\tau_p}(f\,\tk)$ for $\gamma^T$. Thus $L^*$ is fixed by the contour only up
to the residues its two segments wind around --- weighted by $\tau^{s-1}$ on $\gamma^S$ and by $\tk$ on
$\gamma^T$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Within-class constancy is Proposition~\ref{prop:indepHomotopy}. Two pairs whose winding data differ by
one unit at a single $\tau_p$ on one segment bound a contour encircling $\tau_p$ once; the residue
theorem adds $2\pi i$ times the residue of that segment's integrand --- $f\,\tau^{s-1}$ for $\gamma^S$,
$f\,\tk$ for $\gamma^T$ --- and the prefactor $e^{-\pi i s/2}$ of Definition~\ref{def:Lint} multiplies
through. Summing over all crossings gives \eqref{eq:wall-crossing}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The $S$-segment does not drop out]\label{rem:Snonvanish}
For $f\in M^!_k$ the interior is pole-free, so $X_S\equiv X_T\equiv0$ and $L^*$ is path-independent
(Proposition~\ref{prop:indep}). For $f\in F_k$ both windings are live, and the $S$-segment is no
exception. Its endpoints $-1/\tau_0$ and $\tau_0$ are $S$-equivalent, so $\gamma^S$ can be closed up
through the group action into a loop --- but a loop enclosing interior poles does \emph{not} integrate
to zero in the meromorphic case; it equals $2\pi i\sum(\text{residues})$. The identification of the
$S$-endpoints therefore does not make $\gamma^S$ negligible: it carries genuine residue data $X_S$,
weighted by $\tau^{s-1}$, on exactly the same footing as the $T$-segment's $X_T$ weighted by $\tk$.
\end{remark}

The elliptic fixed point $\tau=i$ is constrained by its own symmetry.

\begin{proposition}[Order at $\tau=i$]\label{prop:ellord}
For $f\in F_k$, ${\rm ord}_{\tau=i}(f)\equiv k/2\pmod2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The coordinate $w=(\tau-i)/(\tau+i)$ sends $i\mapsto0$ and conjugates $S:\tau\mapsto-1/\tau$ to
$w\mapsto-w$, with inverse $\tau(w)=i(1+w)/(1-w)$. Hence $g(w):=f(\tau(w))$ obeys $g(-w)=\tau(w)^k g(w)$,
and a leading term $g\sim c\,w^m$ forces $(-1)^m=\tau(0)^k=i^k=(-1)^{k/2}$. Since $w\sim(\tau-i)/2i$, this
$m$ is ${\rm ord}_{\tau=i}(f)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:ellord}
So a pole at $i$ has even order iff $4\mid k$ and odd order iff $k\equiv2\pmod4$; the minimal cases are
the double pole $E_4\Delta/E_6^2\in F_4$ and the simple pole $\Delta/E_6\in F_6$.
\end{remark}

A pole at $i$ lies on the edge ${\rm Im}\,\tau=1$, hence on a chamber wall; only the full $L^*$ stays
finite there.

\begin{proposition}[The elliptic pole]\label{prop:ellfull}
Let $f\in F_k$ have a pole at $i$. As $\tau_0\to i$ the parts $L_S^*$ and $L_T^*$ each diverge, yet their
sum $L^*$ holds at the finite chamber value; the value at $i$ is the principal value between the two
chambers, which differ by the residue of Corollary~\ref{cor:wall}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Off the wall both segments avoid the poles, so $L^*$ is finite (Lemma~\ref{lem:finite}) and constant in
$\tau_0$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:indepHomotopy}). As $\tau_0\to i$ each segment endpoint runs into a pole
of $f$, so $L_S^*$ and $L_T^*$ diverge individually; their sum being the chamber constant, the divergent
parts cancel. The jump across the wall is Corollary~\ref{cor:wall}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Transcendental periods of the elliptic blocks}\label{sec:ellperiods}

Fix, for each even weight $k$, the canonical block whose only pole is on the orbit of $i$,
\begin{equation}
\widehat\Delta_i \;:=\; \frac{f_k}{(j-1728)^M},
\end{equation}
with $f_k$ the Duke--Jenkins minimal form~\cite{DukeJenkins}; by Proposition~\ref{prop:ellord}
the pole at $i$ has order $2M$ for $k\equiv0$ and $2M-1$ for $k\equiv2\pmod4$. The two smallest
weights give $\widehat\Delta_i=E_4\Delta/E_6^2\in F_4$ (double pole) and $\widehat\Delta_i=\Delta/E_6\in F_6$
(simple pole), each $=q+O(q^2)$. We evaluate $L^*(\widehat\Delta_i,s)$ from Definition~\ref{def:Lint}
by direct integration along a \emph{single fixed} admissible pair --- the same path for every $s$, so all
values lie in one homotopy cell --- with $\tau_0$ off the wall through $i$, where $L^*$ is finite and
basepoint-independent (Proposition~\ref{prop:ellfull}). Write $B(s):=e^{\pi i s/2}L^*(\widehat\Delta_i,s)$,
so that $B(k-s)=\overline{B(s)}$ and $B(k/2)$ is real.

Numerically, to $44$ digits (checks in \texttt{elliptic\_period\_checks.py}~\cite{VerifyScripts}),
the central value $B(k/2)$ closes in elementary constants:
\begin{align}
k=4:\quad & B(2)=\frac{1}{864\,\pi}\qquad\Big(\text{equivalently } L(E_4\Delta/E_6^2,2)=-\tfrac{\pi}{216}\Big),\label{eq:ell-k4}\\
k=6:\quad & B(3)=\frac{\varpi^4}{576\,\pi^4}=\frac{\Gamma(1/4)^8}{36864\,\pi^6},\label{eq:ell-k6}
\end{align}
where $\varpi=\Gamma(1/4)^2/(2\sqrt{2\pi})$ is the lemniscate constant. In each case $B(k/2)$ equals
$\pi$ times the residue of $\widehat\Delta_i$ at $i$, and its arithmetic nature tracks the parity of
Proposition~\ref{prop:ellord}: the even-order pole ($k\equiv0$) has residue in $\QQ\cdot\pi^{-2}$, giving an
elementary period; the odd-order pole ($k\equiv2$) carries the complex-multiplication period $\Gamma(1/4)$
of the elliptic curve at $i$. Thus $\Delta/E_6\in F_6$ --- a form usually set aside as merely meromorphic ---
has a genuinely transcendental $L$-value, $\Gamma(1/4)^8/(36864\,\pi^6)$.

\begin{remark}[Eichler--Shimura relations for meromorphic forms]\label{rem:ES-mero}
Coefficient by coefficient, $r_f|(1+S)=0$ is the functional equation
$L^*(f,s)=i^kL^*(f,k-s)$; the substantive further condition on a period polynomial is
$r_f|(1+U+U^2)=0$. For $f\in F_k$ both are properties of the homotopy class: by
Corollary~\ref{cor:wall} the period polynomial shifts by residue polynomials under
wall-crossing, and whether a given class satisfies either relation is arithmetic of those
shifts. In a class satisfying both, $r_f$ is a genuine Eichler--Shimura period
polynomial, and such a class would anchor a canonical $L^*(f,s)$ for meromorphic $f$; we
take this question up elsewhere.
\end{remark}
