\begin{abstract}
In this note, we assign to every meromorphic modular form $f$ of weight $k$ on $\Gamma = \SLZ$ a finite L-integral, which we denote $f \mapsto L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))$; here $\tau_0$ is a base-point in the upper half-plane, and $\gamma_S(\tau_0): -\tfrac{1}{\tau_0} \to \tau_0$ and $\gamma_T(\tau_0): \tau_0 -1 \to \tau_0$ denote integration contours. These L-integrals are finite for all base-points and contours. For the special case of weakly holomorphic modular forms, i.e. where $f$ is either bounded at the cusp or has poles exclusively at the cusp, this L-integral is uniquely defined, i.e. it is fully independent of $\tau_0$ and is fully independent of $\gamma_S(\tau_0)$ and $\gamma_T(\tau_0)$. We prove that this finite L-integral, as we define it, exactly matches the L-integral of holomorphic modular forms, and, separately, that it equals the non-holomorphically regularized L-integrals of~\cite{BFK2011}. When $f$ has poles away from the cusp, i.e.\ inside the fundamental domain, $L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))$ is unique and constant for paths within the same homotopy class; related $L^*$-integrals of $f$, evaluated at $s$, which depend on paths outside the same homotopy class differ by well-defined wall-crossing residues. We prove that they match the L-function construction of~\cite{1806} for meromorphic modular forms regular at the cusps. Finally, we show that a slight modification of this linear functional in the Fourier coefficients of $f$ yields its associated period polynomial when $k$ is such that $\dim(S_k) = 1$. Our period polynomials exactly reproduce the numerical periods reported in~\cite{Brown2017}. All results are both proven analytically and supported, where relevant, by our associated python scripts~\cite{VerifyScripts}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and main results}\label{sec:intro}

%DAM: Start

In this paper, we introduce a general framework that defines L-functions for generic modular forms on the full group $\SLZ$, and extracts their periods for the explicit case when the holomorphic cusp forms are one-dimensional, i.e. when $\dim(S_k) = 1$. Classically, L-functions and periods are both well-defined and well-studied for holomorphic modular forms. However, there is a growing body of work exploring L-functions~\cite{BFK2011} and periods~\cite{Brown2017} for weakly holomorphic modular forms. Similarly, there are natural physical motivations to consider L-functions for properly meromorphic modular forms, which have poles \emph{within} the fundamental domain~\cite{1806}.

In these works, and specifically in~\cite{BFK2011, 1806}, and throughout physics, divergences due to singularities in quantities of interest necessitate the introduction of regulators in order to define finite quantities. Regulators, though, often break symmetries, and care is needed to check that the regulator has not injected undue violence to the system. Of relevance here is that~\cite{BFK2011} introduced a non-holomorphic regulator to deal with the singularities at either end of the classical Mellin contour, $\int_0^\infty$, sufficient to regulate divergences at the cusps, but which violates holomorphicity in the deformed contour-integral, ruining path-dependence. Simultaneously, \cite{1806} concerned itself with L-functions for modular forms which diverge within the interior of the fundamental domain. Here, holomorphic dependence on $\tau$ is paramount, which puts this treatment at odds with the regulator in~\cite{BFK2011}.

In this short note, we introduce manifestly finite contour integral functionals to define L-functions for generic meromorphic modular forms that are of sufficient generality to handle any combination of poles at cusps and poles within the fundamental domain. Our construction is motivated by, and makes precise contact with, work which extracts period-coefficients from weak cusp forms~\cite{Brown2017}. Our integrals are manifestly finite throughout, and reproduce all results in the literature, unifying the quasi-period results and the L-function extensions within one concrete, natural framework.

\paragraph{What we construct.}

Let $\Gamma = \SLZ$, let $f$ be a meromorphic modular form of even
weight $k \in \ZZ$ on $\Gamma$, and let $\tau_0 \in \HH$ be a basepoint
with the property that the pair of line segments
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contour-pair}
  S\text{-segment}\colon\; [-1/\tau_0,\, \tau_0],
  \qquad
  T\text{-segment}\colon\; [\tau_0 - 1,\, \tau_0]
\end{equation}
in $\HH$ avoids the $\Gamma$-orbit of the poles of $f$ in $\HH$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lambda-DR-def}
  %\Lambda^*(f, s; \tau_0)
  L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))
  \;:=\; i^{-s}\!\left[
    \int_{\gamma_S(\tau_0)}%_{-1/\tau_0}^{\tau_0}
    \! f(\tau)\,\tau^{s-1}\,d\tau
    \;+\;
    \int_{\gamma_T(\tau_0)}%_{\tau_0 - 1}^{\tau_0}\! 
    f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \right],
\end{equation}
where the integration contour $\gamma_S(\tau_0)$ runs between $\gamma_S(\tau_0, 0) = -1/\tau_0$ and $\gamma_S(\tau_0, 1) = \tau_0$, the integration contour $\gamma_T(\tau_0)$ runs between $\gamma_T(\tau_0, 0) = \tau_0-1$ and $\gamma_T(\tau_0, 1) = \tau_0$ and the $T$-kernel
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tildekT-def}
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)
  \;:=\; \zeta(1 - s,\, \tau + 1)
  \;-\; e^{i\pi(s-1)}\,\zeta(s - (k - 1),\, \tau + 1)
\end{equation}
is built from two Hurwitz-zeta functions. When the contour avoids poles in $f$,
both contour integrals contributing to $L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))$
in~\eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-def}
%, and the prefactor $i^{-s}$
%matches the standard $L^*(\Delta, s) = (2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)L(\Delta, s)$
%normalisation on the holomorphic-cusp-form subspace.  Both contour
%integrals in \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-def} 
are finite for every basepoint
$\tau_0 \in \HH$ admissible for $f$ and every $s \in \CC$; no regulator,
asymptotic-subtraction argument, or analytic-continuation step is
required.  Further, as we will show, $L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))$ 
is invariant under both $\tau_0$-variations and $\gamma_S(\tau_0), 
\gamma_T(\tau_0)$-variations, as long as neither $\gamma_S(\tau_0)$ nor  
$\gamma_T(\tau_0)$ cross any pole location within the fundamental domain. 
%The construction makes sense for every even integer $k$, of
%either sign, and is linear in $f$.

We will show that $L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))$ is the 
classical L-function for $f \in M_k(\SLZ)$. Further, because of its 
$\tau_0$-independence and finiteness for $f \in M_k^!(\SLZ)$, and because 
we reproduce the analytic formulae in~\cite{BFK2011}, we unambiguously 
show that their candidate L-function is the canonical L-function one would associate 
with any weak modular form, $f \in M_k^!$.

\paragraph{Three theorems.}
The functional $L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))$ %\Lambda^*(f, s; \tau_0)
obeys three properties
that together identify it as the canonical finite-contour $L$-function
of $f$:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.5em]\itemsep0.25em
\item \emph{(Theorem~\ref{thm:periods})}\,
  When $\dim S_k = 1$, the integer-$s$ specialisations of
  \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-def} reduce to a polynomial-kernel form that
  produces the periods $\omega^\pm(f)$ and quasi-periods $\eta^\pm(f)$
  as an explicit linear functional in the Fourier coefficients of $f$.
  For $f = \Delta$ at $k = 12$ this recovers the Brown values of
  $\omega^\pm(\Delta)$; for $f = \Dhat$ this gives the quasi-periods
  $\eta^\pm(\Dhat)$ from a finite-endpoint contour pair, with no
  divergence at the cusp.
\item \emph{(Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq})}\,
  For $f \in M^!_k$,
  $L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))$
  is $\tau_0$-independent on $\HH$ and
  produces the completed $L$-function $L^*(f, s)$ at every $s
  \in \CC$ as a single closed-form incomplete-gamma sum, agreeing
  term by term in the Fourier expansion with \cite{BFK2011}.  Our %At 
  proof in fact exploits the simplicity at the elliptic fixed-point basepoint 
  $\tau_0 = i$, where the $S$-segment collapses
  to a point and \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-def} reduces to a single integral
  on the compact contour $[i - 1, i]$.
\item \emph{(Theorem~\ref{thm:wall-crossing})}\,
  For general meromorphic $f$, $L^*(f, s; \gamma_S, \gamma_T)$ depends
  on $(\gamma_S, \gamma_T)$ only through their homotopy class in
  $\HH \setminus \Gamma\cdot f^{-1}(\infty)$, with explicit
  wall-crossing residue jumps when the homotopy class changes by
  crossing a pole.  Restricted to forms regular at the cusp, this
  reproduces the deformed-Mellin $L$-function $L_f^*(s)_{\rm int}$
  (Corollary~\ref{cor:1806-coincide}; see \cite{1806}).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{theorem}[Periods and quasi-periods via the integer-$s$ specialisation of $L^*$, $\dim S_k \le 1$]\label{thm:periods}
Let $k \in 2\ZZ$ with $\dim S_k \le 1$, and let $f \in S_k^!$.  For
the substantive cases $k \in \{12, 16, 18, 20, 22, 26\}$ where
$\dim S_k = 1$, the period and quasi-period coefficients
$\omega^\pm(f)$ are recovered as the integer-$s = j$ specialisation of
the $L$-integral functional of Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq} via
\begin{equation}\label{eq:periods-statement}
  \omega^\pm(f) \;=\; \frac{(2\pi i)^{k - 1}\,(-1)^{j - 1}\,\binom{k - 2}{j - 1}}{c_S^{\,j - 1}}\!\left[
    \int_{\gamma_S(\tau_0)}\!\! f(\tau)\,\tau^{j - 1}\,d\tau
    \;+\;
    \int_{\gamma_T(\tau_0)}\!\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, j)\,d\tau
  \right]
\end{equation}
for every $f \in S_k^!$, every $\tau_0 \in \HH$, and every interior
$j \in \{2, \ldots, k - 2\}$, with parity $\pm$ determined by
$j - 1 \bmod 2$.  Here
$c_S^{\,j - 1} := [p^\pm_f]_{X^{n - (j - 1)} Y^{j - 1}} \in \QQ$ is the
Brown cuspidal-basis coefficient at the matched monomial
(\cite{Brown2017,KZ1984}), and $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ is the
Hurwitz-zeta $T$-kernel of \eqref{eq:tildekT-def}, whose integer-$s = j$
specialisation collapses via $\zeta(-m, a) = -B_{m + 1}(a)/(m + 1)$
($m \ge 0$) to the $\QQ$-rational polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kS-form}
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, j) \;=\; -\,\frac{B_j(\tau + 1)}{j} \;+\; (-1)^{j - 1}\,\frac{B_{k - j}(\tau + 1)}{k - j}
  \;\in\; \QQ[\tau]
\end{equation}
of degree at most $k - 2$.  The right side of
\eqref{eq:periods-statement} is independent of $\tau_0$ and of the
choice of interior $j$ within its parity sector (lockstep).  The
$S$- and $T$-segment kernels are the bare Mellin and bare Hurwitz
kernels of $L^*(f, s)$: at integer $s = j$, identity
\eqref{eq:periods-statement} is the rescaled integer-$s$ shadow of the
$L$-function functional of Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq}, with all
combinatorial structure absorbed into the prefactor outside the
two-segment bracket.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Higher-dimensional $S_k$.}
For $k \ge 24$ with $\dim S_k \ge 2$, the Brown cuspidal-basis
coefficient $c_S^{\,j - 1}$ in \eqref{eq:periods-statement} becomes
matrix-valued: each $j \in \{2,
\ldots, k-2\}$ gives a $\dim S_k$-component linear functional on
$\{c_f(n)\}$ rather than a scalar.  The structural argument behind
Theorem~\ref{thm:periods} --- coboundary inversion on $V_{k-2}$ via
$\delta_T^{-1}$ in closed Bernoulli-operator form --- generalises
without conceptual change, but the explicit kernel tables grow with
$\dim S_k$, and the lockstep / basepoint-independence statement
becomes a matrix identity.  Working out the explicit matrix-valued
kernels for $k = 24, 28, 30, \ldots$ and connecting them to the
existing Eichler--Shimura matrix-period basis literature is the
natural generalisation of Theorem~\ref{thm:periods}, and we leave it
to a follow-up.  Theorems~\ref{thm:bfk-eq} and~\ref{thm:wall-crossing}
are unaffected and apply at every even $k$.

\begin{theorem}[$L$-function for weakly holomorphic forms]\label{thm:bfk-eq}
Let $k \in 2\ZZ$ and $f \in M^!_k$.  With $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$
the Hurwitz-zeta kernel of \eqref{eq:tildekT-def}, for every $\tau_0
\in \HH$ and every $s \in \CC$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lambda-DR-general}
 L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))
  \;:=\; i^{-s}\!\left[
    \int_{\gamma_S(\tau_0)}\! f(\tau)\,\tau^{s-1}\,d\tau
    \;+\;
    \int_{\gamma_T(\tau_0)}\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \right]
\end{equation}
is finite and independent of both $\tau_0 \in \HH$ and of the
admissible contour choices $\gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0)$.  We
write $L^*(f, s)$ for the common value.

Specialising to the elliptic fixed point $\tau_0 = i$ of the modular
involution $S\colon \tau \mapsto -1/\tau$, the $S$-segment $[-1/i, i]$
collapses to the single point $i$, the $S$-integral in
\eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general} vanishes, and $L^*$ reduces to a single
integral on the compact contour $[i - 1, i] \subset \HH$, with
closed-form sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bfk-eq-statement}
  L^*(f, s)
  \;=\; i^{-s}\!\int_{i - 1}^{i}\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \;=\; -c_f(0)\!\left(\frac{1}{s} + \frac{i^k}{k - s}\right)
  + \sum_{n \ne 0}\! c_f(n)\!\left[
    \frac{\Gamma(s,\, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s}
    + i^k\,\frac{\Gamma(k - s,\, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}
  \right].
\end{equation}
The right side of \eqref{eq:bfk-eq-statement} is the completed
$L$-function $L^*(f, s)$ of $f$: on holomorphic cusp forms it reduces
to the classical Hecke--Weil Mellin transform, and on $M^!_k$ its
equality to the formula of~\cite{BFK2011} \emph{proves} that the
incomplete-gamma construction --- previously obtained via
non-holomorphic regulator deformations --- is the unique canonical
$L$-function for every weakly holomorphic modular form.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Classical Mellin recovery and canonical uniqueness.}
For $f \in S_k$ a holomorphic cusp form, taking the cusp-limit
$\tau_0 \to i\infty$ in \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general} collapses the
$S$-segment onto the classical Mellin contour $[0, i\infty]$ while
the $T$-segment vanishes exponentially (since $f(\tau) =
O(e^{-2\pi\im\tau})$ on the segment as $\im\tau \to \infty$):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Mellin-recovery}
  \lim_{\tau_0 \to i\infty}\!
  L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))
  \;=\; \int_0^\infty\! t^{s - 1}\,f(it)\,dt,
\end{equation}
the classical completed $L$-integral of $f$.  For $f \in M_k$ with
$c_f(0) \ne 0$ the same limit holds after a standard constant-term
subtraction (see \cite[Lemma~2.1]{1806} for the explicit polynomial
correction).

For $f \in M^!_k$ with cusp poles the cusp-limit is genuinely
pathological: $f(\tau) \to \infty$ as $\im\tau \to \infty$, so the
$S$-segment integral diverges along the imaginary axis, and the
$T$-segment integral $\int_{\tau_0 - 1}^{\tau_0}\! f(\tau)\,
\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau$ acquires a matching divergence of
the opposite sign as $\im\tau_0 \to \infty$.

The two divergences cancel at every finite $\tau_0$, so $L^*(f, s)$
is finite and well defined throughout $\HH$, but no naive Mellin

$L^*(f, s)$ on $M^!_k$ by a non-holomorphic regulator at the cusps;
Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq} shows that this regulator and the
finite-contour functional \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general} compute the
same object, term by term in the Fourier expansion.  In particular,
$L^*(f, s)$ is independent of any choice of regularisation: it is
the value of the finite-contour functional at any admissible

\begin{theorem}[$L$-function for meromorphic modular forms]\label{thm:wall-crossing}
Let $k \in 2\ZZ$ and let $f$ be a meromorphic modular form of weight
$k$ on $\Gamma$.  Write $\HH_f := \HH \setminus \Gamma\cdot
f^{-1}(\infty)$ for the upper half-plane with the $\Gamma$-orbit of
the interior poles of $f$ removed, and call the pair
$(\gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))$ \emph{admissible} if
$\tau_0 \in \HH_f$ and both contours lie in $\HH_f$, with
$\gamma_S(\tau_0)$ running from $S^{-1}\tau_0 = -1/\tau_0$ to $\tau_0$
and $\gamma_T(\tau_0)$ from $T^{-1}\tau_0 = \tau_0 - 1$ to $\tau_0$.
Define $L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))$ by
\eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-def} with the Hurwitz-zeta kernel
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ of \eqref{eq:tildekT-def}.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]\itemsep0.25em
\item Both integrals in \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-def} are finite for every
  admissible $(\gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))$ and every $s \in
  \CC$.
\item $L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))$ depends on the
  admissible pair only through its homotopy class in $\HH_f$: any
  continuous one-parameter family of admissible pairs --- including
  variations of the anchoring basepoint $\tau_0$ --- leaves the value
  unchanged.
\item If two admissible configurations $(\gamma_S(\tau_0),
  \gamma_T(\tau_0))$ and $(\gamma_S(\tau_0'), \gamma_T(\tau_0'))$ lie
  in homotopy classes related by a single crossing of an interior
  pole $\tau_p \in \HH$ of $f$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wall-crossing-statement}
  L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))
  \;-\;
  L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0'), \gamma_T(\tau_0'))
  \;=\; \mathcal{R}_{\tau_p}(f, s),
\end{equation}
where for a simple pole $\Res(f, \tau_p)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residue-formula}
  \mathcal{R}_{\tau_p}(f, s)
  \;=\; -2\pi i\,i^{-s}\,\Res(f, \tau_p)\!
  \left[\sigma^S_{\tau_p}\,\tau_p^{s - 1}
  \;+\; \sigma^T_{\tau_p}\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau_p, s)\right],
\end{equation}
with $\sigma^S_{\tau_p}, \sigma^T_{\tau_p} \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$ the
winding-number contributions of $\gamma_S$ and $\gamma_T$ across
$\tau_p$ (the generic case has one nonzero and the other zero,
according to which contour crossed).  Higher-order poles contribute
via the corresponding derivatives in $\tau_p$ of
\eqref{eq:residue-formula}; the closed form is given in
\S\ref{sec:wall-crossing}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[$L^*$ on regular-at-cusps meromorphic forms]\label{cor:1806-coincide}
Let $f \in F_k$ be a meromorphic modular form of weight $k$ that is
regular at the cusp.  Let $L_f^*(s)_{\rm int,\,\gamma}$ denote the
deformed-Mellin $L$-function of $f$ along a contour $\gamma$ from
$0$ to $i\infty$ lying in $\HH_f$ (see \cite[\S2]{1806}).  Then for
every admissible $(\gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))$ whose
homotopy class in $\HH_f$ is compatible with $\gamma$ (in the sense
that the contour $\gamma_S(\tau_0)$ together with the cusp-limit
piece $\lim_{\tau_0 \to i\infty}\gamma_S(\tau_0) \to [0, i\infty]$
can be deformed to $\gamma$ in $\HH_f$),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1806-coincidence}
  L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))
  \;=\; L_f^*(s)_{\rm int,\,\gamma}
  \qquad
  \text{for every } s \in \CC.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{The structural picture.}
The contour pair \eqref{eq:contour-pair} has two natural one-parameter
specialisations of the basepoint, each exploiting a fixed point of the
modular curve $Y(1)$: the cusp specialisation $\tau_0 \to i\infty$
sends the $S$-segment to the classical Mellin contour $[0, i\infty]$
and the $T$-segment to $0$ exponentially; the elliptic specialisation
$\tau_0 = i$ kills the $S$-segment identically and leaves
$L^*$ as a single integral on $[i - 1, i]$.  The cusp
specialisation, applied at finite $\tau_0 = i\Lambda$, gives the
$\Lambda$-truncated Mellin contour of $L_f^*(s)_{\rm int}$
(see \cite{1806}) as the $S$-segment alone, with the $T$-segment
serving as the missing ingredient that produces the cocycle and
renders the $\Lambda \to \infty$ limit unnecessary.  The Hurwitz-zeta kernel
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ in \eqref{eq:tildekT-def} is the closed
form of the $T$-segment kernel: it is the unique $V_{n}$-analog,
valid at every $s \in \CC$, of the polynomial coboundary inversion
of \cite{DR2017} (which itself recovers as the integer-$s$
specialisation, by Theorem~\ref{thm:periods}).

\section{The period-defining cocycle and its moment expansion}\label{sec:cocycle}

\paragraph{The polynomial coefficient system.}
Let $V_n := \CC[X, Y]_n$ be the space of complex homogeneous polynomials
in two auxiliary variables $X, Y$ of degree $n$, equipped with the
right $\Gamma$-action
\[
  (P|_\gamma)(X, Y) \;=\; P\bigl(aX + bY,\, cX + dY\bigr)
  \qquad
  \text{for } \gamma = \begin{pmatrix}a & b\\ c & d\end{pmatrix} \in \Gamma.
\]
For a meromorphic modular form $f$ of weight $k$, write $n := k - 2$
throughout and form the $V_n$-valued $1$-form on $\HH$
\[
  \omega_f(\tau) \;:=\; f(\tau)\,(X - \tau Y)^n\,d\tau.
\]
Modularity of $f$ implies that $\omega_f$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant in
the standard sense \cite{KZ1984}: under $\tau \mapsto \gamma^{-1}\tau$,
the weight-$k$ transformation of $f$ cancels the weight-$(-n)$ slash
on $(X - \tau Y)^n$, so the $V_n$-valued $1$-form $\omega_f$ is
invariant under the simultaneous action of $\Gamma$ on $\tau$ and on
the auxiliary variables $(X, Y)$.  Equivalently, $\omega_f$ is a
well-defined $V_n$-valued $1$-form on the modular curve $Y(1) =
\Gamma\backslash\HH$ (modulo the $\Gamma$-action on $V_n$).

\paragraph{The cocycle.}
The two-segment cocycle construction used in the present note
originates in personal correspondence from F.\ Brown to the author
\cite{Brown2019Email}, which describes the procedure of integrating a
weakly holomorphic modular form against the polynomial coefficient
system $(X - \tau Y)^n$ along contours from $\gamma^{-1}\tau_0$ to
$\tau_0$ for the generators $\gamma \in \{S, T\}$ of $\Gamma$, and
extracting periods and quasi-periods from the resulting $1$-cocycle
modulo coboundaries.  Concretely:
fix a basepoint $\tau_0 \in \HH$ not on the $\Gamma$-orbit of any pole
of $f$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cocycle-def}
  C^f_\gamma(\tau_0)
  \;:=\; (2\pi i)^{n+1}\!\int_{\gamma^{-1}\tau_0}^{\tau_0}\!\omega_f
  \;=\; (2\pi i)^{n+1}\!\int_{\gamma^{-1}\tau_0}^{\tau_0}\!
  f(\tau)\,(X - \tau Y)^n\,d\tau
  \quad \in \quad V_n \otimes \CC,
\end{equation}
the contour being any path from $\gamma^{-1}\tau_0$ to $\tau_0$ in
$\HH$ avoiding the $\Gamma$-orbit of the poles of $f$.  The integral
is independent of the choice of path within the same homotopy class in
$\HH \setminus \Gamma\!\cdot\! f^{-1}(\infty)$.

\begin{lemma}[Cocycle property]\label{lem:cocycle}
For all $g, h \in \Gamma$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cocycle-relation}
  C^f_{gh}(\tau_0) \;=\; C^f_g(\tau_0)|_h \;+\; C^f_h(\tau_0).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the map $\gamma \mapsto C^f_\gamma(\tau_0)$ is a
$1$-cocycle of $\Gamma$ with coefficients in $V_n \otimes \CC$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Path-additivity gives
\[
  \int_{(gh)^{-1}\tau_0}^{\tau_0}
  \;=\; \int_{h^{-1}g^{-1}\tau_0}^{h^{-1}\tau_0}
  \;+\; \int_{h^{-1}\tau_0}^{\tau_0}.
\]
In the first integral substitute $\tau = h^{-1}\sigma$, so $\sigma$
runs from $g^{-1}\tau_0$ to $\tau_0$.  The $\Gamma$-equivariance of
$\omega_f$ converts the integrand to $\omega_f(\sigma)|_h$, yielding
the first summand of \eqref{eq:cocycle-relation}.  The second integral
gives the second summand directly.
\end{proof}

A cocycle on $\Gamma = \SLZ$ is determined by its values on the two
generators
\[
  S \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  T \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix},
\]
subject to the relations $S^2 = (ST)^3 = -I$ (which act trivially on
$V_n$ for $n$ even).  We write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CS-CT-def}
  C^f_S(\tau_0) \;=\; (2\pi i)^{n+1}\!\int_{-1/\tau_0}^{\tau_0}\!\omega_f,
  \qquad
  C^f_T(\tau_0) \;=\; (2\pi i)^{n+1}\!\int_{\tau_0 - 1}^{\tau_0}\!\omega_f,
\end{equation}
since $S^{-1}\tau_0 = -1/\tau_0$ and $T^{-1}\tau_0 = \tau_0 - 1$.
These are the two integrals over the contour pair
\eqref{eq:contour-pair}.

\paragraph{Moment expansion.}
Expanding $(X - \tau Y)^n = \sum_{j=0}^{n} \binom{n}{j}(-\tau)^j
X^{n-j} Y^j$ and pulling the polynomial out of the integral defines
the moment integrals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-def}
  I_S^j(f, \tau_0) \;:=\; \int_{-1/\tau_0}^{\tau_0}\! f(\tau)\,\tau^j\,d\tau,
  \qquad
  I_T^j(f, \tau_0) \;:=\; \int_{\tau_0 - 1}^{\tau_0}\! f(\tau)\,\tau^j\,d\tau,
\end{equation}
for $j = 0, 1, \ldots, n$, in terms of which
\[
  C^f_S(\tau_0)
  \;=\; (2\pi i)^{n+1}\!\sum_{j=0}^{n}\!\binom{n}{j}(-1)^j\,
  I_S^j(f, \tau_0)\,X^{n-j}Y^j,
\]
and similarly for $C^f_T(\tau_0)$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:periods} extracts
$\omega^\pm(f)$ from particular linear combinations of these moments.

\paragraph{Basepoint dependence.}
Varying $\tau_0$ changes $C^f$ by a coboundary, provided $f$ has no
interior poles.  Define a primitive $F(\tau)$ of $\omega_f$ on a
simply connected open subset of $\HH$ avoiding $\Gamma\cdot f^{-1}
(\infty)$.  Then for any two basepoints $\tau_0, \tau_0'$ in the same
connected component, path-additivity gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bp-cocycle-coboundary}
  C^f_\gamma(\tau_0') - C^f_\gamma(\tau_0)
  \;=\; \bigl(P|_\gamma - P\bigr)
  \quad \text{with}\quad
  P \;=\; (2\pi i)^{n+1}\bigl(F(\tau_0') - F(\tau_0)\bigr) \in V_n\otimes\CC.
\end{equation}
This is the coboundary $\delta P(\gamma) := P|_\gamma - P$.  When $f$
has interior poles, dragging $\tau_0$ across a pole picks up a Cauchy
residue contribution beyond \eqref{eq:bp-cocycle-coboundary}; this
wall-crossing structure is the content of Theorem~\ref{thm:wall-crossing}
in \S\ref{sec:wall-crossing}.

\paragraph{Cohomology and periods.}
The cohomology class $[C^f] \in H^1(\Gamma; V_n)$ is independent of
$\tau_0$ (on the component) and independent of the choice of
$\delta P$ representative.  The classical Eichler--Shimura theorem
\cite{KZ1984} identifies the cuspidal subspace $H^1_{\rm cusp}(\Gamma;
V_n)$ with $S_k \oplus \overline{S_k}$, and the linear coordinates of
$[C^f]$ in this cuspidal subspace are the periods and quasi-periods
$\omega^\pm(f), \eta^\pm(f)$ \cite{Brown2017}.

\section{Period extraction: proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:periods}}\label{sec:periods}

Set $n := k - 2$ throughout.  The argument is $k$-uniform on the
$\dim S_k = 1$ family $k \in \{12, 16, 18, 20, 22, 26\}$: every step
below --- the cocycle structure of \S\ref{sec:cocycle}, the
$\delta_T$-solvability of Lemma~\ref{lem:solvability-cuspidality},
the Bernoulli closed-form inversion of
Proposition~\ref{prop:Bernoulli-inv}, the Hurwitz-zeta kernel
identification of Proposition~\ref{prop:kernels}, and the lockstep
of Corollary~\ref{cor:lockstep} --- holds for every even $k$ with
the Brown cuspidal-basis coefficient $c_S^{\,j - 1}$ replaced by the
relevant weight's basis-polynomial coefficient.  The lower even weights
$k \in \{2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 14\}$ in the $\dim S_k \le 1$ family have
$S_k = 0$, so the theorem is vacuous there.  Numerical verification
at $k = 12$ for the canonical pair $f \in \{\Delta, \Dhat\}$, where
Brown's published values $\omega^\pm(\Delta), \eta^\pm(\Dhat)$
\cite{Brown2017} provide a $50$-digit benchmark, is carried out in the
companion script \texttt{verify\_periods.py}~\cite{VerifyScripts};
analogous numerical verification at $k \in \{16, 18, 20, 22, 26\}$
requires only the Brown basis coefficients $c_S^{\,j - 1}$ at those
weights and is structurally identical.

% --- Step 3 anchor: solvability $\iff$ cuspidality ---
\begin{lemma}[Solvability of $\delta_T P = C^f_T$ is cuspidality of $f$]\label{lem:solvability-cuspidality}
Define the linear endomorphism $\delta_T \colon V_n \to V_n$ by
$\delta_T(P) := P|_T - P$, where $T \in \Gamma$ acts on $V_n$ as
$(X, Y) \mapsto (X + Y, Y)$.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]\itemsep0.25em
\item $\ker(\delta_T) = \CC \cdot Y^n$.
\item $\operatorname{im}(\delta_T) = \{Q \in V_n : [Q]_{X^n} = 0\}$.
\item For any meromorphic modular form $f$ of weight $k = n + 2$ and any
  $\tau_0 \in \HH$ admissible for $f$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CfT-Xn-coeff}
  [C^f_T(\tau_0)]_{X^n} \;=\; (2\pi i)^{n+1} \cdot c_f(0),
\end{equation}
where $c_f(0)$ is the constant Fourier coefficient of $f$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, the equation $\delta_T P = C^f_T(\tau_0)$ is solvable for
$P \in V_n$ if and only if $c_f(0) = 0$, i.e.\ if and only if $f$ is a
(weak) cusp form.  When solvable, $P$ is unique modulo $\CC \cdot Y^n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) $T$ acts on $V_n$ as the translation $X \mapsto X + Y$, fixing only
powers of $Y$.  Hence $T$-invariants in $V_n$, equivalently
$\ker(\delta_T)$, are exactly $\CC \cdot Y^n$.

(b) Writing $P = \sum_{j = 0}^{n} p_j X^{n - j} Y^j$ and expanding
$(X + Y)^{n - j}$ binomially,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta-T-matrix}
  [\delta_T P]_{X^{n - m} Y^m}
  \;=\; \sum_{j = 0}^{m - 1}\!\binom{n - j}{m - j}\,p_j
  \qquad (m = 0, 1, \ldots, n).
\end{equation}
This is strictly lower-triangular in the ordered basis
$(X^n, X^{n - 1}Y, \ldots, Y^n)$: the $m = 0$ row is the empty sum,
identically zero, and the off-diagonal entries
$\binom{n - j}{m - j}$ for $j < m$ are positive.  The $m = 0$ row
forces $[\delta_T P]_{X^n} = 0$ for every $P$, while rows
$m = 1, \ldots, n$ determine $p_0, \ldots, p_{n - 1}$ uniquely from any
prescribed right-hand side, with $p_n$ unconstrained.  Hence
$\operatorname{im}(\delta_T) = \{Q \in V_n : [Q]_{X^n} = 0\}$, and the
solution $P$ is unique modulo $\CC \cdot Y^n$.

(c) The moment expansion \eqref{eq:CS-CT-def} gives
$[C^f_T(\tau_0)]_{X^n} = (2\pi i)^{n+1}\,I^0_T(f, \tau_0)$ where
$I^0_T(f, \tau_0) = \int_{\tau_0 - 1}^{\tau_0}\!f(\tau)\,d\tau$.
Substituting the Fourier expansion $f(\tau) = \sum_n c_f(n)\,
e^{2\pi i n \tau}$, absolutely convergent on the compact $T$-segment,
and integrating term by term,
\[
  \int_{\tau_0 - 1}^{\tau_0}\!e^{2\pi i n \tau}\,d\tau
  \;=\; \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } n = 0,\\
                       0 & \text{if } n \ne 0,
        \end{cases}
\]
by $T$-periodicity over a unit segment.  Hence $I^0_T(f, \tau_0) = c_f(0)$
for every admissible $\tau_0$, which proves \eqref{eq:CfT-Xn-coeff}.

Combining (b) and (c) gives the solvability statement.
\end{proof}

% --- Step 4 anchor: Bernoulli closed-form inversion of \delta_T ---
\begin{proposition}[Bernoulli closed form for $\delta_T^{-1}$]\label{prop:Bernoulli-inv}
For every $Q \in V_n$ with vanishing $X^n$-coefficient there exists
$P \in V_n$ unique modulo $\CC \cdot Y^n$ with $\delta_T P = Q$.
Writing $Q = \sum_{r = 1}^{n} q_r\,X^{n - r} Y^r$ and
$P = \sum_{m = 0}^{n} p_m\,X^{n - m} Y^m$, the solution is given in
closed form by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bernoulli-inv}
  p_m \;=\; \sum_{r = 1}^{m + 1}\!\frac{B_{m - r + 1}\,(n - r)!}{(m - r + 1)!\,(n - m)!}\,q_r
  \qquad (m = 0, 1, \ldots, n - 1),
\end{equation}
where $B_\ell$ are Bernoulli numbers ($B_0 = 1$, $B_1 = -\tfrac{1}{2}$,
$B_2 = \tfrac{1}{6}$, $B_{2\ell + 1} = 0$ for $\ell \ge 1$); the
coefficient $p_n$ is unconstrained and parametrises
$\ker(\delta_T) = \CC \cdot Y^n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The operator identity $T = e^{Y \partial_X}$ on $V_n$ (since $T$ acts as
the translation $X \mapsto X + Y$ by $Y$) gives
$\delta_T = e^{Y \partial_X} - 1$.  The Bernoulli generating function
$z/(e^z - 1) = \sum_{k \ge 0}(B_k/k!)\,z^k$ supplies the closed-form
inverse
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta-T-inverse-operator}
  \delta_T^{-1}
  \;=\; (Y \partial_X)^{-1}\,\frac{Y \partial_X}{e^{Y \partial_X} - 1}
  \;=\; \sum_{k \ge 0}\!\frac{B_k}{k!}\,(Y \partial_X)^{k - 1},
\end{equation}
in which the $k = 0$ summand is the formal antiderivative
$(Y \partial_X)^{-1}$, acting on monomials by
$(Y \partial_X)^{-1}(X^a Y^b) = X^{a + 1} Y^{b - 1}/(a + 1)$ (for $b \ge 1$),
and the $k \ge 1$ summands are differential operators that eventually
annihilate any polynomial of bounded $X$-degree.  Applied to
$X^{n - r} Y^r$ for $r \ge 1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta-T-inverse-monomial}
  \delta_T^{-1}\bigl(X^{n - r} Y^r\bigr)
  \;=\; \sum_{k = 0}^{n - r + 1}\!\frac{B_k}{k!}\,
        \frac{(n - r)!}{(n - r - k + 1)!}\,X^{n - r - k + 1}\,Y^{r + k - 1}.
\end{equation}
Summing $Q = \sum_r q_r\,X^{n - r} Y^r$ termwise and reading off the
$X^{n - m} Y^m$ coefficient identifies $k = m - r + 1$ (so $r$ runs over
$\{1, \ldots, m + 1\}$, $k$ over $\{0, \ldots, m\}$), yielding
\eqref{eq:Bernoulli-inv}.  Existence and uniqueness modulo
$\CC \cdot Y^n$ are Lemma~\ref{lem:solvability-cuspidality}(a, b).

The closed form \eqref{eq:Bernoulli-inv} is verified against the
LU-decomposition recursion of \eqref{eq:delta-T-matrix} for
$f \in \{\Delta, \Dhat\}$ in the companion script
\texttt{verify\_periods.py}~\cite{VerifyScripts} to relative residual
$\sim 10^{-40}$ at $50$-digit \texttt{mpmath} precision.
\end{proof}

% --- Step 6 anchor: universal kernels via integer-s specialisation of \widetilde{k}_T ---
\begin{proposition}[Universal kernels: $\tau^{j - 1}$ and $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, j)$]\label{prop:kernels}
Let $f \in S^!_k$ and $\tau_0 \in \HH$.  Let $P \in V_n / \CC Y^n$ be the
unique solution of $\delta_T P = (2\pi i)^{-(n + 1)} C^f_T(\tau_0)$
from Lemma~\ref{lem:solvability-cuspidality} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:Bernoulli-inv}, and define the modified-cocycle
$S$-value
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-def}
  r_f \;:=\; C^f_S(\tau_0) \;-\; (2\pi i)^{n+1}\bigl(P|_S - P\bigr)
  \;\in\; V_n \otimes \CC.
\end{equation}
For each interior $j \in \{2, \ldots, k - 2\}$, the coefficient of the
monomial $X^{n - (j - 1)} Y^{j - 1}$ in $r_f$ admits the integral
representation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-coeff-integral}
  [r_f]_{X^{n - (j - 1)} Y^{j - 1}}
  \;=\; (2\pi i)^{n + 1}\,(-1)^{j - 1}\binom{n}{j - 1}\!
  \left[\,\int_{\gamma_S(\tau_0)}\! f(\tau)\,\tau^{j - 1}\,d\tau
        \;+\; \int_{\gamma_T(\tau_0)}\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, j)\,d\tau\,\right],
\end{equation}
where $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ is the Hurwitz-zeta kernel of
\eqref{eq:tildekT-def} and the integral kernels --- bare Mellin
$\tau^{j - 1}$ on the $S$-segment and bare Hurwitz $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, j)$
on the $T$-segment --- are precisely the integer-$s = j$ specialisations
of the all-$s$ kernels $\tau^{s - 1}$ and $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ of
the $L$-function functional \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general}.  At integer
$s = j \in \{2, \ldots, k - 2\}$, $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, j)$ collapses
to the $\QQ$-rational polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kT-form}
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, j)
  \;=\; -\,\frac{B_j(\tau + 1)}{j} \;+\; (-1)^{j - 1}\,\frac{B_{k - j}(\tau + 1)}{k - j}
  \;\in\; \QQ[\tau],
\end{equation}
of degree $\le k - 2$, via the Hurwitz collapse $\zeta(-m, a) =
-B_{m + 1}(a)/(m + 1)$ for $m \ge 0$ together with $e^{i\pi(j - 1)} =
(-1)^{j - 1}$.

Combined with the Eichler--Shimura / Brown cuspidal-basis decomposition
of $r_f$ (\cite{Brown2017,KZ1984}) --- which sets
$[r_f]_{X^{n - (j - 1)} Y^{j - 1}} = \omega^\pm(f)\,c_S^{\,j - 1}$ with
$c_S^{\,j - 1} := [p^\pm_f]_{X^{n - (j - 1)} Y^{j - 1}}$ Brown's basis
coefficient at the matched monomial --- this proves identity
\eqref{eq:periods-statement} of Theorem~\ref{thm:periods}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{$S$-piece.}  The moment expansion \eqref{eq:CS-CT-def} gives
\[
  C^f_S(\tau_0)
  \;=\; (2\pi i)^{n + 1}\!\sum_{j = 0}^{n}\!\binom{n}{j}(-1)^j\,
  I_S^j(f, \tau_0)\,X^{n - j} Y^j,
\]
so $[C^f_S(\tau_0)]_{X^{n - (j - 1)} Y^{j - 1}} = (2\pi i)^{n + 1}
\binom{n}{j - 1}(-1)^{j - 1}\,I_S^{j - 1}(f, \tau_0)$, with
$I_S^{j - 1} = \int_{\gamma_S(\tau_0)} f(\tau)\,\tau^{j - 1}\,d\tau$;
this contributes the $S$-segment piece of \eqref{eq:rf-coeff-integral}
against the bare Mellin kernel $\tau^{j - 1}$.

\emph{$T$-piece, Bernoulli form.}  $S$ acts on $V_n$ as
$(X, Y) \mapsto (-Y, X)$, so for $P = \sum_l p_l X^{n - l} Y^l$ one has
$[P|_S - P]_{X^{n - (j - 1)} Y^{j - 1}} = (-1)^{j - 1}\,p_{n - j + 1}
- p_{j - 1}$ ($n$ even).  By Proposition~\ref{prop:Bernoulli-inv} each
$p_l$ is an explicit $\QQ$-linear combination of the $T$-moments
$I_T^r$, $r = 1, \ldots, n$, packaging $C^f_T(\tau_0)$; substituting and
collecting on the $T$-integration side yields a $\QQ$-polynomial
$\widehat{k}_T(j, \tau) \in \QQ[\tau]$ of degree $\le n$ such that the
$T$-segment piece of $[r_f]_{X^{n - (j - 1)} Y^{j - 1}}$ reads
$(2\pi i)^{n + 1}(-1)^{j - 1}\binom{n}{j - 1}\!\int_{\gamma_T(\tau_0)}\!
f(\tau)\,[\widehat{k}_T(j, \tau)/\binom{n}{j - 1}(-1)^{j - 1}]\,d\tau$.

\emph{$T$-piece, Hurwitz form.}  The shift-difference equation
\eqref{eq:tildekT-coboundary} characterises $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$
as the unique-modulo-$\CC$-constant kernel whose $T$-integral against
any $f \in S^!_k$ implements the coboundary modification of
Step~2--Step~5; at integer $s = j$, the Hurwitz collapse
$\zeta(-m, a) = -B_{m+1}(a)/(m+1)$ produces
\eqref{eq:kT-form}.  The Bernoulli closed-form $\widehat{k}_T(j, \tau)$
of the preceding paragraph and the Hurwitz collapse
$\binom{n}{j - 1}(-1)^{j - 1}\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, j)$ are two
representatives of the same coboundary-correction polynomial: their
difference lies in the $\CC \cdot Y^n$ Eisenstein direction of the kernel
of $\delta_T$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:solvability-cuspidality}(a)), and
contributes only to the boundary monomials $X^n, Y^n$ of $r_f$, not to
the interior monomial $X^{n - (j - 1)} Y^{j - 1}$ for
$j - 1 \in \{1, \ldots, n - 1\}$.  Hence
$\int_{\gamma_T(\tau_0)} f(\tau)\,[\widehat{k}_T(j, \tau)/\binom{n}{j - 1}(-1)^{j - 1}]\,d\tau
= \int_{\gamma_T(\tau_0)} f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, j)\,d\tau$,
yielding \eqref{eq:rf-coeff-integral} with the bare Hurwitz kernel
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, j)$ inside.

Independence of $f$ and $\tau_0$: every combinatorial coefficient on the
right-hand side of \eqref{eq:rf-coeff-integral}--\eqref{eq:kT-form}
depends only on $n$, $j$, and Bernoulli numbers, not on $f$ or $\tau_0$.

Numerical verification of \eqref{eq:rf-coeff-integral} at $k = 12$,
$f \in \{\Delta, \Dhat\}$, $j = 2$ (parity $-$) reproduces Brown's
$\omega^-(\Delta) = -5\,585\,015.379\,310\,401\,866\ldots\,i$ to relative
residual $\sim 10^{-42}$ at $50$-digit \texttt{mpmath} precision
(\texttt{verify\_periods.py}~\cite{VerifyScripts}); all nine interior
$j \in \{2, \ldots, 10\}$ extractions are in lockstep to comparable
precision.
\end{proof}

% --- Step 7 anchor: lockstep and basepoint independence ---
\begin{corollary}[Lockstep and basepoint independence]\label{cor:lockstep}
The right side of \eqref{eq:periods-statement} is independent of
$\tau_0 \in \HH$ and of the interior index $j \in \{2, \ldots, k - 2\}$
within its parity sector.  At each $k$ with $\dim S_k = 1$, this yields a
$(k - 3)$-fold redundant extraction of $\omega^\pm(f)$ from the Fourier
coefficients of $f$, reflecting the $1$-dimensionality of the cuspidal
direction in $H^1(\Gamma; V_{k - 2})$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\emph{$\tau_0$-independence.}  By \eqref{eq:rf-def}, $r_f$ depends on
$\tau_0$ only through the cohomology class $[C^f] \in H^1(\Gamma;
V_n \otimes \CC)$ and the choice of coboundary representative $P$.  The
class is $\tau_0$-invariant by \eqref{eq:bp-cocycle-coboundary} (valid
for $f$ without interior poles), and the coboundary representative
$P(\tau_0)$ from Proposition~\ref{prop:Bernoulli-inv} is the
unique-modulo-$\CC Y^n$ solution of $\delta_T P = (2\pi i)^{-(n+1)} C^f_T$.
The residual $\CC Y^n$ ambiguity lies in the Eisenstein direction:
$Y^n|_S - Y^n = X^n - Y^n$ is a coboundary that contributes only to
the boundary monomials $X^n, Y^n$ of $r_f$.  Therefore the cuspidal
coordinates of $r_f$ --- the $\omega^\pm(f)$ --- are $\tau_0$-invariant.

\emph{$j$-independence.}  By the Eichler--Shimura decomposition and
Brown's basis (\cite{Brown2017,KZ1984}), $r_f$ projects onto the
$1$-dimensional cuspidal direction as $\omega^+(f)\,p^+_f
+ \omega^-(f)\,p^-_f$ modulo Eisenstein contributions.  Reading off the
coefficient of any interior monomial $X^{n - (j - 1)} Y^{j - 1}$ for
$j \in \{2, \ldots, k - 2\}$ returns $\omega^\pm(f)\,c_S^{\,j - 1}$ with
$c_S^{\,j - 1} := [p^\pm_f]_{X^{n - (j - 1)} Y^{j - 1}}$ the
basis-polynomial coefficient.  The prefactor
$(2\pi i)^{k - 1}(-1)^{j - 1}\binom{k - 2}{j - 1}/c_S^{\,j - 1}$
in \eqref{eq:periods-statement} is exactly the basis-conversion factor
together with the moment-expansion combinatorial structure that
normalises the right side to $\omega^\pm(f)$ independently of $j$.

At $k = 12$ this gives a $9$-fold redundancy ($j \in \{2, \ldots, 10\}$);
at general $k$ with $\dim S_k = 1$, the interior range
$\{2, \ldots, k - 2\}$ has $k - 3$ elements.
\end{proof}

% --- Outer proof of Theorem 1.1: assembles Steps 1-8 ---
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:periods}.]
The proof proceeds in eight steps.  Steps 1--3 motivate the operator
$\delta_T$ as the natural object whose inversion delivers the cuspidal
cocycle representative.  Step 4 (Proposition~\ref{prop:Bernoulli-inv})
performs the inversion in closed form.  Steps 5--7 read $\omega^\pm(f)$
off the resulting period polynomial.  Step 8 connects this integer-$s$
construction to the all-$s$ Hurwitz-zeta extension proved in
Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq}.

\paragraph{Step 1: the canonical cuspidal representative has $C^f_T = 0$.}
Within the cohomology class $[C^f] \in H^1(\Gamma; V_n)$, the
representative with vanishing $T$-component is structurally distinguished.
When $C_T = 0$, the cocycle relations $C_{S^2} = 0$ and $C_{(ST)^3} = 0$
collapse onto $C_S$ alone, and the surviving relations on $C_S$ are
exactly the two Eichler--Shimura period-polynomial relations
$r_f|(1 + S) = 0$ and $r_f|(1 + U + U^2) = 0$ with $U = TS$.  The
$C_T = 0$ representative is therefore the one whose $S$-value lies in
the Eichler--Shimura period-polynomial space, where Brown's basis
$\{p^+_f,\, p^-_f,\, \text{Eisenstein}\}$ is defined and the cuspidal
coordinates are $\omega^\pm(f)$.  Killing $C^f_T$ is the structural
goal of the proof, not a computational convenience.

\paragraph{Step 2: kill $C^f_T$ by a coboundary modification.}
The only freedom inside the cohomology class is to add a coboundary
$\delta P(\gamma) := P|_\gamma - P$ for some $P \in V_n$.  Under this
modification, $C^f_T(\tau_0) \mapsto C^f_T(\tau_0) - (P|_T - P)$.
Killing $C^f_T$ becomes the equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta-T-equation}
  \delta_T P \;:=\; P|_T - P \;=\; C^f_T(\tau_0),
\end{equation}
to be solved for $P \in V_n$.  This identifies the linear endomorphism
$\delta_T \colon V_n \to V_n$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:solvability-cuspidality}
as the precise object whose inversion delivers the desired coboundary
representative.

\paragraph{Step 3: solvability of $\delta_T P = C^f_T$ is exactly cuspidality of $f$.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:solvability-cuspidality} computes $[C^f_T(\tau_0)]_{X^n}
= (2\pi i)^{n+1} c_f(0)$ and shows that $\operatorname{im}(\delta_T) = \{Q
\in V_n : [Q]_{X^n} = 0\}$.  Solvability of \eqref{eq:delta-T-equation}
is therefore equivalent to $c_f(0) = 0$, i.e.\ to $f$ being a (weak)
cusp form.  The cuspidality hypothesis $f \in S^!_k$ is not a separate
input to the construction: it is the solvability condition of the
coboundary equation in Step 2.

\paragraph{Step 4: closed-form Bernoulli inversion of $\delta_T$.}
The operator identity $T = e^{Y \partial_X}$ (since $T$ acts on $V_n$
as the translation $X \mapsto X + Y$ by $Y$) gives $\delta_T = e^{Y
\partial_X} - 1$, and the Bernoulli generating function
$z/(e^z - 1) = \sum_{k \ge 0} B_k z^k/k!$ supplies $\delta_T^{-1}$ as
a finite operator series on $V_n$ (terminating because $\partial_X$
annihilates polynomials of bounded $X$-degree after finitely many
applications).  Proposition~\ref{prop:Bernoulli-inv} records the
explicit closed-form expression for $P \in V_n/\CC Y^n$ in terms of
Bernoulli numbers.  The appearance of Bernoulli numbers is forced by
the operator identity; it is not coincidence but inevitability.

\paragraph{Step 5: the modified cocycle and its $S$-component $r_f$.}
With $P := \delta_T^{-1}\bigl((2\pi i)^{-(n + 1)} C^f_T(\tau_0)\bigr) \in
V_n/\CC Y^n$ from Step 4, the modified cocycle
$C'^f := C^f - (2\pi i)^{n + 1}\delta P$ has $C'^f_T = 0$ by construction,
and its $S$-value \eqref{eq:rf-def} --- which we denote
$r_f := C'^f_S(\tau_0)$ --- is
the Eichler--Shimura period polynomial of $f$.  Brown's
cuspidal-basis decomposition (\cite{Brown2017,KZ1984}) reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Brown-decomp-step5}
  r_f \;=\; \omega^+(f)\,p^+_f \;+\; \omega^-(f)\,p^-_f
       \;+\; (\text{Eisenstein-direction term}),
\end{equation}
identifying $\omega^\pm(f)$ as explicit linear coordinates of $r_f$ in
the $1$-dimensional cuspidal direction.

\paragraph{Step 6: unpacking $r_f$ into bare Mellin and Hurwitz integrals.}
Substituting the moment expansion of $C^f_S(\tau_0)$ from
\eqref{eq:CS-CT-def} and the Bernoulli expression for $P$ from Step 4
into \eqref{eq:rf-def}, and reading off the coefficient of $X^{n - (j - 1)}
Y^{j - 1}$ for each interior $j \in \{2, \ldots, k - 2\}$,
Proposition~\ref{prop:kernels} produces \eqref{eq:periods-statement}
with the bare Mellin kernel $\tau^{j - 1}$ on the $S$-segment and the
bare Hurwitz kernel $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, j)$ on the $T$-segment ---
the integer-$s = j$ specialisations of the $L$-function kernels of
Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq}.  All combinatorial structure
$\bigl((2\pi i)^{k - 1},\,(-1)^{j - 1},\,\binom{k - 2}{j - 1}\bigr)$
and the Brown basis-coefficient $c_S^{\,j - 1}$ sit in the prefactor
outside the bracket.

\paragraph{Step 7: lockstep and basepoint independence.}
Both follow from $r_f$ being a single fixed polynomial in $V_n$ that
lies in the $1$-dimensional cuspidal direction.  Reading off any
interior monomial coefficient of $r_f$ returns a constant multiple of
the same $\omega^\pm(f)$ (parity-selected by $j$), and the prefactor
$(-1)^{j - 1}\binom{k - 2}{j - 1}(2\pi i)^{k - 1}/c_S^{\,j - 1}$ in
\eqref{eq:periods-statement} normalises away the
basis-conversion factor.  Independence in $\tau_0$
follows from $[C^f]$ being a cohomology-class invariant; the residual
$\CC Y^n$ ambiguity in the coboundary representative $P$ contributes
only to the Eisenstein direction.  Corollary~\ref{cor:lockstep} makes
both statements precise; at $k = 12$, the $9$ interior values of $j$
give the $9$-fold redundant extraction.

\paragraph{Step 8: bridge to Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq}.}
The bare Mellin kernel $\tau^{j - 1}$ on the $S$-segment is the
integer-$s = j$ specialisation of $\tau^{s - 1}$, and the bare Hurwitz
kernel $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, j)$ on the $T$-segment is the integer-$s = j$
specialisation of the Hurwitz-zeta kernel
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s) = \zeta(1 - s, \tau + 1) - e^{i\pi(s - 1)}
\,\zeta(s - (k - 1), \tau + 1)$ of \eqref{eq:tildekT-def}, via the
Hurwitz collapse $\zeta(-m, a) = -B_{m + 1}(a)/(m + 1)$ for $m \ge 0$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:periods} is therefore the integer-$s$ avatar of
Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq}; the all-$s$ extension is proved in
\S\ref{sec:hurwitz-extension}.
\end{proof}


\section{The Hurwitz-zeta extension and the $L$-function on $M^!_k$: proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq}}\label{sec:hurwitz-extension}

\subsection{Derivation of the kernel $\widetilde{k}_T$}\label{subsec:kT-derivation}

We seek a kernel $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$, analytic in $\tau \in
\HH$ and meromorphic in $s \in \CC$, such that $L^*(f,
s; \tau_0)$ as defined in \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general} is independent
of $\tau_0$ for every $f \in M^!_k$.  Differentiating
\eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general} in $\tau_0$ and using $\Delta(-1/\tau_0)
= \tau_0^k \Delta(\tau_0)$ (the $S$-modularity of $f$ at the
$S$-segment endpoint $-1/\tau_0$) together with $f(\tau_0 - 1) =
f(\tau_0)$ (the $T$-modularity at $\tau_0 - 1$) gives, after
cancelling the common factor $i^{-s} f(\tau_0)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tildekT-coboundary}
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau_0, s) \;-\; \widetilde{k}_T(\tau_0 - 1, s)
  \;=\; -\tau_0^{s-1} \;+\; e^{i\pi(s-1)}\,\tau_0^{k - 1 - s}.
\end{equation}
The right side picks up the $S$-segment endpoint contribution at
$-1/\tau_0 = e^{i\pi}\tau_0^{-1}/(-1) = e^{i\pi}/\tau_0$ via the
branch identity $(-1/\tau_0)^{s-1} = e^{i\pi(s-1)}\tau_0^{-(s-1)}$
combined with the Jacobian $d(-1/\tau_0)/d\tau_0 = \tau_0^{-2}$ and
the modularity factor $\tau_0^k$, giving $e^{i\pi(s-1)}\tau_0^{k-1-s}$
after simplification.

Equation \eqref{eq:tildekT-coboundary} is the complex-$s$ analog of
the polynomial coboundary equation $\delta_T P = Q$ of
\S\ref{sec:periods}.  The Hurwitz raising identity
\[
  \zeta(\sigma, a + 1) \;-\; \zeta(\sigma, a)
  \;=\; -a^{-\sigma}
\]
inverts the shift operator $\tau \mapsto \tau - 1$ for any complex
$\sigma$, on the analytic function $\zeta(\sigma, \tau + 1)$.
Applying at $(\sigma, a) = (1 - s, \tau_0)$ and $(\sigma, a) =
(s - (k - 1), \tau_0)$, and combining with the sign $e^{i\pi(s-1)}$
gives \eqref{eq:tildekT-def}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:Lambda-DR-tau0-invariant}
For $f \in M^!_k$, $  L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))$
%\Lambda^*(f, s; \tau_0)
defined by
\eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general} is independent of $\tau_0 \in \HH$ for
every $s \in \CC$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Direct $\tau_0$-differentiation, using \eqref{eq:tildekT-coboundary}
to cancel the boundary contributions from the two segments.  Both
integrals in \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general} are finite at every
$\tau_0 \in \HH$ because $f$ has no poles in $\HH$ and the kernels
$\tau^{s-1}$, $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ are analytic in $\tau \in
\HH$ for every fixed $s \in \CC$ (the Hurwitz $\zeta(\sigma, a)$ is
analytic in $a$ on $\mathrm{Re}(a) > 0$).
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Hurwitz-unfolding lemma}\label{subsec:hurwitz-unfold}

\begin{lemma}[Hurwitz unfolding]\label{lem:hurwitz-unfold}
For every positive integer $n$ and every $\nu \in \CC$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hurwitz-unfold}
  \int_i^{i+1}\! e^{2\pi i n \sigma}\,\zeta(\nu, \sigma)\,d\sigma
  \;=\; i^{1 - \nu}\,(2\pi n)^{\nu - 1}\,\Gamma(1 - \nu,\, 2\pi n).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First assume $\mathrm{Re}(\nu) > 1$, where the Hurwitz series $\zeta(\nu,
\sigma) = \sum_{m \ge 0}(\sigma + m)^{-\nu}$ converges uniformly on
the compact segment $[i, i+1]$ \cite[\S25.11]{DLMF}.  Series-integral
exchange and the substitution $u = \sigma + m$ in the $m$-th summand
collapse the sum to a single integral by integer-$n$ periodicity
$e^{2\pi i n m} = 1$:
\[
  \int_i^{i+1}\! e^{2\pi i n \sigma}\,\zeta(\nu, \sigma)\,d\sigma
  \;=\; \sum_{m \ge 0}\!\int_{i + m}^{i + m + 1}\! e^{2\pi i n u}\,u^{-\nu}\,du
  \;=\; \int_i^{i+\infty}\! e^{2\pi i n u}\,u^{-\nu}\,du,
\]
an integral along the horizontal ray $\{i + t : t \ge 0\} \subset \HH$.

Substitute $t = -2\pi i n u$: $u = it/(2\pi n)$, $du = i\,dt/(2\pi n)$,
and $u^{-\nu} = i^{-\nu}(2\pi n)^\nu t^{-\nu}$ on the principal branch
(consistent with $\arg(u) = \pi/2$ at $u = i$ and $\arg(t) = 0$ at
$t = 2\pi n$).  The contour in $t$ runs from $2\pi n$ down to $2\pi n
- i\infty$, in the right half-plane.  Since $e^{-t} t^{-\nu}$ decays
exponentially as $|t| \to \infty$ in $\mathrm{Re}(t) > 0$, Cauchy's theorem
applied to the rectangle $\{2\pi n \le \mathrm{Re}(t) \le R, \; -R \le \im(t)
\le 0\}$ in the limit $R \to \infty$ deforms the vertical contour
$[2\pi n, 2\pi n - i\infty]$ to the real-axis contour $[2\pi n,
+\infty]$:
\[
  \int_{2\pi n}^{2\pi n - i\infty}\! e^{-t}\,t^{-\nu}\,dt
  \;=\; \int_{2\pi n}^{\infty}\! e^{-t}\,t^{-\nu}\,dt
  \;=\; \Gamma(1 - \nu,\,2\pi n)
\]
by the integral definition of the upper incomplete gamma
\cite[\S8.2]{DLMF}.  Collecting prefactors gives
\eqref{eq:hurwitz-unfold} for $\mathrm{Re}(\nu) > 1$.

Both sides of \eqref{eq:hurwitz-unfold} are entire functions of $\nu$:
the upper incomplete gamma $\Gamma(s, x)$ is entire in $s$ for fixed
$x \ne 0$ \cite[\S8.2.7]{DLMF}, and the integral on the left is
entire in $\nu$ because $\zeta(\sigma, \sigma_0)$ is meromorphic in
$\sigma$ with a simple pole only at $\sigma = 1$, whose residue
contribution to $\int_{i-1}^i e^{2\pi i n \tau}d\tau = 0$ for nonzero
integer $n$ (since $e^{2\pi i n (i-1)} = e^{2\pi i n \cdot i}$).
Analytic continuation in $\nu$ extends \eqref{eq:hurwitz-unfold} to
all complex $\nu$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Per-mode evaluation: proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq}}

By Lemma~\ref{lem:Lambda-DR-tau0-invariant}, the value $L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0)) = L^*(f, s)$
is common to all admissible contours and basepoints.  At the
elliptic fixed-point basepoint $\tau_0 = i$, the $S$-segment $[-1/i,
i] = [i, i]$ has zero length and the $S$-integral in
\eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general} vanishes identically, leaving
\[
  L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(i)=0, \gamma_T(i))
  \;=\; i^{-s}\!\int_{i - 1}^{i}\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau.
\]
Expanding $f = \sum_n c_f(n) q^n$ as a $q$-series and swapping sum
and integral (justified by uniform convergence of the $q$-series on
the compact segment $[i - 1, i]$, including any finite number of
polar modes since $|q^{-1}| = e^{2\pi}$ is bounded there) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:per-mode-tau0-i}
  L^*(f, s)
  \;=\; i^{-s}\!\sum_n\! c_f(n)\!\int_{i - 1}^{i}\! e^{2\pi i n \tau}\,
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau.
\end{equation}
We treat the constant Fourier mode $n = 0$ and the non-constant modes
$n \ne 0$ separately.

\paragraph{The constant Fourier mode.}
For $n = 0$, the integrand reduces to $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$
itself.  The Hurwitz zeta has antiderivative $\int\zeta(\sigma,
a)\,da = -\zeta(\sigma - 1, a)/(\sigma - 1) + C$ for $\sigma \ne 1$,
and the Hurwitz raising identity $\zeta(\sigma - 1, a + 1) -
\zeta(\sigma - 1, a) = -a^{1 - \sigma}$ at $a = i$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hurwitz-antideriv}
  \int_{i - 1}^{i}\! \zeta(\sigma, \tau + 1)\,d\tau
  \;=\; \int_i^{i + 1}\!\zeta(\sigma, u)\,du
  \;=\; \frac{i^{1 - \sigma}}{\sigma - 1}.
\end{equation}
Applying \eqref{eq:hurwitz-antideriv} to each half of $\widetilde{k}_T$
at $\sigma = 1 - s$ and $\sigma = s - (k - 1)$:
\[
  \int_{i - 1}^{i}\!\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \;=\; -\frac{i^s}{s}
  \;+\; e^{i\pi(s-1)}\,\frac{i^{k - s}}{k - s}.
\]
The overall $i^{-s}$ prefactor and the phase collapse
$i^{-s}\,e^{i\pi(s-1)}\,i^{k-s} = -i^k$ give the constant-term
contribution
\begin{equation}\label{eq:const-term}
  c_f(0)\cdot i^{-s}\!\int_{i - 1}^{i}\!\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \;=\; -c_f(0)\!\left(\frac{1}{s} + \frac{i^k}{k - s}\right).
\end{equation}
This matches the polar term of \cite[Lemma~2.1]{1806} (equivalently,
the polar piece of \cite[Thm.~1.1]{BFK2011}) at the splitting point
$B = 1$.

\paragraph{The non-constant Fourier modes.}
For $n \ne 0$, substituting $\sigma = \tau + 1$ shifts the contour to
$[i, i + 1]$ with the prefactor $e^{-2\pi i n} = 1$.  Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:hurwitz-unfold} to each half of $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau,
s) = \zeta(1 - s, \tau + 1) - e^{i\pi(s-1)}\zeta(s - (k-1), \tau + 1)$,
i.e.\ at $\nu = 1 - s$ and $\nu = s - (k-1)$:
\begin{align*}
  \int_i^{i+1}\! e^{2\pi i n \sigma}\,\zeta(1 - s, \sigma)\,d\sigma
  \;&=\; i^s\,(2\pi n)^{-s}\,\Gamma(s,\, 2\pi n),\\
  \int_i^{i+1}\! e^{2\pi i n \sigma}\,\zeta(s - (k - 1), \sigma)\,d\sigma
  \;&=\; i^{k - s}\,(2\pi n)^{s - k}\,\Gamma(k - s,\, 2\pi n).
\end{align*}
The first half against the $i^{-s}$ prefactor gives
$\Gamma(s, 2\pi n)/(2\pi n)^s$; the second half against the relative
phase $-e^{i\pi(s-1)}$ collapses via $-i^{-s}\,e^{i\pi(s-1)}\,i^{k-s}
= i^k$ to $i^k\,\Gamma(k - s, 2\pi n)/(2\pi n)^{k - s}$.  Adding the
two halves gives the per-mode summand
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonconst-term}
  c_f(n)\cdot i^{-s}\!\int_{i - 1}^{i}\! e^{2\pi i n \tau}\,
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \;=\; c_f(n)\!\left[\frac{\Gamma(s, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s}
  + i^k\,\frac{\Gamma(k - s, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k - s}}\right],
\end{equation}
which is the $B = 1$ value of the cuspidal summand of
\cite[Lemma~2.1]{1806}.

Summing \eqref{eq:const-term} and \eqref{eq:nonconst-term} over all
Fourier modes reproduces the closed-form sum
\eqref{eq:bfk-eq-statement}.

\subsection{Worked example at $k = 12$: Bernoulli polynomials and numerics}\label{subsec:k12-worked}

At $k = 12$ the kernel of \eqref{eq:tildekT-def} is
\[
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)
  \;=\; \zeta(1 - s,\,\tau + 1)
  \;-\; e^{i\pi(s-1)}\,\zeta(s - 11,\,\tau + 1).
\]
At integer values of $s \in \{1, 2, \ldots, 11\}$, both Hurwitz zeta
factors collapse to Bernoulli polynomials via $\zeta(-m, a) =
-B_{m+1}(a)/(m+1)$ ($m \ge 0$), and $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$
becomes a polynomial of degree $\le 11$ in $\tau$.  Three
representative values:
\[
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, 1)
  \;=\; -\tau - \tfrac{1}{2} \;+\; \tfrac{1}{11}\,B_{11}(\tau + 1),
  \qquad
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, 6)
  \;=\; -\tfrac{1}{3}\,B_6(\tau + 1),
\]
\[
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, 11)
  \;=\; -\tfrac{1}{11}\,B_{11}(\tau + 1) \;+\; \tau \;+\; \tfrac{1}{2}.
\]
The $s \leftrightarrow 12 - s$ symmetry follows from the
$s \leftrightarrow k - s$ structure of the two-term kernel (with
$i^k = +1$ at $k = 12$).

\paragraph{Identification with Theorem~\ref{thm:periods}.}
With the clean form of Theorem~\ref{thm:periods}, the $T$-segment
kernel of \eqref{eq:periods-statement} is exactly the integer-$s = j$
specialisation $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, j)$ of the Hurwitz-zeta kernel
of \eqref{eq:tildekT-def}, with no convention conversion required:
all combinatorial structure $(-1)^{j - 1}\binom{k - 2}{j - 1}/c_S^{\,j - 1}$
governing the $\omega^\pm(f)$ normalisation lives outside the
two-segment bracket.  The period-extraction functional
\eqref{eq:periods-statement} is thus the rescaled integer-$s$ shadow
of the all-$s$ $L$-function functional \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general}
of Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq}, with the bare Mellin $\tau^{s - 1}$ and

\subsection{Wall-crossing across an interior pole}\label{subsec:wall-crossing}

Suppose a continuous one-parameter deformation of $\tau_0$ to
$\tau_0'$ in $\HH$ moves the $S$-segment $[-1/\tau_0, \tau_0]$ across
an interior pole $\tau_p \in \HH$ of $f$ (the $T$-segment case is
identical up to the choice of kernel; see below).  Concretely, let
$\Sigma_t \subset \HH$ denote the $S$-segment at parameter $t \in [0,
1]$, with $\Sigma_0$ the $S$-segment at $\tau_0$ and $\Sigma_1$ at
$\tau_0'$, and assume that the swept-out region $\bigcup_t \Sigma_t$
encloses $\tau_p$ with winding number $\sigma_p^S \in \{+1, -1\}$
(orientation of the crossing).  By Cauchy's theorem applied to the
closed contour $\Sigma_1 - \Sigma_0$,
\[
  \int_{\Sigma_1}\! f(\tau)\,\tau^{s - 1}\,d\tau
  \;-\; \int_{\Sigma_0}\! f(\tau)\,\tau^{s - 1}\,d\tau
  \;=\; 2\pi i\,\sigma_p^S\,\Res(f, \tau_p)\,\tau_p^{s - 1}
\]
for a simple pole of $f$ at $\tau_p$.  An identical argument applies
when the $T$-segment $[\tau_0 - 1, \tau_0]$ sweeps across $\tau_p$,
yielding
\[
  \int_{T\text{-seg at }\tau_0'} \!-\! \int_{T\text{-seg at }\tau_0}
  \;=\; 2\pi i\,\sigma_p^T\,\Res(f, \tau_p)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau_p, s),
\]
where $\sigma_p^T \in \{+1, -1\}$ encodes the orientation of the
$T$-segment crossing.  Combining (with the $i^{-s}$ prefactor) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wall-crossing-explicit}
  %\Lambda^*(f, s; \tau_0)
  L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))
  \;-\; L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0'), \gamma_T(\tau_0'))
  \;=\; -2\pi i\,i^{-s}\,\Res(f, \tau_p)\!\left[
    \sigma_p^S\,\tau_p^{s-1} \;+\; \sigma_p^T\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau_p, s)
  \right].
\end{equation}
This is the residue formula $\mathcal{R}_{\tau_p}(f, s)$ of
\eqref{eq:residue-formula}, with the winding-number indices
$\sigma_p^S, \sigma_p^T$ making explicit the dependence on which
segment(s) cross.  At a generic wall only one of $\sigma_p^S,
\sigma_p^T$ is nonzero; both can be nonzero on the codimension-two
loci where the $S$- and $T$-segments simultaneously cross
$\Gamma$-images of $\tau_p$.

For a pole of order $m \ge 1$ at $\tau_p$ with Laurent expansion
$f(\tau) = \sum_{\ell \ge -m} r_{f, \tau_p}(\ell)(\tau - \tau_p)^\ell$,
the residue formula \eqref{eq:wall-crossing-explicit} generalises by
replacing $\Res(f, \tau_p)\,K(\tau_p, s)$ with
$\sum_{\ell = 1}^{m}\!r_{f, \tau_p}(-\ell)\,
\partial_{\tau_p}^{\ell - 1} K(\tau_p, s)/(\ell - 1)!$ for each
relevant kernel $K \in \{\tau^{s-1}, \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\}$, by
the standard residue formula for higher-order poles.

\subsection{Reduction on regular-at-cusps meromorphic forms}\label{subsec:reduction-to-int}

Let $f \in F_k$ be regular at the cusp $i\infty$, so $f(\tau) =
\sum_{n \ge N} c_f(n)\,q^n$ for some $N \ge 0$.

\begin{lemma}[Cusp decay of the $T$-segment]\label{lem:cusp-decay}
For $f \in F_k$ regular at the cusp with $f(\tau) = O(q^N)$ at
$i\infty$ for $N \ge 1$, every $s \in \CC$, and every $\eps \in \RR$
fixed,
\[
  \int_{\tau_0 - 1}^{\tau_0}\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \;=\; O\!\bigl(|\tau_0|^{C(\mathrm{Re}\, s)}\,
    e^{-2\pi N \,\im(\tau_0)}\bigr)
  \qquad
  \text{as }\im(\tau_0) \to \infty,\;\mathrm{Re}(\tau_0) = \eps.
\]
The exponent $C(\mathrm{Re}\, s) = \max(0,\, 1/2 - \mathrm{Re}(1 - s))
+ \max(0,\, 1/2 - \mathrm{Re}(s - (k - 1))) + \eps_0$ for any $\eps_0
> 0$ is controlled by the standard polynomial-growth bound for the
Hurwitz zeta on vertical strips \cite[\S25.11.36]{DLMF}.  When $N = 0$
(i.e.\ $f$ has a nonzero constant term at $i\infty$), the $T$-segment
is $O(|\tau_0|^{C(\mathrm{Re}\, s)})$ but does not decay; in this
case the constant-term Fourier mode must be split off and treated
separately (see \cite[Lemma~2.1]{1806} for the analogous polynomial
correction in the deformed-Mellin construction).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On the segment $\tau \in [\tau_0 - 1, \tau_0]$ with $\im(\tau) =
\im(\tau_0)$, the bound $|f(\tau)| \le M\,e^{-2\pi N \im(\tau_0)}$
holds for some $M = M(f)$ depending only on $f$.  Combining with the
polynomial-in-$\tau$ growth of $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ from
\cite[\S25.11.36]{DLMF} and integrating over the length-$1$ segment
gives the stated bound.
\end{proof}

To match the deformed-Mellin construction of $L_f^*(s)_{\rm int}$
(see \cite{1806}), take $\tau_0 = \eps + i\Lambda$ for some small
$\eps \in \RR$ chosen so that $\mathrm{Re}(\tau_0) = \eps$ avoids all
$\Gamma$-images of interior poles of $f$.  As $\Lambda \to \infty$:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0.1em
\item The $T$-segment integral $\to 0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:cusp-decay}
  (using $N \ge 1$; for $N = 0$ the analog argument with the
  constant-term subtraction $c_f(0)$ from \cite{1806} applies).
\item The $S$-segment $[-1/\tau_0, \tau_0]$ approaches the
  $\Lambda \to \infty$ limit of the $\Lambda$-truncated Mellin contour
  along $\mathrm{Re}(\tau) = \eps$ (modulo orientation, which fixes the
  $L^*_{\rm int}$ convention).
\end{itemize}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:Lambda-DR-tau0-invariant} (extended to meromorphic
$f$ within a fixed connected component, by
\S\ref{subsec:existence-local-constancy} above), the left side is
$\Lambda$-independent, so
\[
  L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))
  \;=\; \lim_{\Lambda \to \infty}\!L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\eps + i\Lambda), \gamma_T(\eps + i\Lambda))
  \;=\; L_f^*(s)_{\rm int,\,\gamma_\eps},
\]
where $\gamma_\eps$ is the deformed-Mellin contour along $\mathrm{Re}(\tau)
= \eps$.  This proves Corollary~\ref{cor:1806-coincide}.\qed

\subsection{Wall-crossing of the period-polynomial cocycle}\label{subsec:period-wall-crossing}

The wall-crossing residue formula \eqref{eq:residue-formula} acts on
the complex-$s$ functional $L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0),
\gamma_T(\tau_0))$.  Specialised at integer $s = j \in \{2, \ldots,
k-2\}$ and assembled into the $V_{k-2}$ moment expansion of
\S\ref{sec:cocycle}, it gives the wall-crossing of the
period-polynomial cocycle of $f$ under contour deformation across an
interior pole.

\begin{corollary}[Period-polynomial wall-crossing residue]\label{cor:period-wall-crossing}
Let $f$ be a meromorphic modular form of weight $k$ on $\Gamma$, and
let $(\gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0)), (\gamma_S(\tau_0'),
\gamma_T(\tau_0'))$ be two admissible contour pairs related by a
single crossing of an interior pole $\tau_p \in \HH$ of $f$.  Denote
by $C^f_S(\gamma_S(\tau_0)), C^f_T(\gamma_T(\tau_0)) \in V_{k-2}\otimes\CC$
the corresponding $V_{k-2}$-valued cocycle values from
\eqref{eq:cocycle-def}.  For a simple pole $\mathrm{Res}(f, \tau_p)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cocycle-residue-jump}
  C^f_S(\gamma_S(\tau_0)) - C^f_S(\gamma_S(\tau_0'))
  \;=\; 2\pi i\,(2\pi i)^{k-2}\,\sigma^S_{\tau_p}\,
  \mathrm{Res}(f, \tau_p)\,(X - \tau_p Y)^{k-2},
\end{equation}
and analogously for $C^f_T$ with $\sigma^S_{\tau_p}$ replaced by
$\sigma^T_{\tau_p}$.  The integer-$s = j$ specialisation of
\eqref{eq:residue-formula} is the coefficient of
$\binom{k-2}{j}(-1)^j X^{k-2-j} Y^j$ in
\eqref{eq:cocycle-residue-jump} (combined for $S$ and $T$).  For
$\dim S_k = 1$ and $f \in F_k$ meromorphic with interior poles, the
period polynomial $r_f$ extracted via Theorem~\ref{thm:periods} from
$(\gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))$ therefore depends on the
homotopy class of the contour pair, with jump
\eqref{eq:cocycle-residue-jump} across each wall.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The integrand $f(\tau)(X - \tau Y)^{k-2}$ of \eqref{eq:cocycle-def}
has a simple pole at $\tau = \tau_p$ with residue
$\mathrm{Res}(f, \tau_p)(X - \tau_p Y)^{k-2}$, so a contour deformation
crossing $\tau_p$ with winding $\sigma_{\tau_p} \in \{-1, +1\}$
contributes $2\pi i\,\sigma_{\tau_p}\,\mathrm{Res}(f, \tau_p)(X - \tau_p Y)^{k-2}$
to the moment integral.  Multiplying by the cocycle prefactor
$(2\pi i)^{k-2}$ of \eqref{eq:cocycle-def} gives
\eqref{eq:cocycle-residue-jump}.  Expanding $(X - \tau_p Y)^{k-2}$ in
the monomial basis and matching coefficients of $X^{k-2-j} Y^j$ for
each $j \in \{0, \ldots, k-2\}$ reproduces
\eqref{eq:residue-formula} at integer $s = j$ (with $\tau_p^{j-1}$
arising from $X^{k-2-j} Y^j$ at $j \ge 1$ from the $S$-segment
contribution, and analogously for the $T$-segment via
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau_p, j)$ at integer $j$).
\end{proof}

\section{Outlook}\label{sec:outlook}

\paragraph{Wilson-loop / holonomy interpretation.}
The period-defining contour pair, projected to the modular curve $Y(1)$, descends
to the two generators of the orbifold fundamental group
$\pi_1^{\rm orb}(Y(1)) \cong \ZZ/2 * \ZZ/3$.  The polynomial
coefficient system $V_{k-2}$ is a flat local system over $Y(1)$, and
the cocycle $C^f_\gamma$ is the weighted holonomy of this flat
connection along the loop $\gamma$, with $f$ supplying the
gauge-invariant weight.  The cohomology class $[C^f] \in
H^1(\Gamma; V_{k-2})$ is the analog of a gauge-invariant observable;
$\tau_0$ is the analog of a trivialisation frame; $\omega^\pm(f)$ are
coordinates of $[C^f]$ in the cuspidal subspace, the analog of physical
observables read off a Wilson-loop expectation.  The period-defining finite-contour
functional is, in this dictionary, the modular-form analog of a
Wilson-loop holonomy on the modular curve.

\paragraph{The on-shell methods analogy.}
The classical Mellin construction is ``off-shell'' in the same sense as
a Lagrangian gauge-theory amplitude calculation: the intermediate
integrand $f(\tau)\,\tau^{s-1}\,d\tau$ encodes gauge-redundant
information (the basepoint $\tau_0$, the contour to the cusp), and the
gauge-invariant observable $L^*(f, s)$ is reached only after a
non-canonical limit.  Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq} is an ``on-shell''
reformulation in which the gauge-invariant observable is the value of
a finite-contour functional at any admissible basepoint, with no
limit and no regulator.  The modular-form analog of the on-shell
S-matrix programme \cite{McGadyHuang2013, McGadyHuangChen2014} ---
constructing $\omega^\pm$ and $L^*(f, s)$ from $\{c_f(n)\}$ via a
kernel that does not pass through the cocycle / coboundary scaffolding
--- is partially answered by Theorem~\ref{thm:periods} on the period
side and Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq} on the $L$-function side, with the
remaining question being whether the kernels themselves admit a
construction independent of the $\delta_T^{-1}$ Bernoulli operator.

\paragraph{Regularisation as a symptom of bad framing.}
The disappearance of any regularisation step in
\eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-def} is an instance of a recurring methodological
pattern: \emph{when a divergence arises only after one has made a
non-canonical upstream choice, finding the canonical formulation
eliminates the divergence together with the regulator}.  The same
move underlies the on-shell amplitudes programme
\cite{McGadyHuang2013}.  The pattern is not universal --- chiral
anomalies in even dimensions are a counterexample, with
cohomologically robust ``regulator'' content invariant under
reframings --- but it is a useful prior, and the finite-contour
formulation here is a particularly clean instance in which the
canonical $L^*(f, s)$ falls out as the value of a well-defined
linear functional at any admissible basepoint, with no auxiliary
parameter to take to infinity and no contour to deform to a cusp.

\paragraph{The geometric content of the Mellin kernel.}
The polynomial coefficient system $V_{k-2}$ underlying the period
construction is the symmetric-power local system that gives the
polynomial kernel $(X - \tau Y)^{k-2}$ of the period polynomial.  Its
flat connection on the modular curve is the natural geometric home of
$\omega^\pm(f)$.  The Hurwitz-zeta kernel $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ of
\eqref{eq:tildekT-def} extends the polynomial kernel to a one-parameter
analytic family in $s$, but the geometric content of the bare Mellin
kernel $f(\tau)\,\tau^{s-1}\,d\tau$ itself --- as opposed to its
specialisation at integer $s$ to a polynomial-in-$V_{k-2}$ kernel --- is
not visible in the construction of this paper.  A natural conjecture is
that $\tau^{s-1}\,d\tau$ is a section of an infinite-dimensional
principal-series-type local system on $Y(1)$, in the same one-parameter
family as the Lewis--Zagier period functions for Maass wave forms
\cite{LewisZagier2001}.  Identifying the relevant local system
explicitly, and connecting the Hurwitz-zeta inversion of $\delta_T$ to
a coboundary statement in this larger system, is an open question.

\paragraph{Poincar\'e-series structure of the cumulative wall-crossing.}
The residue formula \eqref{eq:residue-formula} controls the jump of
$L^*(f, s; \gamma_S, \gamma_T)$ across a single pole-crossing.
Summing such jumps along a continuous deformation of the contour pair
from one that encloses no interior poles to one that encloses every
pole in a single $\Gamma$-orbit $\Gamma\cdot\tau_p$ produces a
cumulative residue of the structure
\[
  \sum_{\gamma \in \Gamma_{\tau_p}\backslash\Gamma}\!
  (c\tau_p + d)^{k - 2}\,\mathrm{Res}(f, \tau_p)\!
  \left[\sigma^S_\gamma\,(\gamma\tau_p)^{s-1}
  \;+\; \sigma^T_\gamma\,\widetilde{k}_T(\gamma\tau_p, s)\right],
\]
where the cocycle factor $(c\tau_p + d)^{k-2}$ comes from the
transformation $\mathrm{Res}(f, \gamma\tau_p) = (c\tau_p + d)^{k-2}\,
\mathrm{Res}(f, \tau_p)$ under modularity of $f$, $\Gamma_{\tau_p}$ is
the stabiliser of $\tau_p$ in $\Gamma$, and $\sigma^{S, T}_\gamma$
encode the winding numbers of $\gamma_S, \gamma_T$ across the
orbit-point $\gamma\tau_p$.  The summand is structurally a
weight-$k$ Poincar\'e-series term in $\tau_p$, in the $s$-extended
form built from $\tau_p^{s-1}$ and $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau_p, s)$.

The structural parallel with the partial-fraction representation
proved by Hardy and Ramanujan in their 1919 paper on modular
functions with prescribed poles \cite{HR1919} is direct.
\cite[Thm.~2]{HR1919} shows that a modular function $\phi$ of weight
$n$ with a single simple pole at $\tau = \alpha$ admits the
representation
\[
  f(q) \;=\; 2\pi i\,A\!\sum\!\frac{1}{(a + b\alpha)^{n+2}}\,
  \frac{\mathbf{q}^2}{q^2 - \mathbf{q}^2},
  \qquad
  \mathbf{q} \;=\; \exp\!\Bigl(\tfrac{c + d\alpha}{a + b\alpha}\,\pi i\Bigr),
\]
summed over pairs $(a, b)$ giving distinct $\Gamma$-images of
$\alpha$.  Each summand is the residue at one orbit-point,
transported by the modular cocycle factor $(a + b\alpha)^{-(n+2)}$ and
weighted by an explicit kernel evaluated at $\mathbf{q}$.  Our
cumulative wall-crossing residue has the same orbit-of-residues
structure, with $\widetilde{k}_T(\gamma\tau_p, s)$ playing the role
of the $\mathbf{q}^2/(q^2 - \mathbf{q}^2)$ kernel.  Whether the
cumulative wall-crossing converges as a function of $s$ to a
canonical weight-$k$ Poincar\'e series, and what its modular
properties are, is a natural direction for follow-up work.

\paragraph{Towards systematic interior-pole $L$-function theory.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:wall-crossing} establishes existence and wall-crossing
for %\Lambda^*(f, s; \tau_0)
$L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0))$ on arbitrary meromorphic $f$,
but does not give a closed-form per-mode evaluation parallel to the
incomplete-gamma sum \eqref{eq:bfk-eq-statement}.  Developing the
systematic per-mode theory --- in particular adapting the
polylog-projection machinery $\widehat{P}_{\tau_p}$
(see \cite{1806}) to the finite-contour setting and matching the wall-crossing data
\eqref{eq:wall-crossing-statement} to the iterated-Eichler-integral
framework of Brown--Hain on $\mathcal{M}_{1,1}$ and the universal
elliptic curve \cite{Brown2017} --- is the natural sequel.  The
arithmetic of the wall-crossing residues $\mathcal{R}_{\tau_p}(f, s)$
for forms with poles at CM points (e.g.\ $\tau_p \in \{i, \rho\}$) is a
particularly clean target, where the Laurent coefficients of $f$ take
values in number fields and $\mathcal{R}_{\tau_p}$ acquires explicit
arithmetic structure.

\section{Algebraic derivation of the Hurwitz-zeta kernel}\label{app:hurwitz-derivation}

The body of \S\ref{subsec:kT-derivation} derives $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ by
differentiating the two-segment functional in $\tau_0$ and demanding the
result vanish.  Here we record the parallel derivation that proceeds
\emph{from} the classical Mellin transform of a holomorphic cusp form,
splitting it at the contour endpoints $\{-1/\tau_0, \tau_0\}$ and
demanding the resulting tail be representable as a $T$-segment
integral.  Both derivations produce the same shift-difference equation
and the same kernel; this appendix makes the algebraic provenance of
the Hurwitz-zeta combination manifest.

\paragraph{Step 1: Mellin split at the contour endpoints.}
Let $f \in S_k$ with $k$ even.  The classical completed $L$-function
$L^*(f, s) = (2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s) L(f, s)$ has integral representation
\[
  L^*(f, s)
  \;=\; -i \int_0^{i\infty}\! f(\tau)\,(\tau/i)^{s-1}\,d\tau
  \;=\; i^{-s}\!\int_0^{i\infty}\! f(\tau)\,\tau^{s-1}\,d\tau,
\]
the second equality using $(\tau/i)^{s-1} = i^{1-s}\tau^{s-1}$ on the
imaginary axis.  Pick $\tau_0 \in \HH$ and split the imaginary-axis
contour at the two endpoints $\{-1/\tau_0, \tau_0\}$ in
\eqref{eq:contour-pair}.  Reorganising the three resulting pieces
using $S$-modularity $f(-1/\tau) = \tau^k f(\tau)$ on the lower segment
$[0, -1/\tau_0]$ (via the substitution $\tau \mapsto -1/\tau$) and
computing the $q$-series-against-$\tau^{s-1}$ integral on
$[\tau_0, i\infty]$ directly yields, for every $\tau_0 \in \HH$ and
every $s \in \CC$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appB-gap}
  L^*(f, s)
  \;=\; i^{-s}\!\int_{-1/\tau_0}^{\tau_0}\! f(\tau)\,\tau^{s-1}\,d\tau
  \;+\; \sum_{n \ge 1} c_f(n)\!
  \left[\frac{\Gamma(s,\,-2\pi i n \tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^s}
  \;+\; \frac{\Gamma(k - s,\,-2\pi i n \tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^{k - s}}\right].
\end{equation}
The left side is $\tau_0$-independent (it is the classical $L$-value);
both terms on the right side individually depend on $\tau_0$, but
their sum does not.  At $\tau_0 = i$ the argument $-2\pi i n \tau_0 =
2\pi n$ is real and \eqref{eq:appB-gap} reduces to the standard BFK
incomplete-gamma sum of \cite{BFK2011}.

The right side of \eqref{eq:appB-gap} contains the $S$-segment integral
$i^{-s}\int_{-1/\tau_0}^{\tau_0}$ explicitly; the remaining
incomplete-gamma sum plays the role that we want to be a $T$-segment
integral.

\paragraph{Step 2: ansatz for the $T$-segment kernel.}
Postulate that there exists a kernel $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$,
analytic in $\tau \in \HH$ and meromorphic in $s \in \CC$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appB-ansatz}
  i^{-s}\!\int_{\tau_0-1}^{\tau_0}\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \;=\; \sum_{n \ge 1} c_f(n)\!
  \left[\frac{\Gamma(s,\,-2\pi i n \tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^s}
  \;+\; \frac{\Gamma(k - s,\,-2\pi i n \tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^{k - s}}\right]
\end{equation}
for every $f \in S_k$ and every $\tau_0 \in \HH$.  If such a kernel
exists, substituting \eqref{eq:appB-ansatz} into \eqref{eq:appB-gap}
gives the two-segment functional
$L^*(f, s; \gamma_S(\tau_0), \gamma_T(\tau_0)) = i^{-s}[\int_{\gamma_S} +
\int_{\gamma_T}]$ as a $\tau_0$-independent expression equal to
$L^*(f, s)$.

\paragraph{Step 3: $\tau_0$-derivative gives a shift-difference equation.}
Differentiate both sides of \eqref{eq:appB-ansatz} in $\tau_0$.  On the
left, the only $\tau_0$-dependence is in the integration limits, so
$d/d\tau_0$ acts as
\[
  \frac{d}{d\tau_0}\!\int_{\tau_0-1}^{\tau_0}\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \;=\; f(\tau_0)\!\left[\widetilde{k}_T(\tau_0, s)
  - \widetilde{k}_T(\tau_0 - 1, s)\right],
\]
using $f(\tau_0 - 1) = f(\tau_0)$ for the lower limit.  On the right
of \eqref{eq:appB-ansatz}, differentiating each incomplete gamma in
its second argument (the explicit $\tau_0$-dependence) and assembling
the Fourier series yields, after canceling a common factor of
$f(\tau_0)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appB-shift-diff}
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau_0, s) \;-\; \widetilde{k}_T(\tau_0 - 1, s)
  \;=\; -\tau_0^{s-1} \;+\; e^{i\pi(s-1)}\,\tau_0^{k - 1 - s}
  \qquad \text{for all } \tau_0 \in \HH.
\end{equation}
The first term on the right is the contribution of the
$\tau^{s-1}$-Mellin endpoint at $\tau_0$; the second term is the
$S$-modularity image of the same endpoint at $-1/\tau_0$, carrying
the branch factor $(-1/\tau_0)^{s-1} = e^{i\pi(s-1)}\tau_0^{-(s-1)}$
together with the modularity Jacobian $\tau_0^k$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:appB-shift-diff} is a shift-difference equation of order
one in $\tau$, with a sum of two power-of-$\tau$ terms on the right
side.

\paragraph{Step 4: Hurwitz raising solves the shift-difference equation.}
The Hurwitz zeta function $\zeta(\sigma, a)$ satisfies the raising
identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appB-hurwitz-raising}
  \zeta(\sigma, a + 1) \;-\; \zeta(\sigma, a) \;=\; -\,a^{-\sigma}
  \qquad (\sigma \in \CC,\ \mathrm{Re}(a) > 0)
\end{equation}
(see \cite[\S25.11.3]{DLMF}).  Applied at $(\sigma, a) = (1 - s, \tau)$
and $(\sigma, a) = (s - (k - 1), \tau)$ respectively, this gives
\[
  \zeta(1 - s, \tau + 1) - \zeta(1 - s, \tau) \;=\; -\tau^{s-1},
  \qquad
  \zeta(s - (k - 1), \tau + 1) - \zeta(s - (k - 1), \tau)
  \;=\; -\tau^{k - 1 - s}.
\]
Combining with the relative phase $e^{i\pi(s-1)}$ from the second
term of \eqref{eq:appB-shift-diff} gives the kernel
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appB-kT-final}
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)
  \;:=\; \zeta(1 - s,\, \tau + 1)
  \;-\; e^{i\pi(s-1)}\,\zeta(s - (k - 1),\, \tau + 1),
\end{equation}
which by construction satisfies \eqref{eq:appB-shift-diff} and hence
\eqref{eq:appB-ansatz}.  The kernel is analytic in $\tau$ on $\HH$
(both Hurwitz zetas have second argument $\tau + 1$ with
$\mathrm{Re}(\tau + 1) > 0$) and meromorphic in $s$ with simple poles
only at $s = 0$ and $s = k$, where the per-mode integrals collect
removable cancellations (cf.\ Lemma~\ref{lem:hurwitz-unfold}).
Equation \eqref{eq:appB-kT-final} is the kernel
\eqref{eq:tildekT-def} of the main text.

\paragraph{Where DR2022 stops.}
The kernel \eqref{eq:appB-kT-final} has two Hurwitz-zeta terms.  The
construction of \cite[Thm.~1.2]{DR2022} gives the single-term
\[
  \widetilde{k}_T^{\,\rm DR}(\tau, m)
  \;=\; \zeta^*(1 - m, \tau + 1)
\]
on the segment $[i, i+1]$, at integer $m$, which is the first term of
\eqref{eq:appB-kT-final}.  The structural origin of this gap is the
absence of an $S$-segment in their construction: without the
$S$-modularity-transformed contribution at the $-1/\tau_0$ endpoint
in Step 3, the shift-difference equation \eqref{eq:appB-shift-diff}
has only the single right-hand-side term $-\tau_0^{s-1}$, and its
solution is the single-Hurwitz kernel.  The second
$e^{i\pi(s-1)}\,\tau_0^{k-1-s}$ term appears precisely because the
two-segment functional includes the $S$-segment and its endpoint at
$-1/\tau_0$ contributes under $\tau_0$-differentiation via
$S$-modularity.
