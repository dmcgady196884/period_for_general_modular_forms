\begin{abstract}
In this note, we synthesize and generalize previous work, which 
(1) extracted periods for weak cusp forms in a manifestly finite 
manner~\cite{Brown2017}, (2) identified a non-holomorphic regularization 
prescription to define finite analogs of L-functions associated to weakly 
holomorphic modular forms~\cite{BFK2011}, and (3) identified a contour 
prescription to define an L-function for meromorphic modular forms with poles inside 
the fundamental domain~\cite{1806}. Our construction, derived from the 
cohomological period extraction of weak cusp forms in~\cite{Brown2017}, 
shows that these three lines of work not only naturally align, but derive from 
the \emph{same} manifestly finite linear functional acting on the space of $\SLZ$ 
modular forms. 

We call this linear functional the \emph{L-integral}, and denote it 
$L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$; here 
$\gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0}$ are paths from 
$S^{-1}\tau_0, T^{-1}\tau_0$, respectively, to $\tau_0$. When $f \in M_k$, 
$L^*(f,s) = \Gamma(s) \sum_{n\neq0} \tfrac{c_f(n)}{(2\pi n)^s}$: \emph{the} 
classical L-function. Our L-integrals are finite, $\tau_0$-independent, and path 
independent within each homotopy class (if $f$ has poles at $0 < {\rm Im}(\tau) 
< \infty$), for any meromorphic modular form $f$.

We use this L-integral to (1) extract published numerical values of the 
periods of the weak cusp form $\Dhat := \tfrac{1}{q} + O(q^2) \in S_{12}^!$ 
in~\cite{Brown2017}, (2) recover the regularized expressions for the 
divergent Mellin transform of $f$ in~\cite{BFK2011}, and (3) recover the 
L-functions of \cite{1806} for meromorphic forms with interior poles that are 
regular at the cusps. When $f$ has poles inside the fundamental domain, 
$L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ is unique and constant for 
paths within the same homotopy class; related $L^*(f,s)$-integrals along paths 
in different homotopy classes differ by wall-crossing residues. Finally, all major 
results were tested to arbitrary (50 digit) precision in our python scripts~\cite{VerifyScripts}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and main results}\label{sec:intro}\label{sec:intro-results}
 
In this paper, we introduce a general framework that defines L-functions for
generic modular forms on the full group $\SLZ$, and extracts their periods for
the explicit case when the holomorphic cusp forms are one-dimensional, i.e.
when $\dim(S_k) = 1$. Classically, L-functions and periods are both well-defined
and well-studied for holomorphic modular forms. However, there is a growing
body of work exploring periods~\cite{Brown2017} and L-functions~\cite{BFK2011}
for weakly holomorphic modular forms. Similarly, there are natural physical
motivations to consider L-functions for properly meromorphic modular forms,
which have poles \emph{within} the fundamental domain~\cite{1806}.
 
In these works, and specifically in~\cite{BFK2011, 1806}, and throughout physics,
divergences due to singularities in quantities of interest necessitate the
introduction of regulators in order to define finite quantities. Regulators, though,
often break symmetries, and care is needed to check that the regulator has not
injected undue violence to the system. Of relevance here is that~\cite{BFK2011}
introduced a non-holomorphic regulator to deal with the singularities at either
end of the classical Mellin contour, $\int_0^\infty$, sufficient to regulate
divergences at the cusps, but which violates holomorphy in the deformed
contour-integral, ruining path-independence. Simultaneously, \cite{1806}
concerned itself with L-functions for modular forms which diverge within the
interior of the fundamental domain. Here, holomorphic dependence on $\tau$
is paramount, which puts this treatment at odds with the regulator in~\cite{BFK2011}.
 
In this note, we introduce manifestly finite contour integral functionals to define
L-functions for generic meromorphic modular forms that are of sufficient generality
to handle any combination of poles at cusps and poles within the fundamental domain.
Our construction is motivated by, and makes precise contact with, work which extracts
period-coefficients from weak cusp forms~\cite{Brown2017}. Our integrals are
manifestly finite throughout, and reproduce all results in the literature, unifying the
quasi-period results and the L-function extensions within one concrete, natural framework.
 
Throughout, $\Gamma = \SLZ$, and we consider modular forms on $\Gamma$ of
even integer weight and trivial multiplier system.  Write $M_k$ for the
holomorphic modular forms of weight $k$, $S_k \subset M_k$ for the cusp
forms, and $M^!_k$ for the weakly holomorphic forms (holomorphic on
$\HH$, with at most a pole at the cusp).  Write $F_k$ for the
\emph{meromorphic modular forms} of weight $k$: the weight-$k$ part of
the field of fractions of the graded ring $M_* = \bigoplus_k M_k$, i.e.\
the quotients $f = f_1/f_2$ with $f_i \in M_{k_i}$, $f_2 \ne 0$, and
$k = k_1 - k_2$; equivalently, the weight-$k$ functions meromorphic on
$\HH$ and at the cusp.  Thus $M_k \subset M^!_k \subset F_k$.  Finally,
$S^!_k \supset S_k$ denotes the \emph{weakly holomorphic cusp forms}:
those $f = \sum_{n \in \ZZ} c(n)\,q^n \in M^!_k$ with vanishing constant
term, $c(0) = 0$. %DAM: This paragraph might need tightening.
We write $E_k = 1 + O(q) \in M_k$ for the unit-normalized
Eisenstein series, $\Delta := (E_4^3 - E_6^2)/1728 = q + {\cal O}(q^2)
\in S_{12}$ for the modular discriminant,
and $j = 1728\,E_4^3/(E_4^3 - E_6^2) = 1/q + {\cal O}(q^0) \in M^!_0$
for the Hauptmodul of $\SLZ$.

In a nutshell, this note is the natural extension of bridging the gap between the 
manifestly finite extraction of quasi-periods of $\Dhat$ in \cite{Brown2017} and the 
manifestly regulator-dependent derivation of effective L-functions for weak cusp forms 
in \cite{BFK2011}. By leveraging the construction underlying the computation in \cite{Brown2017}, we 
uncover a unified, manifestly finite formalism to define L-functions, and extract L-values 
which define period polynomials, for truly generic meromorphic modular forms. 

\subsection{Manifestly finite extractions of period polynomials for weak cusp forms}

To understand the manifestly finite extraction of the quasi-periods in~\cite{Brown2017}, 
we begin by studying the manifestly $\SLZ$-covariant differential,
\begin{equation}
d\omega := d\tau f(\tau) (X - \tau Y)^{k-2}~,
\end{equation}
where $f$ is a modular form of weight $k$ on $\SLZ$, and contour integrals of 
$P(X, Y) := \int_a^b d\omega$ yield polynomials $P \in V_{k-2} := \CC[X, Y]_n$ 
which have natural action under $\SLZ$, i.e. for each $\gamma = \left( 
\begin{smallmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{smallmatrix} \right) \in \SLZ$, 
$(f|_\gamma)(\tau) = (c\tau + d)^k f(\tau)$ and $(P|_\gamma)(X, Y) = 
P(a X + b Y, c X + d Y)$.

Period polynomials of holomorphic cusp forms $f \in S_k$, which are commonly 
written as

machinery.

important, context of holomorphic cusp-forms. For instance, relaxing from a 
holomorphic cusp form to a weakly holomorphic cusp form introduces essential

\begin{definition}[S- and T-segments at a basepoint]\label{def:contours}
Let $\tau_0 \in \HH$, and consider any $\sigma \in \SLZ$. We denote the
path from $\sigma^{-1}(\tau_0) \to \tau_0$ by
$\gamma^\sigma_{\tau_0}$.
Further, let $f$ be a meromorphic modular form of weight $k \in 2\ZZ$ on
$\SLZ$, which may have poles at cusps and may have poles within the
interior of the upper half-plane.
Now, consider $\sigma \in \{ S, T \} = \{ \left( \begin{smallmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right), \left( \begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right) \}$. Here, $\gamma^\sigma_{\tau_0}$ is a smooth contour,
\begin{align}
\begin{cases}
  S\text{-segment}\colon\:\:\; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}:[0, 1] \to \HH~,&~~
  \gamma^S_{\tau_0}(0)=-1/\tau_0,\, \gamma^S_{\tau_0}(1) =\tau_0,
  \\
  T\text{-segment}\colon\:\:\; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}:[0, 1] \to \HH~,&~~
  \gamma^T_{\tau_0}(0)=\tau_0-1,\, \gamma^T_{\tau_0}(1) =\tau_0,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:contour-pair}
\end{align}
where both contours avoid the $\Gamma$-orbit of the poles of $f$ in
$\HH$.
\end{definition}
 
All contours in this note lie between finite interior points of $\HH$, 
and all contour avoids singular points.  Finiteness of every integral is therefore 
structural; there is never anything to regulate.

The price of trading one divergent contour for a family of finite ones
is redundancy: each $C^f_\gamma(\tau_0)$ depends on an auxiliary
basepoint $\tau_0$ and on a group element $\gamma$, while the period
polynomial must refer to neither.  Degree-one group cohomology is the
standard bookkeeping for exactly this situation, and only three
definitions are needed.  A \emph{$1$-cocycle} is a function
$c\colon \Gamma \to V_n$ satisfying $c_{gh} = c_g|_h + c_h$, and
$\gamma \mapsto C^f_\gamma(\tau_0)$ is one for each fixed $\tau_0$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:cocycle}).  A \emph{$1$-coboundary} is the special
cocycle $\delta P\colon \gamma \mapsto P|_\gamma - P$ built from a
polynomial $P \in V_n$; by \eqref{eq:bp-cocycle-coboundary}, shifting
$\tau_0 \to \tau_0'$ (no pole crossed) shifts the whole family
$\{C^f_\gamma\}_\gamma$ by one such $\delta P$ --- the same $P$ for
every $\gamma$, which is why the quotient retains anything at all.  The
\emph{cohomology class}
$[C^f] \in H^1(\Gamma; V_n) :=
\{\text{cocycles}\}/\{\text{coboundaries}\}$ is therefore
basepoint-free, and a \emph{representative} of it is any cocycle
$C^f - \delta P$.  This kills the
$\tau_0$. %CLAUDE: replaces the former lines 313--320, which described coboundaries as polynomials and the equivalence as acting on each C^f_\gamma separately.

A reader outside the field might object that no machinery should be
needed: the classical period polynomial is a single integral along one
$S$-contour, so why not take the finite $S$-contour $C^f_S(\tau_0)$ and
stop?  Because at a finite basepoint the raw $S$-value is pure
redundancy.  Torsion alone forces
$C^f_S\big|(1+S) = C^f_{S^2} = C^f_{-I} = 0$ at \emph{every} $\tau_0$,
and since $S$ acts on $V_n$ as an involution, its $(-1)$-eigenspace is
also the image of $\delta_S$: the value $C^f_S$ lies in
$\ker(1+S) = \im \delta_S$, exactly the space of shifts that basepoint
motion induces on it.  (At $k = 12$ this kernel is six-dimensional
inside the eleven-dimensional $V_{10}$; membership selects a subspace,
never a point.)  The two relations cutting out the Kohnen--Zagier space
$W_{k-2}$ \cite{KZ1984} therefore have different statuses: the
$S$-relation is \emph{free}, holding before any choice is made, while
the $U$-relation must be \emph{purchased} --- it appears only after the
representative is pinned, and pinning is the role of the other
generator.

The generator $T$ is built for the job.  It has infinite order and acts
on $V_n$ unipotently --- a single Jordan block --- so
$\delta_T := (\,\cdot\,)|_T - \mathrm{id}$ has one-dimensional kernel
and cokernel: the equation has one scalar of obstruction and one scalar
of slack.  We choose, within the class, the representative
$C^f - \delta P$ \emph{whose $T$-value vanishes}, i.e.\ solve
$\delta_T P = (2\pi i)^{-(n+1)} C^f_T(\tau_0)$ on $V_n$.  Solvability is
itself arithmetic: unit-segment $T$-periodicity identifies the
obstruction in $V_n / \im \delta_T \cong \CC \cdot [X^n]$ as the
constant Fourier mode (Lemma~\ref{lem:solvability-cuspidality}),
$[C^f_T]_{X^n} = (2\pi i)^{n+1} c_f(0)$ --- the residue of $\omega_f$ at
the very cusp that $T$ stabilizes.  The normalized representative exists
if and only if $c_f(0) = 0$; classes that pass are the \emph{parabolic}
ones, identified by Eichler--Shimura \cite{KZ1984} with
$S_k \oplus \overline{S_k}$, the lone obstructed direction being
Eisenstein.  Cuspidality, in this framework, is not a convergence
hypothesis; it is the solvability condition for the normalization.

With the $T$-value normalized away, the entire cuspidal content sits on
the surviving generator value,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-normalized}
  r_f \;:=\; C^f_S(\tau_0) \;-\; (2\pi i)^{n+1}\bigl(P|_S - P\bigr),
\end{equation}
well-defined up to the line $\CC\,(X^n - Y^n)$ left over from
$\ker \delta_T = \CC\, Y^n$ --- an ambiguity touching only the boundary
monomials, which is why Theorem~\ref{thm:periods} ranges over the
interior $\ell$ alone.  The $U$-relation now arrives, purchased as
promised: conditioned on the vanishing $T$-value, the group relation
$(ST)^3 = -I$ becomes $r_f|\bigl(1 + (TS) + (TS)^2\bigr) = 0$, and $r_f$
\emph{is} the period polynomial.  Indeed, for holomorphic $f \in S_k$,
sending $\tau_0 \to i\infty$ kills $C^f_T$ outright and
\eqref{eq:rf-normalized} collapses to the classical integral
\eqref{eq:classical-rf} --- whose familiar freedom from $\tau_0$ and
$\gamma$ is thereby revealed as the silent choices $\gamma = S$,
$\tau_0 = i\infty$.  Its interior coefficients are the periods against
Brown's rational basis,
$[r_f]_{X^{k-2-\ell}Y^\ell} = \omega^\pm(f)\, c_S^{\,\ell}$; the precise
coordinate statement is given in Section~\ref{sec:cocycle}.  The finite
$S$-contour was the right object all along, but it requires the
correction term in \eqref{eq:rf-normalized}, sourced entirely by the
$T$-contour --- and producing that correction in closed form is the
business of this paper.

\subsection{Manifestly finite L-functions for generic, meromorphic modular forms}

Nothing in Definition~\ref{def:HurwitzT}, however, requires $s$ to be an
integer --- and this is where the $L$-function arrives for free.
Promoting the Mellin monomial $\tau^{\ell - 1}$ to $\tau^{s - 1}$ on the
$S$-cycle and the Bernoulli pair to its Hurwitz parent on the $T$-cycle
defines the functional at the heart of this note:

\begin{definition}[L-integral of a meromorphic modular form]\label{def:L-integral}
Let $f \in F_k$ be a meromorphic modular form of weight $k \in 2\ZZ$ on $\SLZ$, and let $\tau_0 \in \HH$. Using the contour in Definition~\ref{def:contours} and the Hurwitz T-kernel in Definition~\ref{def:HurwitzT}, we define the \emph{L-integral} of $f$ to be,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lambda-DR-def}
  L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}
  , \gamma^T_{\tau_0})
  \;:=\; i^{-s}\!\left[
    \int_{\gamma^S_{\tau_0}}
    \! f(\tau)\,\tau^{s-1}\,d\tau
    \;+\;
    \int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}}
    f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \right].
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Both integrals are finite for every admissible contour pair and every
$s \in \CC$, by the same structural mechanism as before: compact contours
in the interior of $\HH$.  No regulator, asymptotic-subtraction argument,
or analytic-continuation step is required at any $s$.  And the
construction certifies itself: differentiating \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-def}
in $\tau_0$ leaves only endpoint terms, which the modularity of $f$
collapses to $f(\tau_0)$ multiplied by precisely the combination that the
unit-shift equation defining $\widetilde{k}_T$ sets to zero.  The
identity that defines the kernel is the identity that makes the
functional basepoint-independent: gauge-fixability of the cocycle and
well-definedness of the $L$-function are the same statement.

The specializations of \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-def} now recover the classical
$L$-function and each of its partial extensions as evaluations of one
finite functional.  For holomorphic cusp forms, the cusp limit
$\tau_0 \to i\infty$ sends the $S$-segment to the classical Mellin
contour while the $T$-segment vanishes exponentially: the L-integral is
the Hecke--Weil completed $L$-function.  (At finite $\tau_0 = i\Lambda$,
the $S$-segment alone is the $\Lambda$-truncated Mellin contour of
\cite{1806}; the $T$-segment is the missing piece that completes it to a
cocycle and renders the $\Lambda \to \infty$ limit unnecessary.)  For
weakly holomorphic $f$, where the cusp limit is genuinely pathological,
the opposite specialization is available: at the elliptic fixed point
$\tau_0 = i$ of $S$, the $S$-segment collapses to a point, and a single
unfolding of the surviving compact $T$-integral reproduces the
incomplete-gamma sums of \cite{BFK2011} term by term.  Their
non-holomorphic-regulator prescription is thereby revealed as one
closed-form evaluation of the finite functional --- not an independent
definition --- and, because the functional is $\tau_0$-independent and
finite on all of $M^!_k$, this identifies the BFK candidate as the
canonical $L$-function of any weak modular form.

\begin{theorem}[$L$-function for weakly holomorphic forms]\label{thm:bfk-eq}
Let $k \in 2\ZZ$ and $f \in M^!_k$.  For every $\tau_0 \in \HH$ and every
$s \in \CC$, the L-integral
$L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ of
\eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-def} is finite and independent of both
$\tau_0 \in \HH$ and of the admissible contour choices
$\gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0}$.  We write $L^*(f, s)$ for the
common value. %CLAUDE: trimmed -- the duplicate display (old label eq:Lambda-DR-general) is removed; grep the body for \ref{eq:Lambda-DR-general} and re-point to eq:Lambda-DR-def.
Specializing to $\tau_0 = i$, the $S$-integral in
\eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-def} vanishes, and $L^*$ reduces to a single integral
on the compact contour $[i - 1, i] \subset \HH$, with closed-form sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bfk-eq-statement}
  L^*(f, s)
  \;=\; i^{-s}\!\int_{i - 1}^{i}\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \;=\; -c_f(0)\!\left(\frac{1}{s} + \frac{i^k}{k - s}\right)
  + \sum_{n \ne 0}\! c_f(n)\!\left[
    \frac{\Gamma(s,\, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s}
    + i^k\,\frac{\Gamma(k - s,\, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}
  \right].
\end{equation}
The right side of \eqref{eq:bfk-eq-statement} is the completed
$L$-function $L^*(f, s)$ of $f$. These L-integrals reproduce the
Hecke-Weil Mellin transform for holomorphic modular forms, as
well as reproduce extant formulae from the literature for weakly
holomorphic modular forms~\cite{BFK2011}.
\end{theorem}

We unpack how Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq} naturally reproduces the
Hecke-Weil Mellin transform for holomorphic modular forms, as
well as reproduce extant formulae from the literature for weakly
holomorphic modular forms~\cite{BFK2011} in section~\ref{sec:Connection-w-previous-weak-MF-l-functions}.

Finally, for properly meromorphic $f \in F_k$, nothing in the
construction changes except the topology.  The punctured plane
$\HH_f := \HH \setminus \Gamma\!\cdot\! f^{-1}(\infty)$ fails to be
simply connected, the L-integral descends to homotopy classes of
admissible contour pairs, and dragging a contour across an interior pole
produces a jump given by a Cauchy residue weighted by the kernel carried
by the crossing cycle --- $\tau_p^{s-1}$ for the $S$-segment,
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau_p, s)$ for the $T$-segment.  Meromorphicity is
manifest rather than accommodated: interior poles cannot affect
finiteness, only topology, and the functional answers topology with
computable wall-crossing residues.

\section{The period-defining cocycle and its moment expansion}\label{sec:cocycle}

\paragraph{The polynomial coefficient system.}
Let $V_n := \CC[X, Y]_n$ be the space of complex homogeneous polynomials
in two auxiliary variables $X, Y$ of degree $n$, equipped with the
right $\Gamma$-action
\begin{equation}
  (P|_\gamma)(X, Y) \;=\; P\bigl(aX + bY,\, cX + dY\bigr)
  \qquad
  \text{for } \gamma = \begin{pmatrix}a & b\\ c & d\end{pmatrix} \in \Gamma.
\end{equation}
For a meromorphic modular form $f$ of weight $k$, write $n := k - 2$
throughout and form the $V_n$-valued $1$-form on $\HH$
\begin{equation}
  \omega_f(\tau) \;:=\; f(\tau)\,(X - \tau Y)^n\,d\tau.
\end{equation}
Modularity of $f$ implies that $\omega_f$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant in
the standard sense \cite{KZ1984}: under $\tau \mapsto \gamma^{-1}\tau$,
the weight-$k$ transformation of $f$ cancels the weight-$(-n)$ slash
on $(X - \tau Y)^n$, so the $V_n$-valued $1$-form $\omega_f$ is
invariant under the simultaneous action of $\Gamma$ on $\tau$ and on
the auxiliary variables $(X, Y)$.  %Equivalently, $\omega_f$ is a
%well-defined $V_n$-valued $1$-form on the modular curve $Y(1) =
%\Gamma\backslash\HH$ (modulo the $\Gamma$-action on $V_n$).  %DAM: I don't really understand that sentence, so I am striking it. It is a reframing that I suspect adds no substance to the paper, other than competence cues that I cannot back-up/do not deserve.

The construction of $\omega_f$ above uses only the weight-$k$ modularity
of $f$ and makes no cuspidality assumption: $\omega_f$ is well-defined
for every meromorphic modular form $f \in M^!_k$ (and indeed for
meromorphic forms with poles in the fundamental domain, provided
contours avoid the $\Gamma$-orbit of those poles), with the cuspidal
hypothesis $f \in S^!_k$ entering only later via
Lemma~\ref{lem:solvability-cuspidality}(c) as the solvability
condition for the period-polynomial coboundary equation.

\paragraph{The cocycle.}
The two-segment cocycle construction used in the present note is the
standard Eichler--Shimura cohomological setup~\cite{KZ1984}, in which a
weakly holomorphic modular form is integrated against the polynomial
coefficient system $(X - \tau Y)^n$ along contours from $\gamma^{-1}\tau_0$
to $\tau_0$ for the generators $\gamma \in \{S, T\}$ of $\Gamma$, with
periods and quasi-periods extracted from the resulting $1$-cocycle
modulo coboundaries.  The author was led to this presentation, and to its
quasi-period reformulation, through personal correspondence with
F.\ Brown~\cite{Brown2019Email}.  Concretely:
fix a basepoint $\tau_0 \in \HH$ not on the $\Gamma$-orbit of any pole
of $f$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cocycle-def}
  C^f_\gamma(\tau_0)
  \;:=\; (2\pi i)^{n+1}\!\int_{\gamma^{-1}\tau_0}^{\tau_0}\!\omega_f
  \;=\; (2\pi i)^{n+1}\!\int_{\gamma^{-1}\tau_0}^{\tau_0}\!
  f(\tau)\,(X - \tau Y)^n\,d\tau
  \quad \in \quad V_n \otimes \CC,
\end{equation}
the contour being any path from $\gamma^{-1}\tau_0$ to $\tau_0$ in
$\HH$ avoiding the $\Gamma$-orbit of the poles of $f$.  The integral
is independent of the choice of path within the same homotopy class in
$\HH \setminus \Gamma\!\cdot\! f^{-1}(\infty)$.

\begin{lemma}[Cocycle property]\label{lem:cocycle}
For all $g, h \in \Gamma$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cocycle-relation}
  C^f_{gh}(\tau_0) \;=\; C^f_g(\tau_0)|_h \;+\; C^f_h(\tau_0).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the map $\gamma \mapsto C^f_\gamma(\tau_0)$ is a
$1$-cocycle of $\Gamma$ with coefficients in $V_n \otimes \CC$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Using $(gh)^{-1} = h^{-1}g^{-1}$ and path-additivity through the
intermediate point $h^{-1}\tau_0$,
\begin{equation}
  \int_{(gh)^{-1}\tau_0}^{\tau_0}
  \;=\; \int_{h^{-1}g^{-1}\tau_0}^{\tau_0}
  \;=\; \int_{h^{-1}g^{-1}\tau_0}^{h^{-1}\tau_0}
  \;+\; \int_{h^{-1}\tau_0}^{\tau_0}.
\end{equation}
In the first integral on the right substitute $\tau = h^{-1}\sigma$, so $\sigma$
runs from $g^{-1}\tau_0$ to $\tau_0$.  The $\Gamma$-equivariance of
$\omega_f$ converts the integrand to $\omega_f(\sigma)|_h$, yielding
the first summand of \eqref{eq:cocycle-relation}.  The second integral
gives the second summand directly.
\end{proof}

A cocycle on $\Gamma = \SLZ$ is determined by its values on the two
generators
\begin{equation}
  S \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  T \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
subject to the relations $S^2 = (ST)^3 = -I$ (which act trivially on
$V_n$ for $n$ even).  We write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CS-CT-def}
  C^f_S(\tau_0) \;=\; (2\pi i)^{n+1}\!\int_{-1/\tau_0}^{\tau_0}\!\omega_f,
  \qquad
  C^f_T(\tau_0) \;=\; (2\pi i)^{n+1}\!\int_{\tau_0 - 1}^{\tau_0}\!\omega_f,
\end{equation}
since $S^{-1}\tau_0 = -1/\tau_0$ and $T^{-1}\tau_0 = \tau_0 - 1$.
These are the two integrals over the contour pair
\eqref{eq:contour-pair}.

\paragraph{Moment expansion.}
Expanding $(X - \tau Y)^n = \sum_{\ell=0}^{n} \binom{n}{\ell}(-\tau)^\ell
X^{n-\ell} Y^\ell$ and pulling the polynomial out of the integral defines
the moment integrals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-def}
  I_S^\ell(f, \tau_0) \;:=\; \int_{-1/\tau_0}^{\tau_0}\! f(\tau)\,\tau^\ell\,d\tau,
  \qquad
  I_T^\ell(f, \tau_0) \;:=\; \int_{\tau_0 - 1}^{\tau_0}\! f(\tau)\,\tau^\ell\,d\tau,
\end{equation}
for $\ell = 0, 1, \ldots, n$, in terms of which
\begin{equation}
  C^f_S(\tau_0)
  \;=\; (2\pi i)^{n+1}\!\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\!\binom{n}{\ell}(-1)^\ell\,
  I_S^\ell(f, \tau_0)\,X^{n-\ell}Y^\ell,
\end{equation}
and similarly for $C^f_T(\tau_0)$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:periods} extracts
$\omega^\pm(f)$ from particular linear combinations of these moments.

\paragraph{Basepoint dependence.}
Varying $\tau_0$ changes $C^f$ by a coboundary, provided $f$ has no
interior poles.  Define an antiderivative $F(\tau)$ of $\omega_f$ on a
simply connected open subset of $\HH$ avoiding $\Gamma\cdot f^{-1}
(\infty)$.  Then for any two basepoints $\tau_0, \tau_0'$ in the same
connected component, path-additivity gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bp-cocycle-coboundary}
  C^f_\gamma(\tau_0') - C^f_\gamma(\tau_0)
  \;=\; \bigl(P|_\gamma - P\bigr)
  \quad \text{with}\quad
  P \;=\; (2\pi i)^{n+1}\bigl(F(\tau_0') - F(\tau_0)\bigr) \in V_n\otimes\CC.
\end{equation}
This is the coboundary $\delta P(\gamma) := P|_\gamma - P$.  When $f$
has interior poles, dragging $\tau_0$ across a pole picks up a Cauchy
residue contribution beyond \eqref{eq:bp-cocycle-coboundary}; this
wall-crossing structure is the content of Theorem~\ref{thm:wall-crossing}
in Section~\ref{sec:wall-crossing}.

\paragraph{Parabolic cohomology and periods.}
The cohomology class $[C^f] \in H^1(\Gamma; V_n)$ is independent of
$\tau_0$ (on the connected component) and of the choice of $\delta P$
representative.  Its restriction to the cusp stabilizer
$\langle T \rangle \cong \ZZ$ lands in
$H^1(\langle T \rangle; V_n) = V_n / \im \delta_T \cong \CC\cdot[X^n]$,
and by Lemma~\ref{lem:solvability-cuspidality}(c) the restricted class is
$(2\pi i)^{n+1}\, c_f(0) \cdot [X^n]$: the residue of $\omega_f$ at the
cusp, valued in the $T$-coinvariants.  The class is \emph{parabolic} ---
cohomologous to a cocycle with vanishing $T$-value --- if and only if
this residue vanishes, i.e.\ if and only if $f \in S^!_k$.  The classical
Eichler--Shimura theorem \cite{KZ1984} identifies
$H^1(\Gamma; V_n) \cong S_k \oplus \overline{S_k} \oplus
\CC_{\mathrm{Eis}}$ and the parabolic subspace
$\Hpar(\Gamma; V_n) \cong S_k \oplus \overline{S_k}$; on cocycles
normalized so that $c_T = 0$, the assignment $c \mapsto c_S$ further
identifies $\Hpar(\Gamma; V_n) \cong W_{k-2}/\CC\,(X^n - Y^n)$, with the
quotient line accounting for the residual freedom
$P \mapsto P + \CC\, Y^n$.  When $\dim S_k = 1$, each parity eigenspace
of $\Hpar$ is one-dimensional, and the two coordinates of a parabolic
class in Brown's rational basis $p^\pm_f$ are its periods:
$[C^f]^\pm = \omega^\pm(f)[p^\pm_f]$ for $f \in S_k$, while the
quasi-periods $\eta^\pm$ arise as the coordinates of the class
$[C^{f'}]$ of a weakly holomorphic Hecke partner $f' \in S^!_k$ (at
$k = 12$: $f = \Delta$, $f' = \Dhat$)
\cite{Brown2017}. %CLAUDE: corrected -- the previous version presented \omega^\pm and \eta^\pm jointly as "the linear coordinates of [C^f]"; a single class in the two-dimensional space \Hpar has two coordinates, and the four numbers of the period matrix come from the two classes [C^\Delta] and [C^\Dhat].

\section{Period extraction: proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:periods}}\label{sec:periods}

Set $n := k - 2$ throughout.  The argument is $k$-uniform on the
$\dim S_k = 1$ family $k \in \{12$, $16$, $18$, $20$, $22$, $26\}$: every step
below --- the cocycle structure of Section~\ref{sec:cocycle}, the
$\delta_T$-solvability of Lemma~\ref{lem:solvability-cuspidality},
the Bernoulli closed-form inversion of
Lemma~\ref{lem:Bernoulli-inv}, the Hurwitz-zeta kernel
identification of Proposition~\ref{prop:kernels}, and the
$\ell$- and $\tau_0$-independence of Corollary~\ref{cor:tau0-ell-indep} ---
holds for every even $k$ with
the Brown cuspidal-basis coefficient $c_S^{\,\ell - 1}$ replaced by the
relevant weight's basis-polynomial coefficient.  The lower even weights
$k \in \{2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 14\}$ in the $\dim S_k \le 1$ family have
$S_k = 0$, so the theorem is vacuous there.  Numerical verification at
$k = 12$ against Brown's published values for the canonical pair
$f \in \{\Delta, \Dhat\}$ is consolidated in
Appendix~\ref{sec:numerics}.

% --- Step 3 anchor: solvability $\iff$ cuspidality ---
\begin{lemma}[Solvability of $\delta_T P = C^f_T$ is cuspidality of $f$]\label{lem:solvability-cuspidality}
Define the linear endomorphism $\delta_T \colon V_n \to V_n$ by
$\delta_T(P) := P|_T - P$, where $T \in \Gamma$ acts on $V_n$ as
$(X, Y) \mapsto (X + Y, Y)$.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]\itemsep0.25em
\item $\ker(\delta_T) = \CC \cdot Y^n$.
\item $\operatorname{im}(\delta_T) = \{Q \in V_n : [Q]_{X^n} = 0\}$.
\item For any meromorphic modular form $f$ of weight $k = n + 2$ and any
  $\tau_0 \in \HH$ admissible for $f$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CfT-Xn-coeff}
  [C^f_T(\tau_0)]_{X^n} \;=\; (2\pi i)^{n+1} \cdot c_f(0),
\end{equation}
where $c_f(0)$ is the constant Fourier coefficient of $f$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, the equation $\delta_T P = C^f_T(\tau_0)$ is solvable for
$P \in V_n$ if and only if $c_f(0) = 0$, i.e.\ if and only if $f$ is a
(weak) cusp form.  When solvable, $P$ is unique modulo $\CC \cdot Y^n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) $T$ acts on $V_n$ as the translation $X \mapsto X + Y$, fixing only
powers of $Y$.  Hence $T$-invariants in $V_n$, equivalently
$\ker(\delta_T)$, are exactly $\CC \cdot Y^n$.

(b) Writing $P = \sum_{\ell = 0}^{n} p_\ell X^{n - \ell} Y^\ell$ and expanding
$(X + Y)^{n - \ell}$ binomially,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta-T-matrix}
  [\delta_T P]_{X^{n - m} Y^m}
  \;=\; \sum_{\ell = 0}^{m - 1}\!\binom{n - \ell}{m - \ell}\,p_\ell
  \qquad (m = 0, 1, \ldots, n).
\end{equation}
This is strictly lower-triangular in the ordered basis
$(X^n, X^{n - 1}Y, \ldots, Y^n)$: the $m = 0$ row is the empty sum,
identically zero, and the off-diagonal entries
$\binom{n - \ell}{m - \ell}$ for $\ell < m$ are positive.  The $m = 0$ row
forces $[\delta_T P]_{X^n} = 0$ for every $P$, while rows
$m = 1, \ldots, n$ determine $p_0, \ldots, p_{n - 1}$ uniquely from any
prescribed right-hand side, with $p_n$ unconstrained.  Hence
$\operatorname{im}(\delta_T) = \{Q \in V_n : [Q]_{X^n} = 0\}$, and the
solution $P$ is unique modulo $\CC \cdot Y^n$.

(c) The moment expansion \eqref{eq:CS-CT-def} gives
$[C^f_T(\tau_0)]_{X^n} = (2\pi i)^{n+1}\,I^0_T(f, \tau_0)$ where
$I^0_T(f, \tau_0) = \int_{\tau_0 - 1}^{\tau_0}\!f(\tau)\,d\tau$.
Substituting the Fourier expansion $f(\tau) = \sum_n c_f(n)\,
e^{2\pi i n \tau}$, absolutely convergent on the compact $T$-segment,
and integrating term by term,
\begin{equation}
  \int_{\tau_0 - 1}^{\tau_0}\!e^{2\pi i n \tau}\,d\tau
  \;=\; \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } n = 0,\\
                       0 & \text{if } n \ne 0,
        \end{cases}
\end{equation}
by $T$-periodicity over a unit segment.  Hence $I^0_T(f, \tau_0) = c_f(0)$
for every admissible $\tau_0$, which proves \eqref{eq:CfT-Xn-coeff}.

Combining (b) and (c) gives the solvability statement.
\end{proof}

% --- Step 4 anchor: Bernoulli closed-form inversion of \delta_T ---
\begin{lemma}[Bernoulli closed form for $\delta_T^{-1}$]\label{lem:Bernoulli-inv}
For every $Q \in V_n$ with vanishing $X^n$-coefficient there exists
$P \in V_n$ unique modulo $\CC \cdot Y^n$ with $\delta_T P = Q$.
Writing $Q = \sum_{r = 1}^{n} q_r\,X^{n - r} Y^r$ and
$P = \sum_{m = 0}^{n} p_m\,X^{n - m} Y^m$, the solution is given in
closed form by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bernoulli-inv}
  p_m \;=\; \sum_{r = 1}^{m + 1}\!\frac{B_{m - r + 1}\,(n - r)!}{(m - r + 1)!\,(n - m)!}\,q_r
  \qquad (m = 0, 1, \ldots, n - 1),
\end{equation}
where $B_\ell$ are Bernoulli numbers ($B_0 = 1$, $B_1 = -\tfrac{1}{2}$,
$B_2 = \tfrac{1}{6}$, $B_{2\ell + 1} = 0$ for $\ell \ge 1$); the
coefficient $p_n$ is unconstrained and parametrises
$\ker(\delta_T) = \CC \cdot Y^n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The operator identity $T = e^{Y \partial_X}$ on $V_n$ (since $T$ acts as
the translation $X \mapsto X + Y$ by $Y$) gives
$\delta_T = e^{Y \partial_X} - 1$.  The operator $Y \partial_X$ is
nilpotent on $V_n$: each application reduces the $X$-degree by one, so
$(Y \partial_X)^{n + 1} = 0$ on $V_n$.  Consequently, the formal power
series in $Y \partial_X$ obtained from the Bernoulli generating function
$z/(e^z - 1) = \sum_{k \ge 0}(B_k/k!)\,z^k$ truncates to a finite
$\QQ$-linear combination of operators on $V_n$, and supplies the
closed-form inverse
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta-T-inverse-operator}
  \delta_T^{-1}
  \;=\; (Y \partial_X)^{-1}\,\frac{Y \partial_X}{e^{Y \partial_X} - 1}
  \;=\; \sum_{k \ge 0}\!\frac{B_k}{k!}\,(Y \partial_X)^{k - 1},
\end{equation}
in which the $k = 0$ summand is the formal antiderivative
$(Y \partial_X)^{-1}$, acting on monomials by
$(Y \partial_X)^{-1}(X^a Y^b) = X^{a + 1} Y^{b - 1}/(a + 1)$ (for $b \ge 1$),
and the $k \ge 1$ summands are differential operators that eventually
annihilate any polynomial of bounded $X$-degree.  Applied to
$X^{n - r} Y^r$ for $r \ge 1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta-T-inverse-monomial}
  \delta_T^{-1}\bigl(X^{n - r} Y^r\bigr)
  \;=\; \sum_{k = 0}^{n - r + 1}\!\frac{B_k}{k!}\,
        \frac{(n - r)!}{(n - r - k + 1)!}\,X^{n - r - k + 1}\,Y^{r + k - 1}.
\end{equation}
Summing $Q = \sum_r q_r\,X^{n - r} Y^r$ termwise and reading off the
$X^{n - m} Y^m$ coefficient identifies $k = m - r + 1$ (so $r$ runs over
$\{1, \ldots, m + 1\}$, $k$ over $\{0, \ldots, m\}$), yielding
\eqref{eq:Bernoulli-inv}.  Existence and uniqueness modulo
$\CC \cdot Y^n$ follow from Lemma~\ref{lem:solvability-cuspidality}(a, b).
\end{proof}

% --- Definition: Bernoulli T-segment kernel khat_T(\ell, tau) ---
\begin{definition}[Bernoulli $T$-segment kernel $\widehat{k}_T(\ell, \tau)$]\label{def:khat-T}
For non-negative integers $l$ with $l \le n - 1$, set the
$\QQ$-rational coefficients
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta-def}
  \beta_{l, r}
  \;:=\; \frac{(-1)^r\,\binom{n}{r}\,B_{l - r + 1}\,(n - r)!}{(l - r + 1)!\,(n - l)!}
  \quad \text{for } 1 \le r \le l + 1,
\end{equation}
and $\beta_{l, r} := 0$ for $r$ outside $\{1, \ldots, l + 1\}$.  For each
interior $\ell \in \{2, \ldots, k - 2\}$, define the $\QQ$-rational
polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:khat-T-def}
  \widehat{k}_T(\ell, \tau)
  \;:=\; \sum_{r = 1}^{n}\!\Bigl[\,\beta_{\ell - 1,\,r} \;-\; (-1)^{\ell - 1}\,\beta_{n - \ell + 1,\,r}\,\Bigr]\,\tau^r
  \;\in\; \QQ[\tau],
\end{equation}
of degree $\le n$.
\end{definition}

% --- Hurwitz collapse at integer s = \ell ---
\begin{lemma}[Integer-$s$ collapse of the Hurwitz-zeta $T$-kernel]\label{lem:Hurwitz-collapse}
For each integer $\ell \in \{2, \ldots, k - 2\}$, the Hurwitz-zeta
$T$-kernel $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ of \eqref{eq:tildekT-def}
specialised to $s = \ell$ collapses to the $\QQ$-rational polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kT-form}
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, \ell)
  \;=\; -\,\frac{B_\ell(\tau + 1)}{\ell} \;+\; (-1)^{\ell - 1}\,\frac{B_{k - \ell}(\tau + 1)}{k - \ell}
  \;\in\; \QQ[\tau]
\end{equation}
of degree at most $k - 2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Hurwitz-zeta collapse $\zeta(-m, a) = -B_{m + 1}(a)/(m + 1)$ for
non-negative integer $m$, applied at $m = \ell - 1$ and $m = k - \ell - 1$
(both non-negative for $\ell \in \{2, \ldots, k - 2\}$), together with
$e^{i\pi(\ell - 1)} = (-1)^{\ell - 1}$, converts \eqref{eq:tildekT-def} at
$s = \ell$ to \eqref{eq:kT-form}.
\end{proof}

% --- Bernoulli/Hurwitz polynomial identity + T-segment integral equivalence ---
\begin{lemma}[Bernoulli-form vs.\ Hurwitz-form $T$-kernel: polynomial identity and integral equivalence]\label{lem:T-piece-equiv}
For each interior $\ell \in \{2, \ldots, k - 2\}$, the Bernoulli
$T$-kernel $\widehat{k}_T(\ell, \tau)$ of \eqref{eq:khat-T-def} and the
Hurwitz $T$-kernel $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, \ell)$ of \eqref{eq:kT-form} are
related by the polynomial identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:khat-vs-ktilde}
  \widehat{k}_T(\ell, \tau)
  \;=\; (-1)^{\ell - 1}\,\binom{n}{\ell - 1}\!\left[\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, \ell) \;-\; \widetilde{k}_T(0, \ell)\,\right]
  \in \QQ[\tau]~.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the difference
$\widehat{k}_T(\ell, \tau) - (-1)^{\ell - 1}\binom{n}{\ell - 1}\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, \ell)
= -(-1)^{\ell - 1}\binom{n}{\ell - 1}\widetilde{k}_T(0, \ell) \in \QQ$
is a constant.  Consequently, for every $f \in S^!_k$ and every
$\tau_0 \in \HH$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T-piece-integral-equiv}
  \int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}}\!\! f(\tau)\,\widehat{k}_T(\ell, \tau)\,d\tau
  \;=\; (-1)^{\ell - 1}\binom{n}{\ell - 1}\!\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}}\!\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, \ell)\,d\tau~.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Polynomial identity.}  Both sides of \eqref{eq:khat-vs-ktilde}
are $\QQ$-rational polynomials in $\tau$ of degree $\le k - 2$, and
both have vanishing constant term: the left side by construction
(\eqref{eq:khat-T-def} sums from $r = 1$), and the right side because
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, \ell) - \widetilde{k}_T(0, \ell)$ vanishes at $\tau = 0$.
By direct comparison of the $\tau^r$ coefficients for $1 \le r \le k - 2$
using \eqref{eq:khat-T-def} for the left side and \eqref{eq:kT-form}
for the right side, together with the standard Bernoulli polynomial
expansion $B_n(\tau + 1) = \sum_{s = 0}^{n}\binom{n}{s} B_s \tau^{n - s} + n\,\tau^{n - 1}$,
the two polynomial coefficients agree.  Hence the polynomial identity
\eqref{eq:khat-vs-ktilde} holds in $\QQ[\tau]$.

\emph{Integral equivalence.}  The difference $\widehat{k}_T(\ell, \tau) -
(-1)^{\ell - 1}\binom{n}{\ell - 1}\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, \ell) = -(-1)^{\ell - 1}\binom{n}{\ell - 1}\widetilde{k}_T(0, \ell) \in \QQ$
is constant by \eqref{eq:khat-vs-ktilde}.  For $f \in S^!_k$ the
integral of any constant over the $T$-segment vanishes by cuspidality:
$\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}}\! f(\tau)\,d\tau = c_f(0) = 0$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:solvability-cuspidality}(c)).  Hence the constant
difference contributes zero and \eqref{eq:T-piece-integral-equiv}
follows.
\end{proof}

% --- Step 6 anchor: integral representation of [r_f] at interior monomial ---
\begin{proposition}[Integral representation of $r_f$ at the interior monomial]\label{prop:kernels}
Let $f \in S^!_k$ and $\tau_0 \in \HH$.  Let $P \in V_n / \CC Y^n$ be the
unique solution of $\delta_T P = (2\pi i)^{-(n + 1)} C^f_T(\tau_0)$
from Lemma~\ref{lem:solvability-cuspidality} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:Bernoulli-inv}, and define the modified-cocycle
$S$-value
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-def}
  r_f \;:=\; C^f_S(\tau_0) \;-\; (2\pi i)^{n+1}\bigl(P|_S - P\bigr)
  \;\in\; V_n \otimes \CC.
\end{equation}
For each interior $\ell \in \{2, \ldots, k - 2\}$, the coefficient of the
monomial $X^{n - (\ell - 1)} Y^{\ell - 1}$ in $r_f$ admits the integral
representation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-coeff-integral}
  [r_f]_{X^{n - (\ell - 1)} Y^{\ell - 1}}
  \;=\; (2\pi i)^{n + 1}\,(-1)^{\ell - 1}\binom{n}{\ell - 1}\!
  \left[\,\int_{\gamma^S_{\tau_0}}\! f(\tau)\,\tau^{\ell - 1}\,d\tau
        \;+\; \int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}}\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, \ell)\,d\tau\,\right],
\end{equation}
with $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ the Hurwitz-zeta kernel of
\eqref{eq:tildekT-def}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{$S$-piece.}  The moment expansion \eqref{eq:CS-CT-def} gives
\begin{equation}
  C^f_S(\tau_0)
  \;=\; (2\pi i)^{n + 1}\!\sum_{\ell = 0}^{n}\!\binom{n}{\ell}(-1)^\ell\,
  I_S^\ell(f, \tau_0)\,X^{n - \ell} Y^\ell,
\end{equation}
so $[C^f_S(\tau_0)]_{X^{n - (\ell - 1)} Y^{\ell - 1}} = (2\pi i)^{n + 1}
\binom{n}{\ell - 1}(-1)^{\ell - 1}\,I_S^{\ell - 1}(f, \tau_0)$, with
$I_S^{\ell - 1} = \int_{\gamma^S_{\tau_0}} f(\tau)\,\tau^{\ell - 1}\,d\tau$;
this contributes the $S$-segment piece of \eqref{eq:rf-coeff-integral}
against the bare Mellin kernel $\tau^{\ell - 1}$.

\emph{$T$-piece, Bernoulli form.}  $S$ acts on $V_n$ as
$(X, Y) \mapsto (-Y, X)$, so for $P = \sum_l p_l X^{n - l} Y^l$ one has
$[P|_S - P]_{X^{n - (\ell - 1)} Y^{\ell - 1}} = (-1)^{\ell - 1}\,p_{n - \ell + 1}
- p_{\ell - 1}$ ($n$ even).  By Lemma~\ref{lem:Bernoulli-inv}, each
$p_l$ ($0 \le l \le n - 1$) is the explicit $\QQ$-linear combination
$p_l = \sum_{r = 1}^{l + 1} \beta_{l, r}\,I_T^r(f, \tau_0)$
with $\beta_{l, r}$ from \eqref{eq:beta-def} of
Definition~\ref{def:khat-T}, obtained by substituting the rescaled
$T$-moments $q_r = \binom{n}{r}(-1)^r I_T^r$ of
$(2\pi i)^{-(n + 1)} C^f_T(\tau_0)$ into \eqref{eq:Bernoulli-inv}.  In
terms of $\widehat{k}_T(\ell, \tau)$ of \eqref{eq:khat-T-def}, the
$T$-segment piece of $[r_f]_{X^{n - (\ell - 1)} Y^{\ell - 1}}$ reads
$(2\pi i)^{n + 1}\!\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}}\! f(\tau)\,\widehat{k}_T(\ell, \tau)\,d\tau$.

\emph{$T$-piece, Hurwitz form.}  By Lemma~\ref{lem:T-piece-equiv},
\eqref{eq:T-piece-integral-equiv}, the Bernoulli $T$-integral of the
preceding paragraph equals
$(-1)^{\ell - 1}\binom{n}{\ell - 1}\!\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}}\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, \ell)\,d\tau$.
Combined with the $S$-piece, this yields \eqref{eq:rf-coeff-integral}
with the bare Hurwitz kernel $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, \ell)$ inside.
\end{proof}


% --- Brown-basis extraction of omega^pm and periods identity ---
\begin{lemma}[Brown-basis extraction of $\omega^\pm(f)$]\label{lem:brown-decomp}
Let $r_f \in V_n \otimes \CC$ be the modified-cocycle $S$-value of
\eqref{eq:rf-def}.  The Eichler--Shimura / Brown cuspidal-basis
decomposition (\cite{Brown2017,KZ1984}) reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Brown-decomp-statement}
  r_f \;=\; \omega^+(f)\,p^+_f \;+\; \omega^-(f)\,p^-_f \;+\; (\text{Eisenstein-direction term}),
\end{equation}
with the Eisenstein-direction term contributing only to the boundary
monomials $X^n$, $Y^n$ of $r_f$ (as it is a multiple of the coboundary
$Y^n|_S - Y^n = X^n - Y^n$; see Appendix~\ref{app:eichler-shimura} for
the distinction between this Eisenstein-\emph{direction} contribution
to $r_f$ and the period polynomial of an actual Eisenstein series).  Combined with the integral
representation \eqref{eq:rf-coeff-integral} of
Proposition~\ref{prop:kernels}, this yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:periods-derived}
  \omega^\pm(f)
  \;=\; \frac{(2\pi i)^{k - 1}\,(-1)^{\ell - 1}\,\binom{k - 2}{\ell - 1}}{c_S^{\,\ell - 1}}\!
  \left[\,\int_{\gamma^S_{\tau_0}}\! f(\tau)\,\tau^{\ell - 1}\,d\tau
        \;+\; \int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}}\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, \ell)\,d\tau\,\right]
\end{equation}
for every interior $\ell \in \{2, \ldots, k - 2\}$, with parity $\pm$
determined by $\ell - 1 \bmod 2$ and Brown basis coefficient
$c_S^{\,\ell - 1} := [p^\pm_f]_{X^{n - (\ell - 1)} Y^{\ell - 1}} \in \QQ$.
Identity \eqref{eq:periods-derived} is identity
\eqref{eq:periods-statement} of Theorem~\ref{thm:periods}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For interior $\ell - 1 \in \{1, \ldots, n - 1\}$, the
Eisenstein-direction term in \eqref{eq:Brown-decomp-statement}
contributes zero at the monomial $X^{n - (\ell - 1)} Y^{\ell - 1}$, so
$[r_f]_{X^{n - (\ell - 1)} Y^{\ell - 1}} = \omega^\pm(f)\,c_S^{\,\ell - 1}$.
Substituting \eqref{eq:rf-coeff-integral} and dividing through by
$c_S^{\,\ell - 1}$ yields \eqref{eq:periods-derived}.
\end{proof}

% --- Step 7 anchor: basepoint and index independence ---
\begin{corollary}[Basepoint independence]\label{cor:tau0-ell-indep}
The two-segment linear functional of Theorem~\ref{thm:periods},
$\omega^\pm(f) = \frac{(2\pi i)^{k - 1}(-1)^{\ell - 1}\binom{k - 2}{\ell - 1}}{c_S^{\,\ell - 1}}\!\bigl[\int_{\gamma^S_{\tau_0}}\!f(\tau)\,\tau^{\ell - 1}\,d\tau + \int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}}\!f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, \ell)\,d\tau\bigr]$
of \eqref{eq:periods-statement}, is independent of $\tau_0 \in \HH$, and
returns the same $\omega^\pm(f)$ for every interior
$\ell \in \{2, \ldots, k - 2\}$ in a fixed parity sector.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\emph{$\tau_0$-independence.}  By \eqref{eq:rf-def}, namely
$r_f := C^f_S(\tau_0) - (2\pi i)^{n + 1}(P|_S - P)$,
$r_f$ depends on $\tau_0$ only through the cohomology class
$[C^f] \in H^1(\Gamma; V_n \otimes \CC)$ and the choice of coboundary
representative $P$.  The class is $\tau_0$-invariant by the
basepoint-shift coboundary identity
$C^f_\gamma(\tau_0') - C^f_\gamma(\tau_0) = P|_\gamma - P$ for the
antiderivative $P = (2\pi i)^{n + 1}(F(\tau_0') - F(\tau_0))$ of
\eqref{eq:bp-cocycle-coboundary} (valid
for $f$ without interior poles), and the coboundary representative
$P(\tau_0)$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:Bernoulli-inv} is the
unique-modulo-$\CC Y^n$ solution of $\delta_T P = (2\pi i)^{-(n+1)} C^f_T$.
The residual $\CC Y^n$ ambiguity lies in the Eisenstein direction:
$Y^n|_S - Y^n = X^n - Y^n$ is a coboundary that contributes only to
the boundary monomials $X^n, Y^n$ of $r_f$.  Therefore the cuspidal
coordinates of $r_f$ --- the $\omega^\pm(f)$ --- are $\tau_0$-invariant.

\emph{$\ell$-independence.}  This carries no content beyond
Lemma~\ref{lem:brown-decomp}, where $r_f$ is shown to lie in the
$1$-dimensional cuspidal line $\CC\,p^\pm_f$: reading any interior
monomial of $r_f$ and dividing by
$c_S^{\,\ell - 1} = [p^\pm_f]_{X^{n - (\ell - 1)} Y^{\ell - 1}}$ returns
the same $\omega^\pm(f)$, for every interior $\ell$ in the parity sector.
\end{proof}

With Proposition~\ref{prop:kernels} (integral representation of
$[r_f]$ at interior monomials), Lemma~\ref{lem:Hurwitz-collapse}
(integer-$s$ collapse of $\widetilde{k}_T$), Lemma~\ref{lem:brown-decomp}
(Brown-basis extraction of $\omega^\pm$), and Corollary~\ref{cor:tau0-ell-indep}
($\ell$- and $\tau_0$-independence) in hand, we assemble the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:periods}.  For reference, the theorem statement is:

\begin{theorem}[Periods of $f \in S_k^!$ as an explicit linear functional ($\dim S_k = 1$) -- Theorem~\ref{thm:periods}, restated]\label{thm:periods3}
Let $k \in 2\ZZ$ with $\dim S_k \le 1$, and let $f \in S_k^!$.  For
the substantive cases $k \in \{12, 16, 18, 20, 22, 26\}$ where
$\dim S_k = 1$, the period and quasi-period coefficients
$\omega^\pm(f)$ are given by the explicit two-segment linear
functional
\begin{equation}\label{eq:periods-statement-restated}
  \omega^\pm(f) \;=\; \frac{(2\pi i)^{k - 1}\,(-1)^{\ell - 1}\,\binom{k - 2}{\ell - 1}}{c_S^{\,\ell - 1}}\!\left[
    \int_{\gamma^S_{\tau_0}}\!\! f(\tau)\,\tau^{\ell - 1}\,d\tau
    \;+\;
    \int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}}\!\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, \ell)\,d\tau
  \right]
\end{equation}
for every $f \in S_k^!$, every $\tau_0 \in \HH$, and every interior
$\ell \in \{2, \ldots, k - 2\}$, with parity $\pm$ determined by
$\ell - 1 \bmod 2$.  Here
$c_S^{\,\ell - 1} := [p^\pm_f]_{X^{n - (\ell - 1)} Y^{\ell - 1}} \in \QQ$ is the
Brown cuspidal-basis coefficient at the matched monomial
(\cite{Brown2017,KZ1984}), $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ is the
Hurwitz-zeta $T$-kernel of \eqref{eq:tildekT-def}, and its integer-$s = \ell$
specialisation collapses via $\zeta(-m, a) = -B_{m + 1}(a)/(m + 1)$
($m \ge 0$) to the $\QQ$-rational polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kS-form-restated}
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, \ell) \;=\; -\,\frac{B_\ell(\tau + 1)}{\ell} \;+\; (-1)^{\ell - 1}\,\frac{B_{k - \ell}(\tau + 1)}{k - \ell}
  \;\in\; \QQ[\tau]
\end{equation}
of degree at most $k - 2$.  The right side of
\eqref{eq:periods-statement-restated} is independent of $\tau_0 \in \HH$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:periods}, aka Theorem~\ref{thm:periods3}.]
The proof proceeds in seven steps.  Steps 1--3 motivate the operator
$\delta_T$ as the natural object whose inversion delivers the cuspidal
cocycle representative.  Step 4 (Lemma~\ref{lem:Bernoulli-inv})
performs the inversion in closed form.  Steps 5--7 read $\omega^\pm(f)$
off the resulting period polynomial.

\paragraph{Step 1: the canonical cuspidal representative has $C^f_T = 0$.}
Within the cohomology class $[C^f] \in H^1(\Gamma; V_n)$, consider the
representative with vanishing $T$-component.  When $C_T = 0$, the
cocycle relations $C_{S^2} = 0$ and $C_{(ST)^3} = 0$ collapse onto
$C_S$ alone, and the surviving relations on $C_S$ are exactly the two
Eichler--Shimura period-polynomial relations $r_f|(1 + S) = 0$ and
$r_f|(1 + U + U^2) = 0$ with $U = TS$.  The $C_T = 0$ representative
therefore has its $S$-value in the Eichler--Shimura period-polynomial
space, where Brown's basis $\{p^+_f,\, p^-_f,\, \text{Eisenstein}\}$
is defined and the cuspidal coordinates are $\omega^\pm(f)$.  The
proof below kills $C^f_T$ by a coboundary modification.

\paragraph{Step 2: kill $C^f_T$ by a coboundary modification.}
The only freedom inside the cohomology class is to add a coboundary
$\delta P(\gamma) := P|_\gamma - P$ for some $P \in V_n$.  Under this
modification, $C^f_T(\tau_0) \mapsto C^f_T(\tau_0) - (P|_T - P)$.
Killing $C^f_T$ becomes the equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta-T-equation}
  \delta_T P \;:=\; P|_T - P \;=\; C^f_T(\tau_0),
\end{equation}
to be solved for $P \in V_n$.  This identifies the linear endomorphism
$\delta_T \colon V_n \to V_n$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:solvability-cuspidality}
as the precise object whose inversion delivers the desired coboundary
representative.

\paragraph{Step 3: solvability of $\delta_T P = C^f_T$ is exactly cuspidality of $f$.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:solvability-cuspidality} computes $[C^f_T(\tau_0)]_{X^n}
= (2\pi i)^{n+1} c_f(0)$ and shows that $\operatorname{im}(\delta_T) = \{Q
\in V_n : [Q]_{X^n} = 0\}$.  Solvability of \eqref{eq:delta-T-equation}
is therefore equivalent to $c_f(0) = 0$, i.e.\ to $f$ being a (weak)
cusp form.  The cuspidality hypothesis $f \in S^!_k$ is not a separate
input to the construction: it 
is the solvability condition of the
coboundary equation in Step 2.

\paragraph{Step 4: closed-form Bernoulli inversion of $\delta_T$.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:Bernoulli-inv} inverts $\delta_T$ in closed form, giving
the coboundary representative
$P = \delta_T^{-1}\bigl((2\pi i)^{-(n+1)} C^f_T(\tau_0)\bigr) \in V_n/\CC Y^n$
explicitly in Bernoulli numbers.

\paragraph{Step 5: the modified cocycle and its $S$-component $r_f$.}
With $P := \delta_T^{-1}\bigl((2\pi i)^{-(n + 1)} C^f_T(\tau_0)\bigr) \in
V_n/\CC Y^n$ from Step 4, the modified cocycle
$C'^f := C^f - (2\pi i)^{n + 1}\delta P$ has $C'^f_T = 0$ by construction,
and its $S$-value
$r_f := C'^f_S(\tau_0) = C^f_S(\tau_0) - (2\pi i)^{n + 1}(P|_S - P)$
of \eqref{eq:rf-def} is
the Eichler--Shimura period polynomial of $f$.  By the
Eichler--Shimura / Brown cuspidal-basis decomposition
\eqref{eq:Brown-decomp-statement} of Lemma~\ref{lem:brown-decomp},
$\omega^\pm(f)$ are the explicit linear coordinates of $r_f$ along
$p^\pm_f$ in the $1$-dimensional cuspidal direction.

\paragraph{Step 6: unpacking $r_f$ into bare Mellin and Hurwitz integrals.}
Substituting the moment expansion of $C^f_S(\tau_0)$ from
\eqref{eq:CS-CT-def} and the Bernoulli expression for $P$ from Step 4
into \eqref{eq:rf-def}, and reading off the coefficient of $X^{n - (\ell - 1)}
Y^{\ell - 1}$ for each interior $\ell \in \{2, \ldots, k - 2\}$,
Proposition~\ref{prop:kernels} produces the integral representation
\eqref{eq:rf-coeff-integral} of $[r_f]_{X^{n - (\ell - 1)} Y^{\ell - 1}}$
with the bare Mellin kernel $\tau^{\ell - 1}$ on the $S$-segment and the
bare Hurwitz kernel $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, \ell)$ on the $T$-segment.
Lemma~\ref{lem:Hurwitz-collapse} (the integer-$s$ collapse of
$\widetilde{k}_T$) exhibits the $T$-kernel as a $\QQ$-rational
polynomial of degree $\le k - 2$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:brown-decomp}
(Brown-basis extraction) then divides through by $c_S^{\,\ell - 1}$ to
give \eqref{eq:periods-statement}.%, with all combinatorial structure
%$\bigl((2\pi i)^{k - 1},\,(-1)^{\ell - 1},\,\binom{k - 2}{\ell - 1}\bigr)$
%and the Brown basis-coefficient $c_S^{\,\ell - 1}$ sitting in the
%prefactor outside the bracket.

\paragraph{Step 7: $\ell$- and $\tau_0$-independence.}
Corollary~\ref{cor:tau0-ell-indep} supplies both: $r_f$ is a single
polynomial in the $1$-dimensional cuspidal direction, so every interior
monomial returns the same $\omega^\pm(f)$ (parity-selected by $\ell$),
while $\tau_0$-invariance follows from $[C^f]$ being a cohomology-class
invariant (the residual $\CC Y^n$ ambiguity touching only the
Eisenstein direction).

\end{proof}

\section{The Hurwitz-zeta extension and the $L$-function on $M^!_k$: proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq}}\label{sec:hurwitz-extension}

\subsection{Overview and the origin of the kernel}\label{subsec:hurwitz-overview}

We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq}: the functional
$L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ of
\eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general}, built from the Hurwitz-zeta $T$-kernel
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ of \eqref{eq:tildekT-def}, is finite and
basepoint-independent on $M^!_k$, and at the elliptic basepoint
$\tau_0 = i$ collapses to the completed $L$-function in closed form.
The argument has three movements: (i) the kernel obeys a
shift-difference identity (Lemma~\ref{lem:tildekT-coboundary}) that
renders the two-segment functional finite (Lemma~\ref{lem:Lstar-finite})
and locally constant in its contour data
(Lemma~\ref{lem:Lstar-local-constancy}), so $L^*(f, s)$ is well
defined; (ii) at $\tau_0 = i$ the $S$-segment degenerates and a single
per-mode \emph{unfolding} (Proposition~\ref{prop:hurwitz-unfold}) turns
the surviving $T$-integral into incomplete gammas; (iii) the resulting
sum is the completed $L$-function.  Lemmas~\ref{lem:Lstar-finite}
and~\ref{lem:Lstar-local-constancy} are stated for arbitrary
meromorphic $f$, in preparation for Section~\ref{sec:wall-crossing};
Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq} is their specialisation to $f \in M^!_k$,
where no interior pole obstructs the contour pair.

\paragraph{Why a Hurwitz-zeta kernel.}
The kernel \eqref{eq:tildekT-def} is the all-$s$ continuation of the
integer-$s$ inversion of Section~\ref{sec:periods}.  There, killing the
$T$-cocycle meant solving the shift-difference equation
$\delta_T P = P|_T - P = Q$ on $V_n$, and the operator identity
$\delta_T = e^{Y\partial_X} - 1$ inverted it through the Bernoulli
generating function $z/(e^z - 1) = \sum_k B_k z^k/k!$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:Bernoulli-inv}).  Dropping the polynomial restriction,
the kernel must solve the same shift-difference equation with a
power-law right-hand side --- the identity
\eqref{eq:tildekT-coboundary} of
Lemma~\ref{lem:tildekT-coboundary} below, whose right side is a
combination of pure powers $\tau^{\sigma - 1}$.  The function that
inverts the unit shift against a single power is the Hurwitz zeta,
$\zeta(\sigma, \tau + 1) - \zeta(\sigma, \tau) = -\tau^{-\sigma}$, so a
combination of Hurwitz zetas is the natural solution, and the
Bernoulli inversion of Section~\ref{sec:periods} is exactly its integer-$s$
shadow through $\zeta(-m, a) = -B_{m+1}(a)/(m+1)$ --- the Bernoulli
numbers being zeta values at non-positive integers.  An alternative
derivation, starting from the classical Mellin transform and demanding
contour-invariance, is recorded in
Appendix~\ref{app:hurwitz-derivation}; the present section is
self-contained without it.

\subsection{Well-definedness of the two-segment functional}\label{subsec:well-defined}

Write $\HH_f := \HH \setminus \Gamma\!\cdot\! f^{-1}(\infty)$ for the
upper half-plane with the $\Gamma$-orbit of the interior poles of $f$
removed, and call a contour pair
$(\gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ \emph{admissible} if
$\tau_0 \in \HH_f$ and both segments lie in $\HH_f$.  For
$f \in M^!_k$ there are no interior poles, so $\HH_f = \HH$ and every
pair is admissible.

\begin{lemma}[Shift-difference identity for $\widetilde{k}_T$]\label{lem:tildekT-coboundary}
The kernel $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ of \eqref{eq:tildekT-def} is
analytic in $\tau \in \HH$ and meromorphic in $s \in \CC$, and
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tildekT-coboundary}
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s) \;-\; \widetilde{k}_T(\tau - 1, s)
  \;=\; -\tau^{s-1} \;+\; e^{i\pi(s-1)}\,\tau^{k - 1 - s}
\end{equation}
for every $\tau \in \HH$ and every $s \in \CC$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Analyticity in $\tau$ is immediate: each Hurwitz factor
$\zeta(\,\cdot\,, \tau + 1)$ has second argument with
$\mathrm{Re}(\tau + 1) > 1$ on $\HH$, where $\zeta(\sigma, a)$ is
analytic in $a$.  For the identity, the Hurwitz raising relation
$\zeta(\sigma, a + 1) - \zeta(\sigma, a) = -a^{-\sigma}$
\cite[\S25.11.3]{DLMF} at $(\sigma, a) = (1 - s, \tau)$ and at
$(\sigma, a) = (s - (k - 1), \tau)$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \zeta(1 - s, \tau + 1) - \zeta(1 - s, \tau) = -\tau^{s-1},
  \qquad
  \zeta(s - (k-1), \tau + 1) - \zeta(s - (k-1), \tau) = -\tau^{k - 1 - s}.
\end{equation}
Since
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s) = \zeta(1-s, \tau+1) - e^{i\pi(s-1)}\zeta(s-(k-1), \tau+1)$
and, shifting $\tau \mapsto \tau - 1$,
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau - 1, s) = \zeta(1-s, \tau) - e^{i\pi(s-1)}\zeta(s-(k-1), \tau)$,
subtracting gives
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s) - \widetilde{k}_T(\tau - 1, s) = -\tau^{s-1}
+ e^{i\pi(s-1)}\tau^{k-1-s}$, which is \eqref{eq:tildekT-coboundary}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finiteness]\label{lem:Lstar-finite}
Let $f$ be any meromorphic modular form of weight $k$ on $\Gamma$.
For every admissible contour pair
$(\gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ and every $s \in \CC$, both
integrals in \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general} are finite, so
$L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ is well defined.  In
particular this holds for every $f \in M^!_k$ and every
$\tau_0 \in \HH$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By admissibility both segments are compact and lie in $\HH_f$, on
which $f$ is holomorphic; the kernels $\tau^{s-1}$ and
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ are analytic in $\tau \in \HH$ for each
fixed $s$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:tildekT-coboundary}).  A continuous
integrand on a compact contour has finite integral.  For
$f \in M^!_k$ there are no interior poles, $\HH_f = \HH$, and every
$\tau_0 \in \HH$ yields an admissible pair.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Local constancy and winding-invariance]\label{lem:Lstar-local-constancy}
Let $f$ be any meromorphic modular form of weight $k$ on $\Gamma$.
Then $L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ is unchanged
under any continuous deformation of the admissible data
$(\tau_0, \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ that does not move a
segment across a point of $\Gamma\!\cdot\! f^{-1}(\infty)$;
equivalently, it depends on the admissible pair only through the
winding of the two segments about the interior poles of $f$.  In
particular, for $f \in M^!_k$ --- where $\HH_f = \HH$ is simply
connected --- $L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ is
independent of $\tau_0 \in \HH$ and of the contour pair for every
$s \in \CC$; we write $L^*(f, s)$ for the common value.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Two kinds of variation can change the data: deforming the path shapes
with endpoints fixed, and moving the basepoint $\tau_0$ (which drags
the endpoints $-1/\tau_0$, $\tau_0 - 1$, $\tau_0$).

\emph{Path shape.}  If a segment is deformed within $\HH_f$ with its
endpoints fixed and without crossing a pole, the integrand
$f(\tau)\,K(\tau, s)$ (with $K \in \{\tau^{s-1}, \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\}$)
is holomorphic on the swept region, so the integral is unchanged by
Cauchy's theorem.

\emph{Basepoint.}  Differentiating \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general} in
$\tau_0$ --- legitimate by the finiteness of
Lemma~\ref{lem:Lstar-finite} --- only the endpoints contribute.  The
$T$-segment endpoints give
$f(\tau_0)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau_0, s) - f(\tau_0 - 1)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau_0 - 1, s)$,
and the $S$-segment endpoints give
$f(\tau_0)\,\tau_0^{s-1} - f(-1/\tau_0)\,(-1/\tau_0)^{s-1}\,\tau_0^{-2}$.
Using the modularity relations $f(\tau_0 - 1) = f(\tau_0)$ and
$f(-1/\tau_0) = \tau_0^k f(\tau_0)$ together with
$(-1/\tau_0)^{s-1} = e^{i\pi(s-1)}\tau_0^{1-s}$, the total
$\tau_0$-derivative of $L^*$ reduces to
\begin{equation}
  i^{-s} f(\tau_0)\!\left[\widetilde{k}_T(\tau_0, s) - \widetilde{k}_T(\tau_0 - 1, s) + \tau_0^{s-1} - e^{i\pi(s-1)}\tau_0^{k-1-s}\right] = 0
\end{equation}
by the shift-difference identity \eqref{eq:tildekT-coboundary} of
Lemma~\ref{lem:tildekT-coboundary}.  Hence $L^*$ is constant under any
admissible motion of $\tau_0$.

Combining the two, $L^*$ is locally constant on the space of
admissible configurations and can change only when a segment crosses a
pole: it depends only on the winding data.  For $f \in M^!_k$ there
are no interior poles, $\HH_f = \HH$ is simply connected, every
admissible configuration is joined to every other without crossing a
pole, and $L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ is
therefore globally independent of $\tau_0$ and of the contour pair.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The unfolding engine}\label{subsec:hurwitz-unfold}

\begin{proposition}[Hurwitz unfolding]\label{prop:hurwitz-unfold}
For every nonzero integer $n$ and every $\nu \in \CC$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hurwitz-unfold}
  \int_i^{i+1}\! e^{2\pi i n \sigma}\,\zeta(\nu, \sigma)\,d\sigma
  \;=\; i^{1 - \nu}\,(2\pi n)^{\nu - 1}\,\Gamma(1 - \nu,\, 2\pi n),
\end{equation}
where for $n < 0$ the right side is read on the analytic continuation
with $\arg(2\pi n) = +\pi$.  At the integer values
$\nu \in \{1 - s,\, s - (k-1)\}$ used below (with $s$ an integer in the
critical strip), $1 - \nu$ is a positive integer $N$ and
$\Gamma(1 - \nu, x)$ is the entire polynomial-times-exponential
$\Gamma(N, x) = (N-1)!\,e^{-x}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1} x^j/j!$, single-valued for
either sign of $n$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $\mathrm{Re}(\nu) > 1$ the series
$\zeta(\nu, \sigma) = \sum_{m \ge 0}(\sigma + m)^{-\nu}$ converges
uniformly on $[i, i+1]$ \cite[\S25.11]{DLMF}; substituting
$u = \sigma + m$, the periodicity $e^{2\pi i n m} = 1$ glues the unit
segments into a single ray --- the \emph{unfolding}, after
Rankin--Selberg:
\begin{equation}
  \int_i^{i+1}\! e^{2\pi i n \sigma}\,\zeta(\nu, \sigma)\,d\sigma
  \;=\; \int_i^{i+\infty}\! e^{2\pi i n u}\,u^{-\nu}\,du.
\end{equation}
Substituting $t = -2\pi i n u$ and recalling
$\Gamma(\sigma, x) = \int_x^\infty t^{\sigma - 1}e^{-t}\,dt$
\cite[\S8.2.2]{DLMF} evaluates the ray integral as
\begin{equation}
  \int_i^{i+\infty}\! e^{2\pi i n u}\,u^{-\nu}\,du
  \;=\; i^{1 - \nu}\,(2\pi n)^{\nu - 1}\,\Gamma(1 - \nu,\, 2\pi n),
\end{equation}
which is \eqref{eq:hurwitz-unfold} for $\mathrm{Re}(\nu) > 1$ (for
$n < 0$ the path runs to $+i\infty$, giving the $\arg(2\pi n) = +\pi$
continuation of $\Gamma$).  Both sides are entire in $\nu$
($\Gamma$ by \cite[\S8.2.7]{DLMF}; the left integral since the
$\zeta$-pole at argument $1$ integrates to zero over a unit segment),
so the identity extends to all $\nu \in \CC$.
\end{proof}

The same unfolding at an arbitrary base point $\tau_0 \in \HH$ in place
of $i$ --- the translate-gluing
$\sum_{m \ge 0}\int_{\tau_0 + m}^{\tau_0 + m + 1} = \int_{\tau_0}^{\tau_0 + \infty}$
is base-point-independent --- gives, for every nonzero integer $n$ and
every $\nu \in \CC$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hurwitz-unfold-general}
  \int_{\tau_0}^{\tau_0 + 1}\! e^{2\pi i n \sigma}\,\zeta(\nu, \sigma)\,d\sigma
  \;=\; i^{1 - \nu}\,(2\pi n)^{\nu - 1}\,\Gamma\!\bigl(1 - \nu,\, -2\pi i n\,\tau_0\bigr),
\end{equation}
whose second argument has
$\mathrm{Re}(-2\pi i n\,\tau_0) = 2\pi n\,\im(\tau_0)$ --- positive for
$n > 0$, negative for $n < 0$ --- with the $\arg = +\pi$ continuation in
the latter case.  Equation \eqref{eq:hurwitz-unfold} is the case
$\tau_0 = i$; we use \eqref{eq:hurwitz-unfold-general} in
Section~\ref{sec:wall-crossing}.

\begin{lemma}[Constant-mode integral]\label{lem:const-mode}
For every $s \in \CC$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:const-mode-integral}
  \int_{i - 1}^{i}\!\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \;=\; -\frac{i^s}{s} \;+\; e^{i\pi(s-1)}\,\frac{i^{k - s}}{k - s}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The Hurwitz zeta has antiderivative
$\int \zeta(\sigma, a)\,da = -\zeta(\sigma - 1, a)/(\sigma - 1) + C$ for
$\sigma \ne 1$; evaluating across the shifted segment and applying the
raising identity
$\zeta(\sigma - 1, a + 1) - \zeta(\sigma - 1, a) = -a^{1 - \sigma}$ at
$a = i$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hurwitz-antideriv}
  \int_{i - 1}^{i}\! \zeta(\sigma, \tau + 1)\,d\tau
  \;=\; \int_i^{i + 1}\!\zeta(\sigma, u)\,du
  \;=\; \frac{i^{1 - \sigma}}{\sigma - 1}.
\end{equation}
Applying \eqref{eq:hurwitz-antideriv} to the two halves of
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ at $\sigma = 1 - s$ and
$\sigma = s - (k - 1)$ yields \eqref{eq:const-mode-integral}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Restated theorem and proof}\label{subsec:bfk-proof}

For reference, the theorem statement is the following.

\begin{theorem}[$L$-function for weakly holomorphic forms -- Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq}, restated]\label{thm:bfk-eq2}
Let $k \in 2\ZZ$ and $f \in M^!_k$.  With $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$
the Hurwitz-zeta kernel of \eqref{eq:tildekT-def}, for every $\tau_0
\in \HH$ and every $s \in \CC$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lambda-DR-general}
 L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})
  \;:=\; i^{-s}\!\left[
    \int_{\gamma^S_{\tau_0}}\! f(\tau)\,\tau^{s-1}\,d\tau
    \;+\;
    \int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}}\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \right]
\end{equation}
is finite and independent of both $\tau_0 \in \HH$ and of the
admissible contour choices $\gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0}$.  We
write $L^*(f, s)$ for the common value. Specializing to $\tau_0 = i$ , the 
$S$-integral in \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general} vanishes, and $L^*$ reduces 
to a single integral on the compact contour $[i - 1, i] \subset \HH$, with
closed-form sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bfk-eq-statement}
  L^*(f, s)
  \;=\; i^{-s}\!\int_{i - 1}^{i}\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \;=\; -c_f(0)\!\left(\frac{1}{s} + \frac{i^k}{k - s}\right)
  + \sum_{n \ne 0}\! c_f(n)\!\left[
    \frac{\Gamma(s,\, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s}
    + i^k\,\frac{\Gamma(k - s,\, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}
  \right].
\end{equation}
The right side of \eqref{eq:bfk-eq-statement} is the completed
$L$-function $L^*(f, s)$ of $f$. These L-integrals reproduce the 
Hecke-Weil Mellin transform for holomorphic modular forms, as 
well as reproduce extant formulae from the literature for weakly 
holomorphic modular forms~\cite{BFK2011}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq}, aka Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq2}.]
The proof runs in seven steps: well-definedness (Steps 1--2), the
collapse at $\tau_0 = i$ (Step 3), the per-mode evaluation
(Steps 4--6), and the identification (Step 7).

\paragraph{Step 1: finiteness.}
For $f \in M^!_k$ one has $\HH_f = \HH$, so every $\tau_0 \in \HH$
gives an admissible contour pair and both integrals in
\eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general-restated} are finite for every
$s \in \CC$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:Lstar-finite}.

\paragraph{Step 2: $\tau_0$- and contour-independence.}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:Lstar-local-constancy}, specialised to the
simply-connected $\HH_f = \HH$ of a weak form,
$L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ is independent of
$\tau_0$ and of the admissible contour pair.  This establishes the
well-definedness assertion of \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general-restated}
and licenses writing $L^*(f, s)$ for the common value.

\paragraph{Step 3: collapse at $\tau_0 = i$.}
At the elliptic fixed point $\tau_0 = i$ the $S$-segment endpoints
coincide, $-1/i = i$, so $\gamma^S_i$ has zero length and the
$S$-integral in \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general-restated} vanishes.  By
Step 2 the value is unchanged, leaving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau0-i-collapse}
  L^*(f, s)
  \;=\; i^{-s}\!\int_{i - 1}^{i}\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Step 4: per-mode expansion.}
Expanding $f = \sum_n c_f(n)\,q^n$ in \eqref{eq:tau0-i-collapse} and
swapping sum and integral --- justified by uniform convergence of the
$q$-series on the compact segment $[i - 1, i]$, including the finitely
many polar modes, since $|q^{-1}| = e^{2\pi}$ is bounded there ---
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:per-mode-tau0-i}
  L^*(f, s)
  \;=\; i^{-s}\!\sum_n\! c_f(n)\!\int_{i - 1}^{i}\! e^{2\pi i n \tau}\,
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Step 5: the constant mode.}
For $n = 0$, Lemma~\ref{lem:const-mode} and the phase collapse
$i^{-s}\,e^{i\pi(s-1)}\,i^{k-s} = -i^k$ give the contribution
\begin{equation}\label{eq:const-term}
  c_f(0)\cdot i^{-s}\!\int_{i - 1}^{i}\!\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \;=\; -c_f(0)\!\left(\frac{1}{s} + \frac{i^k}{k - s}\right),
\end{equation}
the polar term of \cite[Lemma~2.1]{1806} (equivalently the polar piece
of \cite[Thm.~1.1]{BFK2011}) at the splitting point $B = 1$.

\paragraph{Step 6: the non-constant modes.}
For $n \ne 0$, substituting $\sigma = \tau + 1$ shifts the contour to
$[i, i + 1]$ with prefactor $e^{-2\pi i n} = 1$, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:hurwitz-unfold} applied to the two halves of
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s) = \zeta(1 - s, \tau + 1) - e^{i\pi(s-1)}\zeta(s - (k-1), \tau + 1)$
at $\nu = 1 - s$ and $\nu = s - (k - 1)$ gives
\begin{align}
  \int_i^{i+1}\! e^{2\pi i n \sigma}\,\zeta(1 - s, \sigma)\,d\sigma
  \;&=\; i^s\,(2\pi n)^{-s}\,\Gamma(s,\, 2\pi n),
  \label{eq:unfold-half1}\\
  \int_i^{i+1}\! e^{2\pi i n \sigma}\,\zeta(s - (k - 1), \sigma)\,d\sigma
  \;&=\; i^{k - s}\,(2\pi n)^{s - k}\,\Gamma(k - s,\, 2\pi n).
  \label{eq:unfold-half2}
\end{align}
The first half against the $i^{-s}$ prefactor gives
$\Gamma(s, 2\pi n)/(2\pi n)^s$; the second against the relative phase
$-e^{i\pi(s-1)}$ collapses via $-i^{-s}\,e^{i\pi(s-1)}\,i^{k-s} = i^k$
to $i^k\,\Gamma(k - s, 2\pi n)/(2\pi n)^{k - s}$.  Hence the per-mode
summand is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonconst-term}
  c_f(n)\cdot i^{-s}\!\int_{i - 1}^{i}\! e^{2\pi i n \tau}\,
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \;=\; c_f(n)\!\left[\frac{\Gamma(s, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s}
  + i^k\,\frac{\Gamma(k - s, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k - s}}\right],
\end{equation}
the $B = 1$ value of the cuspidal summand of \cite[Lemma~2.1]{1806}.
Summing \eqref{eq:const-term} and \eqref{eq:nonconst-term} over all
Fourier modes in \eqref{eq:per-mode-tau0-i} reproduces the closed-form
sum \eqref{eq:bfk-eq-statement-restated}.

\paragraph{Step 7: identification.}
The right side of \eqref{eq:bfk-eq-statement-restated} is the completed
$L$-function $L^*(f, s)$: for holomorphic $f$ it is the classical
Hecke--Weil Mellin transform, and for weak $f$ it is the
incomplete-gamma sum of \cite{BFK2011}, here obtained at every
$s \in \CC$ from a finite contour with no regulator.  This completes
the proof.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Worked example at $k = 12$: Bernoulli polynomials and numerics}\label{subsec:k12-worked}

At $k = 12$ the kernel of \eqref{eq:tildekT-def} is
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)
  \;=\; \zeta(1 - s,\,\tau + 1)
  \;-\; e^{i\pi(s-1)}\,\zeta(s - 11,\,\tau + 1).
\end{equation}
At integer values of $s \in \{1, 2, \ldots, 11\}$, both Hurwitz zeta
factors collapse to Bernoulli polynomials via $\zeta(-m, a) =
-B_{m+1}(a)/(m+1)$ ($m \ge 0$), and $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$
becomes a polynomial of degree $\le 11$ in $\tau$.  Three
representative values:
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, 1)
  \;=\; -\tau - \tfrac{1}{2} \;+\; \tfrac{1}{11}\,B_{11}(\tau + 1),
  \qquad
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, 6)
  \;=\; -\tfrac{1}{3}\,B_6(\tau + 1),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, 11)
  \;=\; -\tfrac{1}{11}\,B_{11}(\tau + 1) \;+\; \tau \;+\; \tfrac{1}{2}.
\end{equation}
The $s \leftrightarrow 12 - s$ symmetry follows from the
$s \leftrightarrow k - s$ structure of the two-term kernel (with
$i^k = +1$ at $k = 12$).

\paragraph{Identification with Theorem~\ref{thm:periods}.}
With the clean form of Theorem~\ref{thm:periods}, the $T$-segment
kernel of \eqref{eq:periods-statement} is exactly the integer-$s = \ell$
specialisation $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, \ell)$ of the Hurwitz-zeta kernel
of \eqref{eq:tildekT-def}, with no convention conversion required:
all combinatorial structure $(-1)^{\ell - 1}\binom{k - 2}{\ell - 1}/c_S^{\,\ell - 1}$
governing the $\omega^\pm(f)$ normalisation lives outside the
two-segment bracket.  The period-extraction functional
\eqref{eq:periods-statement} is thus the rescaled integer-$s$ shadow
of the all-$s$ $L$-function functional \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general}
of Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq}, with the bare Mellin $\tau^{s - 1}$ and
Hurwitz $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ kernels mapped to their integer-$s = \ell$
specialisations and the analytic prefactor $i^{-s}$ replaced by the
combinatorial Brown-basis factor.

\paragraph{Numerical values.}
The companion \texttt{mpmath} scripts \cite{VerifyScripts}, run at
$50$-digit precision with $f \in \{\Delta, \Dhat\}$, reproduce the
periods $\omega^\pm(\Delta)$ and quasi-periods $\eta^\pm(\Dhat)$ of
\cite{Brown2017} listed in Table~\ref{tab:k12-periods}, to relative
residual $\lesssim 10^{-40}$, the same for every interior
$\ell \in \{2, \ldots, 10\}$ and every basepoint
$\tau_0 \in \{0.3 + 1.2 i,\; i,\; 1.5 i,\; -0.4 + 0.8 i\}$.

\paragraph{Complex-$s$ verification.}
Beyond the integer-$\ell$ period extraction, the all-$s$ functional
\eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general} was checked directly against the canonical
completed $L$-function $L^*(\Delta, s)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq} at
non-integer $s$ \cite{VerifyScripts}.  At half-integer points
$s \in \{1.5, 2.5, \ldots, 6.5\}$, off-axis complex points
$s \in \{2.7 + 0.4i,\; 5 + i,\; 5.5 + 2.5i\}$, and points outside the
critical strip $s \in \{0.5,\, -0.3\}$, the finite-contour value agrees

\subsection{Classical Mellin recovery and canonical uniqueness.}\label{sec:Connection-w-previous-weak-MF-l-functions}
For $f \in S_k$ a holomorphic cusp form, taking the cusp-limit
$\tau_0 \to i\infty$ in \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general} collapses the
$S$-segment onto the classical Mellin contour $[0, i\infty]$ while
the $T$-segment vanishes exponentially (since $f(\tau) =
O(e^{-2\pi\im\tau})$ on the segment as $\im\tau \to \infty$):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Mellin-recovery}
  \lim_{\tau_0 \to i\infty}\!
  L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})
  \;=\; \int_0^\infty\! t^{s - 1}\,f(it)\,dt,
\end{equation}
the classical completed $L$-integral of $f$.  For $f \in M_k$ with
$c_f(0) \ne 0$ the same limit holds after a standard constant-term
subtraction (see \cite[Lemma~2.1]{1806} for the explicit polynomial
correction).

For $f \in M^!_k$ with cusp poles the cusp-limit is genuinely
pathological: $f(\tau) \to \infty$ as $\im\tau \to \infty$, so the
$S$-segment integral diverges along the imaginary axis, and the
$T$-segment integral $\int_{\tau_0 - 1}^{\tau_0}\! f(\tau)\,
\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau$ acquires a matching divergence of
the opposite sign as $\im\tau_0 \to \infty$ --- made precise in
Lemma~\ref{lem:cusp-decay}, where the $T$-segment is shown to equal an
explicit incomplete-gamma sum whose polar mode $q^{-M}$ contributes a
term growing like $|c_f(-M)|\,e^{2\pi M \im(\tau_0)}$.
The two divergences cancel at every finite $\tau_0$, so $L^*(f, s)$
is finite and well defined throughout $\HH$, but no naive Mellin
contour limit exists. By throwing-out $\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$ at the outset, 
one does violence to the result; the $\tau_0 \to i \infty$ limit of the S-contour 
alone diverges, necessitating regularizations of the type introduced in~\cite{BFK2011}. 
The construction of \cite{BFK2011} recovers
$L^*(f, s)$ on $M^!_k$ by a non-holomorphic regulator at the cusps;
Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq} shows that this regulator and the
finite-contour functional \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general} compute the
same object, term by term in the Fourier expansion.  In particular,
$L^*(f, s)$ is independent of any choice of regularisation: it is
the value of the finite-contour functional at any admissible
$(\gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$, and the BFK construction is
one closed-form evaluation of it (at $\tau_0 = i$).


\section{Wall-crossing for meromorphic forms: proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:wall-crossing}}\label{sec:wall-crossing}

\subsection{Overview}\label{subsec:wall-crossing-overview}

Theorem~\ref{thm:wall-crossing} has three parts.  Parts (a)
(finiteness) and (b) (dependence on the admissible contour pair only
through its homotopy class in $\HH_f$) hold for arbitrary meromorphic
$f$ and were already proved, without any cuspidality hypothesis, as
Lemma~\ref{lem:Lstar-finite} and Lemma~\ref{lem:Lstar-local-constancy}
of Section~\ref{sec:hurwitz-extension} --- stated there in exactly this
generality so as to serve here.  The new content of this section is
part (c): the explicit residue $\mathcal{R}_{\tau_p}(f, s)$ by which
$L^*$ jumps when the contour pair crosses an interior pole
(Lemma~\ref{lem:segment-crossing},
Proposition~\ref{prop:wall-crossing-residue}), assembled into the proof
of Theorem~\ref{thm:wall-crossing} below.  We then reduce $L^*$ to the
deformed-Mellin $L$-function $L_f^*(s)_{\rm int}$ of \cite{1806} on
forms regular at the cusp (Corollary~\ref{cor:1806-coincide}), record
the integer-$s$ period-polynomial form of the wall-crossing
(Corollary~\ref{cor:period-wall-crossing}), and state the per-mode
closed form for interior-pole forms regular at the cusp
(Proposition~\ref{prop:interior-pole-modes}).

\subsection{The wall-crossing residue}\label{subsec:wall-crossing}

We compute the jump in $L^*$ when a contour deformation drags one
segment across a single interior pole.

\begin{lemma}[Single-segment crossing]\label{lem:segment-crossing}
Let $f$ be a meromorphic modular form of weight $k$ with a simple pole
at $\tau_p \in \HH$, and let
$K(\tau, s) \in \{\tau^{s-1},\, \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\}$ be either
kernel.  Suppose a continuous deformation carries a segment from
$\Sigma_0$ to $\Sigma_1$ (within $\HH_f$ away from $\tau_p$) so that
the closed loop $\Sigma_1 - \Sigma_0$ encircles $\tau_p$ once with
orientation $\sigma \in \{+1, -1\}$ and no other pole.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:segment-crossing}
  \int_{\Sigma_1}\! f(\tau)\,K(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \;-\; \int_{\Sigma_0}\! f(\tau)\,K(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \;=\; 2\pi i\,\sigma\,\Res(f, \tau_p)\,K(\tau_p, s).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The kernel $K(\,\cdot\,, s)$ is analytic on $\HH$, so
$f(\tau)\,K(\tau, s)$ has a simple pole at $\tau_p$ with residue
$\Res(f, \tau_p)\,K(\tau_p, s)$ and no other singularity in the region
swept between $\Sigma_0$ and $\Sigma_1$.  The difference of the two
integrals is the integral over the closed loop $\Sigma_1 - \Sigma_0$,
which by the residue theorem equals
$2\pi i\,\sigma\,\Res(f, \tau_p)\,K(\tau_p, s)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Wall-crossing residue]\label{prop:wall-crossing-residue}
Let $f$ be a meromorphic modular form of weight $k$, and let
$(\gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ and
$(\gamma^S_{\tau_0'}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0'})$ be two admissible contour
pairs whose homotopy classes in $\HH_f$ differ by a single crossing of
a simple interior pole $\tau_p \in \HH$ of $f$, with $S$- and
$T$-segment winding numbers
$\sigma^S_{\tau_p}, \sigma^T_{\tau_p} \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wall-crossing-explicit}
  L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})
  \;-\; L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0'}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0'})
  \;=\; -2\pi i\,i^{-s}\,\Res(f, \tau_p)\!\left[
    \sigma^S_{\tau_p}\,\tau_p^{s-1} \;+\; \sigma^T_{\tau_p}\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau_p, s)
  \right],
\end{equation}
which is the residue $\mathcal{R}_{\tau_p}(f, s)$ of
\eqref{eq:residue-formula}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:segment-crossing} to the $S$-segment with kernel
$\tau^{s-1}$ and winding $\sigma^S_{\tau_p}$, and to the $T$-segment
with kernel $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ and winding $\sigma^T_{\tau_p}$.
Carried through the common prefactor $i^{-s}$ of
\eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-def}, the two contributions sum to
\eqref{eq:wall-crossing-explicit}.  At a generic wall exactly one of
$\sigma^S_{\tau_p}, \sigma^T_{\tau_p}$ is nonzero, according to which
segment crosses; both are nonzero only on the codimension-two locus
where the two segments meet $\Gamma$-images of $\tau_p$ simultaneously.
\end{proof}

For a pole of order $m \ge 1$ the same computation applies verbatim,
with $\Res(f, \tau_p)\,K(\tau_p, s)$ in \eqref{eq:segment-crossing}
replaced by the order-$m$ residue
$\sum_{\ell = 1}^{m} r_{f, \tau_p}(-\ell)\,\partial_{\tau_p}^{\ell - 1} K(\tau_p, s)/(\ell - 1)!$,
where $r_{f, \tau_p}(-\ell)$ are the principal-part Laurent
coefficients of $f$ at $\tau_p$; the generalization is routine, and we
record only the simple-pole case.

\subsection{Restated theorem and proof}\label{subsec:wall-crossing-proof}

For reference, the theorem statement is the following.

\begin{theorem}[$L$-function for meromorphic modular forms -- Theorem~\ref{thm:wall-crossing}, restated]\label{thm:wall-crossing2}
Let $k \in 2\ZZ$ and let $f$ be a meromorphic modular form of weight
$k$ on $\Gamma$.  Write $\HH_f := \HH \setminus \Gamma\cdot
f^{-1}(\infty)$ for the upper half-plane with the $\Gamma$-orbit of
the interior poles of $f$ removed, and call the pair
$(\gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ \emph{admissible} if
$\tau_0 \in \HH_f$ and both contours lie in $\HH_f$, with
$\gamma^S_{\tau_0}$ running from $S^{-1}\tau_0 = -1/\tau_0$ to $\tau_0$
and $\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$ from $T^{-1}\tau_0 = \tau_0 - 1$ to $\tau_0$.
Define $L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ by
\eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-def} with the Hurwitz-zeta kernel
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ of \eqref{eq:tildekT-def}.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]\itemsep0.25em
\item Both integrals in \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-def} are finite for every
  admissible $(\gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ and every $s \in
  \CC$.
\item $L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ depends on the
  admissible pair only through its homotopy class in $\HH_f$: any
  continuous one-parameter family of admissible pairs --- including
  variations of the anchoring basepoint $\tau_0$ --- leaves the value
  unchanged.
\item If two admissible configurations $(\gamma^S_{\tau_0},
  \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ and $(\gamma^S_{\tau_0'}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0'})$ lie
  in homotopy classes related by a single crossing of an interior
  pole $\tau_p \in \HH$ of $f$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wall-crossing-statement-restated}
  L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})
  \;-\;
  L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0'}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0'})
  \;=\; \mathcal{R}_{\tau_p}(f, s),
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:residue-formula-restated}
  \mathcal{R}_{\tau_p}(f, s)
  \;=\; -2\pi i\,i^{-s}\,\Res(f, \tau_p)\!
  \left[\sigma^S_{\tau_p}\,\tau_p^{s - 1}
  \;+\; \sigma^T_{\tau_p}\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau_p, s)\right],
\end{equation}
with $\sigma^S_{\tau_p}, \sigma^T_{\tau_p} \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$ the
winding-number contributions of $\gamma^S_{\tau_0}$ and $\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$ across
$\tau_p$ (the generic case has one nonzero and the other zero,
according to which contour crossed).  Higher-order poles contribute
via the corresponding derivatives in $\tau_p$ of
\eqref{eq:residue-formula-restated}, as recorded after
Proposition~\ref{prop:wall-crossing-residue}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Reduction on regular-at-cusps meromorphic forms}\label{subsec:reduction-to-int}

The two-segment functional makes the cusp behaviour of the $T$-segment
completely explicit: it is the BFK incomplete-gamma sum with the split
height promoted to the endpoint $\tau_0$.  We record this closed form
and its asymptotics, then specialise to forms regular at the cusp to
reduce $L^*$ to the deformed-Mellin $L$-function $L_f^*(s)_{\rm int}$
of \cite{1806}.

\begin{lemma}[$T$-segment closed form and cusp asymptotics]\label{lem:cusp-decay}
Let $f \in M^!_k$ with Fourier expansion $f = \sum_n c_f(n)\,q^n$.  For
every $\tau_0 \in \HH$ and every $s \in \CC$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T-segment-closed}
  i^{-s}\!\!\int_{\tau_0 - 1}^{\tau_0}\!\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  = -c_f(0)\!\left[\frac{(\tau_0/i)^s}{s} + i^k\frac{(\tau_0/i)^{k-s}}{k-s}\right]
  + \sum_{n \ne 0}\! c_f(n)\!\left[\frac{\Gamma(s, -2\pi i n\tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^s} + i^k\frac{\Gamma(k - s, -2\pi i n\tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}\right].
\end{equation}
Consequently, as $\im(\tau_0) \to \infty$ with $\mathrm{Re}(\tau_0)$
fixed:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]\itemsep0.1em
\item if $f$ is regular at the cusp with $f = O(q^N)$, $N \ge 1$, the
  $T$-segment integral decays as $O(e^{-2\pi N \im(\tau_0)})$ (up to a
  polynomial in $\tau_0$);
\item if $f$ has a cusp pole $f = c_f(-M)q^{-M} + \cdots$ with
  $c_f(-M) \ne 0$, $M \ge 1$, the $T$-segment integral grows as
  $|c_f(-M)|\,e^{2\pi M \im(\tau_0)}$, matching the cusp-pole growth of
  $f$ itself.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expand $f = \sum_n c_f(n)\,q^n$ on the compact segment
$[\tau_0 - 1, \tau_0]$ (uniformly convergent --- the finitely many
polar modes are bounded there, $|q|$ being bounded below) and integrate
term by term.  For $n \ne 0$, the base-$\tau_0$ unfolding
\eqref{eq:hurwitz-unfold-general} applied to the two halves of
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s) = \zeta(1 - s, \tau + 1) - e^{i\pi(s-1)}\zeta(s - (k-1), \tau + 1)$
at $\nu = 1 - s$ and $\nu = s - (k - 1)$, with the phase collapse
$-i^{-s}e^{i\pi(s-1)}i^{k-s} = i^k$ of Section~\ref{subsec:bfk-proof}, gives
the $n \ne 0$ summand of \eqref{eq:T-segment-closed}.  For $n = 0$, the
Hurwitz antiderivative of Lemma~\ref{lem:const-mode} at base $\tau_0$,
$\int_{\tau_0 - 1}^{\tau_0}\zeta(\sigma, \tau + 1)\,d\tau = \tau_0^{1-\sigma}/(\sigma - 1)$,
produces the bracketed constant-mode term (which at $\tau_0 = i$
reduces to the $-c_f(0)(1/s + i^k/(k-s))$ of
\eqref{eq:bfk-eq-statement}).

For the asymptotics, the constant-mode term is a polynomial in $\tau_0$,
hence sub-exponential.  In each $n \ne 0$ summand the incomplete-gamma
argument satisfies $\mathrm{Re}(-2\pi i n\tau_0) = 2\pi n\,\im(\tau_0)$,
and the uniform asymptotic $\Gamma(\sigma, z) \sim z^{\sigma - 1}e^{-z}$
as $|z| \to \infty$ for $|\arg z| < \tfrac{3\pi}{2}$ \cite[\S8.11]{DLMF}
gives $|c_f(n)|$-weighted decay $e^{-2\pi n\,\im(\tau_0)}$ for $n > 0$
and growth $e^{2\pi|n|\,\im(\tau_0)}$ for $n < 0$.  Taking the lowest
present mode yields (a) and (b).
\end{proof}

For $f \in F_k$ regular at the cusp ($f = \sum_{n \ge N}c_f(n)q^n$,
$N \ge 0$), part (a) of Lemma~\ref{lem:cusp-decay} drives the
$T$-segment to zero as $\im\tau_0 \to \infty$, which gives the
reduction to $L_f^*(s)_{\rm int}$.

\begin{corollary}[$L^*$ on regular-at-cusps meromorphic forms -- Corollary~\ref{cor:1806-coincide}, restated]\label{cor:1806-coincide2}
Let $f \in F_k$ be a meromorphic modular form of weight $k$ that is
regular at the cusp.  Let $L_f^*(s)_{\rm int,\,\gamma}$ denote the
deformed-Mellin $L$-function of $f$ along a contour $\gamma$ from
$0$ to $i\infty$ lying in $\HH_f$ (see \cite[\S2]{1806}).  Then for
every admissible $(\gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ whose
homotopy class in $\HH_f$ is compatible with $\gamma$ (in the sense
that the contour $\gamma^S_{\tau_0}$ together with the cusp-limit
piece $\lim_{\tau_0 \to i\infty}\gamma^S_{\tau_0} \to [0, i\infty]$
can be deformed to $\gamma$ in $\HH_f$),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1806-coincidence-restated}
  L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})
  \;=\; L_f^*(s)_{\rm int,\,\gamma}
  \qquad
  \text{for every } s \in \CC.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:1806-coincide}, aka Corollary~\ref{cor:1806-coincide2}.]
Take $\tau_0 = \eps + i\Lambda$ with $\eps \in \RR$ chosen so that
$\mathrm{Re}(\tau) = \eps$ avoids every $\Gamma$-image of an interior
pole of $f$, fixing the homotopy class compatible with $\gamma$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:Lstar-local-constancy} the value
$L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ is independent of
$\Lambda$ within that class.  As $\Lambda \to \infty$, the $T$-segment
integral tends to $0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:cusp-decay} (for $N \ge 1$;
for $N = 0$ the same holds after the constant-term subtraction of
\cite[Lemma~2.1]{1806}), while the $S$-segment $[-1/\tau_0, \tau_0]$
approaches the deformed-Mellin contour $\gamma_\eps$ along
$\mathrm{Re}(\tau) = \eps$ from $0$ to $i\infty$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
  L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})
  \;=\; \lim_{\Lambda \to \infty}\!L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\eps + i\Lambda}, \gamma^T_{\eps + i\Lambda})
  \;=\; L_f^*(s)_{\rm int,\,\gamma_\eps},
\end{equation}
which is \eqref{eq:1806-coincidence-restated}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Special values and the bare-Mellin residue}\label{subsec:special-values}

The wall-crossing residue $\mathcal{R}_{\tau_p}(f, s)$ of
\eqref{eq:residue-formula-restated} inherits its $s$-dependence from the
two kernels; only the $T$-kernel is singular in $s$.

\begin{lemma}[$s$-poles of the $T$-kernel]\label{lem:kT-spoles}
For fixed $\tau_p \in \HH$ and $k \in 2\ZZ \setminus \{0\}$, the kernel
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau_p, s)$ of \eqref{eq:tildekT-def} is meromorphic in
$s$ with exactly two simple poles, at $s = 0$ and $s = k$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kT-spole-residues}
  \Res_{s=0}\widetilde{k}_T(\tau_p, s) = -1,
  \qquad
  \Res_{s=k}\widetilde{k}_T(\tau_p, s) = +1,
\end{equation}
both independent of $\tau_p$; the bare-Mellin kernel $\tau_p^{\,s-1}$ is
entire in $s$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The Hurwitz zeta $\zeta(\nu, a)$ has a single simple pole, at $\nu = 1$,
of residue $1$ independent of $a$ \cite[\S25.11]{DLMF}.  In
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau_p, s) = \zeta(1 - s, \tau_p + 1) - e^{i\pi(s-1)}\zeta(s - (k - 1), \tau_p + 1)$,
the first term is singular only at $1 - s = 1$, i.e.\ $s = 0$, where
$\zeta(1 - s, \cdot) = -1/s + O(1)$ gives residue $-1$; the second only
at $s - (k - 1) = 1$, i.e.\ $s = k$, where
$\zeta(s - k + 1, \cdot) = 1/(s - k) + O(1)$ and the prefactor
$-e^{i\pi(k-1)} = +1$ ($k$ even) give residue $+1$.  Both residues are
$\tau_p$-independent, and $\tau_p^{\,s-1} = e^{(s-1)\log\tau_p}$ is
entire.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[$s$-poles of the wall-crossing residue]\label{cor:R-spoles}
The residue $\mathcal{R}_{\tau_p}(f, s)$ of
\eqref{eq:residue-formula-restated} is entire in $s$ in its $S$-segment
part, while its $T$-segment part has simple poles only at $s = 0, k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R-spole-residues}
  \Res_{s=0}\mathcal{R}_{\tau_p} = +2\pi i\,\sigma^T_{\tau_p}\,\Res(f, \tau_p),
  \qquad
  \Res_{s=k}\mathcal{R}_{\tau_p} = -2\pi i\,i^{-k}\,\sigma^T_{\tau_p}\,\Res(f, \tau_p).
\end{equation}
In particular $\mathcal{R}_{\tau_p}$ is entire in $s$ if and only if
$\sigma^T_{\tau_p} = 0$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Immediate from Lemma~\ref{lem:kT-spoles} and the form
$\mathcal{R}_{\tau_p}(f, s) = -2\pi i\,i^{-s}\Res(f, \tau_p)\bigl[\sigma^S_{\tau_p}\tau_p^{\,s-1} + \sigma^T_{\tau_p}\widetilde{k}_T(\tau_p, s)\bigr]$,
with $i^{-s} = 1$ at $s = 0$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Remark.}
The constant Fourier mode contributes the special-value poles
$-c_f(0)(1/s + i^k/(k-s))$ of \eqref{eq:bfk-eq-statement}.  By
Corollary~\ref{cor:R-spoles} an $S$-segment crossing shifts $L^*$ by an
amount entire in $s$ and so cannot disturb these special values, whereas
a $T$-segment crossing can.  This is the finite-contour reading of the
statement of \cite[\S2.4]{1806} that the special values are insensitive
to the wall-crossing ambiguity: it is exactly the assertion
$\sigma^T_{\tau_p} = 0$ at every crossed pole, which holds in the
cusp-limit class of Proposition~\ref{prop:1806-reduction}.

\begin{proposition}[Bare-Mellin reduction in the cusp limit]\label{prop:1806-reduction}
Let $f \in F_k$ be regular at the cusp, and let two admissible contour
pairs in the cusp-limit class of Corollary~\ref{cor:1806-coincide2}
differ by a single crossing of an interior pole $\tau_p$.  Then
$\sigma^T_{\tau_p} = 0$ --- the $T$-segment has retreated to the cusp,
where its $\widetilde{k}_T$-weighted integral vanishes by
Lemma~\ref{lem:cusp-decay}(a) --- so the jump is the single bare-Mellin
term
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bare-mellin-residue}
  \mathcal{R}_{\tau_p}(f, s)
  \;=\; -2\pi i\,i^{-s}\,\sigma^S_{\tau_p}\,\Res(f, \tau_p)\,\tau_p^{\,s-1},
\end{equation}
entire in $s$.  This is the single-pole term of the deformed-Mellin
wall-crossing \cite[Eq.~(2.65)]{1806} under the dictionary
$\chi(\tau_p) = -\sigma^S_{\tau_p}$, with the bare-Mellin residue
$i^{-s}\Res(f, \tau_p)\tau_p^{\,s-1}$ of the integrand
$i^{-s} f(\tau)\,\tau^{s-1}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the cusp-limit class the $T$-segment is sent to the cusp; for
$f$ regular there its $\widetilde{k}_T$-integral vanishes by
Lemma~\ref{lem:cusp-decay}(a), so no $T$-crossing occurs and
$\sigma^T_{\tau_p} = 0$.  Setting $\sigma^T_{\tau_p} = 0$ in
\eqref{eq:residue-formula-restated} gives
\eqref{eq:bare-mellin-residue}, entire in $s$ by
Corollary~\ref{cor:R-spoles}; the match with \cite[Eq.~(2.65)]{1806} is
Corollary~\ref{cor:1806-coincide2} applied to the residue.
\end{proof}

\noindent\emph{Honest weight.}  Proposition~\ref{prop:1806-reduction} is
a consistency repackaging of Corollary~\ref{cor:1806-coincide2} and the
residue theorem; the genuinely new content is
Lemma~\ref{lem:kT-spoles} and its special-value consequence,
Corollary~\ref{cor:R-spoles}.

\subsection{Wall-crossing of the period-polynomial cocycle}\label{subsec:period-wall-crossing}

The wall-crossing residue formula \eqref{eq:residue-formula} acts on
the complex-$s$ functional $L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$.  
Specialised at integer $s = \ell \in \{2, \ldots,
k-2\}$ and assembled into the $V_{k-2}$ moment expansion of
Section~\ref{sec:cocycle}, it gives the wall-crossing of the
period-polynomial cocycle of $f$ under contour deformation across an
interior pole.

\begin{corollary}[Period-polynomial wall-crossing residue]\label{cor:period-wall-crossing}
Let $f$ be a meromorphic modular form of weight $k$ on $\Gamma$, and
let $(\gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0}), (\gamma^S_{\tau_0'},
\gamma^T_{\tau_0'})$ be two admissible contour pairs related by a
single crossing of an interior pole $\tau_p \in \HH$ of $f$.  Denote
by $C^f_S(\gamma^S_{\tau_0}), C^f_T(\gamma^T_{\tau_0}) \in V_{k-2}\otimes\CC$
the corresponding $V_{k-2}$-valued cocycle values from
\eqref{eq:cocycle-def}.  For a simple pole $\mathrm{Res}(f, \tau_p)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cocycle-residue-jump}
  C^f_S(\gamma^S_{\tau_0}) - C^f_S(\gamma^S_{\tau_0'})
  \;=\; 2\pi i\,(2\pi i)^{k-2}\,\sigma^S_{\tau_p}\,
  \mathrm{Res}(f, \tau_p)\,(X - \tau_p Y)^{k-2},
\end{equation}
and analogously for $C^f_T$ with $\sigma^S_{\tau_p}$ replaced by
$\sigma^T_{\tau_p}$.  The integer-$s = \ell$ specialisation of
\eqref{eq:residue-formula} is the coefficient of
$\binom{k-2}{\ell}(-1)^\ell X^{k-2-\ell} Y^\ell$ in
\eqref{eq:cocycle-residue-jump} (combined for $S$ and $T$).  For
$\dim S_k = 1$ and $f \in F_k$ meromorphic with interior poles, the
period polynomial $r_f$ extracted via Theorem~\ref{thm:periods} from
$(\gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ therefore depends on the
homotopy class of the contour pair, with jump
\eqref{eq:cocycle-residue-jump} across each wall.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The integrand $f(\tau)(X - \tau Y)^{k-2}$ of \eqref{eq:cocycle-def}
has a simple pole at $\tau = \tau_p$ with residue
$\mathrm{Res}(f, \tau_p)(X - \tau_p Y)^{k-2}$, so a contour deformation
crossing $\tau_p$ with winding $\sigma_{\tau_p} \in \{-1, +1\}$
contributes $2\pi i\,\sigma_{\tau_p}\,\mathrm{Res}(f, \tau_p)(X - \tau_p Y)^{k-2}$
to the moment integral.  Multiplying by the cocycle prefactor
$(2\pi i)^{k-2}$ of \eqref{eq:cocycle-def} gives
\eqref{eq:cocycle-residue-jump}.  Expanding $(X - \tau_p Y)^{k-2}$ in
the monomial basis and matching coefficients of $X^{k-2-\ell} Y^\ell$ for
each $\ell \in \{0, \ldots, k-2\}$ reproduces
\eqref{eq:residue-formula} at integer $s = \ell$ (with $\tau_p^{\ell-1}$
arising from $X^{k-2-\ell} Y^\ell$ at $\ell \ge 1$ from the $S$-segment
contribution, and analogously for the $T$-segment via
$\widetilde{k}_T(\tau_p, \ell)$ at integer $\ell$).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Remark on the polylog-projection machinery}\label{subsec:polylog-remark}

The polylog projection $\widehat{P}_{\tau_p}\colon f \mapsto
\sum_{m > 0}r_{f, \tau_p}^*(m)\,\mathrm{Li}_{1 - m}(e(\tau - \tau_p))$
(see \cite[\S2.2]{1806}) subtracts the leading polar tails of $f$ at
each interior pole $\tau_p$, ensuring that the $\Lambda$-truncated
Mellin integral against $f$ has a finite $\Lambda \to \infty$ limit
(necessary in the deformed-Mellin construction because of the
Hagedorn-rate Fourier coefficients $|c_f(n)| \sim n^{p-1}\,
e^{2\pi n\,\im(\tau_p)}$ generated by interior poles
\cite[Prop.~1.1, 2.7]{1806}).  In the finite-contour formulation
\eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-def} the $\Lambda \to \infty$ limit is not taken,
so $\widehat{P}_{\tau_p}$ is not required for the existence or
local-constancy properties of $L^*$.  The
polylog-projection machinery does remain relevant, however, for the
\emph{closed-form per-mode evaluation} of $L^*$ in
incomplete gammas parallel to \eqref{eq:bfk-eq-statement}, where the
Hagedorn-rate Fourier modes must be split off and dealt with
separately before each mode contributes a clean incomplete-gamma
term.  For forms regular at the cusp this per-mode evaluation is
available in closed form, as follows.

\begin{proposition}[Per-mode closed form for interior-pole forms regular at the cusp]\label{prop:interior-pole-modes}
Let $f \in F_k$ be regular at the cusp, with interior poles at the
points $\tau_p$ of a fundamental domain and principal-part data
$r^*_{f, \tau_p}(m)$.  Then, for every admissible contour pair in the
homotopy class of Corollary~\ref{cor:1806-coincide}, $L^*(f, s)$ has
the per-mode closed form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interior-pole-modes}
\begin{aligned}
  L^*(f, s)
  \;=\;& -c_f(0)\!\left(\frac{1}{s} + \frac{i^k}{k - s}\right)
  \;+\; \sum_{n \ne 0} c_{Rf}(n)\!\left[\frac{\Gamma(s, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s} + i^k\,\frac{\Gamma(k - s, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k - s}}\right]\\
  &+\; \sum_{\tau_p}\sum_{m > 0} r^*_{f, \tau_p}(m)\,\bigl[\,I_{m-1}(s, \tau_p) \;+\; i^k\,I_{m-1}(k - s, \tau_p)\,\bigr],
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $c_{Rf}(n)$ are the Fourier coefficients of the
polylog-regularised part $(1 - \widehat{P})f$ (with $\widehat{P} =
\sum_{\tau_p}\widehat{P}_{\tau_p}$), and $I_{N}(s, \tau_p)$ are the
polylog integrals of \cite[\S2]{1806}.
\end{proposition}

We state Proposition~\ref{prop:interior-pole-modes} without
reproducing its derivation: it is the $B = 1$ specialisation of the
$L$-function of \cite[\S2]{1806} for $L_f^*(s)_{\rm int}$, obtained
there by the polylog projection $\widehat{P}_{\tau_p}$, and is
inherited here through Corollary~\ref{cor:1806-coincide}.  The
genuinely new regime --- $f$ with poles \emph{both} in $\HH$ and at the
cusp --- is reached by combining the interior projection with the
finite-contour cusp treatment of Section~\ref{sec:hurwitz-extension}, through
a Hauptmodul split that keeps both pieces modular.

\begin{lemma}[Hauptmodul split]\label{lem:j-split}
Let $k \in 2\ZZ$ with $S_{2-k} = \{0\}$ --- equivalently $k \ge 0$, or
$k \in \{-2, -4, -6, -8, -12\}$ --- and let $f \in F_k$.  Then $f$ splits
as $f = g + h$, with $g \in M^!_k$ having poles only at the cusp and $h$
regular at the cusp, carrying exactly $f$'s interior principal parts.
For $k \ge 4$ the split is explicit: $f/E_k$ is a weight-$0$ modular
function ($E_k = 1 + O(q)$ at the cusp), hence rational in the Hauptmodul
$j$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:j-split}
  f \;=\; \underbrace{E_k\,P(j)}_{\textstyle g}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\sum_{\tau_p}\sum_{l \ge 1} a_{\tau_p, l}\,
        \frac{E_k}{(j - j(\tau_p))^{l}}}_{\textstyle h}
\end{equation}
is its polynomial part plus the partial fractions over the interior
poles $\tau_p$, each $E_k/(j - j(\tau_p))^{l}$ being regular at the cusp
($O(q^{l})$ there).  Equivalently, clearing the interior poles by
$\Phi = \prod_{\tau_p}(j - j(\tau_p))^{m_p}$ gives $f\Phi \in M^!_k$, and
\eqref{eq:j-split} is the $j$-partial-fraction of $f = (f\Phi)/\Phi$.
Both $g$ and $h$ are weight-$k$ modular.

\emph{Role of $S_{2-k}$.}  What the split needs is that $f$'s cusp
principal part be realizable in $M^!_k$, and this is exactly the
condition $S_{2-k} = \{0\}$.  Indeed, for $G \in S_{2-k}$ the product
$f\,G\,d\tau$ has weight $k + (2-k) = 2$ and so is a meromorphic
differential on $X(1)$, whose residues sum to zero:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residue-obstruction}
  \Res_{\infty}(f\,G\,d\tau) \;=\; -\sum_{\tau_p}\Res_{\tau_p}(f\,G\,d\tau).
\end{equation}
The cusp principal part is realizable --- equivalently the split exists
--- iff the left-hand side vanishes for every $G \in S_{2-k}$, which is
automatic when $S_{2-k} = \{0\}$.  At the exotic weights $k = -10$ and
$k \le -14$, where $S_{2-k} \ne \{0\}$, \eqref{eq:residue-obstruction}
is a genuine constraint that can force $h$ to retain a cusp pole; there
one evaluates $L^*(f)$ directly from the finite contour (still finite by
Lemma~\ref{lem:Lstar-finite}).  (At $\tau_p \in \{i, \rho\}$ the powers
$l$ follow the ramification of $j$.)
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[Per-mode closed form, poles interior and at the cusp]\label{prop:both-poles}
Let $k \in 2\ZZ$ with $S_{2-k} = \{0\}$ and $f \in F_k$ have poles both
in $\HH$ and at the cusp.  Then $L^*(f, s)$ is finite for every $s \in \CC$ at every
admissible $\tau_0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:Lstar-finite}) and has the per-mode
closed form \eqref{eq:interior-pole-modes}, where now $c_{Rf}(n)$ are
the Fourier coefficients of the cusp part $g \in M^!_k$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:j-split}, including its cusp-polar modes $n < 0$.  The
cusp-regularity hypothesis of Proposition~\ref{prop:interior-pole-modes}
is thereby removed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $f = g + h$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:j-split}: $g \in M^!_k$, $h$ is
regular at the cusp with $f$'s interior poles, and both share a contour
admissible for $f$.  By linearity, $L^*(f, s) = L^*(g, s) + L^*(h, s)$.
The cusp part is evaluated by Theorem~\ref{thm:bfk-eq} /
\eqref{eq:bfk-eq-statement} --- valid for $g \in M^!_k$ of any cusp
order --- giving $-c_f(0)(1/s + i^k/(k-s))$ and the incomplete-gamma
sum over the Fourier modes of $g$; the cusp-regular $h$ is evaluated by
Proposition~\ref{prop:interior-pole-modes}, contributing the interior
polylog terms
$\sum_{\tau_p}\sum_m r^*_{f, \tau_p}(m)\bigl[I_{m-1}(s, \tau_p) + i^k I_{m-1}(k-s, \tau_p)\bigr]$.
Their sum is \eqref{eq:interior-pole-modes}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Remark.}
The two parts are read at opposite fixed points: $g \in M^!_k$ at
$\tau_0 = i$, where the $S$-segment degenerates and
\eqref{eq:bfk-eq-statement} applies; $h$ in the cusp limit
$\tau_0 \to i\infty$, where the $T$-segment dies
(Lemma~\ref{lem:cusp-decay}(a)) and the deformed-Mellin form of
\cite{1806} applies.  No new integral is computed:
\eqref{eq:interior-pole-modes} is a regrouping of
\eqref{eq:bfk-eq-statement} and the interior polylog terms.  The split
is available exactly when $S_{2-k} = \{0\}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:j-split});
at the exotic weights where it is not, $L^*(f)$ is still finite
(Lemma~\ref{lem:Lstar-finite}) and is taken directly from the finite
contour.

\section{Outlook}\label{sec:outlook}

Two questions are left open.  First, the geometric content of the bare
Mellin kernel $f(\tau)\,\tau^{s-1}\,d\tau$: its integer-$s$
specialisation is the polynomial $V_{k-2}$-kernel $(X - \tau Y)^{k-2}$,
but at general $s$ we conjecture that $\tau^{s-1}\,d\tau$ is a section
of an infinite-dimensional principal-series local system on $Y(1)$, in
the one-parameter family of the Lewis--Zagier period functions for
Maass forms \cite{LewisZagier2001}; identifying it, and recasting the
Hurwitz inversion of $\delta_T$ as a coboundary statement there, is
open.  Second, a systematic per-mode evaluation of $L^*$ on
interior-pole forms parallel to \eqref{eq:bfk-eq-statement}, adapting
the polylog projection $\widehat{P}_{\tau_p}$ of \cite{1806} to the
finite-contour setting and matching the wall-crossing residues
$\mathcal{R}_{\tau_p}(f, s)$ to the iterated-Eichler-integral framework
of \cite{Brown2017}; the CM cases $\tau_p \in \{i, \rho\}$, where the
Laurent data is arithmetic, are a clean target.  Summed over a
$\Gamma$-orbit, the residues \eqref{eq:residue-formula} assemble into a
weight-$k$ Poincar\'e-style sum in the pole location, taken up
separately.

\section{Algebraic derivation of the Hurwitz-zeta kernel}\label{app:hurwitz-derivation}

The body of Section~\ref{subsec:well-defined} derives $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$ by
differentiating the two-segment functional in $\tau_0$ and demanding the
result vanish.  Here we record the parallel derivation that proceeds
\emph{from} the classical Mellin transform of a holomorphic cusp form,
splitting it at the contour endpoints $\{-1/\tau_0, \tau_0\}$ and
demanding the resulting tail be representable as a $T$-segment
integral.  Both derivations produce the same shift-difference equation
and the same kernel; this appendix makes the algebraic provenance of
the Hurwitz-zeta combination manifest.

\paragraph{Step 1: Mellin split at the contour endpoints.}
Let $f \in S_k$ with $k$ even.  The classical completed $L$-function
$L^*(f, s) = (2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s) L(f, s)$ has integral representation
\begin{equation}
  L^*(f, s)
  \;=\; -i \int_0^{i\infty}\! f(\tau)\,(\tau/i)^{s-1}\,d\tau
  \;=\; i^{-s}\!\int_0^{i\infty}\! f(\tau)\,\tau^{s-1}\,d\tau,
\end{equation}
the second equality using $(\tau/i)^{s-1} = i^{1-s}\tau^{s-1}$ on the
imaginary axis.  Pick $\tau_0 \in \HH$ and split the imaginary-axis
contour at the two endpoints $\{-1/\tau_0, \tau_0\}$ in
\eqref{eq:contour-pair}.  Reorganising the three resulting pieces
using $S$-modularity $f(-1/\tau) = \tau^k f(\tau)$ on the lower segment
$[0, -1/\tau_0]$ (via the substitution $\tau \mapsto -1/\tau$) and
computing the $q$-series-against-$\tau^{s-1}$ integral on
$[\tau_0, i\infty]$ directly yields, for every $\tau_0 \in \HH$ and
every $s \in \CC$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appB-gap}
  L^*(f, s)
  \;=\; i^{-s}\!\int_{-1/\tau_0}^{\tau_0}\! f(\tau)\,\tau^{s-1}\,d\tau
  \;+\; \sum_{n \ge 1} c_f(n)\!
  \left[\frac{\Gamma(s,\,-2\pi i n \tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^s}
  \;+\; i^k\,\frac{\Gamma(k - s,\,-2\pi i n \tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^{k - s}}\right].
\end{equation}
The left side is $\tau_0$-independent (it is the classical $L$-value);
both terms on the right side individually depend on $\tau_0$, but
their sum does not.  At $\tau_0 = i$ the argument $-2\pi i n \tau_0 =
2\pi n$ is real and \eqref{eq:appB-gap} reduces to the standard BFK
incomplete-gamma sum of \cite{BFK2011}.

The right side of \eqref{eq:appB-gap} contains the $S$-segment integral
$i^{-s}\int_{-1/\tau_0}^{\tau_0}$ explicitly; the remaining
incomplete-gamma sum plays the role that we want to be a $T$-segment
integral.

\paragraph{Step 2: ansatz for the $T$-segment kernel.}
Postulate that there exists a kernel $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$,
analytic in $\tau \in \HH$ and meromorphic in $s \in \CC$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appB-ansatz}
  i^{-s}\!\int_{\tau_0-1}^{\tau_0}\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \;=\; \sum_{n \ge 1} c_f(n)\!
  \left[\frac{\Gamma(s,\,-2\pi i n \tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^s}
  \;+\; i^k\,\frac{\Gamma(k - s,\,-2\pi i n \tau_0)}{(2\pi n)^{k - s}}\right]
\end{equation}
for every $f \in S_k$ and every $\tau_0 \in \HH$.  If such a kernel
exists, substituting \eqref{eq:appB-ansatz} into \eqref{eq:appB-gap}
gives the two-segment functional
$L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0}) = i^{-s}[\int_{\gamma^S_{\tau_0}} +
\int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}}]$ as a $\tau_0$-independent expression equal to
$L^*(f, s)$.

\paragraph{Step 3: $\tau_0$-derivative gives a shift-difference equation.}
Differentiate both sides of \eqref{eq:appB-ansatz} in $\tau_0$.  On the
left, the only $\tau_0$-dependence is in the integration limits, so
$d/d\tau_0$ acts as
\begin{equation}
  \frac{d}{d\tau_0}\!\int_{\tau_0-1}^{\tau_0}\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \;=\; f(\tau_0)\!\left[\widetilde{k}_T(\tau_0, s)
  - \widetilde{k}_T(\tau_0 - 1, s)\right],
\end{equation}
using $f(\tau_0 - 1) = f(\tau_0)$ for the lower limit.  On the right
of \eqref{eq:appB-ansatz}, differentiating each incomplete gamma in
its second argument (the explicit $\tau_0$-dependence) and assembling
the Fourier series yields, after canceling a common factor of
$f(\tau_0)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appB-shift-diff}
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau_0, s) \;-\; \widetilde{k}_T(\tau_0 - 1, s)
  \;=\; -\tau_0^{s-1} \;+\; e^{i\pi(s-1)}\,\tau_0^{k - 1 - s}
  \qquad \text{for all } \tau_0 \in \HH.
\end{equation}
The first term on the right is the contribution of the
$\tau^{s-1}$-Mellin endpoint at $\tau_0$; the second term is the
$S$-modularity image of the same endpoint at $-1/\tau_0$, carrying
the branch factor $(-1/\tau_0)^{s-1} = e^{i\pi(s-1)}\tau_0^{-(s-1)}$
together with the modularity Jacobian $\tau_0^k$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:appB-shift-diff} is a shift-difference equation of order
one in $\tau$, with a sum of two power-of-$\tau$ terms on the right
side.

\paragraph{Step 4: Hurwitz raising solves the shift-difference equation.}
The Hurwitz zeta function $\zeta(\sigma, a)$ satisfies the raising
identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appB-hurwitz-raising}
  \zeta(\sigma, a + 1) \;-\; \zeta(\sigma, a) \;=\; -\,a^{-\sigma}
  \qquad (\sigma \in \CC,\ \mathrm{Re}(a) > 0)
\end{equation}
(see \cite[\S25.11.3]{DLMF}).  Applied at $(\sigma, a) = (1 - s, \tau)$
and $(\sigma, a) = (s - (k - 1), \tau)$ respectively, this gives
\begin{equation}
  \zeta(1 - s, \tau + 1) - \zeta(1 - s, \tau) \;=\; -\tau^{s-1},
  \qquad
  \zeta(s - (k - 1), \tau + 1) - \zeta(s - (k - 1), \tau)
  \;=\; -\tau^{k - 1 - s}.
\end{equation}
Combining with the relative phase $e^{i\pi(s-1)}$ from the second
term of \eqref{eq:appB-shift-diff} gives the kernel
\begin{equation}\label{eq:appB-kT-final}
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)
  \;:=\; \zeta(1 - s,\, \tau + 1)
  \;-\; e^{i\pi(s-1)}\,\zeta(s - (k - 1),\, \tau + 1),
\end{equation}
which by construction satisfies \eqref{eq:appB-shift-diff} and hence
\eqref{eq:appB-ansatz}.  The kernel is analytic in $\tau$ on $\HH$
(both Hurwitz zetas have second argument $\tau + 1$ with
$\mathrm{Re}(\tau + 1) > 0$) and meromorphic in $s$ with simple poles
only at $s = 0$ and $s = k$, where the per-mode integrals collect
removable cancellations (see Proposition~\ref{prop:hurwitz-unfold}).
Equation \eqref{eq:appB-kT-final} is the kernel
\eqref{eq:tildekT-def} of the main text.

\paragraph{Where DR2022 stops.}
The kernel \eqref{eq:appB-kT-final} has two Hurwitz-zeta terms.  The
construction of \cite[Thm.~1.2]{DR2022} gives the single-term
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde{k}_T^{\,\rm DR}(\tau, m)
  \;=\; \zeta^*(1 - m, \tau + 1)
\end{equation}
on the segment $[i, i+1]$, at integer $m$, which is the first term of
\eqref{eq:appB-kT-final}.  The structural origin of this gap is the
absence of an $S$-segment in their construction: without the
$S$-modularity-transformed contribution at the $-1/\tau_0$ endpoint
in Step 3, the shift-difference equation \eqref{eq:appB-shift-diff}
has only the single right-hand-side term $-\tau_0^{s-1}$, and its
solution is the single-Hurwitz kernel.  The second
$e^{i\pi(s-1)}\,\tau_0^{k-1-s}$ term appears precisely because the
two-segment functional includes the $S$-segment and its endpoint at
$-1/\tau_0$ contributes under $\tau_0$-differentiation via
$S$-modularity.

\section{Introduction and main results}\label{sec:intro}

In this paper, we introduce a general framework that defines L-functions for 
generic modular forms on the full group $\SLZ$, and extracts their periods for 
the explicit case when the holomorphic cusp forms are one-dimensional, i.e. 
when $\dim(S_k) = 1$. Classically, L-functions and periods are both well-defined 
and well-studied for holomorphic modular forms. However, there is a growing 
body of work exploring periods~\cite{Brown2017} and L-functions~\cite{BFK2011} 
for weakly holomorphic modular forms. Similarly, there are natural physical 
motivations to consider L-functions for properly meromorphic modular forms, 
which have poles \emph{within} the fundamental domain~\cite{1806}.

In these works, and specifically in~\cite{BFK2011, 1806}, and throughout physics, 
divergences due to singularities in quantities of interest necessitate the 
introduction of regulators in order to define finite quantities. Regulators, though, 
often break symmetries, and care is needed to check that the regulator has not 
injected undue violence to the system. Of relevance here is that~\cite{BFK2011} 
introduced a non-holomorphic regulator to deal with the singularities at either 
end of the classical Mellin contour, $\int_0^\infty$, sufficient to regulate 
divergences at the cusps, but which violates holomorphy in the deformed 
contour-integral, ruining path-independence. Simultaneously, \cite{1806} 
concerned itself with L-functions for modular forms which diverge within the 
interior of the fundamental domain. Here, holomorphic dependence on $\tau$ 
is paramount, which puts this treatment at odds with the regulator in~\cite{BFK2011}.

In this note, we introduce manifestly finite contour integral functionals to define 
L-functions for generic meromorphic modular forms that are of sufficient generality 
to handle any combination of poles at cusps and poles within the fundamental domain. 
Our construction is motivated by, and makes precise contact with, work which extracts 
period-coefficients from weak cusp forms~\cite{Brown2017}. Our integrals are 
manifestly finite throughout, and reproduce all results in the literature, unifying the 
quasi-period results and the L-function extensions within one concrete, natural framework.

\subsection{Central definitions and theorems}\label{sec:intro-results}

Throughout, $\Gamma = \SLZ$. In this note, we focus on modular forms on 
$\SLZ$ of integer weight and trivial multiplier system.  Write
$M_k$ for the holomorphic modular forms of weight $k$, $S_k \subset M_k$
for the cusp forms, $M^!_k$ for the weakly holomorphic forms
(holomorphic on $\HH$, with at most a pole at the cusp), and $F_k$ for
the \emph{meromorphic modular forms} of weight $k$: the weight-$k$ part
of the field of fractions of the graded ring $M_* = \bigoplus_k M_k$,
i.e.\ the quotients $f = f_1/f_2$ with $f_i \in M_{k_i}$, $f_2 \ne 0$,
and $k = k_1 - k_2$.  Thus $M_k \subset M^!_k \subset F_k$, and
$F_k = g_0\,\CC(j)$ for any nonzero $g_0 \in F_k$; equivalently, $F_k$
is the space of weight-$k$ functions meromorphic on $\HH$ and at the
cusp. %DAM: This paragraph might need tightening.

Further, throughout, $S_k$ denotes the standard space of 
\emph{holomorphic} cusp-forms of weight-$k$ in $\SLZ$ and $S_k^!$ denotes 
the space of \emph{weakly holomorphic} cusp forms of weight $k$ for $\SLZ$. 
Clearly, $S_k \subset S_k^!$ and any $f \in S_k^!$ has a q-series with zero 
constant term, i.e. if $f = \sum_{n \in \ZZ} c(n) q^n$ then $c(0) = 0$ and 
$c(n) = 0$ for every $n \leq N$ for some finite integer $N$.%DAM: This paragraph might need tightening.

We denote the unit-normalized Eisenstein-series by $E_k$:
$E_k = 1 + O(q) \in M_k$. In this context, the modular discriminant, $\Delta$, is
$\Delta := (E_4^3-E_6^2) = q + {\cal O}(q^2) \in S_{12}$. Similarly, the j-invariant 
$j = 1/q + {\cal O}(q^0) \in M_0^!$ is given by 
$j = 1728 E_4^3/(E_4^3-E_6^2)$, and denotes the Hauptmodul of $\SLZ$. 
Further, at $k = 12$ we may define $\Dhat := 1/q + {\cal O}(q^2) \in S_{12}^!$. 
This $\Dhat = (j^2 - c_1 j + c_0) \Delta$ for very easily computed integers $c_1, c_0$. %DAM: This paragraph might need tightening. In particular, we want to write the correct $c_1, c_0$.

We begin with three definitions:

\begin{definition}[Hurwitz T-kernel]\label{def:HurwitzT}
Let $\zeta(s, x)$ denote the classical Hurwitz zeta-function, $\zeta(s,x):= \sum_{n \geq 0} (n+x)^{-s}$. We define the \emph{Hurwitz T-kernel} as,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tildekT-def}
  \widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)
  \;:=\; \zeta(1 - s,\, \tau + 1)
  \;-\; e^{i\pi(s-1)}\,\zeta(s - (k - 1),\, \tau + 1)~.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[General paths along torus cycles]\label{def:contours}
Let $\tau_0 \in \HH$, and consider any $\sigma \in \SLZ$. We denote the path from $\sigma^{1}(\tau_0) \to \tau_0$ by $\gamma^\sigma_{\tau_0}$. Further, let $k \in 2\ZZ$ on $\SLZ$ be a meromorphic modular form which may have poles at cusps and may have poles within the interior of the upper half-plane. Now, consider $\sigma \in \{ S, T \} = \{ \left( \begin{smallmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right), \left( \begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right) \}$. Here, $\gamma^\sigma_{\tau_0}$ is a smooth contour,
\begin{align}
\begin{cases}
  S\text{-segment}\colon\:\:\; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}:[0, 1] \to \HH~,&~~ 
  \gamma^S_{\tau_0}(0)=-1/\tau_0,\, \gamma^S_{\tau_0}(1) =\tau_0,
  \\
  T\text{-segment}\colon\:\:\; \gamma^T_{\tau_0}:[0, 1] \to \HH~,&~~ 
  \gamma^T_{\tau_0}(0)=\tau_0-1,\, \gamma^T_{\tau_0}(1) =\tau_0,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:contour-pair}
\end{align}
in $\HH$ avoids the $\Gamma$-orbit of the poles of $f$ in $\HH$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[L-integral of a meromorphic modular form]\label{def:L-integral}
Let $f \in F_k$ be a meromorphic modular form of weight $k \in 2\ZZ$ on $\SLZ$, and let $\tau_0 \in \HH$. Using the contour in Definition~\ref{def:contours} and the Hurwitz T-kernel in Definition~\ref{def:HurwitzT}, we define the \emph{L-integral} of $f$ to be,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lambda-DR-def}
  L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}
  , \gamma^T_{\tau_0})
  \;:=\; i^{-s}\!\left[
    \int_{\gamma^S_{\tau_0}}
    \! f(\tau)\,\tau^{s-1}\,d\tau
    \;+\;
    \int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}} 
    f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \right].
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

When the contour avoids poles in $f$, both contour integrals contributing to 
$L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$
in~\eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-def} are finite for every basepoint
$\tau_0 \in \HH$ admissible for $f$ and every $s \in \CC$; no regulator,
asymptotic-subtraction argument, or analytic-continuation step is
required.  Further, as we will show, $L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ 
is invariant under both $\tau_0$-variations and $\gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0}$-variations, 
as long as neither $\gamma^S_{\tau_0}$ nor  $\gamma^T_{\tau_0}$ cross any pole location within 
the fundamental domain. 

We will show that $L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})$ is the

\begin{theorem}[Periods of $f \in S_k^!$ as an explicit linear functional ($\dim S_k = 1$)]\label{thm:periods}

\begin{theorem}[$L$-function for weakly holomorphic forms]\label{thm:bfk-eq}
Let $k \in 2\ZZ$ and $f \in M^!_k$.  With $\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)$
the Hurwitz-zeta kernel of \eqref{eq:tildekT-def}, for every $\tau_0
\in \HH$ and every $s \in \CC$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lambda-DR-general}
 L^*(f, s; \gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0})
  \;:=\; i^{-s}\!\left[
    \int_{\gamma^S_{\tau_0}}\! f(\tau)\,\tau^{s-1}\,d\tau
    \;+\;
    \int_{\gamma^T_{\tau_0}}\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \right]
\end{equation}
is finite and independent of both $\tau_0 \in \HH$ and of the
admissible contour choices $\gamma^S_{\tau_0}, \gamma^T_{\tau_0}$.  We
write $L^*(f, s)$ for the common value. Specializing to $\tau_0 = i$ , the 
$S$-integral in \eqref{eq:Lambda-DR-general} vanishes, and $L^*$ reduces 
to a single integral on the compact contour $[i - 1, i] \subset \HH$, with
closed-form sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bfk-eq-statement}
  L^*(f, s)
  \;=\; i^{-s}\!\int_{i - 1}^{i}\! f(\tau)\,\widetilde{k}_T(\tau, s)\,d\tau
  \;=\; -c_f(0)\!\left(\frac{1}{s} + \frac{i^k}{k - s}\right)
  + \sum_{n \ne 0}\! c_f(n)\!\left[
    \frac{\Gamma(s,\, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^s}
    + i^k\,\frac{\Gamma(k - s,\, 2\pi n)}{(2\pi n)^{k-s}}
  \right].
\end{equation}
The right side of \eqref{eq:bfk-eq-statement} is the completed
$L$-function $L^*(f, s)$ of $f$. These L-integrals reproduce the 
Hecke-Weil Mellin transform for holomorphic modular forms, as 
well as reproduce extant formulae from the literature for weakly 
holomorphic modular forms~\cite{BFK2011}.
\end{theorem}

well as reproduce extant formulae from the literature for weakly 
holomorphic modular forms~\cite{BFK2011} in section~\ref{sec:Connection-w-previous-weak-MF-l-functions}.

\paragraph{The structural picture.}
The contour pair \eqref{eq:contour-pair} has two natural one-parameter
specialisations of the basepoint, each exploiting a fixed point of the
modular curve $Y(1)$: the cusp specialisation $\tau_0 \to i\infty$

$L^*$ as a single integral on $[i - 1, i]$.  The cusp

$\Lambda$-truncated Mellin contour of $L_f^*(s)_{\rm int}$

\section{Introduction and main results}\label{sec:intro}\label{sec:intro-results}

In this paper, we introduce a general framework that defines L-functions for
generic modular forms on the full group $\SLZ$, and extracts their periods for
the explicit case when the holomorphic cusp forms are one-dimensional, i.e.
when $\dim(S_k) = 1$. Classically, L-functions and periods are both well-defined
and well-studied for holomorphic modular forms. However, there is a growing
body of work exploring periods~\cite{Brown2017} and L-functions~\cite{BFK2011}
for weakly holomorphic modular forms. Similarly, there are natural physical
motivations to consider L-functions for properly meromorphic modular forms,
which have poles \emph{within} the fundamental domain~\cite{1806}.

In these works, and specifically in~\cite{BFK2011, 1806}, and throughout physics,
divergences due to singularities in quantities of interest necessitate the
introduction of regulators in order to define finite quantities. Regulators, though,
often break symmetries, and care is needed to check that the regulator has not
injected undue violence to the system. Of relevance here is that~\cite{BFK2011}
introduced a non-holomorphic regulator to deal with the singularities at either
end of the classical Mellin contour, $\int_0^\infty$, sufficient to regulate
divergences at the cusps, but which violates holomorphy in the deformed
contour-integral, ruining path-independence. Simultaneously, \cite{1806}
concerned itself with L-functions for modular forms which diverge within the
interior of the fundamental domain. Here, holomorphic dependence on $\tau$
is paramount, which puts this treatment at odds with the regulator in~\cite{BFK2011}.

In this note, we introduce manifestly finite contour integral functionals to define
L-functions for generic meromorphic modular forms that are of sufficient generality
to handle any combination of poles at cusps and poles within the fundamental domain.
Our construction is motivated by, and makes precise contact with, work which extracts
period-coefficients from weak cusp forms~\cite{Brown2017}. Our integrals are
manifestly finite throughout, and reproduce all results in the literature, unifying the
quasi-period results and the L-function extensions within one concrete, natural framework.

The construction is easiest to motivate by asking what, precisely, breaks
when the classical definitions are moved from holomorphic to weakly
holomorphic or meromorphic forms.  The period polynomial of a cusp form
$f \in S_k$ is
\[
  r_f(X, Y) \;=\; \int_0^{i\infty}\! f(\tau)\,(X - \tau Y)^{k-2}\,d\tau,
\]
an integral between the cusps $i\infty$ and $0 = S\!\cdot\! i\infty$.  The
choice of endpoints is doing real work here: because they are exchanged by
the modular inversion $S$, the modularity of $f$ converts the contour data
into the algebraic constraints --- the period relations --- that $r_f$
satisfies.  The cusp is where the group enters the classical construction.
It is also, for $f$ with a pole at the cusp, exactly where the integral
diverges.  The extensions in the literature keep the contour and repair
the divergence: \cite{BFK2011} with a non-holomorphic regulator at the
endpoints, \cite{1806} with contour deformation and asymptotic
subtraction.  We do the opposite: we keep the group and discard the
contour's boundary anchoring, replacing the cusp's role with a
$1$-cocycle for $\Gamma$.

To this end, set $n := k - 2$ and let $V_n = \CC[X, Y]_n$ carry the right
$\Gamma$-action $(P|_\gamma)(X, Y) = P(aX + bY,\, cX + dY)$.  The
weight-$k$ modularity of $f$ makes the $V_n$-valued form
$\omega_f = f(\tau)(X - \tau Y)^n\,d\tau$ invariant under the simultaneous
action of $\Gamma$ on $\tau$ and on $(X, Y)$, so the integrals
\[
  C^f_\gamma(\tau_0)
  \;=\; (2\pi i)^{n+1}\!\int_{\gamma^{-1}\tau_0}^{\tau_0}\!\omega_f,
  \qquad \gamma \in \Gamma,
\]
based at an arbitrary interior point $\tau_0 \in \HH$, satisfy the cocycle
relation $C^f_{gh} = C^f_g|_h + C^f_h$.  This relation is the bookkeeping
the cusp used to perform, now executed entirely in the interior of $\HH$.
Since $\Gamma = \langle S, T\rangle$, the cocycle is determined by its two
generator values: the integrals of $\omega_f$ over the contour pair that
we now fix once and for all.

Both segments run between finite interior points of $\HH$.  Finiteness of
every integral in this paper is therefore structural rather than
arranged: no contour ever reaches the locus where divergence lives, so

The price of trading the contour for a cocycle is a redundancy.  Moving
the basepoint $\tau_0$ shifts $C^f$ by a coboundary
$\delta P = P|_{(\cdot)} - P$, so the well-defined invariant is the
cohomology class $[C^f] \in H^1(\Gamma; V_n)$, and by Eichler--Shimura
\cite{KZ1984, Brown2017} the periods $\omega^\pm(f)$ and quasi-periods
$\eta^\pm(f)$ are coordinates of this class on the cuspidal subspace.
Coordinates, however, are read off a representative, not a class, so
extracting numbers requires fixing the gauge.  There is a canonical
choice.  Among the generators, $T$ is the unique one subject to no
relation of its own ($T$ has infinite order in $\Gamma$, while $S$ and
$ST$ have finite order); it is the direction one is free to spend, and we
spend it by demanding $C^f_T \mapsto 0$, i.e.\ by solving the coboundary
equation $\delta_T P = (2\pi i)^{-(n+1)} C^f_T(\tau_0)$ on $V_n$.
Solvability is itself arithmetic: $T$ acts on $V_n$ as the translation
$X \mapsto X + Y$, so the operator $\delta_T = e^{Y\partial_X} - 1$
misses exactly one coefficient, and unit-segment $T$-periodicity
identifies that coefficient of $C^f_T$ as the constant Fourier mode of
$f$.  The gauge exists if and only if $c_f(0) = 0$.  Cuspidality, in this
framework, is not a convergence hypothesis; it is the integrability
condition of the gauge.

Fixing the gauge does two things at once.  First, it forces the entire
cuspidal content onto the surviving cycle: with $C^f_T$ gauged away, the
period polynomial \emph{is} the $S$-cycle value of the gauge-fixed
cocycle, $r_f = C^f_S - (2\pi i)^{n+1}(P|_S - P)$, and the classical
period relations on $r_f$ are the group relations of $\Gamma$ conditioned
on the gauge.  Second, the gauge is not free of charge, and the receipt
is the central object of this paper.

The price of trading one divergent contour for a family of finite ones
is redundancy: each $C^f_\gamma(\tau_0)$ depends on an auxiliary
basepoint $\tau_0$ and on a group element $\gamma$, while the period
polynomial must refer to neither.  Degree-one group cohomology is the
standard bookkeeping for exactly this situation, and only three
definitions are needed.  A \emph{$1$-cocycle} is a function
$c\colon \Gamma \to V_n$ satisfying $c_{gh} = c_g|_h + c_h$, and
$\gamma \mapsto C^f_\gamma(\tau_0)$ is one for each fixed $\tau_0$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:cocycle}).  A \emph{$1$-coboundary} is the special
cocycle $\delta P\colon \gamma \mapsto P|_\gamma - P$ built from a
polynomial $P \in V_n$; by \eqref{eq:bp-cocycle-coboundary}, shifting
$\tau_0 \to \tau_0'$ (no pole crossed) shifts the whole family
$\{C^f_\gamma\}_\gamma$ by one such $\delta P$ --- the same $P$ for
every $\gamma$, which is why the quotient retains anything at all.  The
\emph{cohomology class}
$[C^f] \in H^1(\Gamma; V_n) :=
\{\text{cocycles}\}/\{\text{coboundaries}\}$ is therefore
basepoint-free, and a \emph{representative} of it is any cocycle
$C^f - \delta P$.  This kills the
$\tau_0$. %CLAUDE: replaces the former lines 313--320, which described coboundaries as polynomials and the equivalence as acting on each C^f_\gamma separately.

A reader outside the field might object that no machinery should be
needed: the classical period polynomial is a single integral along one
$S$-contour, so why not take the finite $S$-contour $C^f_S(\tau_0)$ and
stop?  Because at a finite basepoint the raw $S$-value is pure
redundancy.  Torsion alone forces
$C^f_S\big|(1+S) = C^f_{S^2} = C^f_{-I} = 0$ at \emph{every} $\tau_0$,
and since $S$ acts on $V_n$ as an involution, its $(-1)$-eigenspace is
also the image of $\delta_S$: the value $C^f_S$ lies in
$\ker(1+S) = \im \delta_S$, exactly the space of shifts that basepoint
motion induces on it.  (At $k = 12$ this kernel is six-dimensional
inside the eleven-dimensional $V_{10}$; membership selects a subspace,
never a point.)  The two relations cutting out the Kohnen--Zagier space
$W_{k-2}$ \cite{KZ1984} therefore have different statuses: the
$S$-relation is \emph{free}, holding before any choice is made, while
the $U$-relation must be \emph{purchased} --- it appears only after the
representative is pinned, and pinning is the role of the other
generator.

The generator $T$ is built for the job.  It has infinite order and acts
on $V_n$ unipotently --- a single Jordan block --- so
$\delta_T := (\,\cdot\,)|_T - \mathrm{id}$ has one-dimensional kernel
and cokernel: the equation has one scalar of obstruction and one scalar
of slack.  We choose, within the class, the representative
$C^f - \delta P$ \emph{whose $T$-value vanishes}, i.e.\ solve
$\delta_T P = (2\pi i)^{-(n+1)} C^f_T(\tau_0)$ on $V_n$.  Solvability is
itself arithmetic: unit-segment $T$-periodicity identifies the
obstruction in $V_n / \im \delta_T \cong \CC \cdot [X^n]$ as the
constant Fourier mode (Lemma~\ref{lem:solvability-cuspidality}),
$[C^f_T]_{X^n} = (2\pi i)^{n+1} c_f(0)$ --- the residue of $\omega_f$ at
the very cusp that $T$ stabilizes.  The normalized representative exists
if and only if $c_f(0) = 0$; classes that pass are the \emph{parabolic}
ones, identified by Eichler--Shimura \cite{KZ1984} with
$S_k \oplus \overline{S_k}$, the lone obstructed direction being
Eisenstein.  Cuspidality, in this framework, is not a convergence
hypothesis; it is the solvability condition for the normalization.

With the $T$-value normalized away, the entire cuspidal content sits on
the surviving generator value,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-normalized}
  r_f \;:=\; C^f_S(\tau_0) \;-\; (2\pi i)^{n+1}\bigl(P|_S - P\bigr),
\end{equation}
well-defined up to the line $\CC\,(X^n - Y^n)$ left over from
$\ker \delta_T = \CC\, Y^n$ --- an ambiguity touching only the boundary
monomials, which is why Theorem~\ref{thm:periods} ranges over the
interior $\ell$ alone.  The $U$-relation now arrives, purchased as
promised: conditioned on the vanishing $T$-value, the group relation
$(ST)^3 = -I$ becomes $r_f|\bigl(1 + (TS) + (TS)^2\bigr) = 0$, and $r_f$
\emph{is} the period polynomial.  Indeed, for holomorphic $f \in S_k$,
sending $\tau_0 \to i\infty$ kills $C^f_T$ outright and
\eqref{eq:rf-normalized} collapses to the classical integral
\eqref{eq:classical-rf} --- whose familiar freedom from $\tau_0$ and
$\gamma$ is thereby revealed as the silent choices $\gamma = S$,
$\tau_0 = i\infty$.  Its interior coefficients are the periods against
Brown's rational basis,
$[r_f]_{X^{k-2-\ell}Y^\ell} = \omega^\pm(f)\, c_S^{\,\ell}$; the precise
coordinate statement is given in Section~\ref{sec:cocycle}.  The finite
$S$-contour was the right object all along, but it requires the
correction term in \eqref{eq:rf-normalized}, sourced entirely by the
$T$-contour --- and producing that correction in closed form is the
business of this paper.

\paragraph{Parabolic cohomology and periods.}
The cohomology class $[C^f] \in H^1(\Gamma; V_n)$ is independent of
$\tau_0$ (on the connected component) and of the choice of $\delta P$
representative.  Its restriction to the cusp stabilizer
$\langle T \rangle \cong \ZZ$ lands in
$H^1(\langle T \rangle; V_n) = V_n / \im \delta_T \cong \CC\cdot[X^n]$,
and by Lemma~\ref{lem:solvability-cuspidality}(c) the restricted class is
$(2\pi i)^{n+1}\, c_f(0) \cdot [X^n]$: the residue of $\omega_f$ at the
cusp, valued in the $T$-coinvariants.  The class is \emph{parabolic} ---
cohomologous to a cocycle with vanishing $T$-value --- if and only if
this residue vanishes, i.e.\ if and only if $f \in S^!_k$.  The classical
Eichler--Shimura theorem \cite{KZ1984} identifies
$H^1(\Gamma; V_n) \cong S_k \oplus \overline{S_k} \oplus
\CC_{\mathrm{Eis}}$ and the parabolic subspace
$\Hpar(\Gamma; V_n) \cong S_k \oplus \overline{S_k}$; on cocycles
normalized so that $c_T = 0$, the assignment $c \mapsto c_S$ further
identifies $\Hpar(\Gamma; V_n) \cong W_{k-2}/\CC\,(X^n - Y^n)$, with the
quotient line accounting for the residual freedom
$P \mapsto P + \CC\, Y^n$.  When $\dim S_k = 1$, each parity eigenspace
of $\Hpar$ is one-dimensional, and the two coordinates of a parabolic
class in Brown's rational basis $p^\pm_f$ are its periods:
$[C^f]^\pm = \omega^\pm(f)[p^\pm_f]$ for $f \in S_k$, while the
quasi-periods $\eta^\pm$ arise as the coordinates of the class
$[C^{f'}]$ of a weakly holomorphic Hecke partner $f' \in S^!_k$ (at
$k = 12$: $f = \Delta$, $f' = \Dhat$)
\cite{Brown2017}. %CLAUDE: corrected -- the previous version presented \omega^\pm and \eta^\pm jointly as "the linear coordinates of [C^f]"; a single class in the two-dimensional space \Hpar has two coordinates, and the four numbers of the period matrix come from the two classes [C^\Delta] and [C^\Dhat].
